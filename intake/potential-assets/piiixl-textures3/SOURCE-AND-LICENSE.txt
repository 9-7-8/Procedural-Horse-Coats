Every tile is 16x16 and FULLY OPAQUE - not one non-opaque pixel in the 162, so
any transparency a block needs is ours to cut.

THE PALETTE IS DAWNBRINGER 16, exactly
  All 162 tiles together use sixteen colours and no more, and they are byte-for-
  byte DB16 - Richard "DawnBringer" Fenske's 2012 community palette (140c1c,
  442434, 30346d, 4e4a4e, 854c30, 346524, d04648, 757161, 597dce, d27d2c,
  8595a1, 6daa2c, d2aa99, 6dc2ca, dad45e, deeed6).
  "2-bit" is about each tile, not the pack: most tiles use three or four of
  those colours (105 use four, 38 use three, 5 use two), which is why they read
  as two-bit art.
  This matters twice over. It makes recolouring trivial and exact - a 16-entry
  lookup, no resampling, no antialiasing to fight, which is the mod's own
  gradient trick applied to a tile. And it means the palette itself carries none
  of the author's rights: DB16 is a community palette in wide use, so a tile
  redrawn on it is not thereby derivative of this pack.

At 16x16 these are the smallest tiles of any pack here, and seamless, which is
exactly the shape Minecraft block art wants. That is what makes the licence
below worth settling rather than shrugging at.

THE OWNER'S DIRECTION, 2026-09-29
  These are not used one-to-one, here or in any pack: "at the very least we'll
  recolor it", to make new blocks. Worth being precise about what that buys,
  because it is not what it looks like: the No Redistribution clause below says
  "whether original or modified", so recolouring alone does not step outside it.
  What actually carries the use is the grant to use and modify in projects, plus
  not putting the pack in the repo, plus the carve-back in the notices file.

*** THE PROBLEM: "No Extraction" versus a Minecraft mod ***
============================================================
Read that third clause against what this project actually ships. A mod jar is a
zip. Every texture in it is a PNG that any player can pull out with any archive
tool, and resource-pack authors are expected to. Putting one of these tiles in
`assets/horsegenetics/textures/` therefore does exactly what the No Extraction
clause forbids: it distributes the asset as a standalone file, and it lets others
extract and reuse it as one. "Whether original or modified" closes the obvious
escape, so recolouring a tile or pasting it into a sheet does not clear it.

Two further points, neither of them in our favour:
  - This mod is CC BY-NC. Forks may redistribute what is in the repo. We cannot
    pass on rights we do not hold, so a file from this pack in the repo would be
    handing forks a permission the author explicitly withholds. Compare the Raven
    pack, whose notes carve that back explicitly - but the Raven licence has a
    MODS clause that contemplates exactly this and permits it on conditions.
    This licence has no mods clause at all.
  - The mod's own coat pipeline reads source PNGs out of the jar at runtime, so
    even art that is only an input, never a shipped sheet, is still a standalone
    file sitting in the archive.

WHAT IS STILL CLEARLY ALLOWED
  - Looking at them. "Use" and "Modify" are granted; it is only distribution
    that is barred. They can sit on the machine and inform work.
  - Redrawing. A tile studied and then drawn fresh by us is our file, and the
    seamless 16x16 idiom is a technique, not the author's property.

WHAT NEEDS THE AUTHOR'S PERMISSION FIRST
  - Any tile, modified or not, that ends up inside the jar or the repo.
    The ask is small and specific, and piiixl answers their comments: may these
    textures be included in a free, open-source Minecraft mod, whose files are
    necessarily extractable from the jar and whose own licence lets others
    redistribute the mod? Paste the reply here verbatim. Until then the answer
    is no, and this is a note in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md we cannot write honestly.
