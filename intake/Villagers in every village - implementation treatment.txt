Villagers in every village - implementation treatment

Drafted in a session confined to intake/ (nothing outside this folder was touched).
Nothing here is built or run. "Decided" lines are the owner's request as given;
"Open" lines are for the building session to settle; "UNVERIFIED" lines are facts
this file has not proved. Source of truth once routed: wiki/villagers.html (its
Gameplay and Coding tabs, and a Roadmap tab until it ships) and wiki/stables.html
("who made each building"). Retired (deleted, per START HERE step 3) as each of the
two pieces ships.

The request, as given
======================
1) Make the villagers spawn in every single village, regardless of biome.
2) Add a command that idempotently adds them to already-generated villages.

"The villagers" means this mod's horse-trade people: the COWBOY (an entity, in
the barn) and the FOUR EQUESTRIANS (ordinary villagers who take a job at one of
four posts). Open, confirm with the owner: that reading. Nothing else in the mod
is a villager.

What already exists, so the building session does not redo it
==============================================================
- Both groups come out of ONE structure: neoforge-26.1.2/src/main/resources/data/
  horsegenetics/structure/cowboy_barn.nbt (baked from tools/barn/*.source.nbt by
  tools/barn/bake-barn.py). It carries a horsegenetics:cowboy entity with nothing
  in it, the Cowboy Hitch, and the equestrians' posts as blocks. CowboyHandler's
  class note says it outright: the equestrians "are not built here at all: their
  post is a block in cowboy_barn.nbt ... and the villager who takes the job off it
  is" an ordinary villager claiming a free job site (ModPoiTypes: four POI types,
  maxTickets 1 each, in the acquirable_job_site tag). So "villagers in every
  village" is "the barn in every village" - there is no separate villager spawn,
  and a village whose barn did not generate has no cowboy and no equestrians.
  (An equestrian also needs an unemployed villager to be there to take the job;
  every vanilla village has some. A modded village with no villagers will have
  posts and no one at them - note it, do not solve it.)
- worldgen/BarnPoolInjector (ServerAboutToStartEvent) appends the barn to ONE
  vanilla pool: minecraft:village/plains/terminators. That is the whole reason
  the barn is a PLAINS thing today. Its class note explains the terminator
  mechanism (the ends of streets; both streets and houses name it as fallback)
  and why it is code and not a datapack (a datapack can only REPLACE a pool,
  never add to it; replacing freezes vanilla's entries and deletes other mods').
  Its idempotency pattern (compare against rawTemplates, append only what is
  missing, bump templates by weight, reset maxSize to Integer.MIN_VALUE) is the
  pattern to reuse; it already survives quit-to-title-and-reload.
- It needs the access transformer on StructureTemplatePool (rawTemplates,
  templates, maxSize). No new transformer is needed to inject into more pools.
- worldgen/HomesteadCensus counts how often the homestead lands in a village,
  over many seeds, "without generating a single chunk" (93.2% single-connector,
  98.9% three-connector, measured over 265 plains villages, per the injector's
  note). It is the tool that turns "does the barn reach every village" from an
  argument into a number; the last time it was argued instead of counted the
  argument was out by a factor of five. Extend it to the new pools and READ THE
  NUMBERS before choosing a weight.
- CowboyHandler founds exactly ONE cowboy per village (ONE_PER_RADIUS = 128): of
  cowboys within that radius, one founds and the rest remove themselves. A
  village that rolls two barns gets two barns. This already makes "inject into
  more pools" safe against doubling, and is also what the backfill command must
  not defeat (see piece 2).
- The barn's look: baked from plains-village materials (the template pool applies
  minecraft:mossify_10_percent). UNVERIFIED but likely: a spruce/oak plains barn
  in a desert or snowy village will read as foreign, and as it comes from a baked
  .nbt it will NOT take on the biome's wood the way vanilla's own houses do
  (vanilla ships a separate .nbt per village style). Stables.html names the
  precedent: three generated stables, each with its own builder and style.

Two pieces, smallest blast radius first, each shippable alone.

1. The barn generates in EVERY village style
---------------------------------------------
What the player sees: a cowboy's barn at the edge of any village they find - desert,
savanna, snowy, taiga, plains, and a modded village if it can take one - not only
plains.
- Decided: regardless of biome. So the plains-only injection widens to the
  other four vanilla styles and, as far as it can, other mods' villages.
- Pools to append to (UNVERIFIED names - read them from the vanilla data pack
  in the SDK jar rather than trusting this list):
    minecraft:village/plains/terminators      (already done)
    minecraft:village/desert/terminators
    minecraft:village/savanna/terminators
    minecraft:village/snowy/terminators
    minecraft:village/taiga/terminators
  Check each exists, that each is the end-of-street fallback in its own style's
  streets/houses pools, and that the junction type (minecraft:street) the barn's
  three connectors use is the SAME in every style - the barn's connectors are
  `minecraft:street` jigsaws, and the plains barn's note says a mismatch means
  the piece is thrown out silently. UNVERIFIED that the snowy and taiga streets
  use the same name; if one differs, a barn variant with that connector name is
  needed (a second source .nbt through bake-barn.py, which already splices two
  source files and says "Splicing two files together by palette index is a bug
  waiting to happen" - read the warning before touching it).
- Open, and it is the main design call: ONE barn or a barn PER STYLE.
    (a) one plains barn everywhere - the cheapest, the least pretty, and the
        only one that needs no art. A desert village gets a spruce barn.
    (b) a variant per style - the same layout, swapped palette, baked by
        bake-barn.py with a per-style block remap (planks, logs, stairs, slabs,
        fences, roof, path). Four more nbt files, four more pool entries, and
        the mossify processor chosen per style (moss on a desert barn is wrong;
        use no processor or a sand-friendly one). This is what vanilla does and
        what "regardless of biome" implies for a build that looks intended.
    (c) one barn, but with a RUN-TIME palette swap at placement via a
        StructureProcessor keyed on the village's style. Fewest files; a
        processor is real code, and per-block remaps of stairs/slabs/doors keep
        their state only if written carefully. Rejected unless (b) turns out to
        need too many files.
  Recommend (b), with (a) as the first slice so the mechanism is proven and the
  art follows. Ship (a) for all five pools; then replace the entries one style
  per session.
- Open: the weight. The injector gave plains weight 3 against four vanilla
  terminators of weight 1 and reached 98.9% of villages. Other styles have a
  DIFFERENT number of vanilla terminators; do not copy 3. Run HomesteadCensus
  per style and pick the weight that gets to the same ballpark, remembering the
  note's warning: raising the weight crowds out vanilla's own endpieces and
  changes how every village of that style looks.
- Open: other mods' villages. A mod that adds its own village style has its own
  pools, and the injector cannot know their names. Two honest options: a
  data-driven tag listing terminator pools to inject into (a resource-pack-style
  file the mod reads at ServerAboutToStart - NOT a worldgen tag, which a missing
  entry would fail whole, the way the Cowboy Hitch in a POI tag broke vanilla's
  village POIs for four days, see ModPoiTypes), or "scan the registry for every
  pool whose path ends in /terminators and whose element names a minecraft:street
  connector". The scan is more automatic and more dangerous (it would also hit
  non-village jigsaws). Recommend the config list with the five vanilla pools as
  the default, and a compatibility.html line for the rest. Not a promise.
- Open: where in common/. The decision "which pools, at which weight" is a table
  of resource-location strings and integers; it needs nothing from Minecraft, so
  keep the table in common/ (so the 1.12.2 backport and the wiki see it) and
  keep only the pool surgery in worldgen/BarnPoolInjector. Check that common/
  already has a Region / BiomeAnalogues idiom this should follow, and that it
  adds nothing third-party.
- Also verify the cowboy's own biome-dependent behaviour does not assume plains:
  CowboyHandler founds a paddock "at the barn's own level" and picks breeds via
  Breeds.forBiome(biome, BreedSource.COWBOY). In a desert village the breed list
  for desert may be empty or small, and the cowboy would found with nothing.
  UNVERIFIED how it falls back; read CowboyHandler.found before shipping, and
  decide: a desert cowboy sells the desert's breeds, or the world's, or Feral
  Mixed? ("Regardless of biome" is about where the villagers APPEAR, not about
  what they trade; do not flatten the breed system by accident.)
- Ships with: the pool entries (code, in the injector), any new .nbt through
  bake-barn.py (CLAUDE.md table: regenerate, commit the nbt), the config list if
  option (a) of the modded half is taken, a gametest that generates a village of
  each style at a fixed seed and asserts the barn is there (ModGameTests already
  does this for plains via the census; extend it), and a wiki update.
- In-game matrix (record on villagers.html's Verification tab, unplayed):
  find one village of each style, and a modded one if installed; the barn is at
  the edge; the cowboy founds a paddock and a herd; the four posts are present and
  four villagers take them over a day or two; the barn is not buried or floating
  on each style's terrain; it does not double up; nothing else in the village
  moved or vanished (the weight is shared with vanilla's endpieces).

2. A command that adds them to villages that already exist
-----------------------------------------------------------
What the player (an OP) sees: a command they run once on a world that is already
explored, and every village that has no barn gets one; run it again and nothing
changes.
- Decided: the command is IDEMPOTENT. Running it N times equals running it once.
- Decided: it works on ALREADY-GENERATED villages. Jigsaw generation runs once,
  at chunk generation; it cannot be re-run on a village that is already in the
  world. So piece 2 cannot be "regenerate" - it must PLACE a barn structure into
  the existing village after the fact. This is the hard half of the request.
- Name and shape (Open, suggest): `/horsevillage backfill` with arguments
  `[radius]` (default: the villages within a number of chunks of the executor)
  and `dry-run`. Brigadier, op level, registered through RegisterCommandsEvent
  the way HorseResurrectCommand and DebugTestWorldHandler register theirs (copy
  the permission style of the existing non-debug command, HorseResurrectCommand,
  not the testkit's). Open: a `/horsevillage list` to show villages with and
  without a barn, so an owner can see before they act. Both are cheap.
- Finding the villages: each village is a StructureStart in the chunk that holds
  its centre. Use the structure manager (ServerLevel.structureManager()) and the
  structure registry with the five village structure ids
  (minecraft:village_plains, _desert, _savanna, _snowy, _taiga - UNVERIFIED
  exact ids; and the TagKey minecraft:village if the SDK has one for structures,
  which would also cover modded villages that tag themselves, UNVERIFIED).
  Only look at chunks that are already generated: iterate the loaded and
  on-disk chunk range without forcing generation - getChunkNow / the chunk
  holder map, or the per-region file listing. UNVERIFIED which of these the
  SDK allows without generating. The failure to avoid is a command that, by
  asking about a chunk, makes the server generate thousands of chunks; it must
  be bounded by the argument radius and must refuse to generate. Using a
  one-shot `/locate`-style search (ChunkGenerator.findNearestMapStructure) is
  the cheap alternative for "nearest N villages" but it finds only
  not-yet-generated starts as readily as generated ones, so check
  hasChunk(...) before touching.
- Deciding a village already HAS a barn - this is the idempotency key. Three
  candidate signals; use more than one and let the command report which it saw:
    (i)  a StructureStart piece whose template is horsegenetics:cowboy_barn
         (works for villages generated AFTER piece 1 ships, because the barn is
         a jigsaw piece of the village's own StructureStart; HomesteadCensus
         already walks PoolElementStructurePiece to find it - reuse that code);
    (ii) the Cowboy Hitch block, or a cowboy entity, within the village's
         bounding box plus a margin (CowboyHitchHandler finds the hitch "by
         looking for the block", so a block search is already a pattern here);
    (iii) a record the command writes itself: a per-world SavedData keyed on the
         village's StructureStart chunk position ("backfilled"), written
         AFTER a successful placement. This is the one that makes a second run
         a true no-op even if the player has since demolished the barn - and
         the one that is wrong if they WANT it back. Decide: a `--force` to
         ignore (iii). Without (iii) alone, a barn the owner removed is rebuilt
         on every run, which is a defect, not idempotency.
  A village is skipped if ANY of (i)-(iii) is true. Write the log line with the
  reason so "why did it skip this one" is answerable from the log.
- Placing the barn (the genuinely new code; nothing like it exists in the mod):
    * Find a site on the village's edge. A village is a set of pieces, each with
      a BoundingBox; the barn's footprint (read the nbt size; the injector's note
      says "the better part of a chunk square") must fit outside every piece's
      box with a margin, on ground that is close enough to level, not water, not
      lava, not inside another structure. Candidate sites: for each of the four
      sides of the village's bounding box, sample positions along it; take the
      one with the least height variation. Skip the village if none fits and
      SAY SO in the report - never place a barn into a house.
    * Rotate it to face the village (the barn's doors face its road; the
      injector's note on three connectors says the walk is the thing that should
      meet a street). Four rotations x candidate sites, choose by lowest
      overlap. This is the same problem the jigsaw generator solves; a
      simplified version (no connection to the road network) is enough, but a
      road/path block run from the barn's walk to the nearest village path is a
      nice-to-have, not required. Open.
    * Place with StructureTemplate.placeInWorld, fed through the SAME processor
      list the pool element uses (mossify) so a backfilled barn and a generated
      one agree. Spawn the cowboy entity the way the barn does: the template
      carries the entity, and CowboyHandler.found then runs on the tick, so
      nothing in piece 2 needs to build the cowboy.
    * Add the StructurePiece to the village's StructureStart as well? UNVERIFIED
      whether that is possible to do safely after generation. Skip it: the
      backfill record (iii) plus the block/entity signal (ii) are enough, and
      mutating a saved StructureStart is a way to corrupt a world. Do not.
    * Chunk-boundary care: the barn is bigger than one chunk, so every chunk it
      touches must be loaded for the placement and stay loaded until the
      template finishes. Use a ticket (addRegionTicket) and release it, and do
      the placement from a server tick, not from inside chunk work (the
      force-load re-entrancy crash that HorseFoundingTickHandler documents).
      Large worlds: process ONE village per tick or per N ticks so the command
      cannot stall the server; report progress.
- Safety, because this edits someone's world:
    * `dry-run` lists what WOULD be placed and where, and places nothing.
      Default to dry-run unless the owner passes a confirm word - mutating
      world blocks in already-played chunks is exactly the action to make
      explicit.
    * Never overwrite a non-air, non-replaceable block that is not natural
      terrain (a player's build, a village piece). Test the whole footprint
      first; skip the site if it fails.
    * It must not change the world when run on a world with no villages, in
      another dimension, or on the realm, the debug corridor, or the Nether
      (check the dimension; vanilla villages exist only in the overworld).
- Idempotency test, the heart of this piece: run, record the set of placed
  barns and the world's changed blocks, run again, assert nothing changed - and
  assert a barn placed by the command is recognised by signal (ii) on a LATER
  run in a fresh server (so persistence of the SavedData and of the blocks is
  actually exercised, not just in-memory state).
- Ships with: the command class (server/, name it for what it does), a
  SavedData for the backfill record, a lang entry for each report line, a
  gametest that generates a village of a known seed WITHOUT the barn (needs a
  way to suppress the injection for the test - UNVERIFIED how; a test-only flag
  on BarnPoolInjector is the least bad), runs the command, and asserts exactly
  one barn and one cowboy appear and that a second run adds nothing; and a wiki
  update.
- In-game matrix (record on villagers.html's Verification tab, unplayed):
  on a world explored before piece 1, run dry-run and read the list; run for
  real; fly to three villages of three styles and look at the barn; run again and
  confirm nothing changes; demolish one barn and run again (skipped, per (iii));
  run with --force (rebuilt); try a village with no free edge (skipped, reported);
  try a village that already generated its barn (skipped via signal (i)); try
  with no villages in range (clean message, no error); do all this on a server
  as well as in singleplayer, with a non-op (refused).

Order, and what must not be done in the same session
=====================================================
1. Piece 1, option (a): add the other four vanilla terminator pools to the
   injector, run HomesteadCensus per style, pick weights, gametest, wiki. One
   session.
2. Piece 1, option (b): one style's art per session (bake-barn.py remap, nbt,
   pool entry, check the look in a real village of that style).
3. Piece 2: the command. It depends on piece 1 only for signal (i) and for the
   barn nbt itself; it can be built against the plains barn alone. One session
   for the finder and the report (dry-run only, places nothing), a second for
   placement. Do NOT combine them; the placement is the one that can damage a
   world, and the finder is what makes it testable.
- Do not touch the equestrians' own code (EquestrianHandler, ModPoiTypes,
  ModVillagerProfessions): they already work wherever a post exists.
- Re-bake what the table requires: bake-barn.py -> cowboy_barn.nbt for any art
  change; check-recipes / check-block-models only if a block is added (none
  expected); bake-agent-text, build-search-index and check-links for the wiki
  prose; bake-verification-index and bake-roadmap-index for the tabs. A new
  structure .nbt is not a coat change, so no golden moves, and nothing in
  common/ moves unless the pool table is put there, in which case
  :web:bakeDesignerAssets and check-designer-boots.mjs follow.
