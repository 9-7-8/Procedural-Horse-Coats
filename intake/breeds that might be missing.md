## Important corrections to common assumptions

Several famous breeds are **not good examples** of a fully closed studbook under your definition:

- **KWPN** is explicitly an **open** studbook and accepts approved outside genetic input to advance its breeding goal. [kwpn-na](https://kwpn-na.org/breeding/kwpn-erkend/)
- **Appaloosa** is not fully closed because the registry permits certain outside ancestry, historically including Quarter Horse, Thoroughbred, and Arabian blood under specific requirements. [liquisearch](https://www.liquisearch.com/horse_breed/purebreds_and_registries)
- **American Quarter Horse** is not an ideal closed-book example because of its Appendix system and historic Thoroughbred-cross pathway to full registration. [liquisearch](https://www.liquisearch.com/horse_breed/purebreds_and_registries)
- Many warmbloods—Hanoverian, Holsteiner, Oldenburg, Selle Français, Swedish Warmblood, Danish Warmblood, and Belgian Warmblood—are better classified as **selective open/graded books**, even though they may impose extremely demanding mare inspection, stallion licensing, pedigree, health, and performance requirements.
- **Trakehner** deserves an asterisk: it is conventionally called the closed warmblood studbook, but its definition includes limited recognized refining ancestry and approved breeding policies, so it is not equivalent to a genetically sealed island. [aab.copernicus](https://aab.copernicus.org/articles/69/323/2026/aab-69-323-2026.html)
