Tusks gene - implementation treatment

Scoped with the owner. Every "Decided" line below is her call; "Recommended" lines are mine, not
asked; "UNVERIFIED" lines are facts this file has not proved and the building session must check
first. Source of truth for what exists is wiki/model-parts.html and the unicorn horn and antler
gene pages. This file is the handoff for ONE gene whose alleles are three mouth-and-muzzle growths:
boar tusks, sabre fangs and a narwhal horn. It sits beside "Head parts - implementation treatment.txt"
and "Body parts - implementation treatment.txt" and follows the same pattern. It is retired (deleted,
per /process-intake step 8) as each slice ships and moves onto that gene's Gameplay and Coding tabs.

The gene, in one paragraph
==========================
A single locus, horsegenetics.tusks, with one allele per form and a wild type:
    Tsk  - boar tusks      (short, curved, rising from the lower jaw at each side)
    Sab  - sabre fangs     (long, straight, blade-like canines hanging from the upper lip)
    Nar  - narwhal horn    (one long spiral tusk, pointing straight forward from the front of the muzzle)
    n    - wild type
Token spellings are proposals; check them for a clash with every existing allele token before using them.

What already exists, so the building session does not redo it
==============================================================
Everything the head-parts treatment says about the existing machinery applies and is not repeated:
AttachedPartLayer, PartKind / PartAnchor / PartShape / PartGenerators / PartNode / PartSheet, GrownParts.of as
the one door, the "adding a part" recipe on model-parts.html's Coding tab, the cost-sorted variation rule, and the
deferred-submit trap in PartModel. Read that file's first section. What is specific here:
- HornGenerator already builds "a tapering chain of boxes" with four styles (SMOOTH, LOOSE, SPIRAL, TIGHT). Its own
  note says a straight tapered spiral is "the storybook horn and a narwhal tusk both", and that base width
  grows with length but not proportionally, because a narwhal horn is thicker at the root than a nub and far more
  slender for its length. So the narwhal horn is NOT a new generator: it is a horn kind with a longer length
  ceiling and the SPIRAL / TIGHT styles, at a different anchor.
- The boar tusks and the sabre fangs are the two parts that need a NEW generator (a tapering curved chain for the
  tusk, a flat tapering blade for the fang). AntlerGenerator and HornGenerator are the two worked examples.
- Dominant multi-allele genes already exist: AntlersGene (Ant, Antm, n) is the template for "any copy of a form
  grows it". It reads values from the expressing copy.
- The epigenome API reads per-copy values: GeneEpigenetics gives expressed() and copy(int slot). This matters
  here, because two different forms on one horse each need THEIR OWN copy's numbers.
- A foal grows no hard part (PartKind.showsOnFoal() false); the unicorn horn is the only hard part a foal wears.

Decided
=======
- ONE gene, with a form per allele. Not three genes.
- Dominant, and the forms COMBINE. One copy of any form grows that form. A horse with two DIFFERENT forms grows BOTH
  (Tsk/Sab: boar tusks and sabre fangs; Sab/Nar: fangs and a narwhal horn; Tsk/Nar: tusks and a horn). Parts can
  stack (decided for the head parts), so there is no suppression rule. Two copies of the SAME form grow that form once,
  from one copy's numbers (below).
- The plain "tusks" form is BOAR tusks: short, curved, rising from the lower jaw on each side. Walrus tusks are
  not a form; they would overlap the sabre fangs.
- The narwhal horn is CENTRED on the front of the muzzle, one horn pointing straight forward. Not offset to the left.
  It does not sit on the forehead where the unicorn horn is, so the two can stack.
- NO sex limit. Mares and stallions grow every form. (The antlers' Antm stallion-only allele has no counterpart
  here; say so on the page so nobody assumes it.)
- Foals wear none of the three (hard parts, like the antlers).
- The part does NOTHING. No stat, ability or hitbox. Say it plainly on the Gameplay tab.
- No config key beyond the existing client parts.* switches.
Carried over from the head parts, NOT re-asked (Recommended, change if she says otherwise):
- Colour is its own horn-style colour gene: horsegenetics.tusk_colour, codominant, the horn's allele set (white wild
  type, the rainbow, pink, black, grey, and chaos), two different copies giving a two-tone part from base to tip,
  hue and tone on each copy. ONE colour gene for the whole tusks gene, so every form on a horse is tinted by the same
  gene (a horse with fangs and a horn has both ivory, or both red). White is the wild type, which reads as ivory.
  Reuse the shared AbstractPartColourGene if the head or body parts have shipped it; if not, settle which session
  owns it before this one starts, so two copies do not appear.
- Everything can stack with every other grown part (horn, antlers, the head and body parts).

How each form is built
======================
Five new PartKinds (the hard pairs are two kinds, one per side, not a negative scale):
    TUSK_RIGHT, TUSK_LEFT      boar tusks, anchors JAW_RIGHT / JAW_LEFT
    SABRE_RIGHT, SABRE_LEFT    sabre fangs, anchors LIP_RIGHT / LIP_LEFT
    NARWHAL                    narwhal horn, anchor SNOUT
Each kind carries its anchor and its default sheet region; foal gate false for all five.
- JAW_*: the corner of the lower jaw at each side, level with the mouth line. Boar tusks curve UP and slightly
  outward and back, past the upper lip.
- LIP_*: the front of the upper lip, each side of centre. Sabre fangs hang DOWN and a little forward, long enough
  to reach below the chin.
- SNOUT: the centre of the front of the muzzle, pointing forward along the head's own axis.
Geometry below is a starting proposal in tapered boxes; the owner's eyes in the dev yard decide the numbers, and every
proportion is a pose-stack transform so tuning is cheap. All static, parented to the head bone.
- Boar tusk: a tapering chain of three to four boxes, curving. Styles (topology, baked): short (a small upward curl),
  long (a larger sweep) and hooked (curling back on itself near the tip). Epigenome: length, girth, curve.
- Sabre fang: one flat tapering blade, with a slight forward lean, one or two segments. Styles: straight, curved-back
  and serrated (a notched edge from a few extra small boxes). Epigenome: length, girth, lean.
- Narwhal horn: HornGenerator's SPIRAL or TIGHT style at a longer length ceiling, pointing forward. Epigenome: length,
  girth, twist (a style category), tilt. Its length ceiling is the thing to pick by eye: it must read as a long tusk
  without the whole horse looking like a unicorn on its face.
- Sheet: HORN and HORN_TIP for all three (ivory grain, existing, no new region).
- Epigenome schema: one set of generic numbers on the gene, read by the form the copy carries: length, girth, curve
  (a generic bend or lean), a style category, and a SEED for any variation (a serration pattern, a hook). A copy
  carries every value whatever its allele, and only the ones its form uses are read, the way the antlers carry
  asymmetry whether or not it is above zero. Founders roll them like the horn's.
- Which copy drives which form: a homozygote (Sab/Sab) uses the first copy's numbers and grows the pair symmetrically
  (a decision carried from the antlers: symmetric by default, no asymmetry knob). A mixed pair (Sab/Nar) grows the sabre
  from the Sab copy's numbers and the narwhal horn from the Nar copy's. UNVERIFIED: that copy(slot) is stable and
  canonical for a given allele, so a copy's numbers always follow its own allele and never swap with the other copy.
  Read GeneEpigenetics and AllelePair's canonical order, and test it with a mixed pair both ways round.

Expressions and founders
========================
Expressions (what the info screen says): wild; each single form (Tsk, Sab, Nar, from one or two copies); and the three
combinations (Tsk+Sab, Tsk+Nar, Sab+Nar). Write each one as the player will read it. Two copies of the same form is the
same expression as one copy; a form does not get stronger from a second copy. (UNVERIFIED how AllelePair.count and
Expression naming handle "any copy of X" across three dominant alleles; AntlersGene.expressionOf is the pattern.)
Founder frequencies: numbers to tune in a dev yard, not here. Recommended starting point: three dominant alleles each at
a small share near the antlers' (a small fraction of a percent of horses showing each form), the narwhal horn the rarest
of the three.
Copy the horn's/antlers' isNatural() (false) and rarity (RARE). UNVERIFIED which GeneFamily; reuse the horn's.
Priority: a magical-band code-order slot (a part gene paints nothing), kept beside its colour gene. UNVERIFIED which
slots are free; read the Genes registry for the next free run.
No breed is changed. A breed can pin the gene later through the ordinary genes block (a boar breed with tusks is an
obvious later use).

Slices (one bullet per session, each shippable alone)
=====================================================
  Slice A: the gene, its colour gene, the new SNOUT anchor and the narwhal horn, end to end. It is the cheapest form
           (no new generator; the horn's), so it proves the gene, the multi-form expression, the per-copy numbers and
           the colour gene once.
  Slice B: the boar tusks and the sabre fangs together (the two new generators, the four new anchors JAW_* and LIP_*,
           the mixed-pair behaviour made visible: fangs plus a horn on one horse).
If the head parts' Slice A has shipped, this reuses the colour-gene base class; if not, see the ownership note above.

Tests and checks (CLAUDE.md: no full suite; run by name)
=========================================================
- PartGeneratorTest: build every mesh of every new kind, style and size bucket, parents before children.
- A TusksGene test (copy AntlersGeneTest and UnicornHornGeneTest): each form from one and from two copies, each mixed
  pair grows both forms, wild grows none, founders at the stated shares, the epigenome defaults, and the check that a
  mixed pair's numbers follow their own alleles whichever way round the pair is written.
- A colour gene test: two different copies make a two-tone part, a chaos copy ignores hue and tone.
- The coat goldens must NOT move (the gene paints nothing); the coat-bake golden gains rows, so regenerate it and check
  only NEW rows appeared.
- Play checks (the gene page's Verification tab, never seen in game): each form on a tiny and a huge horse (the size
  gene scales parts through the pose scale already on the stack, which the page records as a claim from reading); a horse
  with two forms; the horse grazing (the head lowers: does the narwhal horn clip the ground, do the fangs clip the jaw);
  the head moving and the mouth opening, since the vanilla upper_mouth is a separate part and the lower jaw may animate
  (UNVERIFIED: whether it does; anchor to the head bone and say which was used, so the tusks do not follow a jaw that
  is not the one they grew from); a horn plus a narwhal horn on one head; and the tusks with a bridle or head armour.
