1. The request, as given
========================
"We're going to add a bunch of sheep genes into this, to widen the marking pool." Plus the owner's research,
whose coat section says: pigment and pattern are different things; ASIP (the A locus), MC1R (E), TYRP1 (B), a
classical white-spotting (S) locus, MITF and KIT as associations only, and fading/dilution as modifiers. "Do not
assign every visible marking a single dominant or recessive gene." "A sensible rendering order is: determine an
ASIP pattern; apply any dominant-black MC1R override; use TYRP1 to recolor dark areas; overlay genuine white
spotting; then apply age-related fleece changes. That is a simulation design, not a claim that all those classical
labels have been mapped to independent causal genes."
Reading, confirmed: natural-sheep loci that paint on a horse, honest-hybrid (real alleles where established, a
labelled analogue where not), shipped as hand- or data-authored genes after the kind exists.


2. In scope, and what is not
============================
In scope
 - Piece 1: three pigment loci - sheep ASIP, sheep MC1R, sheep TYRP1 - and the rule that they paint over the horse
   base coat in that order.
 - Piece 2: one genuine white-spotting locus (the "S" locus), as a breed-model-dependent analogue.
 - Piece 3: age/fade modifiers - only if the horse pipeline can already do age (UNVERIFIED), otherwise a plan.
Not in scope (all re-askable)
 - Wool and fleece look. The coat pipeline has no hair axis (wiki/curly-coat.html); the fleece is a plan there. Do
   not build a woolly texture here.
 - MITF and KIT sheep genes. The research says no sheep-wide dominance rule, and "avoid importing named dog or horse
   spotting alleles into sheep". Decided by scope: they appear as a note on the S gene's Science tab, no loci.
   Re-askable.
 - Horns, body, disease: the sibling treatments.
 - Sheep breeds that pin these genes. Decided: later, with the owner.
 - Any change to the horse's own Extension, Agouti, Brown, Kit or Mitf genes. They are not touched or renamed.


3. What already exists, so the building session does not redo it
=================================================================
3a. Horse loci that read like these ones, which must NOT be reused or have their alleles borrowed:
    ExtensionGene (KEY "horsegenetics.extension", alleles E/e), AgoutiGene (KEY "horsegenetics.agouti", A/a,
    calls BayCoat.apply; dependsOn ShadeGene), ShadeGene, KitGene, MitfGene, TobianoGene, BrindleGene.
    Sheep ASIP/MC1R/TYRP1 are NEW genes under new keys, in the new kind, so a horse with E/e and a sheep E^D carries
    both and neither reads the other's alleles.
3b. Pipeline facts (read wiki/pipeline.html before relying on this): three phases; the natural phase restricts red
    and black per pixel and the survivors are looked up in a gradient and multiplied onto the white template; a
    natural layer "reads the coat as the layer above it left it" (GeneSpecParser error text, line ~926). 'over' and
    'emissive' are magical-phase only and a natural gene may not use them.
3c. Data-driven genes are JSON under common/src/main/resources/horsegenetics/genes/, with masks, ops and an
    "effects" block; making-a-gene.html owns the format and #contracts lists the four or five files each new
    mask/op/verb touches. Prefer data genes; a Java gene only if no op can do it.
3d. Gene census / unverified-genes: gene-census.html lists every registered gene; bakeUnverifiedGenes writes
    unverified-genes.txt, the horse dimension's coat column - a new gene is unverified until played.
3e. Marking facts for the breed designer: MarkingFactsTool writes wiki/breed-designer/assets/marking-facts.json.
3f. Never regenerate genes/index.json from the folder (hard rule 6): an absence is a killswitch. Add the new
    genes to it by hand.
Looks built but is not
  - Any sheep gene or pattern; "badgerface", "mouflon", "moorit" are not names in the mod. No age-fade mechanism is
    confirmed for naturals (Grey changes with age on horses - UNVERIFIED how, read the Grey gene page).
  - The natural-sheep kind and its sub-band: built by the kind treatment, not here.


4. The pieces
=============
Piece 1 - Sheep pigment: ASIP, MC1R, TYRP1
------------------------------------------
What the player sees: horses can carry sheep-style colour: a dominant white/tan, a badger-faced or mouflon-like
 light-and-dark pattern, a dominant-black override, and a brown shift of the dark areas - on top of a normal horse
 coat. A horse with no sheep genes looks exactly as it does now.
 Decided: sheep genes paint AFTER the horse base coat, as extra per-pixel red/black restrictions; an inert
   genotype changes nothing, so existing horses keep their coats.
 Decided: honest hybrid. Established loci are real alleles; traditional pattern names are labelled "analogous, not
   measured" on the Science tab (precedent gene-ram-horns.html#real-sheep). Each gene's page states the evidence
   level: classical phenotype allele, associated marker, or validated causal variant.
 Decided: ASIP's intermediate patterns are NOT ranked. Wt (dominant white/tan) is dominant to the rest, self-colour
   is the recessive end, and each heterozygous pair among the intermediates gets an explicit pair outcome (labelled).
 Genes and rules (PROPOSALS for review; tokens must be grepped against every existing allele token first):
   Sheep ASIP (key like "horsegenetics.sheep_asip"; alleles Wt dominant white/tan, Mf wild/mouflon-like, Bf
     badgerface, Gr a gray/blue pattern, Sc self-colour, the recessive end. Allele order permanent, append only.
     Founder frequencies: each in a breed pool only; none in ordinary founders (the kind is opt-in).
     UNVERIFIED that the data format's "combinations" can state every intermediate pair without a dominance order -
     if not, a Java gene (like TobianoGene) is the fallback; say so on the page.
   Sheep MC1R (E^D dominant black over E+; the building session picks ASCII tokens). Dominant: one copy darkens an
     otherwise patterned or ASIP-white/tan animal; true white spotting (Piece 2) still shows. Note on the page:
     "particular MC1R effects are breed-specific; not a universal rule".
   Sheep TYRP1 (black dominant, brown recessive; "moorit" as the name of the brown). Moves the colour of the dark
     areas, not where they occur. Note the Soay variant (dark dominant to light) as a Science-tab sentence, NOT a
     second allele set - "do not equate every use of light or brown across populations". Open (owner): a second
     TYRP1-style allele for Soay? Recommended: no.
 Recommended: paint order inside the sub-band is ASIP, then MC1R, then TYRP1, then (Piece 2) spotting. The
   priority numbers come from the kind treatment's sub-band; the order is what this treatment fixes.
 Open (building session): which horse red/black the sheep layers act on. Recommended: both pigments, through the
   existing red/black restriction ops, so a bay with sheep Wt shows tan where bay was dark.
 Where: common/ - three gene files (data first), horsegenetics/genes/index.json entries by hand, a gene page per gene
   with three tabs (Gameplay, Coding, Science; check-gene-tabs.mjs requires Science on a natural page). NeoForge:
   nothing but what the bakes write.
 Data and saves: gene keys and allele order permanent. A world with these genes keeps its genotype codes if the
   painters change later; a RENAMED key is a loss of genes, never rename.
 Sync: none. Config: none.
 Tests: a *CoatBakeGoldenTest row per gene (every gene, seconds); *CoatPipelineGoldenTest unchanged unless a pinned
   horse gains sheep genes (it should not - then both goldens move by the new keys only, check which rows moved);
   *DeadLayerTest (a layer that paints nothing fails; fix the gene, then delete its dead-layers.txt line);
   a diagnostic-cross test: E^D/E+ x E+/E+ transmits E^D to half the offspring on average, over a fixed seed set;
   a test that a horse with no sheep genes is pixel-identical to before; GeneFilesTest; EditorRules parity.
 Play checks: each gene alone on a bay, a chestnut, a black and a grey; the three together; dominance (Wt over Sc,
   E^D over E+); a foal; both editors show them under the natural-sheep group; none in a wild horse.
 Docs: three gene pages, gene-census.html, timeline-of-genes.html, the generated spans of wiki/pages.js and
   index.html (bakeGeneWikiPages, never by hand), pipeline.html (one line: the sheep sub-band).

Piece 2 - Genuine white spotting (the S locus)
----------------------------------------------
What the player sees: unpigmented patches that stay white whatever the pigment genes say - cap, blaze, socks,
 tail-tip, or larger piebald patches - more extensive with two copies.
 Decided: it is a separate mask from pigment: a sheep with E^D can still show spotting.
 Decided: honest hybrid. The research says the S locus is breed-model dependent and not a universal molecular locus.
   Model the Romney-style breeder convention (a visible effect in heterozygotes, more extensive with two copies),
   name it as that, and say on the page "a breeder's S symbol is not a KIT or MITF mutation".
 Recommended: patch extent and placement come from a stochastic mask seeded by the horse (the mod is deterministic
   throughout - philosophy.html - so seed from the genotype/horse seed, not a fresh roll), conditioned on the
   genotype and on modifiers; patch shape reuses an existing noise or mask op if one fits. UNVERIFIED which.
 Recommended: alleles S+ (none) and S (spotting), incompletely dominant; epigenome knobs for patch size and edge
   (the epigenome is "literal named numbers on each allele copy, inherited and slightly drifted", genetics-model).
 Open (owner): does S spot the face and legs only, the whole body, or by knob? Recommended: knob-driven, defaulting
   to face and legs.
 Where, data/saves/sync/config/tests/docs: as Piece 1. Extra test: homozygous covers strictly more pixels than
   heterozygous across a fixed seed set; spotting never paints red or black.
 Play checks: spotting on each of the three Piece-1 backgrounds; across foal and adult; no overlap with the horse's
   own white genes beyond what the layers imply.

Piece 3 - Fading and dilution of the fleece colour
--------------------------------------------------
What the player sees: a sheep-style coat that lightens with age or at shearing time, separate from the horse's Grey.
 The research keeps gradual fading and faster dilution as SEPARATE modifiers and warns that a gray-looking fleece is
 not the same as a gray-patterned lamb or a white-spotted one.
 Recommended: do NOT build until the coat pipeline can do an age-dependent natural layer and a fleece axis
   exists (curly-coat.html). Until then this piece is a Roadmap tab entry on the coat pages, nothing more.
 Open (building session): whether any age-dependent natural layer exists today (read Grey's gene and the epigenome
   age handling). If one does, build fading as a modifier on TYRP1's dark areas; if not, defer.
 Everything else: "None - a plan only" unless the above resolves.

Implications sweep
  Genetics ... three or four genes. Pipeline/goldens ... a sheep sub-band, the bake golden gains rows, the whole-horse
  golden should not move. Eye colour ... untouched here (the research has blue eyes only with the EDNRB deletion,
  which is the disorders treatment). Body space ... untouched. Breeding ... untouched; the genes inherit like any
  gene. Breeds ... none. Horse body ... untouched. Care ... untouched. AI ... untouched. Items/carrots ... maybe
  rows. Tack ... untouched. Villagers ... untouched. Realm ... unverified-genes bake. H menu ... gene info text.
  Stasis ... untouched. Designers ... both editors list them. Gene creator ... not extended (rule 3). Config ...
  none. Mods ... none. Advancements ... none. Backport ... all in common/. Wiki ... a page per gene.
Three likeliest ways it goes wrong for a real player
  1. A sheep gene changes an existing horse's coat. Handled: inert genotype is identical; pixel test.
  2. ASIP white reads as the lethal EDNRB white, or spotting reads as deafness. Handled: no health coupling anywhere
     in this treatment; pages say so (the research warns against it).
  3. The painters imply knowledge the science does not have. Handled: every gene page's Science tab labels the
     evidence level and "analogous, not measured".


6. UNVERIFIED (riskiest first)
===============================
 1. That a data-driven gene can express ASIP's unranked pair table, or a Java gene is needed.
 2. A free sub-band that paints after the horse base coat and before dilution (see the kind treatment).
 3. That the red/black restriction ops let a sheep layer act on both pigments after the horse base without a new op
    (a new op or mask touches four or five files, #contracts).
 4. That the wiki page names in the header exist; read wiki/text/index.txt.
 5. Whether any age-dependent natural layer exists (Piece 3).
 6. That the allele tokens chosen clash with none of the existing ones.
