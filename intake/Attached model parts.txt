Attached model parts - treatment and implementation guide

Status: PLAN ONLY. Nothing here is built and no code was written for it.
Anything that touches a Minecraft or NeoForge API is marked UNVERIFIED and must be
read out of the 26.1.2 sources (or proved in a running client) before it is relied
on - CLAUDE.md hard rule 8. Nothing has been seen in-game.

Where this goes: intake/ is an inbox, not a plan (/process-intake). When this is
picked up it is routed onto wiki/model-parts.html's Roadmap tab (that page already
exists, already has the tab and its summary box) and this file is deleted. Section 9
says exactly what goes where.


1. Why this one, and why now
=============================
Four separate plans are waiting on the same piece of machinery and each of them
currently describes its own private version of it:
- wiki/model-parts.html      horns, dorsal spines, crystals, wings, tusks, antlers.
- Horse shoes.txt (step 4)   a HorseShoeLayer that has to find four hoof anchors.
- wiki/saddle-pads.html      a pad drawn over the coat and under the saddle.
- wiki/breed-book.html       the reindeer breeds - "content for machinery that does
                             not exist yet" - need antlers and wings.
roadmap-execution.html already lists "gear attachment and render contract" as a
foundation to settle before a catalogue of tack, wings or model parts. The shoe
treatment is the first thing that would write a bespoke layer if this is not
scoped first, and the gear treatment's "render" line would be the second.

The owner's own words on the page: a batch of these is "a HUGE win for how cool
they look". High priority: unicorn horn, spikes / dorsal spines, crystalline
growths - small, head- or spine-parented, big visual payoff. Lower: pegasus wings,
lion and tufted tails, hoof feathering, tusks, antlers.

This is the mod's first visual that is not a texture. That is the whole reason it
is a foundation and not a content batch: the coat pipeline is texture-only, and
this is the first time common/ has to say "this horse has a THING" rather than
"this pixel is this colour".


2. What already exists (so a session does not redo it)
=======================================================
Read these in this order; nothing else is needed to start.
- wiki/model-parts.html - the plan, the wings-off-the-elytra research (texture
  side transfers entirely, model and layer classes do not, mesh is a dozen lines to
  copy), and two open questions left open on purpose (what drives wings; one
  texture or many). The plain-layer-versus-GeckoLib question is SETTLED there in
  the plain layer's favour. Do not reopen it. This treatment inherits that answer.
- neoforge/client/CutieMarkLayer.java - the worked example of a body-parented
  layer on THIS renderer. It gets getParentModel().root().getChild("body"), calls
  body.translateAndRotate(poseStack) to inherit the animated transform, and then
  works in body-local space (x in [-5,5], y in [-8,2], z in [-17,5], front is -z).
  Every part in this treatment is that move with a different child name and offset.
- neoforge/client/GeneticHorseRenderer.java - the layer list. Registration order is
  draw order: EmissiveCoatLayer, CutieMarkLayer, BraidLayer, then vanilla's
  body-armour and saddle SimpleEquipmentLayers. A new layer goes in here and the
  position is a real decision (section 6, "Draw order").
- neoforge/client/HdHorseModel.java - the horse's mesh. Named children present
  today: body, head_parts, head, mane, tail, left_/right_hind_leg,
  left_/right_front_leg, left_/right_ear. So head and leg anchors EXIST by name; the
  shoe treatment's worry that "HorseModel does not expose named hoof ModelParts" is
  about vanilla's HorseModel - the HD model already has the legs. (UNVERIFIED that
  the foot end of a leg part is a usable anchor without a per-leg offset; the stage 0
  spike proves it.)
- common/.../genetics/CutieMarkContribution.java and EyePatchContribution - the
  house pattern for "a gene has an opinion the renderer must honour": a record in
  common/, folded over by the expressing gene, handed to the client, and its own
  javadoc says the record's fields are exactly what the render layer knows how to
  draw and both change in the same commit. Attached parts follow that shape.
- GeneticHorseRenderState - where per-horse data crosses from extract to submit.
  Everything a layer reads goes through it (see how braidMane is copied out of the
  HorseGear attachment in extractRenderState).
- HORSE_GEAR / HorseTackSlot - already the authority for anything the PLAYER puts on
  a horse. Parts that are worn are gear; parts that are grown are genes. See 4.

What does NOT exist: any Blockbench model, any baked LayerDefinition beyond the HD
horse and its foal, any registry of parts, any way for common/ to name a part, any
per-part tint or emissive path, any scale-with-the-horse rule for an attached mesh.


3. The one-sentence outcome
============================
A gene or a breed can say "this horse has a unicorn horn", common/ carries that as
an id and nothing else, and the client draws the horn parented to the animated head
- with every part and every horse going through ONE layer, ONE registry and ONE
placement contract, so the fiftieth part is a model file and a line of data.


4. The design calls this treatment proposes (all need an owner answer)
=======================================================================
These go to wiki/decisions.html as D-entries when routed. They are proposals, in
the order they block work. Each has a recommendation so the owner is choosing, not
authoring.

P1. Grown versus worn - one system or two?
  Grown = gene- or breed-driven, part of the horse (horn, spines, wings, feathering,
  tusks, antlers). Worn = a player item in a HorseTackSlot (shoes, and later any
  tack). Recommendation: TWO SOURCES, ONE RENDERER. A grown part is resolved from
  the genotype in common/; a worn part is resolved from the HORSE_GEAR attachment on
  the client. Both reduce to the same thing by the time they reach the layer: a list
  of "part id + anchor + tint + emissive". The layer does not know which kind it is
  drawing. This is what stops the shoe layer, the horn layer and the pad layer
  becoming three layers with three z-fighting rules.
  Not settled by this treatment: whether a worn item may EVER replace a grown part
  (a helmet over a horn). Recommendation: no, out of scope, note it as a known
  interaction on the Verification tab the first time it is visible.

P2. Where does a part get its geometry?
  REVISED after section 11: the owner does NOT need Blockbench for the first batch.
  The HD horse itself is built in Java from CubeListBuilder boxes (HdHorseModel), and
  so are the cart models (EasyMeshBuilder); a Blockbench export is only a nicer way
  to author the same list of rotated boxes. Options:
  (a) hand-authored boxes in a small JSON box-list format (from, size, rotation,
      pivot, uv) that is exactly what a Blockbench "generic" export reduces to, so a
      Blockbench mesh can be dropped in later without a second loader;
  (b) GENERATED boxes: a parametric generator (taper, spiral, branch, fan, cluster)
      expands a few numbers into the box list in code;
  (c) vanilla geometry reused (the elytra mesh for wings; section 11.5).
  Recommendation: (a)+(b) sharing one PartSpec of boxes; Blockbench becomes an
  optional importer, not a prerequisite. UNVERIFIED: whether 26.1.2 lets a
  LayerDefinition be baked at run time (LayerDefinition.bakeRoot()) so a per-horse
  variant can be built and cached, or whether every LayerDefinition must be
  registered during EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterLayerDefinitions as ClientSetup
  does for HD_HORSE. If the latter, variety comes from a finite baked set plus
  per-horse pose scale/tilt (section 11.6). Stage 0 answers this first.

P3. How does common/ name a part?
  Recommendation: a plain string id, like every other cross-module identity in this
  mod ("ids are plain strings, resolved against the live registry" - the house
  convention; gap 265 is the standing warning about bare-string duplicates, so the
  id constants live in ONE class in common/, not typed at each use site). common/
  declares a part by id and a slot; it never knows a mesh exists. This is the same
  shape as GradientLut arriving in the renderer as an int[].
  Proposed record, in common/genetics beside CutieMarkContribution:
     AttachedPartContribution(String partId, PartAnchor anchor, float scale,
                              int tint /* 0 = untinted */, boolean emissive)
  PartAnchor is a small enum, NOT a bone name: HEAD_TOP, HEAD_SIDE_PAIR, SPINE_ROW,
  WITHERS_PAIR, TAIL_BASE, HOOF_x4, ... The client maps anchor -> ModelPart + offset.
  That keeps Minecraft's model-part names out of common/ (hard rule 1) and means
  retargeting an anchor is a client edit, not a gene edit.

P4. Does a part carry an epigenome value?
  Recommendation: NO for the first slice. A horn that varies per horse (length,
  twist, tint) is the natural fit for the per-allele-copy epigenome and is a
  tempting extension, but CutieMarkGene.markFor reading epigenetic seed for size and
  tilt shows the cost: every varied field must be honoured by the layer or it is a
  broken promise. Ship fixed-shape parts first; variation is a Roadmap entry on this
  page once the fixed version has been SEEN.

P5. How does a foal look?
  The mod has a separate HdBabyHorseModel. Recommendation: parts scale with the
  render state's age scale and use the SAME anchor names, and a part may declare
  "hidden on foals" (a horn on a newborn is a design call, not a rendering one;
  default hidden until the owner says otherwise). UNVERIFIED that the baby model's
  head and body children share the adult's names and origin - check both models
  before trusting a shared anchor table.

P6. What does the horse designer / CustomHorseSpawnScreen show?
  Hard rule 5 makes these one screen in two places. Both draw a 2D coat today. If a
  part is invisible in the designer, the designer lies about what the horse is. This
  is the biggest open cost of the whole feature and is NOT solved here.
  Recommendation: v1 the designer shows the part as a LISTED TRAIT with an icon and
  says plainly that it is not drawn in the preview; the in-game paper doll on the
  gear screen (which already renders a live horse) is where it is seen. Anything
  fancier is a separate plan. Record the divergence in a comment on BOTH files per
  hard rule 5.


5. The plan, in slices (one bullet, one context window)
========================================================
Per /process-intake each slice is one session, ends with the closeout routine, and
the next slice is not started in the same conversation.

Stage 0 - the spike. One horn, one horse, prove the pipeline. NO gene.
  Hypothesis: a single hand-written ModelPart child of the HD model's "head" cannot
  be added (that moves the mesh the coat UVs are baked against - the one thing that
  cannot shift; see model-parts.html), but a SEPARATE LayerDefinition rendered by a
  new RenderLayer, parented via getParentModel().root().getChild("head") and
  translateAndRotate, follows every animation.
  Disconfirming check: put the horn on a horse in a dev build, watch it through
  walk, gallop, rear, graze (head down) and the foal. If it lags, floats, or is
  wrong-scaled at any of the four, the parenting is wrong and nothing later stands.
  Also answers P2 (registration route) and the leg-anchor UNVERIFIED in section 2.
  Deliverable: a facts paragraph on model-parts.html's Verification tab, and a
  go / no-go on the registry shape. No common/ change, no golden moves.

Stage 1 - the registry and the layer.
  common/: the part-id constants class and PartAnchor, no rendering.
  neoforge/client: AttachedPartLayer (one class), a part registry mapping id ->
  {model layer, texture, anchor offset table}, and the extract step that fills a
  list of resolved parts on GeneticHorseRenderState. Still no gene - drive it from a
  debug command so the layer can be seen without breeding anything.
  Acceptance: three different parts on three different anchors, on an adult and a
  foal, in a running client, no z-fighting against coat, mane, braid or saddle.

Stage 2 - the first gene.
  A gene that requests a part through AttachedPartContribution, folded the same way
  CutieMarkGene folds its contributions. Which gene is the owner's call; the natural
  candidate is a unicorn-horn locus. This stage is where it becomes a GENE and
  therefore inherits the full making-a-gene checklist (wiki/making-a-gene.html
  #contracts): the gene page with its three tabs, the bakes in CLAUDE.md's
  regenerate table (bakeGeneBundle, bakeGeneIcons, bakeGeneWikiPages, census,
  timeline, pages.js spans), and the designer's WASM. It does NOT move either coat
  golden, because it paints no pixels - state that in the session notes so nobody
  hunts for a golden that should not move. UNVERIFIED that no golden reads a
  contribution list that a new record type would change; run
  *CoatBakeGoldenTest before assuming.

Stage 3 - the head-parented batch.
  Horns, spikes / dorsal spines, crystals, per the owner's priority list. Data and
  art only, if stages 0-2 held. "One render layer, many models" is the point.

Stage 4 - the breed hook.
  Let a breed file declare parts. This touches the breed format
  (wiki/breed-format.html), BreedSpecWriter, and the breed-file bakes, and the
  breed designer. It is a separate slice precisely because it drags in the whole
  breed bake table. Unblocks the reindeer breeds.

Stage 5 - the worn side.
  Point the shoe treatment's HorseShoeLayer at THIS layer rather than a bespoke one:
  HorseGear stacks resolve to the same resolved-part list (P1). Saddle pads follow.
  Done by editing the shoe treatment's step 4 to say "use AttachedPartLayer", and
  by deleting the bespoke-layer language from it.

Stage 6 - wings.
  The settled route from model-parts.html: a copy of the elytra mesh (left_wing and
  right_wing, 10x20x2 boxes, CubeDeformation(1.0F), texOffs(22,0), right mirrored,
  64x32 LayerDefinition), texture pointed at minecraft's own wings/elytra.png so
  any elytra retexture pack is the art. New here: a folded and a spread pose keyed
  off the isFallFlying shared flag GeneAbilityHandler already sets, which needs one
  boolean on the render state. Blocked on the two open questions (what drives them;
  one texture or many) - both are owner calls, not rendering ones.

Later (lower priority, not scoped): tails, hoof feathering, tusks, antlers.


6. Risks the plan already knows about
======================================
- Draw order and z-fighting. Saddle, armour and third-party tack render at the same
  time as a part. Decide once where AttachedPartLayer sits in the addLayer list and
  write the reason in the constructor comment the way the renderer already does for
  the emissive and braid layers. Parts on the head sit clear of the saddle; the
  risk is the mane, the bridle-side armour and the spines against a body-armour
  layer. Test with body armour ON at stage 1.
- Scale. Attached meshes must scale with the horse. The renderer already stretches
  gait to size (stretchGaitToSize) and the mod has a size gene, so a part parented to
  an animated ModelPart inherits scale IF the parent transform includes it - do not
  assume, check on a tiny and a huge horse at stage 0.
- The HD model's UV bake. Never add children to "body" or "head" inside
  createHdBodyMesh for a part. Separate LayerDefinition, separate layer, always.
- Third-party renderers. Other mods' horse renderers will not draw a part. That is
  acceptable and is a compatibility.html line, not a blocker; do not try to solve it.
- Cost per frame. A horse with no parts must cost one early return, the way BraidLayer
  does ("the state fields are zero and this returns on the first line"). Herds are
  common; measure a 20-horse pen at stage 1.
- Licence. Blockbench models the owner authors are the owner's; copied vanilla
  meshes (elytra) are vanilla geometry with the texture supplied at run time, which
  is the reason the wing route was chosen. Do not vendor third-party models.
- Never-baked, never-listed coats (hard rule 4) is unaffected: parts add variety,
  they do not add a pre-generated subset.


7. What must not regress
=========================
- The coat pipeline: no golden moves, no texture bake changes, HD UVs untouched.
- The existing layer stack: cutie mark, braid, emissive glow, armour, saddle all
  still draw, in the same order relative to each other.
- Rendering with the part absent: byte-for-byte the same frame as today.
- common/ imports nothing from Minecraft (rule 1) and adds no JDK API TeaVM lacks
  (rule 2); CommonPortabilityTest guards the second.
- Designer parity (rule 5) if any editor-visible field is added.


8. Verification the finished feature needs (goes on the Verification tab)
=========================================================================
In a running client, not a unit test:
1. Each part on adult and foal, standing, walking, galloping, rearing, grazing.
2. A part on a tiny and a huge horse; on a horse with a dyed-leather saddle and
   body armour worn; on a horse with a braid and a cutie mark.
3. Two players' horses side by side (client cache path, not the local one).
4. Save, reload, breed: the part follows the gene and skips a horse without it.
5. Invisible horse (potion): no part draws. The existing layers already return on
   isInvisible; a new one must.
6. A 20-horse herd: no frame-time cliff.
Unit tests (common/): the contribution folds, the anchor enum is total over the
part-id list, and every part id in the constants class has a registry row (a
test that fails when the two lists drift, the way check-recipes.mjs guards recipes -
this treatment's registry is a third list that will otherwise drift silently).

10. What this treatment deliberately does not decide
=====================================================
- Which gene, breed or allele frequency gets the first horn (owner's call at stage 2).
- What drives wings, and one texture versus many (model-parts.html, open on purpose).
- Whether a designer preview can ever draw a part (P6).

11. Feasibility: what needs Blockbench, what is code, what needs animation
===========================================================================
Requested: flesh out unicorn horns, antlers, crystals, hoof feathering, then the
harder set (wings, lion tails), and say how much is possible (a) with no Blockbench,
(b) with JUST code, (c) with NO new animation.

11.1 Three facts that decide most of it
----------------------------------------
F1. Everything on this renderer is already boxes. HdHorseModel is CubeListBuilder
    boxes with a PartPose (offset + rotation) and a texScale. A "mesh" here means a
    tree of rotated axis-aligned boxes. A horn, a spine, a crystal, an antler tine
    and a feather card are all expressible that way. Blockbench produces the same
    thing; it is an editor, not a capability. So "no Blockbench" costs blockiness,
    not possibility - and blocky is the game's own look.
F2. A part parented to an animated ModelPart inherits its animation for free. The
    HD model already animates head_parts (head bob, graze, rear), the four legs, the
    tail (child of body) and the ears (children of head). A part that is a child, or
    that calls translateAndRotate on that ModelPart the way CutieMarkLayer does for
    body, needs no animation of its own. "No new animation" is therefore the DEFAULT
    for anything rigidly fixed to a moving bone.
F3. Layers run after the model is posed, so a layer can READ the parent's already
    computed rotation (leg.xRot, tail.xRot, head_parts.xRot) and apply a formula:
    counter-swing, amplify, damp. That is code, not an animation asset, and it needs
    no state. (UNVERIFIED that the part rotations are still the posed values at
    RenderLayer.submit time in 26.1.2's submit-node model; stage 0 prints them.)

11.2 The colour problem, and the trick that removes the art
------------------------------------------------------------
Geometry is half of a part. The other half is what colour it is, and that is where
"no Blockbench, no texture painting" usually breaks. Three zero-art routes, in order
of how well they fit this mod:
 T1. SAMPLE THE HORSE'S OWN COAT TEXTURE. A part's UVs can point into a patch of the
     horse's generated coat - the mane patch, the tail patch, a leg patch, a hoof
     patch - so the part takes that colour from every gene automatically, for every
     coat, including glowing and dilute ones. HorseSkinGeometry already knows where
     each body region lives on the sheet (it is how genes paint by body-space). This
     is the natural answer for anything that should look like the horse's own hair
     (feathering, lion tuft, mane-coloured crest) and for hoof-coloured horn. It
     also honours the philosophy: the part is as unbounded as the coat.
     (UNVERIFIED which regions a hoof or a mane sample can reach without the coat
     genes writing a marking over them; check HorseSkinGeometry and a whitelisted
     "clean sample" patch.)
 T2. A FLAT MASK TEXTURE + A TINT, exactly BraidLayer's method: a shipped white mask
     and a per-horse int colour from the contribution record. One file covers every
     colour. Right for horn ivory/keratin, bone-white antlers, crystal colours.
 T3. A GENERATED TEXTURE: the mod already bakes textures in code (the coat pipeline,
     the noise classes). A small procedural sheet (feather barbs, a crystal facet
     gradient) can be baked once at load in common/ - game-free, deterministic, and
     WASM-runnable, so the wiki designer could show it later.
None of these need a painted texture from the owner. A hand-painted texture stays an
upgrade path, not a dependency.

11.3 Part by part
------------------
Legend: Blockbench = does the owner have to model it? Code = can it be done with
JUST code (no new asset)? Anim = new animation needed?

UNICORN HORN  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.  New animation: NONE.
  A tapered cone is a stack of 6-10 boxes, each smaller than the last, on the head
  bone (head_parts > head), tilted forward ~20-30 degrees. A SPIRAL is the same stack
  with a small yaw increment per box plus a thin ridge box on one corner of each
  section - reads as twisted at Minecraft resolution. Parameters that make it a
  gene's alleles instead of a model: length, base width, twist turns, tilt, tip
  sharpness, colour. Placement: forehead, between the ears (ears are at head-local
  y -13..-10, z 4..5; a horn anchors just forward of them - UNVERIFIED offset, tune in
  stage 0). Colour: T2 tint (ivory / gold / pearl), or T1 hoof sample for a "keratin"
  horn. Emissive is one flag (reuse EmissiveCoatLayer's approach) for a glowing horn.
  Verdict: fully doable now; the best stage 2 candidate.

ANTLERS  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.  New animation: NONE.
  Antlers are the best case for a GENERATOR, because a real antler is a recursive
  branch: a main beam with tines that fork. One function - beam(length, girth, angle),
  then N tines each a smaller beam at an angle from a point on the parent - expands
  seed + parameters into 10-40 boxes. Feed it the allele copy's epigenetic seed and
  every horse grows a different rack while staying a recognisable species: this is
  the "functionally infinite space" premise (hard rule 4) applied to geometry, and
  it is something a hand-modelled Blockbench antler cannot do at all. Sits on the head
  bone behind the ears; inherits head motion, so a graze or a head-toss carries them.
  Sexual dimorphism, seasonal shedding and antler growth stages are just conditions
  on the record, not animation. Colour: T2 tint (bone, velvet) or T1.
  Verdict: fully doable, and BETTER in code than in Blockbench. The generator would
  live in common/ as plain arithmetic emitting a box list (no Minecraft imports), so
  hard rule 1 holds and the wasm designer could preview it.

CRYSTALS (growths on the back, poll, shoulders)  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.
  New animation: NONE.
  A crystal is a hexagonal-ish prism: two or three long boxes at slightly different
  yaws, plus a shorter offset one - a cluster. A cluster generator scatters 3-7 of
  them over an anchor patch (SPINE_ROW / WITHERS / SHOULDER) with seeded lengths and
  lean. Needs the two properties the other parts do not: translucency and glow. The
  glow is the emissive pass that already exists for coats; translucency is a
  different render type (UNVERIFIED which translucent entity render types 26.1.2's
  submit API offers a layer). If translucency is not cheap, opaque emissive crystals
  are still a good look and the gene ships without it.
  Verdict: doable; the render type, not the geometry, is the only unknown.

HOOF FEATHERING (fetlock feathers)  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.  New animation: NONE.
  Feathering is long hair hanging from the fetlock of the draught breeds. Build it as
  a fringe of thin hanging boxes (or crossed flat cards) wrapped around the lower
  quarter of each leg, children of the four leg parts, so they swing with the leg
  for nothing. Because it is hair, sample T1 from the TAIL/MANE patch and it comes
  out the right colour on every horse, exactly as real feathering matches the mane.
  Optional polish with no animation asset: a counter-swing formula from F3
  (feather.xRot = -0.3 * leg.xRot) so it trails the hoof rather than swinging like a
  rigid sleeve. Length and density are the allele parameters; the breeds that want it
  (draught, per the page) get it from their breed file at stage 4.
  Verdict: doable; the two cost points are the number of boxes (4 legs x ~10 = 40
  extra cubes per horse - measure a herd) and the sampled patch, not the art.

HARDER SET

LION TAIL (thin tail with a tuft)  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES, with one caveat.
  New animation: NONE (rigid).
  The horse tail is a 3x14x4 box child of body with its own animation. Two moves:
  (1) make the horse tail thin - scale the tail part down on the model (the HD model
  is ours, so this is a setupAnim-level scale on the one part; UNVERIFIED that
  ModelPart carries xScale/yScale/zScale in 26.1.2, else hide it and draw a thin
  replacement child); (2) hang a tuft - a small cluster of boxes at the end, a child
  of "tail" so it follows the swish, coloured by T1 from the mane patch. The caveat:
  a truly whippy lion tail curves along its length. A single rigid box cannot. A
  2-3 segment chain, each segment a child of the last, CAN, and a chain needs a per-
  segment rotation rule. That rule can be a pure formula off the parent's rotation
  (each segment rotates a fixed fraction of the one above), which is F3 again - no
  animation asset, no per-entity state. Rigid straight tail + tuft is the stage-3
  version; the chain is an upgrade.
  Tufted tails (the page's "lion and tufted tails") are the rigid version.

WINGS  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.  New animation: TWO STATIC POSES, no flapping.
  Already researched on model-parts.html: copy the elytra mesh (two 10x20x2 boxes,
  CubeDeformation 1.0, texOffs(22,0), 64x32), point the texture at vanilla's own
  wings/elytra.png, so any elytra retexture pack is the wing art. No modelling. The
  ONLY new thing is pose, and the honest ladder is:
   W1  Always folded against the flanks. Zero animation, zero state. A static pair of
       rotated boxes. Ships with the horn batch.
   W2  Folded on the ground, spread when airborne or gliding: a boolean from the
       render state (GeneAbilityHandler already sets shared flag 7, isFallFlying).
       Two constant poses; the switch is a snap. Still no animation.
   W3  The same two poses with a smoothed blend (a single float per horse on the
       render state, lerped in extract) so the wings open instead of popping. This
       is the first thing that is arguably "an animation", and it is one number.
   W4  Flap: wing.zRot = A * sin(ageInTicks * k) while airborne. Still a formula, no
       asset, but it needs a tuned amplitude and it is where wings stop looking like
       an elytra and start looking like wings, so it is polish, not v1.
  Cost of NOT doing W3/W4: wings that snap open. Cost of doing them: one float and one
  sine. The owner's stated "no new animations" constraint is satisfied through W2.
  Membrane or feathered pegasus wings (rather than elytra-shell wings) are the one
  visual in the whole page that genuinely wants hand modelling - or a generator of
  primary feathers (a fan of flat cards, seeded lengths), which is the antler
  technique again and gets 80% of the way without Blockbench. Decide when W1-W2 have
  been seen; do not pre-decide.

11.6 Variety without a bake per horse
--------------------------------------
Because LayerDefinitions are normally registered once, "every horse has a different
antler" needs care:
 - Finite baked set + per-horse transform: bake ~8-16 seeded variants at registration
   and pick by seed; vary the rest with scale, tilt and tint per horse. Cheap, safe.
 - Or run-time bake and cache by seed bucket, IF stage 0 proves LayerDefinition can be
   baked outside the registration event.
 - Or build the part as many small children whose VISIBILITY the record toggles (tine
   count) - no bake at all.
Recommendation: visibility + transform first; it is the same trick the mod already
uses for size (a genetic scale on the state) and needs nothing UNVERIFIED.

12.1 The texture pack - what I could and could not check
---------------------------------------------------------

- "CC universal" almost certainly means CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain dedication):
  no attribution needed, compatible with this repo's CC BY-NC 4.0. But it must be
  READ off the page, because "Universal" also appears in CC BY 4.0-style text, and if
  the pack is actually CC BY it needs a line in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md and a copy under
  LICENSES/ (CLAUDE.md: check a third-party licence is compatible before vendoring).

- 256x256 is roughly sixteen times the resolution of this mod's art. Downsampled to a
  16x16 region it becomes a coarse pattern, which suits Minecraft's look, but any fine
  detail (veins, cracks) is lost. If a tile looks good only at full size, raise
  PartSheet.REGION to 32 and the sheet to 128x128; the baker and PartSheet are the only
  places that know the number.
- The baker keeps only LUMINANCE from a pack tile and throws the colour away. Colour is a
  per-horse tint at draw time (the BraidLayer method), so one sheet serves every colour
  and the pack contributes pattern and light, not hue. For crystals, whose tiles will be
  opaque, alpha is synthesised from brightness (dark = clearer).
- Which tiles? Not knowable from here. Wanted: fine-grained pale stone / marble / bone /
  ivory-like for HORN and BONE; anything glassy, faceted or crystalline (ice, quartz,

12.2 The common/ code (compiled and checked; files in intake/attached-parts-draft/common-parts/)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Layout when routed: common/src/main/java/com/example/horsegenetics/common/parts/.
Shape of it, so the code below reads quickly:
- PartNode is one box in a tree: {parent, t, ox,oy,oz, rx,ry,rz, len, girth, tex}. It
  hangs from its own origin toward -y (up in model space) and a child sits at fraction
  t of its parent's length, then rotates. A curve or a spiral is just a chain of nodes
  with a small rotation each; the parent's pose composes for us, so no matrix code.
- PartKind = HORN | ANTLERS | CRYSTALS, each with its PartAnchor and default texture region.
- PartGenerators.build(kind, size 0..1, seed) is the one door. Deterministic, so the
  server, the client and the wasm designer would agree on the shape without shipping it.
- HORN sizes: HornSize NUB 3 / SHORT 6 / STANDARD 10 / LONG 15 / NARWHAL 24 units, and
  HornSize.lengthFor(s) slides geometrically between them, so an allele can pick a class
  now and an epigenome number can slide between classes later with no generator change.
  Base width grows with length but not proportionally, and never thinner than 0.6 units.
- ANTLERS: a beam of 3-5 segments sweeping back, tines forking forward, a long tine
  forking once more; mirrored by replaying the same seed. size 0 = spike-and-fork
  yearling, 1 = many-tined stag; every seed differs.
- CRYSTALS: each crystal is two shaft boxes crossed at 45 degrees (reads as an eight-
  sided prism), a narrower cap and a point: six boxes. Lean grows with distance from the
  spine midline so the cluster fans.

----- PartNode.java -----
package com.example.horsegenetics.common.parts;

/**
 * One box of an attached part, in the horse model's own units (1 unit = 1/16 block,
 * y DOWN, front is -z - the same space {@code HdHorseModel} is written in).
 *
 * <p>The box hangs from its own origin along <b>-y</b> (up, in model space): its cube is
 * {@code x,z in [-girth/2, +girth/2]}, {@code y in [-len, 0]}. A child sits at
 * {@code t} of the way along its parent's length (1 = the tip), then rotates. Chaining
 * nodes with a small rotation each is how a curve or a helix is built out of boxes with
 * no matrix maths: the parent's pose composes for us.
 *
 * <p>Roots ({@code parent < 0}) are placed at the anchor plus {@code (ox, oy, oz)}.
 *
 * @param tex which 16x16 region of the part sheet the box samples ({@link PartSheet})
 */
public record PartNode(int parent, float t, float ox, float oy, float oz,
                       float rx, float ry, float rz, float len, float girth, int tex) {
}

/**

    public static final int BONE = 2;      // antler beam, ridged

    public static final int CRYSTAL_CORE = 5; // brighter core, alpha ~0.85

    private PartSheet() {
    }

    public static int u(int region) {
        return (region % (SIZE / REGION)) * REGION;
    }

    public static int v(int region) {
        return (region / (SIZE / REGION)) * REGION;
    }

----- HornSize.java -----
package com.example.horsegenetics.common.parts;

/**
 * Named horn lengths, in model units (a horse's head box is 6x5x7, so STANDARD is a
 * little longer than the whole head is tall). {@link HornGenerator} takes any float
 * length, so an allele can pick a class today and an epigenome number can slide
 * between them later without touching the generator.
 */
public enum HornSize {
    NUB(3.0f), SHORT(6.0f), STANDARD(10.0f), LONG(15.0f), NARWHAL(24.0f);

    public final float length;

    HornSize(float length) {
        this.length = length;
    }

    /** {@code s} in [0,1] -> a length between a nub and a narwhal, geometric so each step reads as "bigger". */
    public static float lengthFor(double s) {
        double c = Math.max(0.0, Math.min(1.0, s));
        return (float) (NUB.length * Math.pow(NARWHAL.length / NUB.length, c));
    }
}

----- HornGenerator.java -----
package com.example.horsegenetics.common.parts;

import java.util.ArrayList;
import java.util.List;

/**
 * A unicorn horn: a tapering chain of boxes. Each segment is a child of the one below,
 * turned a little about its own axis (twist) and tipped a little forward (curve), so a
 * spiral falls out of the parenting and needs no trigonometry here.
 *
 * <p>Base width grows with length so a narwhal horn is thicker at the root than a nub
 * is, but not proportionally - a horn scaled uniformly would look like a photograph
 * enlarged, not a different animal.
 */
public final class HornGenerator {

    private HornGenerator() {
    }

    /**
     * @param length   model units along the axis
     * @param twist    degrees of yaw added per segment (0 = smooth; ~25 = a clear spiral)
     * @param curve    degrees of forward tip added per segment (0 = straight)
     * @param tilt     radians forward of the head's own up, at the root
     */
    public static List<PartNode> generate(float length, float twist, float curve, float tilt) {
        float base = 1.3f + 0.055f * length;
        int n = Math.max(3, Math.round(length / 1.6f));
        float seg = length / n;
        List<PartNode> nodes = new ArrayList<>();
        float rTwist = (float) Math.toRadians(twist);
        float rCurve = (float) Math.toRadians(curve);
        for (int i = 0; i < n; i++) {
            float f = (float) i / n;
            // linear taper to a 0.6-unit point; never thinner (a sub-1/16 box shimmers)
            float girth = Math.max(0.6f, base * (1.0f - 0.9f * f));
            int tex = i >= n - 2 ? PartSheet.HORN_TIP : PartSheet.HORN;
            if (i == 0) {
                nodes.add(new PartNode(-1, 0f, 0f, 0f, 0f, tilt, 0f, 0f, seg, girth, tex));
            } else {
                // 0.9 not 1.0: a turned child at the exact tip leaves a wedge gap on the outside of the bend
                nodes.add(new PartNode(i - 1, 0.9f, 0f, 0f, 0f, rCurve, rTwist, 0f, seg, girth, tex));
            }
        }
        return nodes;
    }

    public static List<PartNode> generate(HornSize size) {
        return generate(size.length, size == HornSize.NUB ? 0f : 22f, 3f, 0.15f);
    }
}

----- AntlerGenerator.java -----
package com.example.horsegenetics.common.parts;

import com.example.horsegenetics.common.Rng;
import java.util.ArrayList;
import java.util.List;

/**
 * A mirrored pair of antlers grown by a tiny recursive rule: a beam is a short chain that
 * sweeps back and out; at some segments a tine forks off forward and outward; a long tine
 * may fork once more. Seed and size are the only inputs, so every horse's rack is its own
 * and still unmistakably an antler - geometry that is unbounded the way a coat is.
 *
 * <p>Angles are radians in the parent's frame. -y is up, -z is forward, so a positive
 * {@code rx} leans a limb forward and a positive {@code rz} swings its tip toward -x
 * (this horse's right); the left antler is the same list with the signs of {@code ry} and
 * {@code rz} flipped and its root moved to +x.
 */
public final class AntlerGenerator {

    private static final int MAX_DEPTH = 2;
    /** Hard cap on boxes; a herd of these must stay cheap. See "Cost per frame". */
    public static final int MAX_NODES = 44;

    private AntlerGenerator() {
    }

    /**
     * @param size   0..1: 0 is a spike-and-fork yearling, 1 a many-tined stag
     */
    public static List<PartNode> generate(Rng rng, double size) {
        double s = Math.max(0.0, Math.min(1.0, size));
        List<PartNode> out = new ArrayList<>();
        // one RNG draw list, replayed for the mirror, so both sides are the same antler
        long sideSeed = rng.nextLong();
        for (int side = -1; side <= 1; side += 2) {
            Rng r = new com.example.horsegenetics.common.SeededRng(sideSeed);
            float beam = (float) (8.0 + 10.0 * s);
            float girth = (float) (1.4 + 0.7 * s);
            int segs = 3 + (int) Math.round(2.0 * s);
            int tinesWanted = 1 + (int) Math.round(3.0 * s);
            grow(out, r, side, -1, beam / segs, girth, segs, tinesWanted, 0, true);
        }
        return out.size() > MAX_NODES ? new ArrayList<>(out.subList(0, MAX_NODES)) : out;
    }

    private static void grow(List<PartNode> out, Rng r, int side, int parent, float segLen, float girth,
                             int segs, int tines, int depth, boolean isBeam) {
        int prev = parent;
        int tinesLeft = tines;
        for (int i = 0; i < segs; i++) {
            float g = Math.max(0.6f, girth * (1.0f - 0.55f * i / segs));
            int tex = (i >= segs - 1) ? PartSheet.BONE_TIP : PartSheet.BONE;
            int me = out.size();
            if (prev < 0) {
                // root, behind the ear, pointing up and a little out and back
                out.add(new PartNode(-1, 0f, side * 2.0f, 0f, 0f,
                        -0.20f, 0f, side * 0.22f, segLen, g, tex));
            } else if (i == 0) {
                // first segment of a tine: forward and outward off its parent, from a chosen point along it
                out.add(new PartNode(prev, 0.55f + 0.35f * r.nextFloat(), 0f, 0f, 0f,
                        0.55f + 0.3f * r.nextFloat(), 0f, side * (0.35f + 0.25f * r.nextFloat()), segLen, g, tex));
            } else {
                // continuing segment: sweep back a touch and out - the beam's curve
                out.add(new PartNode(prev, 0.9f, 0f, 0f, 0f,
                        isBeam ? -0.20f : 0.12f, 0f, side * (isBeam ? 0.05f : 0.10f), segLen, g, tex));
            }
            // Tines fork off this segment, the beam only, from its second segment up
            if (isBeam && i >= 1 && tinesLeft > 0 && out.size() + 4 < MAX_NODES) {
                tinesLeft--;
                float tineLen = segLen * (0.9f + 0.9f * r.nextFloat()) * (1.0f - 0.12f * i);
                int tineSegs = tineLen > segLen * 1.4f && depth < MAX_DEPTH ? 2 : 1;
                growFrom(out, r, side, me, tineLen / tineSegs, girth * 0.62f, tineSegs, depth + 1);
            }
            prev = me;
        }
    }

    private static void growFrom(List<PartNode> out, Rng r, int side, int parent, float segLen, float girth,
                                 int segs, int depth) {
        grow(out, r, side, parent, segLen, girth, segs, 0, depth, false);
    }
}

----- CrystalGenerator.java -----
package com.example.horsegenetics.common.parts;

import com.example.horsegenetics.common.Rng;
import java.util.ArrayList;
import java.util.List;

/**
 * A cluster of crystals rooted along the spine. Each crystal is two shaft boxes crossed at
 * 45 degrees (which reads as an eight-sided prism), a narrower cap box and a point box on
 * top - six boxes for a crystal that looks faceted from any side. Lean grows with distance
 * from the spine's midline, so the cluster fans out the way a real druse does.
 *
 * <p>Root offsets are along z on the body bone (front is -z); {@link PartAnchor#SPINE}
 * puts the origin at the top of the body, so y = 0 sits on the back.
 */
public final class CrystalGenerator {

    public static final int MAX_CRYSTALS = 7;

    private CrystalGenerator() {
    }

    /**
     * @param count  crystals in the cluster, 1..{@link #MAX_CRYSTALS}
     * @param size   0..1 scales every crystal's length
     */
    public static List<PartNode> generate(Rng r, int count, double size) {
        int n = Math.max(1, Math.min(MAX_CRYSTALS, count));
        double s = Math.max(0.0, Math.min(1.0, size));
        List<PartNode> out = new ArrayList<>();
        for (int c = 0; c < n; c++) {
            float across = n == 1 ? 0f : (float) c / (n - 1) * 2f - 1f;          // -1 .. 1 along the spine
            float z = across * (1.5f + 1.0f * n);                                 // units fore-aft
            float x = (r.nextFloat() - 0.5f) * 3.0f;                              // scatter off the midline
            float len = (float) ((3.5 + 5.0 * s) * (0.55 + 0.7 * r.nextFloat()) * (1.0 - 0.35 * Math.abs(across)));
            float girth = Math.max(1.0f, len * (0.22f + 0.06f * r.nextFloat()));
            float lean = 0.15f + 0.5f * Math.abs(across) * r.nextFloat();
            float leanX = across * 0.6f * r.nextFloat();                          // fore-aft lean, away from the middle
            float rz = (x >= 0 ? -1f : 1f) * lean;
            int shaftA = out.size();
            out.add(new PartNode(-1, 0f, x, 0f, z, leanX, 0f, rz, len, girth, PartSheet.CRYSTAL));
            out.add(new PartNode(-1, 0f, x, 0f, z, leanX, (float) (Math.PI / 4), rz, len, girth, PartSheet.CRYSTAL));
            // cap and point ride on shaft A, so they lean with it
            int cap = out.size();
            out.add(new PartNode(shaftA, 1.0f, 0f, 0f, 0f, 0f, (float) (Math.PI / 8), 0f, len * 0.28f, girth * 0.72f, PartSheet.CRYSTAL_CORE));
            out.add(new PartNode(cap, 1.0f, 0f, 0f, 0f, 0f, 0f, 0f, len * 0.18f, Math.max(0.5f, girth * 0.36f), PartSheet.CRYSTAL_CORE));
        }
        return out;
    }
}

package com.example.horsegenetics.common.parts;

import com.example.horsegenetics.common.SeededRng;
import java.util.List;

/**
 * The one door the client uses: (kind, size, seed) -> boxes. Deterministic, so the server,
 * the client and the wasm designer would all agree on the shape without shipping it.
 */
public final class PartGenerators {

    private PartGenerators() {
    }

    /** @param size 0..1; means "length" for a horn, "tine count and reach" for antlers, "crystal size" for crystals */
    public static List<PartNode> build(PartKind kind, double size, long seed) {
        SeededRng rng = new SeededRng(seed, kind.name());
        return switch (kind) {
            case HORN -> HornGenerator.generate(HornSize.lengthFor(size), 22f, 3f, 0.15f);
            case ANTLERS -> AntlerGenerator.generate(rng, size);
            case CRYSTALS -> CrystalGenerator.generate(rng, 3 + (int) Math.round(4 * size), size);
        };
    }
}

    /** Builds one baked ModelPart tree from a generated box list. */
    final class PartModelFactory {
        private PartModelFactory() {}

        static ModelPart bake(List<PartNode> nodes) {
            MeshDefinition mesh = new MeshDefinition();
            PartDefinition root = mesh.getRoot();
            PartDefinition[] defs = new PartDefinition[nodes.size()];
            for (int i = 0; i < nodes.size(); i++) {
                PartNode n = nodes.get(i);
                float g = n.girth();
                // Box unwrap is 4g wide and (len + g) tall; scale it into one 16x16 region
                // so a face never bleeds into its neighbour's region. HdHorseModel does the
                // same with its constant HD = 0.5F.
                float s = Math.min(1.0F, (PartSheet.REGION - 1) / Math.max(4.0F * g, n.len() + g));
                CubeListBuilder cube = CubeListBuilder.create()
                        .texOffs(PartSheet.u(n.tex()), PartSheet.v(n.tex()))
                        .addBox(-g / 2, -n.len(), -g / 2, g, n.len(), g, CubeDeformation.NONE, s, s);
                PartDefinition parent = n.parent() < 0 ? root : defs[n.parent()];
                // a child sits t of the way along its parent, toward -y
                float along = n.parent() < 0 ? 0f : -nodes.get(n.parent()).len() * n.t();
                defs[i] = parent.addOrReplaceChild("n" + i, cube,
                        PartPose.offsetAndRotation(n.ox(), n.oy() + along, n.oz(), n.rx(), n.ry(), n.rz()));
            }
            // UNVERIFIED: that a LayerDefinition can be baked at run time, outside
            // EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterLayerDefinitions. If it cannot, bake the finite
            // set (kind x size bucket x 16 seeds) at registration instead - same cache key.
            return LayerDefinition.create(mesh, PartSheet.SIZE, PartSheet.SIZE).bakeRoot();
        }
    }

    /** What the extract step hands the layer: cheap, immutable, comparable (it is the cache key). */
    record ResolvedPart(Key key, int tint, boolean emissive) {
        /** The MODEL cache key: shape only. Tint and glow are per-draw, never part of it -
         *  keying on tint would bake one model per colour per horse. */
        record Key(PartKind kind, int sizeBucket, int seedBucket) {
            static final int SIZE_BUCKETS = 16;   // size 0..1 -> 0..15
            static final int SEED_BUCKETS = 16;   // one horse's seed -> one of 16 racks/clusters
            double size() { return (sizeBucket + 0.5) / SIZE_BUCKETS; }
            long seed() { return seedBucket; }
        }
        PartKind kind() { return key.kind(); }
    }
    // Quantising is what keeps a herd cheap: 3 kinds x 16 x 16 = at most 768 baked models
    // in the whole game, ever, however many horses exist - and the look still varies by
    // horse because tint, tilt and (for horns) the continuous length are per horse.
    // A horn should NOT be bucketed by seed (it has none): use SEED_BUCKETS = 1 for it.

    public class AttachedPartLayer extends RenderLayer<HorseRenderState, HorseModel> {

        /** HdHorseModel.createHdLayer applies MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F). It scales the
         *  ModelPart CUBES and POSES but is not in the poseStack, so an anchor written in
         *  model units must be scaled by it here, and so must the part itself.
         *  UNVERIFIED - if the parts sit 10% off the head, this is the first suspect. */
        private static final float HORSE_SCALE = 1.1F;
        private static final Identifier SHEET = Identifier.fromNamespaceAndPath(
                HorseGenetics.MOD_ID, "textures/entity/horse/part_sheet.png");

        private final Map<ResolvedPart.Key, PartModel> models = new LinkedHashMap<>(64, 0.75F, true) {
            @Override protected boolean removeEldestEntry(Map.Entry<ResolvedPart.Key, PartModel> e) {
                return size() > 96;     // LRU cap; the world's working set is a handful
            }
        };

        public AttachedPartLayer(RenderLayerParent<HorseRenderState, HorseModel> renderer) {
            super(renderer);
        }

        @Override
        public void submit(PoseStack poseStack, SubmitNodeCollector collector, int light,
                           HorseRenderState state, float yRot, float xRot) {
            // one early return for the common case - a horse with no parts costs nothing
            if (!(state instanceof GeneticHorseRenderState g) || state.isInvisible || g.parts.isEmpty()) {
                return;
            }
            for (ResolvedPart p : g.parts) {
                if (state.isBaby) {
                    continue;   // P5: hidden on foals until the owner says otherwise
                }
                PartModel model = models.computeIfAbsent(p.key(), k ->
                        new PartModel(PartModelFactory.bake(PartGenerators.build(k.kind(), k.size(), k.seed()))));
                poseStack.pushPose();
                anchor(p.kind().anchor, poseStack);
                poseStack.scale(HORSE_SCALE, HORSE_SCALE, HORSE_SCALE);
                int tint = p.tint() != 0 ? p.tint() : defaultTint(p.kind());
                // same call, same argument order, as BraidLayer.draw
                collector.order(1).submitModel(model, state, poseStack,
                        RenderTypes.entityTranslucent(SHEET), light, OverlayTexture.NO_OVERLAY,
                        state.outlineColor, null, tint, null);
                if (p.emissive()) {     // second, full-bright pass: the HayPortalRenderer call
                    collector.order(2).submitModel(model, state, poseStack,
                            RenderTypes.entityTranslucentEmissive(SHEET, false), 0x00F000F0,
                            OverlayTexture.NO_OVERLAY, state.outlineColor, null, tint, null);
                }
                poseStack.popPose();
            }
        }

        /** Maps the common/ enum to the model-part chain. The only place that knows a bone name. */
        private void anchor(PartAnchor a, PoseStack ps) {
            ModelPart root = this.getParentModel().root();
            switch (a) {
                case FOREHEAD, CROWN -> {
                    // head_parts carries the neck angle; head is its child (PartPose.ZERO)
                    ModelPart headParts = root.getChild("head_parts");
                    headParts.translateAndRotate(ps);
                    headParts.getChild("head").translateAndRotate(ps);
                    // head box: x -3..3, y -11..-6, z -2..5 (front is -z). Forehead is the
                    // top surface just behind the eyes; the crown is the top-rear edge
                    // between the ears (ears are at z 4..5). UNVERIFIED offsets - tune live.
                    if (a == PartAnchor.FOREHEAD) { ps.translate(0, -11 * S, -0.5 * S); }
                    else { ps.translate(0, -11 * S, 3.0 * S); }
                }
                case SPINE -> {
                    root.getChild("body").translateAndRotate(ps);
                    // body box top is y = -8; withers are near z = -6..-8 in body space.
                    ps.translate(0, -8 * S, -6 * S);
                }
            }
        }
        // where S = HORSE_SCALE / 16F, the unit conversion the poseStack expects (model
        // units are 1/16 block) folded with the 1.1 transform above.
        private static final float S = HORSE_SCALE / 16F;

        private static int defaultTint(PartKind k) {
            return switch (k) {
                case HORN -> 0xFFF2E6C8;        // ivory
                case ANTLERS -> 0xFFC9B48C;     // aged bone
                case CRYSTALS -> 0xCC66CCFF;    // pale blue, alpha 0.8 - the translucency
            };
        }
    }

12.5 Honest limits of "basic code"
-----------------------------------
- Boxes facet. A horn is a stack of small prisms, not a smooth cone; at Minecraft scale
  with a fine grain texture it reads as a carved horn. A smooth cone would need custom
  vertex geometry (submitCustomGeometry, as the hay portal uses) - possible, and out of
  this treatment's scope.
- The twist is shallow on purpose (22 degrees per segment) because a square section
  turned more than that shows visible gaps between segments. Tightening it needs the
  ridge-box trick from section 11.3, not a bigger number.
- Crystals overlap their own shafts, and with alpha the overlap doubles the blend. If it
  looks muddy, the fix is the cap and point drawn opaque and only the shafts translucent
  (two passes), not a new model.
- Antlers are symmetric on purpose. Asymmetric racks are a one-line change (a second
  seed for the mirror) and a genetics decision: do the two sides inherit independently?
- Nothing above animates. Every part rides a bone that already does.

13. Epigenome-driven shape: one gene, many horns, antlers and crystals
=======================================================================
Requested: can the epigenome control the SHAPE of a part (longer or shorter horn,
more tines, more crystal points), all from the same gene, with performance in mind.
Answer: yes, and the cheap way is to sort every shape number by what it costs to
change, not by what it means. Nothing here is built or run; the Minecraft-side
claims are UNVERIFIED and stage 0 must prove them (CLAUDE.md hard rule 8).

13.1 Why the epigenome fits this exactly
-----------------------------------------
- A gene declares an EpiSchema of named numbers on each allele copy
  (EpiValue.uniform / gaussian / power / category / seed / colour). Founders roll
  them, EpiDrift nudges them at every breeding (median move 0.1% of the design
  span), and the inspector and storage are generic. A part gene therefore needs
  NO new machinery: it declares "length", "girth", "tines", ... and the epigenome
  already inherits, drifts and stores them.
- Drift is tiny and continuous for SCALARs, and rare and wholesale for SEEDs and
  CATEGORYs. That maps onto geometry by itself: a scalar should move a part
  smoothly (a line's horn grows longer over generations); a seed or category
  should swap it for a different one (a re-rolled rack, a different crystal
  habit) - a real event, exactly as the epigenome docs already say.
- Mind the clamp. EpiValue's hard bound is +/-8 design spans by default, on
  purpose ("a line bred for 300 generations may leave the wild range"). For a part
  that is a horn a chunk long. Every part value gets .clampedTo(lo, hi) at the
  schema AND a min/max in the mapping, so a drifted value is never a rendering
  hazard. Design range = what a wild horse shows; clamp = the most the renderer
  will ever draw.

13.2 The rule: classify each number by what it costs to change
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tier A - FREE (pose-stack transform at submit time; no rebake, no allocation)
  Overall length, girth, tilt, curve-of-the-whole, lean, tint (EpiValue.colour),
  emissive on/off, scale with the horse. All are a poseStack.scale / rotate before
  submitModel, and a PoseStack transform is copied into the submit node, so it is
  safe with the deferred submit model. Non-uniform scale on the whole part is one
  affine map of the finished shape, so a bent or spiral chain stays connected and
  only its faces stretch. This tier alone gives "longer or shorter unicorn horn" and
  "wider or slimmer", continuously, for zero cost.
Tier B - CHEAP (visibility and per-child scale on a part baked once at its MAXIMUM)
  Counts: tines, crystals, spines. Bake the part with every element it can ever
  have, ordered so the first k are always a sensible k-element part, then show k.
  Two consequences worth having:
   1. THE CAP IS STRUCTURAL. A drifted "tines" value of 200 cannot draw 200
      boxes; the model only contains MAX_NODES. Performance is bounded by
      construction, not by a clamp someone remembers to write.
   2. FRACTIONAL GROWTH. A count of 4.6 is four full elements plus a fifth at 60%
      scale. Drift is continuous, so the look is continuous: no pop when a line
      crosses an integer. (A count that should NEVER be fractional is a CATEGORY.)
  Antlers: tines ordered base-to-tip on the beam, alternating sides only by mirror;
  crystals: ordered centre-out, so 1 is a single spire and 7 is a druse. "More
  points on a crystal" is the same trick on the point boxes of one crystal.
Tier C - EXPENSIVE (topology changes: needs a different baked model)
  Twist per segment, curve per segment, tip sharpness, branching pattern. Do NOT
  rebake per horse. Quantise to a small fixed grid and cache by key:
     Key(kind, styleBucket, seedVariant)     styleBucket 0..3, seedVariant 0..15
  A seed or category picks the variant; a scalar in a tier C role is bucketed to 4
  steps. Worst case the whole client ever holds kinds x 4 x 16 baked models (about
  200 small models, built lazily, and in practice a handful). The draft's
  Key(kind, (int)(size*15), (int)(seed&15)) is the same idea; the change here is to
  stop putting size in the key, because size is tier A or B.
HornSize's five classes fit this: NUB..NARWHAL are the tier C length CLASSES (a
3-unit nub does not want the 24-unit horn's segment count, and squashing 14
segments to 3 units leaves sub-1/16 boxes that shimmer), and tier A stretches
0.8x-1.25x WITHIN a class so the slide between classes is smooth. Class from
floor of a length value, stretch from its remainder - one epigenome number, no seam.

13.3 The three parts, concretely (one gene each, numbers on the allele copy)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UNICORN HORN gene ("horn")
  length     uniform 0..1 -> class (tier C, 5 steps) + stretch (tier A)
  girth      uniform 0.7..1.3 -> tier A (x/z scale)
  twist      category 4 -> smooth / loose / spiral / tight  (tier C bucket)
  curve      uniform -> tier C bucket, plus tier A whole-part tilt
  tilt       uniform -> tier A rotation
  tip_r/g/b  EpiValue.colour -> tier A tint (pearl to gold; drifts as a lineage)
  Cost per horse: one submit, 3-15 boxes. Emissive is a second submit of the same
  model (HayPortalRenderer pattern) and doubles that, so only glowing horns pay it.
ANTLER gene ("antlers")
  rack       seed -> seedVariant 0..15 (tier C); rare re-roll = a new rack
  tines      uniform 1..6 -> tier B visibility + fractional last tine
  reach      uniform -> tier A beam-length scale
  asymmetry  uniform -> second seed for the mirror only when past a threshold
  Cost: up to MAX_NODES boxes baked, only the visible ones drawn; a typical horse
  draws 10-20. The draft's worst case over 2000 seeds was 20 boxes.
CRYSTAL gene ("crystals")
  count      uniform 1..7 -> tier B visibility, centre-out, fractional last crystal
  height     uniform -> tier A scale, and per-crystal jitter in the baked data
  habit      category 3 (needle / stubby / cluster-fan) -> tier C
  points     uniform 1..3 -> tier B on each crystal's cap boxes
  tint       colour channels, tier A. Translucent = the one render type that costs
             more per box (blending, and it cannot be batched with opaque); keep the
             opaque cap and point in the opaque pass and only the shafts translucent
             (section 12.5), so the expensive pass is the smallest.
"All from the same gene": the three above are three PartKinds, but a single gene
can own several as separate contributions and one "growth" schema can feed them
(e.g. a horned line with a "vigour" number that lengthens horn AND adds a tine).
Cross-modification is the CutieMarkContribution move: other genes fold over the
contribution (a size gene scales it, a glow gene sets emissive) and it can only
change a part that some gene has already granted.

13.4 Performance budget
------------------------
The frame cost is a function of visible boxes and submits, so hold both down:
1. RESOLVE ONCE, NOT PER FRAME. CutieMarkLayer calls markFor on every submit, and
   markFor folds over Genes.codeOrder() each time. Fine for one emblem on a few
   horses; wrong to copy for parts on a herd. Resolve the horse's parts in
   extractRenderState (or better, cache the resolved list on the entity and
   recompute only when its genotype/epigenome changes - the HORSE_GEAR attachment is
   the precedent for per-entity client data) and hand the layer a ready, immutable
   List<ResolvedPart>. Steady state allocates nothing.
2. EARLY EXIT. A horse with no parts costs one isEmpty() on the first line, the way
   BraidLayer does. Invisible horses and foals return before anything.
3. SHARED MODELS. Baked models are shared across every horse with the same Key, so
   memory scales with variety in the cache, not with the herd.
4. DISTANCE LOD. Past roughly 24 blocks draw tier C models only (no fractional last
   element, no second emissive pass); past roughly 48 skip the part. State carries a
   camera distance (UNVERIFIED which field in 26.1.2's EntityRenderState).
5. HARD BOX CAP. Total visible boxes per horse is bounded by structure (13.2 tier B)
   and asserted in a common/ test over many seeds, as Check.java already does.
6. NO PER-FRAME MUTATION OF A SHARED MODEL. Setting visible / scale on the cached
   ModelPart and then submitting is the obvious tier B implementation and a trap:
   the submit is deferred, so the next horse can overwrite it before the first is
   drawn. Drive tier B from state inside the model's setupAnim (the vanilla
   pattern), or keep a tiny per-count model variant in the Key. UNVERIFIED whether
   26.1.2 calls setupAnim per submit or at collect time - stage 0 tests it with two
   horses of different tine counts on screen in the same frame.
7. MEASURE. Budget: 20 antlered horses in one pen, against the same pen without
   parts. Look for a cliff, not a percentage.

13.5 What needs proving before any of this is trusted (stage 0 additions)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ModelPart / PoseStack non-uniform scale behaves as tier A assumes (poseStack.scale
  is certain; ModelPart xScale/yScale/zScale existing in 26.1.2 is not verified, and
  it is what per-element tier B scaling would use - the fallback is per-count model
  variants in the Key).
- Deferred submit and setupAnim ordering (13.4 item 6).
- Scale composition: the part inherits the horse's genetic size scale only if the
  parent transform includes it; check on a tiny and a huge horse.
- A non-uniform-scaled part's lighting normals look right (PoseStack maintains a
  normal matrix, but a strongly squashed part is worth looking at).

13.6 Consequences for the plan and the earlier decisions
----------------------------------------------------------
- P4 ("does a part carry an epigenome value?") flips from "no for the first slice"
  to "yes, and it is what makes the gene a gene". The concern behind the original NO
  was a broken promise: a value the layer does not honour. The answer is the same
  rule CutieMarkGene uses - every schema field has a row in 13.3 saying which tier
  draws it, and a test fails when a declared value has no consumer.
- Stage 2 (first gene = unicorn horn) declares the horn schema above. Ship tier A
  and the class steps first; tier B and the antler/crystal counts are stage 3.
- PartGenerators.build(kind, size, seed) becomes build(kind, PartParams, variant):
  the generator builds the MAXIMUM part for a style bucket and variant, and
  PartParams is what tier A/B read at draw time. Generators stay in common/, plain
  arithmetic, so the wasm designer can preview a horn's silhouette from the same
  numbers (hard rule 3: an @JSExport, not a JS port).
- Designer parity (P6, rule 5): new editor-visible epigenome values appear in the
  horse designer's epigenome list for free, since it walks the schema; only the
  3D drawing is missing, and P6's "listed trait, not drawn" still stands.
- The breed format (stage 4) can pin these numbers per breed the way it already pins
  epigenetic values, so a "reindeer" breed is a band on "tines", not new code.
- Known limit: boxes facet (12.5). Epigenome control changes proportions and
  counts; it does not make a horn smooth. That needs submitCustomGeometry, which
  would also make tier C unnecessary for horns (a mesh built from the numbers)
  at the price of owning the vertex code. Not recommended for v1.

13.7 Recommended order
-----------------------
1. Stage 0 as written, plus 13.5.
2. Horn gene with the tier A numbers and the five classes; visible payoff, no
   deferred-model risk.
3. Antler rack variants (tier C seed) and tine count (tier B) - the first thing
   that needs the setupAnim answer, so it is the natural second test.
4. Crystals with the opaque/translucent split.
5. Then the breed hook, so a breed's band on "tines" gives the reindeer.


14. Batching parts like coats, and the performance switches
============================================================
Requested: batch the part renders the way the rest of the renders are batched, and
give players a config way to turn the new visuals down or off. Disabling stops
RENDERING only; the gene, the part, the inheritance and the horse are untouched.
Read off the repo, not run: the mechanism to copy is GeneticCoatTextureFactory's
(mayBake / timed / placeholder) and ClientConfig's coats.* keys.

14.1 What "batched like coats" means here (four pieces, all already in the mod)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DISTANCE GATE. Coats: withinDetailDistance() reads distanceToCameraSq (written by
   vanilla's EntityRenderer.extractRenderState; the renderer's own javadoc says it was
   checked in bytecode on 2026-09-13) against coats.detailDistance. Parts reuse the
   same field with their own key. This SETTLES the "UNVERIFIED which camera-distance
   field" in 13.4 item 4: use distanceToCameraSq, no new lookup.
2. TIME BUDGET. Coats: mayBake() allows the first bake in each 50 ms window
   unconditionally and further ones only while spentInWindow is under
   coats.bakeBudgetMs; timed() charges each bake to the window and to session totals.
   Every tier C bake (13.2) goes through the same shape: a PartBakeBudget with its own
   window and its own key, first-in-window always runs so nothing is starved. The
   stage 0 spike measures one bake; the default budget is set from that number.
3. STAND-IN WHILE WAITING. Coats wear a shared wild-type placeholder that is exempt
   from the budget. A part has no useful stand-in, so a horse whose part is over
   budget simply DRAWS NO PART for that frame and picks it up the next window
   (a one-to-few-frame pop-in). If stage 0 shows that reads badly, the fallback is
   the nearest already-baked key of the same kind. Do not invent a placeholder model.
4. SHARED, KEYED CACHE. Coats: CACHE.get(coatKey, loader). Parts: the Key(kind,
   styleBucket, seedVariant) map of 13.2. Same reason - the cost belongs to the
   variety, not the herd.
Not batched, on purpose: tier A and tier B (13.2) are per-frame transforms and
visibility, not bakes, so they never wait on a budget. Only tier C does.
Also keep the submit side grouped: submit all parts of a kind consecutively with the
same render type per frame is what collector.order(n) already gives (the draft uses
order(1) for the body pass and order(2) for the emissive pass). Do not interleave
translucent and opaque submits per horse; one order per pass.

14.2 The config: a `parts.*` block in ClientConfig, beside coats.*
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Client-only, per player, per machine - the same reason coats.* is: nothing about the
world changes, so two players on one server may disagree and still see the same
horses. Same shape as coats.*: a static ModConfigSpec value, a DEFAULT_ constant, and
a safe getter that returns the default on IllegalStateException (config not loaded).

  parts.enabled          boolean, default true. MASTER. false: no attached part of any
                         kind draws, and the layer returns on its first line.
  parts.kinds.disabled   list of PartKind ids, default empty. Turns off horns, antlers,
                         crystals, wings, ... one by one. A list of ids rather than one
                         boolean per kind, so a NEW kind needs no config edit and no
                         new key; an id that no longer resolves is dropped on read
                         (the GENE_FILTER rule), never a crash.
  parts.emissive         boolean, default true. false: the second, full-bright submit
                         is skipped. The part still draws, lit normally. Removes one
                         submit per glowing part.
  parts.translucent      boolean, default true. false: a translucent part (crystals)
                         draws in the opaque pass with its alpha forced to full. Saves
                         the blended pass, which cannot be sorted or batched with
                         opaque work. The look changes (solid crystal), the horse does
                         not.
  parts.detailDistance   int blocks, default a value picked after stage 0, range 8-256.
                         Past it, no part draws. (Coats keep a made coat at any range
                         because a swap flickers; a skipped part just is not drawn, so
                         no such rule is needed.)
  parts.bakeBudgetMs     int ms per 50 ms, range 0-50, 0 = "one bake at a time"
                         (identical wording to coats.bakeBudgetMs).
"Anything else": tier B's fractional-last-element growth and the foal case are not
switches, because they cost nothing measurable. If stage 0 finds another expensive
axis it becomes another key here, decided by a measurement, not in advance.
Reading them: resolve ALL of these once in extractRenderState into plain booleans on
GeneticHorseRenderState, never inside the per-part loop, so a disabled part costs one
field test and a config read is not paid per box.

14.3 The rule that makes "disable" safe
----------------------------------------
- Render-only. Every one of these keys gates a submit; none touches genotype,
  epigenome, breeding, the inspector, the designer's listed trait (P6), the horse
  browser or a save. A horse with a horn and horns switched off still HAS the horn:
  it breeds, drifts and shows in the gene list, and comes back the moment the switch
  does.
- No gameplay coupling. A part must never move the hitbox, the size, the stats or the
  riding position, or "disabled" would change the world for one player and not another.
  State that in the layer's class comment and test it: nothing in the part path is
  read by the server.
- Not in any cache key. The switches decide whether to submit a model, never which
  model exists, so flipping one needs no rebake and no restart, and the shared cache
  is the same for every player.
- Live. Read each frame in extract (one field read each), so the change applies as
  soon as the config screen closes, with no world reload.
- Server config is untouched. A dedicated server cannot read a client config and has
  nothing to render; this is not a place for a server-side "parts off".

14.4 The note for people with performance problems
---------------------------------------------------
Three places, each one line, written when stage 1 ships:
- The config comments themselves (each key's comment says what it saves, the way
  coats.detailDistance says "lower it if walking toward a big herd stutters").
- README.md, in the section on performance or settings: "If horns, antlers or crystals
  cost you frames, open the client config and set parts.enabled to false, or turn off
  only parts.emissive / parts.translucent. Nothing about your horses changes." README
  is user-facing only (CLAUDE.md), and this is exactly that.
- wiki/model-parts.html's Gameplay tab when it grows one, and wiki/compatibility.html
  for the shader-pack line (translucency and emissive are the parts most likely to
  misbehave under a shader pack, and turning them off is the workaround).

14.5 Changes to the plan and to verification
---------------------------------------------
- Stage 1 (registry and layer) also lands the parts.* block and PartBakeBudget, so a
  tester can turn things off from the first playable build. It is where the measuring
  is done, so the switches exist before the numbers do.
- Stage 0 uses the switches as its measuring tool: the 20-horse pen, run with
  parts.enabled off, then on, then emissive and translucent off one at a time. The
  difference IS the per-axis cost, and the defaults are picked from it.
- Verification tab gains: each switch toggled live with no restart; parts.enabled off
  leaves a horse's gene list, breeding and save byte-for-byte unchanged; two players
  with different settings on one server see the same horses drawn differently;
  translucent off looks acceptable, not broken; a part over budget appears within a
  few frames and never flickers; a stale id in parts.kinds.disabled is dropped, not a
  crash.
- Unit tests: the kinds list drops an unknown id; the getters return defaults before
  the config loads (the IllegalStateException path).
- Section 8's "20-horse herd: no frame-time cliff" becomes measurable on both sides of
  the master switch.
