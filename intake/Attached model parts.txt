Attached model parts - treatment and implementation guide

Status: PLAN ONLY. Nothing here is built and no code was written for it.
Anything that touches a Minecraft or NeoForge API is marked UNVERIFIED and must be
read out of the 26.1.2 sources (or proved in a running client) before it is relied
on - CLAUDE.md hard rule 8. Nothing has been seen in-game.

Where this goes: intake/ is an inbox, not a plan (START HERE.txt). When this is
picked up it is routed onto wiki/model-parts.html's Roadmap tab (that page already
exists, already has the tab and its summary box) and this file is deleted. Section 9
says exactly what goes where.


1. Why this one, and why now
=============================
Four separate plans are waiting on the same piece of machinery and each of them
currently describes its own private version of it:
- wiki/model-parts.html      horns, dorsal spines, crystals, wings, tusks, antlers.
- Horse shoes.txt (step 4)   a HorseShoeLayer that has to find four hoof anchors.
- wiki/saddle-pads.html      a pad drawn over the coat and under the saddle.
- wiki/breed-book.html       the reindeer breeds - "content for machinery that does
                             not exist yet" - need antlers and wings.
roadmap-execution.html already lists "gear attachment and render contract" as a
foundation to settle before a catalogue of tack, wings or model parts. The shoe
treatment is the first thing that would write a bespoke layer if this is not
scoped first, and the gear treatment's "render" line would be the second.

The owner's own words on the page: a batch of these is "a HUGE win for how cool
they look". High priority: unicorn horn, spikes / dorsal spines, crystalline
growths - small, head- or spine-parented, big visual payoff. Lower: pegasus wings,
lion and tufted tails, hoof feathering, tusks, antlers.

This is the mod's first visual that is not a texture. That is the whole reason it
is a foundation and not a content batch: the coat pipeline is texture-only, and
this is the first time common/ has to say "this horse has a THING" rather than
"this pixel is this colour".


2. What already exists (so a session does not redo it)
=======================================================
Read these in this order; nothing else is needed to start.
- wiki/model-parts.html - the plan, the wings-off-the-elytra research (texture
  side transfers entirely, model and layer classes do not, mesh is a dozen lines to
  copy), and two open questions left open on purpose (what drives wings; one
  texture or many). The plain-layer-versus-GeckoLib question is SETTLED there in
  the plain layer's favour. Do not reopen it. This treatment inherits that answer.
- neoforge/client/CutieMarkLayer.java - the worked example of a body-parented
  layer on THIS renderer. It gets getParentModel().root().getChild("body"), calls
  body.translateAndRotate(poseStack) to inherit the animated transform, and then
  works in body-local space (x in [-5,5], y in [-8,2], z in [-17,5], front is -z).
  Every part in this treatment is that move with a different child name and offset.
- neoforge/client/GeneticHorseRenderer.java - the layer list. Registration order is
  draw order: EmissiveCoatLayer, CutieMarkLayer, BraidLayer, then vanilla's
  body-armour and saddle SimpleEquipmentLayers. A new layer goes in here and the
  position is a real decision (section 6, "Draw order").
- neoforge/client/HdHorseModel.java - the horse's mesh. Named children present
  today: body, head_parts, head, mane, tail, left_/right_hind_leg,
  left_/right_front_leg, left_/right_ear. So head and leg anchors EXIST by name; the
  shoe treatment's worry that "HorseModel does not expose named hoof ModelParts" is
  about vanilla's HorseModel - the HD model already has the legs. (UNVERIFIED that
  the foot end of a leg part is a usable anchor without a per-leg offset; the stage 0
  spike proves it.)
- common/.../genetics/CutieMarkContribution.java and EyePatchContribution - the
  house pattern for "a gene has an opinion the renderer must honour": a record in
  common/, folded over by the expressing gene, handed to the client, and its own
  javadoc says the record's fields are exactly what the render layer knows how to
  draw and both change in the same commit. Attached parts follow that shape.
- GeneticHorseRenderState - where per-horse data crosses from extract to submit.
  Everything a layer reads goes through it (see how braidMane is copied out of the
  HorseGear attachment in extractRenderState).
- HORSE_GEAR / HorseTackSlot - already the authority for anything the PLAYER puts on
  a horse. Parts that are worn are gear; parts that are grown are genes. See 4.

What does NOT exist: any Blockbench model, any baked LayerDefinition beyond the HD
horse and its foal, any registry of parts, any way for common/ to name a part, any
per-part tint or emissive path, any scale-with-the-horse rule for an attached mesh.


3. The one-sentence outcome
============================
A gene or a breed can say "this horse has a unicorn horn", common/ carries that as
an id and nothing else, and the client draws the horn parented to the animated head
- with every part and every horse going through ONE layer, ONE registry and ONE
placement contract, so the fiftieth part is a model file and a line of data.


4. The design calls this treatment proposes (all need an owner answer)
=======================================================================
These go to wiki/decisions.html as D-entries when routed. They are proposals, in
the order they block work. Each has a recommendation so the owner is choosing, not
authoring.

P1. Grown versus worn - one system or two?
  Grown = gene- or breed-driven, part of the horse (horn, spines, wings, feathering,
  tusks, antlers). Worn = a player item in a HorseTackSlot (shoes, and later any
  tack). Recommendation: TWO SOURCES, ONE RENDERER. A grown part is resolved from
  the genotype in common/; a worn part is resolved from the HORSE_GEAR attachment on
  the client. Both reduce to the same thing by the time they reach the layer: a list
  of "part id + anchor + tint + emissive". The layer does not know which kind it is
  drawing. This is what stops the shoe layer, the horn layer and the pad layer
  becoming three layers with three z-fighting rules.
  Not settled by this treatment: whether a worn item may EVER replace a grown part
  (a helmet over a horn). Recommendation: no, out of scope, note it as a known
  interaction on the Verification tab the first time it is visible.

P2. Where does a part get its geometry?
  REVISED after section 11: the owner does NOT need Blockbench for the first batch.
  The HD horse itself is built in Java from CubeListBuilder boxes (HdHorseModel), and
  so are the cart models (EasyMeshBuilder); a Blockbench export is only a nicer way
  to author the same list of rotated boxes. Options:
  (a) hand-authored boxes in a small JSON box-list format (from, size, rotation,
      pivot, uv) that is exactly what a Blockbench "generic" export reduces to, so a
      Blockbench mesh can be dropped in later without a second loader;
  (b) GENERATED boxes: a parametric generator (taper, spiral, branch, fan, cluster)
      expands a few numbers into the box list in code;
  (c) vanilla geometry reused (the elytra mesh for wings; section 11.5).
  Recommendation: (a)+(b) sharing one PartSpec of boxes; Blockbench becomes an
  optional importer, not a prerequisite. UNVERIFIED: whether 26.1.2 lets a
  LayerDefinition be baked at run time (LayerDefinition.bakeRoot()) so a per-horse
  variant can be built and cached, or whether every LayerDefinition must be
  registered during EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterLayerDefinitions as ClientSetup
  does for HD_HORSE. If the latter, variety comes from a finite baked set plus
  per-horse pose scale/tilt (section 11.6). Stage 0 answers this first.

P3. How does common/ name a part?
  Recommendation: a plain string id, like every other cross-module identity in this
  mod ("ids are plain strings, resolved against the live registry" - the house
  convention; gap 265 is the standing warning about bare-string duplicates, so the
  id constants live in ONE class in common/, not typed at each use site). common/
  declares a part by id and a slot; it never knows a mesh exists. This is the same
  shape as GradientLut arriving in the renderer as an int[].
  Proposed record, in common/genetics beside CutieMarkContribution:
     AttachedPartContribution(String partId, PartAnchor anchor, float scale,
                              int tint /* 0 = untinted */, boolean emissive)
  PartAnchor is a small enum, NOT a bone name: HEAD_TOP, HEAD_SIDE_PAIR, SPINE_ROW,
  WITHERS_PAIR, TAIL_BASE, HOOF_x4, ... The client maps anchor -> ModelPart + offset.
  That keeps Minecraft's model-part names out of common/ (hard rule 1) and means
  retargeting an anchor is a client edit, not a gene edit.

P4. Does a part carry an epigenome value?
  Recommendation: NO for the first slice. A horn that varies per horse (length,
  twist, tint) is the natural fit for the per-allele-copy epigenome and is a
  tempting extension, but CutieMarkGene.markFor reading epigenetic seed for size and
  tilt shows the cost: every varied field must be honoured by the layer or it is a
  broken promise. Ship fixed-shape parts first; variation is a Roadmap entry on this
  page once the fixed version has been SEEN.

P5. How does a foal look?
  The mod has a separate HdBabyHorseModel. Recommendation: parts scale with the
  render state's age scale and use the SAME anchor names, and a part may declare
  "hidden on foals" (a horn on a newborn is a design call, not a rendering one;
  default hidden until the owner says otherwise). UNVERIFIED that the baby model's
  head and body children share the adult's names and origin - check both models
  before trusting a shared anchor table.

P6. What does the horse designer / CustomHorseSpawnScreen show?
  Hard rule 5 makes these one screen in two places. Both draw a 2D coat today. If a
  part is invisible in the designer, the designer lies about what the horse is. This
  is the biggest open cost of the whole feature and is NOT solved here.
  Recommendation: v1 the designer shows the part as a LISTED TRAIT with an icon and
  says plainly that it is not drawn in the preview; the in-game paper doll on the
  gear screen (which already renders a live horse) is where it is seen. Anything
  fancier is a separate plan. Record the divergence in a comment on BOTH files per
  hard rule 5.


5. The plan, in slices (one bullet, one context window)
========================================================
Per START HERE.txt each slice is one session, ends with the closeout routine, and
the next slice is not started in the same conversation.

Stage 0 - the spike. One horn, one horse, prove the pipeline. NO gene.
  Hypothesis: a single hand-written ModelPart child of the HD model's "head" cannot
  be added (that moves the mesh the coat UVs are baked against - the one thing that
  cannot shift; see model-parts.html), but a SEPARATE LayerDefinition rendered by a
  new RenderLayer, parented via getParentModel().root().getChild("head") and
  translateAndRotate, follows every animation.
  Disconfirming check: put the horn on a horse in a dev build, watch it through
  walk, gallop, rear, graze (head down) and the foal. If it lags, floats, or is
  wrong-scaled at any of the four, the parenting is wrong and nothing later stands.
  Also answers P2 (registration route) and the leg-anchor UNVERIFIED in section 2.
  Deliverable: a facts paragraph on model-parts.html's Verification tab, and a
  go / no-go on the registry shape. No common/ change, no golden moves.

Stage 1 - the registry and the layer.
  common/: the part-id constants class and PartAnchor, no rendering.
  neoforge/client: AttachedPartLayer (one class), a part registry mapping id ->
  {model layer, texture, anchor offset table}, and the extract step that fills a
  list of resolved parts on GeneticHorseRenderState. Still no gene - drive it from a
  debug command so the layer can be seen without breeding anything.
  Acceptance: three different parts on three different anchors, on an adult and a
  foal, in a running client, no z-fighting against coat, mane, braid or saddle.

Stage 2 - the first gene.
  A gene that requests a part through AttachedPartContribution, folded the same way
  CutieMarkGene folds its contributions. Which gene is the owner's call; the natural
  candidate is a unicorn-horn locus. This stage is where it becomes a GENE and
  therefore inherits the full making-a-gene checklist (wiki/making-a-gene.html
  #contracts): the gene page with its three tabs, the bakes in CLAUDE.md's
  regenerate table (bakeGeneBundle, bakeGeneIcons, bakeGeneWikiPages, census,
  timeline, pages.js spans), and the designer's WASM. It does NOT move either coat
  golden, because it paints no pixels - state that in the session notes so nobody
  hunts for a golden that should not move. UNVERIFIED that no golden reads a
  contribution list that a new record type would change; run
  *CoatBakeGoldenTest before assuming.

Stage 3 - the head-parented batch.
  Horns, spikes / dorsal spines, crystals, per the owner's priority list. Data and
  art only, if stages 0-2 held. "One render layer, many models" is the point.

Stage 4 - the breed hook.
  Let a breed file declare parts. This touches the breed format
  (wiki/breed-format.html), BreedSpecWriter, and the breed-file bakes, and the
  breed designer. It is a separate slice precisely because it drags in the whole
  breed bake table. Unblocks the reindeer breeds.

Stage 5 - the worn side.
  Point the shoe treatment's HorseShoeLayer at THIS layer rather than a bespoke one:
  HorseGear stacks resolve to the same resolved-part list (P1). Saddle pads follow.
  Done by editing the shoe treatment's step 4 to say "use AttachedPartLayer", and
  by deleting the bespoke-layer language from it.

Stage 6 - wings.
  The settled route from model-parts.html: a copy of the elytra mesh (left_wing and
  right_wing, 10x20x2 boxes, CubeDeformation(1.0F), texOffs(22,0), right mirrored,
  64x32 LayerDefinition), texture pointed at minecraft's own wings/elytra.png so
  any elytra retexture pack is the art. New here: a folded and a spread pose keyed
  off the isFallFlying shared flag GeneAbilityHandler already sets, which needs one
  boolean on the render state. Blocked on the two open questions (what drives them;
  one texture or many) - both are owner calls, not rendering ones.

Later (lower priority, not scoped): tails, hoof feathering, tusks, antlers.


6. Risks the plan already knows about
======================================
- Draw order and z-fighting. Saddle, armour and third-party tack render at the same
  time as a part. Decide once where AttachedPartLayer sits in the addLayer list and
  write the reason in the constructor comment the way the renderer already does for
  the emissive and braid layers. Parts on the head sit clear of the saddle; the
  risk is the mane, the bridle-side armour and the spines against a body-armour
  layer. Test with body armour ON at stage 1.
- Scale. Attached meshes must scale with the horse. The renderer already stretches
  gait to size (stretchGaitToSize) and the mod has a size gene, so a part parented to
  an animated ModelPart inherits scale IF the parent transform includes it - do not
  assume, check on a tiny and a huge horse at stage 0.
- The HD model's UV bake. Never add children to "body" or "head" inside
  createHdBodyMesh for a part. Separate LayerDefinition, separate layer, always.
- Third-party renderers. Other mods' horse renderers will not draw a part. That is
  acceptable and is a compatibility.html line, not a blocker; do not try to solve it.
- Cost per frame. A horse with no parts must cost one early return, the way BraidLayer
  does ("the state fields are zero and this returns on the first line"). Herds are
  common; measure a 20-horse pen at stage 1.
- Licence. Blockbench models the owner authors are the owner's; copied vanilla
  meshes (elytra) are vanilla geometry with the texture supplied at run time, which
  is the reason the wing route was chosen. Do not vendor third-party models.
- Never-baked, never-listed coats (hard rule 4) is unaffected: parts add variety,
  they do not add a pre-generated subset.


7. What must not regress
=========================
- The coat pipeline: no golden moves, no texture bake changes, HD UVs untouched.
- The existing layer stack: cutie mark, braid, emissive glow, armour, saddle all
  still draw, in the same order relative to each other.
- Rendering with the part absent: byte-for-byte the same frame as today.
- common/ imports nothing from Minecraft (rule 1) and adds no JDK API TeaVM lacks
  (rule 2); CommonPortabilityTest guards the second.
- Designer parity (rule 5) if any editor-visible field is added.


8. Verification the finished feature needs (goes on the Verification tab)
=========================================================================
In a running client, not a unit test:
1. Each part on adult and foal, standing, walking, galloping, rearing, grazing.
2. A part on a tiny and a huge horse; on a horse with a dyed-leather saddle and
   body armour worn; on a horse with a braid and a cutie mark.
3. Two players' horses side by side (client cache path, not the local one).
4. Save, reload, breed: the part follows the gene and skips a horse without it.
5. Invisible horse (potion): no part draws. The existing layers already return on
   isInvisible; a new one must.
6. A 20-horse herd: no frame-time cliff.
Unit tests (common/): the contribution folds, the anchor enum is total over the
part-id list, and every part id in the constants class has a registry row (a
test that fails when the two lists drift, the way check-recipes.mjs guards recipes -
this treatment's registry is a third list that will otherwise drift silently).

11. Feasibility: what needs Blockbench, what is code, what needs animation
===========================================================================
Requested: flesh out unicorn horns, antlers, crystals, hoof feathering, then the
harder set (wings, lion tails), and say how much is possible (a) with no Blockbench,
(b) with JUST code, (c) with NO new animation.

11.1 Three facts that decide most of it
----------------------------------------
F1. Everything on this renderer is already boxes. HdHorseModel is CubeListBuilder
    boxes with a PartPose (offset + rotation) and a texScale. A "mesh" here means a
    tree of rotated axis-aligned boxes. A horn, a spine, a crystal, an antler tine
    and a feather card are all expressible that way. Blockbench produces the same
    thing; it is an editor, not a capability. So "no Blockbench" costs blockiness,
    not possibility - and blocky is the game's own look.
F2. A part parented to an animated ModelPart inherits its animation for free. The
    HD model already animates head_parts (head bob, graze, rear), the four legs, the
    tail (child of body) and the ears (children of head). A part that is a child, or
    that calls translateAndRotate on that ModelPart the way CutieMarkLayer does for
    body, needs no animation of its own. "No new animation" is therefore the DEFAULT
    for anything rigidly fixed to a moving bone.
F3. Layers run after the model is posed, so a layer can READ the parent's already
    computed rotation (leg.xRot, tail.xRot, head_parts.xRot) and apply a formula:
    counter-swing, amplify, damp. That is code, not an animation asset, and it needs
    no state. (UNVERIFIED that the part rotations are still the posed values at
    RenderLayer.submit time in 26.1.2's submit-node model; stage 0 prints them.)

11.2 The colour problem, and the trick that removes the art
------------------------------------------------------------
Geometry is half of a part. The other half is what colour it is, and that is where
"no Blockbench, no texture painting" usually breaks. Three zero-art routes, in order
of how well they fit this mod:
 T1. SAMPLE THE HORSE'S OWN COAT TEXTURE. A part's UVs can point into a patch of the
     horse's generated coat - the mane patch, the tail patch, a leg patch, a hoof
     patch - so the part takes that colour from every gene automatically, for every
     coat, including glowing and dilute ones. HorseSkinGeometry already knows where
     each body region lives on the sheet (it is how genes paint by body-space). This
     is the natural answer for anything that should look like the horse's own hair
     (feathering, lion tuft, mane-coloured crest) and for hoof-coloured horn. It
     also honours the philosophy: the part is as unbounded as the coat.
     (UNVERIFIED which regions a hoof or a mane sample can reach without the coat
     genes writing a marking over them; check HorseSkinGeometry and a whitelisted
     "clean sample" patch.)
 T2. A FLAT MASK TEXTURE + A TINT, exactly BraidLayer's method: a shipped white mask
     and a per-horse int colour from the contribution record. One file covers every
     colour. Right for horn ivory/keratin, bone-white antlers, crystal colours.
 T3. A GENERATED TEXTURE: the mod already bakes textures in code (the coat pipeline,
     the noise classes). A small procedural sheet (feather barbs, a crystal facet
     gradient) can be baked once at load in common/ - game-free, deterministic, and
     WASM-runnable, so the wiki designer could show it later.
None of these need a painted texture from the owner. A hand-painted texture stays an
upgrade path, not a dependency.

11.3 Part by part
------------------
Legend: Blockbench = does the owner have to model it? Code = can it be done with
JUST code (no new asset)? Anim = new animation needed?

UNICORN HORN  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.  New animation: NONE.
  A tapered cone is a stack of 6-10 boxes, each smaller than the last, on the head
  bone (head_parts > head), tilted forward ~20-30 degrees. A SPIRAL is the same stack
  with a small yaw increment per box plus a thin ridge box on one corner of each
  section - reads as twisted at Minecraft resolution. Parameters that make it a
  gene's alleles instead of a model: length, base width, twist turns, tilt, tip
  sharpness, colour. Placement: forehead, between the ears (ears are at head-local
  y -13..-10, z 4..5; a horn anchors just forward of them - UNVERIFIED offset, tune in
  stage 0). Colour: T2 tint (ivory / gold / pearl), or T1 hoof sample for a "keratin"
  horn. Emissive is one flag (reuse EmissiveCoatLayer's approach) for a glowing horn.
  Verdict: fully doable now; the best stage 2 candidate.

ANTLERS  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.  New animation: NONE.
  Antlers are the best case for a GENERATOR, because a real antler is a recursive
  branch: a main beam with tines that fork. One function - beam(length, girth, angle),
  then N tines each a smaller beam at an angle from a point on the parent - expands
  seed + parameters into 10-40 boxes. Feed it the allele copy's epigenetic seed and
  every horse grows a different rack while staying a recognisable species: this is
  the "functionally infinite space" premise (hard rule 4) applied to geometry, and
  it is something a hand-modelled Blockbench antler cannot do at all. Sits on the head
  bone behind the ears; inherits head motion, so a graze or a head-toss carries them.
  Sexual dimorphism, seasonal shedding and antler growth stages are just conditions
  on the record, not animation. Colour: T2 tint (bone, velvet) or T1.
  Verdict: fully doable, and BETTER in code than in Blockbench. The generator would
  live in common/ as plain arithmetic emitting a box list (no Minecraft imports), so
  hard rule 1 holds and the wasm designer could preview it.

CRYSTALS (growths on the back, poll, shoulders)  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.
  New animation: NONE.
  A crystal is a hexagonal-ish prism: two or three long boxes at slightly different
  yaws, plus a shorter offset one - a cluster. A cluster generator scatters 3-7 of
  them over an anchor patch (SPINE_ROW / WITHERS / SHOULDER) with seeded lengths and
  lean. Needs the two properties the other parts do not: translucency and glow. The
  glow is the emissive pass that already exists for coats; translucency is a
  different render type (UNVERIFIED which translucent entity render types 26.1.2's
  submit API offers a layer). If translucency is not cheap, opaque emissive crystals
  are still a good look and the gene ships without it.
  Verdict: doable; the render type, not the geometry, is the only unknown.

HOOF FEATHERING (fetlock feathers)  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.  New animation: NONE.
  Feathering is long hair hanging from the fetlock of the draught breeds. Build it as
  a fringe of thin hanging boxes (or crossed flat cards) wrapped around the lower
  quarter of each leg, children of the four leg parts, so they swing with the leg
  for nothing. Because it is hair, sample T1 from the TAIL/MANE patch and it comes
  out the right colour on every horse, exactly as real feathering matches the mane.
  Optional polish with no animation asset: a counter-swing formula from F3
  (feather.xRot = -0.3 * leg.xRot) so it trails the hoof rather than swinging like a
  rigid sleeve. Length and density are the allele parameters; the breeds that want it
  (draught, per the page) get it from their breed file at stage 4.
  Verdict: doable; the two cost points are the number of boxes (4 legs x ~10 = 40
  extra cubes per horse - measure a herd) and the sampled patch, not the art.

HARDER SET

LION TAIL (thin tail with a tuft)  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES, with one caveat.
  New animation: NONE (rigid).
  The horse tail is a 3x14x4 box child of body with its own animation. Two moves:
  (1) make the horse tail thin - scale the tail part down on the model (the HD model
  is ours, so this is a setupAnim-level scale on the one part; UNVERIFIED that
  ModelPart carries xScale/yScale/zScale in 26.1.2, else hide it and draw a thin
  replacement child); (2) hang a tuft - a small cluster of boxes at the end, a child
  of "tail" so it follows the swish, coloured by T1 from the mane patch. The caveat:
  a truly whippy lion tail curves along its length. A single rigid box cannot. A
  2-3 segment chain, each segment a child of the last, CAN, and a chain needs a per-
  segment rotation rule. That rule can be a pure formula off the parent's rotation
  (each segment rotates a fixed fraction of the one above), which is F3 again - no
  animation asset, no per-entity state. Rigid straight tail + tuft is the stage-3
  version; the chain is an upgrade.
  Tufted tails (the page's "lion and tufted tails") are the rigid version.

WINGS  - Blockbench: NO.  Code: YES.  New animation: TWO STATIC POSES, no flapping.
  Already researched on model-parts.html: copy the elytra mesh (two 10x20x2 boxes,
  CubeDeformation 1.0, texOffs(22,0), 64x32), point the texture at vanilla's own
  wings/elytra.png, so any elytra retexture pack is the wing art. No modelling. The
  ONLY new thing is pose, and the honest ladder is:
   W1  Always folded against the flanks. Zero animation, zero state. A static pair of
       rotated boxes. Ships with the horn batch.
   W2  Folded on the ground, spread when airborne or gliding: a boolean from the
       render state (GeneAbilityHandler already sets shared flag 7, isFallFlying).
       Two constant poses; the switch is a snap. Still no animation.
   W3  The same two poses with a smoothed blend (a single float per horse on the
       render state, lerped in extract) so the wings open instead of popping. This
       is the first thing that is arguably "an animation", and it is one number.
   W4  Flap: wing.zRot = A * sin(ageInTicks * k) while airborne. Still a formula, no
       asset, but it needs a tuned amplitude and it is where wings stop looking like
       an elytra and start looking like wings, so it is polish, not v1.
  Cost of NOT doing W3/W4: wings that snap open. Cost of doing them: one float and one
  sine. The owner's stated "no new animations" constraint is satisfied through W2.
  Membrane or feathered pegasus wings (rather than elytra-shell wings) are the one
  visual in the whole page that genuinely wants hand modelling - or a generator of
  primary feathers (a fan of flat cards, seeded lengths), which is the antler
  technique again and gets 80% of the way without Blockbench. Decide when W1-W2 have
  been seen; do not pre-decide.

11.5 Free things vanilla already gives us (no modelling at all)
----------------------------------------------------------------
- The elytra mesh and texture for wings (model-parts.html).
- The horse's own ear box as a template for any small pointed head part.
- CutieMarkLayer's proof that a vanilla ITEM model can be submitted through
  ItemStackRenderState at an anchor. So a fallback for a quick prototype is to draw a
  vanilla item or block model as the part: an amethyst cluster for crystals, a
  pointed dripstone for a horn, an end rod for a spike. No mesh at all. Limits: the
  colour is the item's, tinting is awkward, and it is a flat-shaded block look. Good
  for the stage 0 spike, wrong for the finished feature. (UNVERIFIED for block models
  in 26.1.2's submit API; items are proven by CutieMarkLayer.)

11.6 Variety without a bake per horse
--------------------------------------
Because LayerDefinitions are normally registered once, "every horse has a different
antler" needs care:
 - Finite baked set + per-horse transform: bake ~8-16 seeded variants at registration
   and pick by seed; vary the rest with scale, tilt and tint per horse. Cheap, safe.
 - Or run-time bake and cache by seed bucket, IF stage 0 proves LayerDefinition can be
   baked outside the registration event.
 - Or build the part as many small children whose VISIBILITY the record toggles (tine
   count) - no bake at all.
Recommendation: visibility + transform first; it is the same trick the mod already
uses for size (a genetic scale on the state) and needs nothing UNVERIFIED.

11.7 What this changes in the slice plan (section 5)
-----------------------------------------------------
- Stage 0 also prints the posed leg/tail/head rotations at submit time (F3) and tries
  one code-built horn AND one vanilla-item stand-in, to compare.
- Stage 2's first gene is best the unicorn horn (simplest generator, no new render
  type, no new animation).
- Stage 3 becomes: horn, spines, antlers (adds the branch generator), crystals (adds
  emissive + the translucency question), in that order.
- A hoof-feathering stage joins stage 4 next to the breed hook, because the breeds
  that want it are the ones that need a breed-declared part, and it is the first part
  that adds four anchors at once and the first T1 (coat-sampled) colour.
- Wings gain the W1-W4 ladder; only W1-W2 are in scope until seen.
- New standing rule for this page: no part ships needing an animation asset. A part
  that cannot be expressed as a rigid child, a static pose pair, or a formula off the
  parent's rotation is a different plan and goes to its own Roadmap entry.
