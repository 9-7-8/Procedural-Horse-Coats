Magical gene / codominant / colours the back sail, paints nothing

Back sail colour

What colour a horse’s back sail is: its membrane and its spines. The same colours and rules as horn colour (white, the rainbow, pink, black, grey or chaos), on a locus of its own. Two different copies split the sail rather than fading it. On a horse without a sail it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

The colours, and two-tone sails

Exactly the horn colour table. Two copies of one colour give a sail all that colour, its membrane a see-through version of its spines. Two different ones make the membrane one colour and the spines the other. The membrane takes whichever comes earlier in the list, so Red/Blu is a red membrane on blue spines, either way round. Every copy carries a little shade of its own, so no two red sails are quite the same red.

Bare bone

Two Bon copies are the sail’s bone version: bare ivory rays with no membrane at all, the way a skeleton’s wing would be its bones alone. No wild horse carries Bon. The skeleton horses do, so a skeleton’s sail is a row of bare bones. One Bon copy is only a colour, and keeps its membrane.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all. It is only the colour of the sail.

One short subclass, and a split instead of a fade

A key, a name, a priority and the words its outcomes are written in. Everything else is AbstractPartColourGene’s. Its Words carry a split sentence, because the shared two-tone wording (“its tip the other”) is wrong for a part whose two colours are two regions rather than two ends. A two-tone sail costs nothing extra: the sail is already drawn in two passes, the membrane blended and the spines solid, and each pass takes one of the two colours (translucent regions). The bone version is boneOnly(pair), read by GrownParts.of, which sets the sail’s opacity to 0.

Invented

A sail is not real on a horse, so neither is its colour. It is a separate locus from every other part’s because the owner chose one colour gene per part. A shared one would force every part on a horse to match.

What needs verifying here

No coloured sail has been checked in a running game: the membrane-and-spines split is unit-tested, not watched.

The membrane in one colour, the spines in the other

Check a Red/Blu sail on a dark horse and on a grey one. The membrane should read red with the coat showing through it, and the spines solid blue. A very faint membrane on a dark coat may read as no colour at all; whether that wants a floor on the membrane’s brightness is a call for after it has been seen.
