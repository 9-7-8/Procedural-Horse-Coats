Dorsal spines

One copy and the horse grows a row of spines along the top of its back, from the withers to the croup. Whether they are all one height, fall away toward the tail or alternate tall and short, and how tall, thick, sharp and many they are, is written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, dorsal spine colour. The first of the body parts, and the first grown part that is not on the head.

Crossing two of them

One copy, either sex

Dominant: Dsp/n and Dsp/Dsp both grow the row, so a horse with spines shows them and a horse without carries none. A spined horse bred to a plain one gives spined foals about half the time. It is not sex-limited. Foals grow none; the row comes in when the horse grows up, and stays.

Under a saddle

The row runs through where a saddle and a rider sit. A bare horse nobody is riding shows all of it. Put a saddle on, or get on, and the spines under the saddle are hidden; the ones at the withers and over the croup stay. A horse steered bare at the top bond has no saddle to draw, but its rider still hides the middle of the row.

Finding them

Rare in the wild: about three horses in a thousand have a row. Every one of them shows it, so there are no hidden carriers to find. The skeleton horses can grow a row of bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts.

What varies

  • Form: uniform (every spine one height), graduated (tallest at the withers, under half that at the croup) or alternating (tall and short in turn).
  • Height: four classes, low to towering. Low to tall in the wild, and a line bred for height reaches towering and stops.
  • Count: how many spines there are, from the withers back. From about half a row to a full one in the wild; a line bred down can wear a short crest at the withers.
  • Taper: blunt pegs to sharp needles.
  • Girth: thicker or thinner.

Everything stacks: a horse can wear spines and every head part at once.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no attack, no hitbox. It is a look.

Slice A of the body parts

The first of the four body parts (model parts), built to settle three things the sail, plates and crystals reuse: the first anchor off the head, the saddle zone and its gate, and a row whose elements scale on their own. Like the dragon horns, it adds no layer, render-state field or config key. It is one kind (SPINES, centred, so nothing to mirror), DorsalSpineGenerator and SpineSize, and one line in GrownParts.of dressed by its colour locus. The granting locus is in GrownParts.shapes’s every-part baseline.

The epigenome, by cost

  • length: the class is the mesh’s size bucket (four; a taller spine is more boxes), the remainder a stretch.
  • form and taper: baked, DorsalSpineGenerator.style(form, taper), three forms by three taper buckets.
  • count: shown out of the most baked, front to back, the antler’s tine rule: the mesh always holds MAX_SPINES, and a fraction grows the next one in.
  • girth: a draw-time scale across each spine.

length and girth scale each spine about its own root, not the row on the pose stack. Across a row, a whole-part scale would also stretch the row lengthwise past the croup. See model parts.

The spine anchor and the saddle zone

Body-local, on the adult: (0, -7.6, -11). That is the withers, where the neck’s back edge and the mane meet the top of the body box, and the row runs fifteen units back from there. The saddle covers body z -9.5..0.5, read from vanilla’s EquineSaddleModel. A spine rooted within a unit of it is hidden while a saddle is drawn or the horse is ridden, which leaves about one spine at the withers and two over the croup. How the zone works: model parts.

Invented

No horse has dorsal spines. The look borrows from animals that do have them: the bony neural spines of a Dimetrodon or a Spinosaurus, and the keratin crest of an iguana. This is fantasy and makes no claim about any real gene. Dominant on purpose (owner, 2026-10-02): the spines are the simple body part, one you can see on any horse that carries it, unlike the rare recessive sail planned beside it.

What needs verifying here

No dorsal spine row has been seen in a running game: where the spine anchor puts the row, the saddle zone, and each spine scaling on its own are all read from the vanilla boxes and unchecked.

Where the row sits on the back

The spine anchor was placed from the vanilla body, neck and mane boxes, not by eye. Spawn a Dsp/n adult and look from the side: the first spine should stand just behind the mane at the withers, and the last a little short of where the tail leaves the croup. The roots should sit on the back with no gap and not sink into it. Check every form at low and towering height while the horse walks, grazes (the neck swings down past the first spine) and rears (the body bone tilts, and the row should tilt with it).

The saddle zone

On the same horse, check four states: bare (the whole row), saddled (the middle hidden, about one spine left at the withers and two over the croup, none poking through the saddle), ridden with a saddle, and ridden bare at the top bond (phantom saddle; the middle hidden though no saddle is drawn). Then two spined horses side by side, one saddled and one not, in the same frame. Each should keep its own row, which is the deferred-submit check.

Each spine scales on its own

Two horses with the same form and size class, one at the lowest girth and one at the highest, should have rows the same length with thicker spines on the second. If the thick row is also longer, the per-element scale is not being applied. Then a tiny and a huge horse: the row should scale with the body, because the size gene works through the pose stack. That claim comes from reading the code.

Under horse armour

Body armour is vanilla’s whole-horse bake, drawn after the parts. Look at what a tall row does under each armour: it is expected to poke through. Decide with the owner what an armoured spined horse should look like once it has been seen.
