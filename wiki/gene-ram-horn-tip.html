Magical gene / recessive / colours the horn tips, paints nothing

Ram horn tip

Two copies and a horse’s ram horns keep their own shade at the base and fade into a colour of their own toward the points — any colour, inherited with the allele. Hoofprints from the molten hooves MltH allele come out in the same colour. On a polled horse it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

Finding one

Recessive: one copy shows nothing, and in the wild a horse has two copies or none — one in twenty has two, and almost none of those has horns. The Mirrorgazer carries it on every horse, over black horns.

Matching hoofprints

A horse with two copies of molten hooves’ MltH leaves glowing prints in its horn-tip colour — or, without coloured tips, in its horns’ own shade — so its prints always match the ends of its horns.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill.

How it reaches the horns, and the prints

GrownParts.of dresses each ram horn with base = the horn’s shade and tip = tipColourOf, so it becomes a two-tone part, drawn a box at a time along each branch (see ram horns). The prints ask printColourOf(genotype, epigenome) — this gene’s colour if tipped, the horn shade if not — reaching the whole horse’s epigenome through GeneEpigenetics.epigenome(), as horn dust does for the unicorn horn. One answer, asked twice, so the two can never disagree.

There is no real locus here

A magical gene with no counterpart. Its own locus, rather than a number on the horn allele, so that it can be bred in or out on its own — the antler modifiers’ reasoning.

What needs verifying here

A tipped ram horn and its matching hoofprints have not been seen side by side in game.

Black to colour, and prints to match

A Mirrorgazer: the horns should be black for their first quarter, fade, and end in the tip colour, on every horn of a four-horned one; its hoofprints should be that colour. Several in one frame should each keep their own colour.
