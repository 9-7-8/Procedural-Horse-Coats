Magical gene / no coat effect

Magic pull

How much the horse can shift. The fifth magical body-stat locus, beside size, speed, health and jump — and the one whose number is a score out of ten rather than a multiplier. A horse carrying nothing here pulls at 5; a Shire pulls at 10 and a Falabella at 1. Both copies count and they add, one allele positive and one negative.

What reads it

Three things, all added 2026-09-18. Carts — the score sets a ceiling on how fast this horse can drag a given load, and the horse’s own speed decides how close to that ceiling it gets (carts §2). A second rider — a score of 6 or better and the horse carries two. Machinery — the score and speed blend into the horsegenetics:draft_power attribute that animal-labour mods can read (compatibility).

The conversion is common/cart/CartDraft and lives there rather than here on purpose: what a score is worth in cart loads is a decision for whatever hitches the horse to something, not a fact about the horse’s genetics.

Crossing two of them

The scale

Pull is written and read on the same 1–10 scale a breed sheet scores its stats with, and nothing converts it in between. Five is an ordinary horse. The draught breeds are the top of it — Shire, Percheron, Belgian Draft, Clydesdale and Suffolk Punch all score 10 — and the miniatures the bottom, with the Falabella and the American Miniature at 1.

Why it is not an AbstractMagicStatGene

It is that class’s shape — a codominant locus, one allele up, one down, a per-copy number written on the copy and inherited with it, both copies added — and it differs in the two ways that matter.

The sum is added, not multiplied. Speed, health, jump and scale multiply because they have to scale whatever the natural loci settled on, so a magically fast pony stays slower than a magically fast racehorse. Pull has no natural loci to compose with — this gene is the only thing that moves it — so multiplying would buy nothing and cost the one property that makes the number worth having, which is that a copy worth +2.5 is worth two and a half points on every horse that inherits it. There is deliberately no multiplyPullUnclamped.

There is no vampiric allele. The other three carry one because the Dhampir’s strength had to travel with the horse. Pull instead gives the Dhampir a per-strain score on its breed sheet — see below.

common/genetics/genes/MagicPullGene.java
@Override
public void contribute(AllelePair pair, Genotype genotype, GeneEpigenetics epigenetics,
                       TraitBuilder out) {
    double sum = signedDelta(pair.first(),  epigenetics.copy(0))
               + signedDelta(pair.second(), epigenetics.copy(1));
    if (sum != 0.0) {
        out.addPull(sum);
    }
}

The contribution is signed — Strong positive, Weak negative — so the whole combination table falls out of one line and Strong/Weak cancels because a positive and a negative are added, not because anything checks for it. Like every codominant locus it asks its GeneEpigenetics for copy(0) and copy(1) rather than for the expressed copy, which would count one allele twice and the other not at all.

A breed’s score reaches its founders

"pull": 10 in a breed file’s stats block is the number its horses resolve to. It is the one scored axis with no curve in between: BreedStatCurve.pullBand is the score padded out, where the three additive axes run their score through a multiplier ceiling first. BreedFounder then makes the founder homozygous for the pushing allele and splits the distance from 5.0 across its two copies, unevenly, exactly as it does for the other four.

A breed scored within 0.4 of the baseline carries no pull alleles at all rather than being forced homozygous for an allele worth nothing — the same near-baseline rule the other axes follow. So a breed written "pull": 5 and a breed that omits pull entirely produce the same horse; the difference is only whether the sheet says the 5 was a decision.

The Dhampir, and why strains can score stats

A white Dhampir pulls at 9 and a seal brown at 6. Copy count cannot say that. The other three stat loci give the Dhampir its strength through the Vmp allele, where one copy is worth half of two — but 9 is four times as far above the baseline as 6 is, and two copies of anything are only ever worth twice one.

So a Strain carries its own optional stats block, laid over the breed’s axis by axis (Breed.StatScores.overlaidWith), and the founder roll picks the strain before it reads any band. The Dhampir is the only breed that uses it today; any strain may.

Where the numbers come from

A copy is worth a bounded normal draw of mean 0.5 points, σ 0.35, floored at 0.05 so a Strong copy can never come out subtracting. That is the three additive stats’ “a tenth either way” (mean 0.10 against a baseline of 1.0) translated into score points against a baseline of 5.

DrawOne copyTwo copies
−6σ (the floor)5.055.1
mean5.56.0

The design range therefore tops out just past a score of ten at two maximal copies — which is exactly where the breed sheets put a Shire. The two agreeing is the calibration, not a coincidence: it means a breed’s stamped founders sit inside what the gene can produce on its own rather than out on a tail it could never reach. Drift is clamped eight design-spans wide rather than to the design range, so a line bred for pull can leave 10 behind — there is no ceiling on Traits.pull(), only the 0.5 floor.

Deliberately pull only

It moves no other number. A strong puller is not thereby fast, healthy, big or springy — the five axes are bred independently, which is the whole reason there are five loci rather than one.

What needs verifying here

Carts finally read pulling ability, which makes the parked questions askable: whether a 10 and a 1 feel like different animals, whether the step from 4 to 6 earns its second-rider threshold, and whether an uncapped Traits.pull() survives a bred line climbing past 10.

Pulling ability, now that something reads it (2026-09-18, NOT played)

This section used to say there was nothing to look at. As of the cart port there is: magic pull now sets how fast a horse drags a load, whether it carries a second rider, and its draft_power work rate. The questions that were parked here are now askable.

  • Does a 10 feel like a draught horse and a 1 like a miniature? The scale’s shape was never testable before. Hitch both to a wagon and see whether the difference reads as a different animal or as a slightly different number.
  • Is the gap between 4 and 6 worth breeding for? 6 is also the second-rider threshold, so that one step should feel like a real gain. If it does not, the threshold and the curve disagree.
  • Does a bred line running past 10 break anything? Traits.pull() has a floor and no ceiling. CartDraft handles arbitrary values by construction — the test checks up to a million — but nothing has watched a real line climb.
  • Pull on the info screen. It now sits beside Jump with a word for it (feeble … a true draft horse), and a Draught block below. Read it on a horse and check the word matches the feeling.

A debug-yard pen is still not possible, for the original reason: the stat pens read a named attribute’s range across a pen, and pull is not an attribute. draft_power is, though, and a pen on that would answer the shape question indirectly — worth considering if the above proves hard to eyeball.
