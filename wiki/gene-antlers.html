Magical gene / dominant, sex-limited allele / grows geometry, paints nothing

Antlers

One copy and the horse grows a rack of antlers from the top of its skull — on a mare or a stallion with Ant, on a stallion only with Antm. No two racks are alike: how big, how many tines, how far they reach, how thick and what shade of bone are all written on the allele and inherited with it. The second gene in the mod, after the unicorn horn, that grows a piece of the horse rather than colouring it.

Crossing two of them

Two ways to have antlers

  • Ant — antlers on every horse that carries it, mare or stallion, the way a reindeer has them.
  • Antm — antlers on a stallion only, the way a red deer has them. A mare with it shows nothing at all and passes it on. The genotype alone will not tell you: a mare's Antm/n reads exactly like a stallion's. Breed her to a stallion and look at the colts.

Both are dominant, so one copy is enough, and Ant wins over Antm. Foals of either sex grow no antlers; the rack comes in when the horse grows up, and stays — antlers here are permanent, never cast.

Finding one

Rare: about four wild horses in a thousand carry an antler allele, and the stallion-only one is the commoner. So most antlered horses you find in the wild are stallions — and somewhere out there are mares carrying Antm that look like nothing at all. The Rimehart is the one breed that is antlered on every horse, mares included.

Two racks are not alike

Every one of these is on the antler allele, and a foal that inherits the allele inherits the rack:

  • Size — five classes, yearling, small, standard, large and massive. Wild horses are small to standard; breeding for size climbs to massive and stops there. A bigger rack is more beam and more tines, not a small one stretched.
  • Tines — how many points. One to six in the wild; a bred line can reach eight on a massive rack. It grows continuously: a line bred toward more tines grows the next one in gradually rather than snapping it on.
  • The rack itself — where the tines sit and how they turn. It almost never changes between parent and foal.
  • Reach and girth — a little longer or shorter, a little thicker or thinner, on top of the size.
  • Shade — from pale bone to dark umber.
  • Asymmetry — none on any wild horse. Every wild rack is the same on both sides. Only a breed can make a rack lopsided — the Rimehart does — and once a line has it, the foals carry it on.

Five genes make a rack

This gene decides whether, and how big. Four more finish the rack, and each does nothing to a horse without antlers:

  • Antler form — how it grows: forked, brow-tined, palmate, crowned or a plain spike.
  • Antler glow — the points glow in the dark.
  • Crystal antlers — see-through, in a gem colour.
  • Blooming antlers — leaves, moss or blossom on the tines.

A horse can have a unicorn horn and antlers at once.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing at all, for good or ill. Antlers move no stat, grant no attack, change no hitbox, and do not stop the horse going through a doorway — however big the rack looks. It is worth exactly what you think it looks worth.

If it costs you frames

A rack is a few dozen small boxes, more than a horn. The same three client settings the horn uses cover antlers: parts.enabled draws grown parts at all, parts.glow the extra glowing pass, and parts.detailDistance how close a horse must be for its parts to be drawn. Turning them off hides the antlers on your screen only; the horse still has them, breeds them and shows them to every other player.

The sex gate is not in the expression

expressionOf never reads the sex locus: a gene's expression reading another locus is a cross-locus read the coat pipeline is built to avoid. The gate is in AntlersGene.grows(genotype), which racksFor — and so GrownParts.of — asks. That is safe for a part for the reason it is safe for the cutie mark's emblem: the part is drawn after the coat, nothing reads it back, and it is not in the coat texture's key. The cost is the one the gameplay tab states, and the expression's own text says: the genotype does not show the rule.

How asymmetry is "breed-pinned only" with no special case

The treatment left it open whether the epigenome could declare a value "fixed at zero, breed-pin only". It can, with nothing new: EpiValue.uniform(asymmetry, 0, 0).clampedTo(0, 1). A zero-wide design range means EpiRoll rolls exactly 0 for every founder and EpiDrift's step, a fraction of the design span, is exactly 0 — so drift never moves it. A breed band is stamped through the hard clamp (BreedFounder.stampBands), which is 0 to 1, so a breed can still write any value. AntlersGeneTest holds both halves. The one tool that divided by the design span — the breed designer's slider — now runs such a value's slider over the clamp instead.

How a rack reaches the screen

GrownParts.of — the one door the client uses — asks racksFor(genotype, epigenome, form) with the habit antler form picked, gets two AttachedParts, right then left, and dresses each with the three modifier loci. Everything else is the horn's machinery, unchanged in shape:

  • One mesh per side. Two PartKinds, ANTLER_RIGHT and ANTLER_LEFT, rooted at the CROWN_RIGHT / CROWN_LEFT anchors just inside vanilla's ears. A mirrored copy by negative scale would turn boxes inside out under a culled render type, and a symmetric rack is just the same variant asked of both kinds.
  • Baked at the maximum, shown by count. AntlerGenerator builds every tine the size class can hold, numbered base to tip in PartNode.group. PartModel.setupAnim hides the groups past shown and scales the fractional one, per submit — the deferred-submit rule the horn's two-tone slices already obey. ModelPart.xScale/yScale/zScale exist in 26.1.2 (read in the sources), which the treatment had left unverified.
  • A region mask per pass. The solid bone, a crystal antler's see-through shafts, the bloom clumps and the glowing points are each one submit of the same mesh drawing only its own PartSheet regions (BONE, BONE_TIP, BLOOM).
  • No foal. PartKind.showsOnFoal() is false for both antler kinds, and the layer skips them on a baby before anchoring.

What the owner decided before it was built

Put to the owner on the intake treatment and answered there, 2026-09-30; built as decided.

  • D1 — both alleles. Ant on both sexes and Antm on stallions only.
  • D2 — procedural. Racks come from AntlerGenerator, not hand-modelled meshes.
  • D3 — symmetric by default. Asymmetry is an epigenetic number that founders roll as zero and drift never moves; only a breed can set it. Antler form is a plain dominance series, never codominant, and there are no per-parent-copy sides.
  • D4 — permanent. No casting, no velvet, no year clock, no antler-cycle locus. D5, a shed-antler item, is moot under it.
  • D6 — crystal is a modifier. Crystal antlers change a rack; crystal growths on the body stay a separate part.
  • D7 — rare and hand-seeded, like flying: the founder pairs are written out by hand. This is a Java gene, so the tools/founders.py skip list the treatment mentioned — for gene files — does not apply.
  • D8 — invented names; the science tab says the biology is analogous, not measured.
  • D9 — size is one number on the allele, breedable and bandable, not a separate size locus.
  • The three cosmetic modifiers kept: glow, crystal and bloom. A charge attack is out of scope; nothing here assumes one.

What it costs, and the cache bound

An antler is at most AntlerGenerator.MAX_NODES boxes, asserted over every shape. Five habits × sixteen racks × five sizes × two sides is the whole set of antler meshes the client can ever bake, lazily, and PartGeneratorTest now bounds the cache by boxes over PartGenerators.allShapes() rather than by mesh count, since the antlers took that count past the horn-era limit of 256. A pen of antlered horses has not been measured.

Analogous, not measured

There is no antler locus in horses, and these are invented names for invented genes (decision D8). The design borrows from deer, and says so rather than claiming a mechanism. Real antlers are bone, grown from pedicles on the skull and cast and regrown every year under the control of testosterone — which is why in almost every deer only the males carry them. Reindeer and caribou are the exception, with antlers on both sexes. Ant and Antm are that difference as two alleles: the reindeer arrangement and the red-deer one.

The mod does not cast antlers (decision D4): a rack here is permanent, with no velvet and no year clock. And it does not borrow a real horn gene's name. The sheep horn locus, RXFP2, is the famous horned-versus-polled gene in sheep, but whether anything like it governs antlers in deer is not established, so the page makes no such claim.

Why dominant, when the horn is recessive

A unicorn is meant to be a find, and recessive makes it one. Antlers are meant to be a line: dominant means one antlered parent is enough, so a breeder who has one can keep it. The sex-limited allele puts the find back in — a mare carrying Antm looks like nothing, and is the horse that makes antlered colts.

Why symmetric by default

Real racks are rarely perfectly symmetric, and reindeer are often strikingly lopsided. But a lopsided rack on every wild horse reads as a rendering bug before it reads as biology. So the owner's call (D3) is that wild racks mirror exactly, and lopsidedness is a thing a breed brings — the Rimehart is that breed.

What needs verifying here

No antler has been seen in a running game: placement, the sex and foal gates, tines by count, size extremes, asymmetry and cost are all unchecked.

Where a rack sits, through every animation

The CROWN_RIGHT/CROWN_LEFT anchors are worked out from the model's boxes (just inside the ears, half a unit into the skull), not tuned on a live horse. Look at an antlered adult standing, walking, galloping, rearing and grazing: both antlers should stay rooted to the skull and lean out and back. Then a horse in a headstall or mask — the Head gear zone — and an invisible horse, which should show nothing.

The sex gate and the foal gate

An Antm/n mare should show no antlers and an Antm/n stallion should; an Ant/n mare should. A foal of any genotype should show none and grow its rack when it grows up. The spawn egg's preview should obey the same gates.

Tines by count, two horses in one frame

Two antlered horses with different tines values on screen together should each keep their own count — the test of the per-submit setupAnim claim, which is read from the sources and has not been watched. A line whose tines drifts across a whole number should grow the next tine in, not snap it on.

Size extremes, and the ceiling

All five size classes on a tiny and a huge horse; whether the rack scales with the horse's genetic size. The massive rack against a low ceiling, a doorway and the rider's view — the class lengths in AntlerSize are a first placement and may need shortening.

Sixteen racks reading as sixteen

Whether the sixteen racks of each habit look like different racks on a live horse. If they do not, AntlerGenerator.VARIANTS is the one number to raise. This is the one place the render cache and the no-pre-baked-subsets rule touch: the seed is a long on the genome, and the cache maps it to this many shapes.

A breed-pinned lopsided rack

A Rimehart should have two different antlers, the left one smaller and with fewer tines. Save, reload and breed it to an unrelated antlered horse: the foal that inherits the Rimehart's antler allele should be lopsided too.

A pen of antlered horses

Twenty antlered horses in one pen against the same pen without parts — look for a cliff, not a percentage. Racks are bigger than horns.

Natural or magical

The treatment proposed natural for this gene and antler form; they ship as magical, in the grown-parts family, because a natural gene that paints nothing files under the disorders and because that family was made for antlers. The owner’s call; moving them is two lines in GeneFamily and two isNatural() overrides, plus a science tab each (they already have one).
