Magical gene / no coat effect

Passification

The way in to a horse that would otherwise kill you. It adds no aggression of its own — it says what you can offer the animal to make it stop, and while the bargain holds it will not target you for any reason.

Built, and not yet seen in game

New on 2026-09-17, and nothing here has been watched happening to a horse. The feeding interaction and the veto were written against the 26.1.2 sources and are flagged unverified in their own comments. What to look at is on the Verification tab.

What it is for

Aggression can produce a wild horse that attacks players on sight, and a horse like that is not a difficult horse, it is an enemy: you cannot get near it, so you cannot feed it, so you cannot tame it. Wherever that allele lands it is a dead end. This locus is the answer to it.

It is the answer to the opposite temperament too. Skittishness produces a horse that runs from every player it sees, and that is the same dead end reached from the other side — you cannot get near it, so nothing that needs you near it can happen. Since 2026-09-25 the veto covers fleeing as well as fighting, so one offering answers both: the horse stops treating you as a threat, whether the threat was one it meant to fight or one it meant to run from. It still adds no aggression and no fear of its own, and it never changes a horse's temperament toward anybody else.

The two foal-only alleles are the cruel ones: miss the window while the animal is small and the adult is beyond reach for good.

One copy is enough, and two different ones stack

Unlike almost everything else in the behaviour family this is not a recessive. A single copy expresses, and a horse carrying two different passification alleles offers both routes independently — feed it either thing, either way. A mixed horse is therefore worth more than either of its parents, which is the point: there should be many ways to passify a horse, not a precedence ladder that wastes half of them.

What it wants is written on the horse

Which item, and how many of it — and for the temporary routes, how long the calm lasts and how long before you can buy it again — are all epigenetic. They are rolled once for a wild horse, written on the allele copy, inherited with it, and drift slightly each generation. Two Prm/n horses are not the same horse: one may want four apples and the other a single golden carrot, and a line can be bred toward an offering you can actually afford.

What it does not do

Passification is about how the horse treats you — whether it comes for you and whether it runs from you — and nothing else. A horse with a damaging aura goes on damaging what stands near it; a player who walks into one has not been betrayed by this gene. Nor does it make the animal brave: a calmed horse still bolts from fire, from freezing, from a mob, and from any player who has not paid. It is per player: a horse you have calmed is still perfectly willing to kill your friend, or to flee from them.

Two of the fourteen offerings are not overworld food

The list a horse may ask from grew on 2026-09-25, when the Netherhorse and the Nightmare needed nether wart and cake. Adding to that list is the only safe way to change it — see the Coding tab — but it is not free, because a wild horse rolls its offering uniformly across the whole list. So roughly one passifiable wild horse in seven now asks for nether wart or cake rather than something you can grow, and a horse that wants nether wart is one you cannot buy off until you have been to the Nether. That is the owner's call and it stands; it is written here because nothing in the game says it.

The veto has to be asked at every chokepoint, not unpicked afterwards

“Will not select the player as a target for any reason” only means something if each reason asks. Passification.suppresses is consulted in five places: the temper verb's attacking mood, GeneAbilityHandler.flee (the same verb's fleeing mood), HorseAggroHandler.aggro (hit-it-and-its-herd), HerdGoals (a dam defending her foal) and GeneReactionHandler (guardian's retaliation). A gene added later that targets a player and does not ask here is a bug in that gene.

flee was the fifth, and it was missing for eight days after this box was written promising there were no more — the box was right about the principle and wrong about the count, which is the failure mode it exists to warn about. It went unnoticed because no breed pinned a skittish allele until the Nightmare did, so no horse had ever been both calmable and frightened at once.

A flee is a path already issued, so the veto alone does not stop one

The other four chokepoints guard a target, which is re-read every tick, so dropping it is enough. flee issues a moveTo FLEE_DISTANCE blocks away and only re-picks on the temper beat, so a horse calmed mid-flight would run the leg out and only then turn round. Passification.forget therefore calls GeneAbilityHandler.stopFleeingFrom, which drops the FLEEING entry only when it is keyed to that player — a horse running from two people is still running from the other one and re-picks it on the next beat. flee's own no-candidate path stops the navigation too, but likewise only when it was the thing that started it: stopping unconditionally every beat would cut the legs off whatever goal has the navigation instead, FoodTemptGoal walking a calmed horse back over most of all.

The item is an index, so the table may only grow at the end

The epigenome stores numbers, so “the item this horse wants” can only ever be a position in a list the gene owns (PassificationGene.OFFERINGS). Appending is the only safe edit. Re-ordering it silently changes what every existing horse in every save is asking for, and nothing will tell you.

Appending is safe, not free. The schema is EpiValue.category(ITEM, OFFERINGS.size()) and EpiRoll rolls rng.nextInt((int) v.max()), so the length of the list is the width of the wild roll: every entry added is an entry wild founders start asking for. The Netherhorse's own file claimed the opposite when it added two — that “every wild horse still only ever rolls the first twelve entries” — which was wrong, and is the reason a breed that only wants a band on itself still has to think about the whole population. PotionMilkGene has the same shape by a different route: it spreads a fixed WILD_CARRIER_PERCENT across however many brews exist, so appending dilutes every existing one.

Why it is not an effect verb

It would have been a verb with exactly one user, which is the same test lycanthropy and sun sensitivity failed. So the gene owns the genetics and the numbers and exposes one pure function, routesOf; the NeoForge module owns the feeding, the clock and the veto. It is also not a standing effect but a veto on somebody else's, which no verb in the set can express.

“Extremely high priority” is not the priority number

The locus is required to override every other aggression effect. A gene's priority() could not do that: for a gene that paints nothing the number is only a slot in the genotype code's layout. The override is enforced by where the check lives — at the moment a target is acquired, after whatever decided to acquire it. The number here is merely high enough that a reader sorting the registry finds it after the loci it talks over.

A bargain, not a switch

The interesting thing about this locus is that it makes a dangerous horse into a negotiation rather than a wall. The animal is not tamed, not broken, and not fixed; it has been paid, and for the temporary routes it will want paying again. That is a different relationship from the one every other horse in the mod offers, and it only exists because aggression is allowed to be genuinely unmanageable.

Why the calm is per player

A horse that one person could defuse for everybody would make an aggressive line a single afternoon's problem on any shared world. Keeping the bargain between the animal and the person who fed it means an aggressive bloodline stays dangerous to everyone who has not done the work — which is what makes breeding one a decision rather than a novelty.

What needs verifying here

A pacified horse must drop you as a target mid-charge and nobody else, and the veto has to hold at all five chokepoints — aggro, flee, herd alert, dam defence, guardian — with temporary routes expiring and re-buyable, foal-only ones refusing an adult, and nether wart and cake really reachable as offerings.

The veto now covers fleeing (2026-09-25, NOT played)

Written for the Nightmare, which is the first horse that has ever been both frightened of you and calmable. Nothing here has been watched happening.

  • A calmed player stops being a reason to run. Pass: a horse with a skittish allele that names players flees you; you feed it its offering; it stops fleeing you and does not pick you again. Fail: it keeps running, or it stops fleeing everything — a second, unpaid player must still set it off, and so must a mob.
  • The leg already issued is dropped, not walked out. This is the half that is easy to get wrong and easy to misread as working, because the horse does turn round eventually either way. Pass: pay it while it is running and it stops where it stands. Fail: it carries on to roughly twelve blocks away first. Watch the feet, not the eventual position.
  • Environmental panic is untouched. Pass: a fully calmed horse still runs from fire and from freezing. If it stands in a fire it is not this change working, it is this change having taken too much.
  • The tempt lure gets you within reach at all. A skittish horse flees at 16 blocks; FoodTemptGoal notices a passification offering at 20 and re-issues its path every tick against the flee's every twenty, so on paper the lure wins. Pass: holding cake, an untamed Nightmare closes on you instead of retreating. Fail: it oscillates, or drifts away slowly — either means the two paths are fighting and the arithmetic above is wrong somewhere.

Nether wart and cake joined the wild roll (2026-09-25, NOT played)

  • A wild horse can now ask for either. Not a defect — see the Gameplay tab — but unmeasured. Pass: across a decent sample of wild passifiable horses, nether wart and cake come up at about the same rate as apples, and feeding them works exactly like any other offering. Fail: an offering nothing can satisfy, or a horse that takes the item and never settles.
