Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing

Crystal growths

One copy and the horse grows clusters of crystals out of its back along the spine, from the withers to the croup: see-through shafts with solid points, fanning out from the midline. Where every crystal stands is the horse’s own and is inherited whole. How big they are, how many clusters there are and how far they fan are written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, crystal growth colour.

Crossing two of them

One copy, either sex

Dominant: Crg/n and Crg/Crg both grow crystals, so a crystal-grown horse bred to a plain one gives a crystal-grown foal at least half the time. It is not sex-limited. Foals grow none; the crystals come in when the horse grows up, and stay.

Where they grow

Up to six clusters in a row along the top of the back, from the withers to the croup, each of three to seven crystals: a tall one in the middle and shorter ones around it, leaning away from the spine. A saddle or a rider hides the clusters under the saddle and leaves the ones at the withers and the croup.

Finding one

Rare in the wild: about three horses in a thousand grow crystals, and because one copy is enough, every horse that carries the gene shows it. The skeleton horses can grow them too, as one of their horn-like parts. A crystal has no bone in it, so on a skeleton they are ordinary crystals in any colour.

What varies

  • Arrangement: where each crystal of each cluster stands, how many crystals a cluster has and how long each is against its cluster’s tallest. There are eight arrangements. A foal inherits its parent’s whole, and it only rarely changes in a line.
  • Size: three classes, small, large and great. Small to large in the wild; a line bred for size reaches great and stops.
  • Count: how many clusters show, from the withers back, two to six in the wild. A line can be bred down to a single cluster at the withers.
  • Spread: how far the outer crystals of a cluster lean away from the spine, from nearly upright to a wide fan.

Everything stacks: a horse can wear crystals, a sail, a row of dorsal spines, plates and every head part at once. They do not glow. Nothing makes them glow yet.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no ability, no hitbox. It is a look.

Slice D of the body parts

The last body part (model parts), and it needed no new render path. It reuses the spine row’s anchor, count and saddle zone, and the sail’s see-through regions. The client is unchanged: a new kind with translucentRegions() naming the new CRYSTAL sheet region is all the renderer needs. It is one kind, CrystalGenerator and CrystalSize, and one line in GrownParts.of dressed by its colour locus. The granting locus is in GrownParts.shapes’s every-part baseline.

The mesh: one cluster, one tree

Each cluster’s central crystal is the root, standing upright at the cluster’s place along the row. Every other crystal of the cluster hangs off that root at t = 0, set out to the side and along the row, and leaned away from the midline by rz (further out leans further). So a cluster is one group with one root. The count hides it and the fractional grow shrinks it as a unit, the per-element scale grows it about its own centre, and PartGeneratorTest’s one-tree-per-element rule holds unchanged. Because the root is upright, its children’s offsets are in the body’s own frame, and a crystal set to the side neither sinks nor floats.

A crystal is three boxes. The first is a shaft. The second is the same shaft rolled π/4 about its own axis, and the two crossed squares read as an octagonal prism. The third is a short, narrower cap at the top, rolled π/8. The shafts sample CRYSTAL, the cap BONE_TIP. A roll is only ever on a box that does not bend. The crystal antlers have no prism geometry of their own (they are the antler mesh drawn see-through), so the prism is new here, despite what the treatment assumed.

The two end clusters sit exactly at the withers and the croup, and the inner four are jittered along the row by their arrangement. So under a saddle the first and last always show and the middle ones hide; the fifth cluster can fall either side of the saddle’s back edge. BackCrystalsGeneTest holds the numbering, the prism shape, the fan and the saddle.

The epigenome, by cost

  • seed: one of CrystalGenerator.VARIANTS arrangements, the antler rack’s rule (EpiValue.seed, never nudged by drift). Each arrangement is one SeededRng walk with a fixed number of draws per cluster and per crystal, so the spread and the size class change only angles and lengths, never where anything stands.
  • spread: baked, CrystalGenerator.style(variant, spread), three buckets; it scales every outer crystal’s lean.
  • size: the class is the mesh’s size bucket (three; the crystals’ lengths and thicknesses), the remainder a uniform per-element grow, so stretch and girth are equal.
  • count: shown out of the most baked, the antler’s tine rule.

See-through shafts, solid points

The part’s opacity is AttachedPart.CRYSTAL_ALPHA, the crystal antler’s. It is not on the copy, because the treatment’s four numbers do not include it, and every number on the copy has to reach the part. The blended pass draws only the shafts in the base colour, and the solid pass draws the caps in the tip colour. No glow: glowRegions() answers SOLID as for every part nothing lights, which is where a glow gene would go.

Invented

No animal grows crystals. The look borrows from geodes and quartz druses, clusters of hexagonal points breaking out of rock. It is drawn as eight-sided prisms because two crossed boxes is the cheapest prism the renderer has. This is fantasy and makes no claim about any real gene. It is dominant (the treatment’s call), so like the dorsal spines and the plates it shows on every carrier, and the sail is the body part you have to breed for.

What needs verifying here

The crystals have never been seen in a game: whether they stand on the back without sinking or floating, read as see-through prisms with solid points, hide under a saddle, and look right at great size and in motion are all open.

On the back, standing still

A crystal-grown horse from the side, from above and from behind, at each spread. Every crystal’s base should sit in the top of the back with no gap, and none should sink below the coat or float above it. The outer crystals should lean away from the spine, more at a wide spread. Each crystal should read as an eight-sided prism with a solid point. Use a -PphotoShoot list as the plates were.

See-through, and two-tone

The shafts should be see-through in the base colour, with the coat visible through them, and the points solid in the tip colour (Red/Blu: red glass, blue points). Several crystals overlap in a cluster, so look for blended faces sorting wrongly: a far face drawn over a near one. That is the same open question as the sail’s (sail sorting).

Under a saddle and a rider

Saddle a horse with a full row of six clusters, then ride it. The middle clusters should vanish and the ones at the withers and the croup stay; no crystal should poke up through the saddle or the rider. Take the saddle off and the whole row comes back.

Great size, in motion, and two counts

A great-sized horse walking, grazing and rearing: the clusters should ride the back, and none should reach under a low ceiling’s collision. Two crystal horses side by side with different counts (one and six clusters) should each show their own; a horse wearing the other’s count is the deferred-submit trap.
