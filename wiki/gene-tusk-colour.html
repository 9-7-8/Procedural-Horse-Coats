Magical gene / codominant / colours the tusks, paints nothing

Tusk colour

What colour everything the tusks gene grows is: the narwhal horn, the boar tusks and the sabre fangs. The same colours and rules as horn colour (white, the rainbow, pink, black, grey or chaos), on a locus of its own. White is the wild type, and it reads as ivory. On a horse without tusks it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

The colours, and two-tone tusks

Exactly the horn colour table. Two copies of one colour make the whole tusk that colour. Two different ones make it two-tone from root to tip: the colour that comes earlier in the list at the root, fading to the other at the point, so Red/Blu is red at the face and blue at the tip, either way round. Every copy carries a little shade of its own.

One colour gene covers every form. A horse with fangs and a narwhal horn wears both in the same colour.

Bare bone

Two Bon copies are the bone version: a tusk is a tooth, so on a skeleton it is bare bone. No wild horse carries Bon; the skeleton horses do, so a skeleton that grows a narwhal horn, boar tusks or sabre fangs grows them in bone.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all. It is only the colour of the tusks.

One short subclass

A key, a name, a priority and the words its outcomes are written in; everything else is AbstractPartColourGene’s. A narwhal horn is the horn’s chain, and a boar tusk and a sabre fang are chains root to point too, so the shared root-to-tip wording describes all three and the Words carry no split. GrownParts.of puts one pair of tints on every part TusksGene.partsFor returns, which is what makes it one colour for every form.

Invented

Real tusks are ivory: dentine, cream to yellowish, darkening with age. White here stands for that. The rainbow, the chaos and the two-tone are fantasy. It is a separate locus from every other part’s because the owner chose one colour gene per part, and one locus for all the tusks gene’s forms because the treatment did.

What needs verifying here

The colours are unit-tested and were seen in still frames (2026-10-05); a chaos tusk, and a pale tusk under coloured light, are still to be seen.

What has been seen

Still frames (2026-10-05) showed an ivory horn, a Red/Blu horn red at the root and blue at the tip, a black horn beside a white unicorn horn (each its own colour), and a bone horn on a skeleton. Still to see: a Cha horn, which should wear a colour of its own, and the horn under coloured light, where a pale tusk could wash out.
