Natural gene / phase 1

Markings — the star and the sock

The ordinary white a horse can have without carrying any pattern gene at all: a star, a stripe, a snip, a coronet, a sock, a stocking. Most marked horses in life are marked by this and not by splash or sabino, and until now the mod had no way to say so.

Crossing two of them

More copies, more white

An additive dosage locus, the same shape as sooty and shade: two working alleles that stack, so what matters is how many copies and how strong, not which parent they came from.

combinationdosagewhat shows
m/m0clean — no white anywhere
M1/m1a star, or a coronet or two above the hooves
M1/M1, M2/m2a star and often a snip, socks on a leg or two
M2/M13a stripe or a narrow blaze, and socks
M2/M24a broad blaze and high stockings

Two horses of the same dosage still differ, because each carries its own stored offset and its own face and leg rolls. That is what stops a herd of M1/m horses all wearing the same star.

Not every leg is marked

Each leg rolls separately and has to clear a threshold before it whitens anything, which is why a three-socked horse with one clean leg is common and four identical socks are not. The rolls are stored, not rolled fresh, so a horse marks the same way every time it is drawn — and its foals inherit the tendency.

Pattern genes stack on top of this

Markings runs at priority 65, before every white pattern locus (tobiano is 72, and KIT, MITF, PAX3 and EDNRB run after it). So a sabino horse that also carries three markings copies is a horse with a blaze that its sabino then widens, rather than two genes fighting over the same face.

It consumes no randomness at paint time

Unlike sooty, which spends a nextFloat() on its expression roll, every choice this gene makes is already in the epigenome: the face seed and its star, stripe and snip rolls come from the shared WhitePattern.faceSchema(), and each leg has its own EpiValue.perLeg entry. The horse therefore marks identically every time it is drawn, and a foal inherits the tendency rather than re-rolling from scratch.

The face vocabulary is shared, deliberately

The blaze is drawn by WhitePattern.faceMarking — the same call EDNRB, KIT, MITF and PAX3 use. A markings blaze and a sabino blaze are therefore the same code with a different jag, and cannot drift apart. This locus passes a small jag, because ordinary markings have the clean edge of a splash rather than the torn, roaned margin of a sabino.

One locus is not what “polygenic” means

Real ordinary markings are many small-effect variants plus developmental noise. This is a single additive locus standing in for that family, in the same way sooty and shade stand in for a chromosome-22 signal whose causal mutation nobody has identified. What the locus buys over an allele-free per-horse roll is that a breeder can select for it: the dosage is inherited Mendelianly, shows in the genotype code, and prints on a paper.

Why this is its own thing and not splash

The single most useful finding here is a negative one. The study that identified SW1 deliberately assembled 112 horses with minimal white markings — the population you would expect a minimally-expressed splash allele to be hiding in — and found no splash allele at all. Ordinary stars and socks are simply not splash.

They are also not one gene. Marking extent behaves like a quantitative trait: continuously distributed, moderately heritable, and responsive to selection, which is the signature of many loci of small effect rather than one locus with big ones. Breeders have selected both for and against white for centuries and moved the population gradually in both directions, which is exactly what you cannot do with a single dominant.

On top of the genetics sits genuine developmental noise. Pigment cells migrate from the neural crest during gestation, and how far they get before the coat is fixed is not fully determined by the genome — which is why full siblings, and even clones, differ in their markings, and why a marking is usable as an individual identifier on a registration diagram at all.

The confusion this locus reproduces on purpose

At its top end this gene draws a broad blaze and high stockings, which is very hard to tell from a minimally expressed sabino or splash by eye. That ambiguity is not a shortcoming of the model — it is the thing the literature keeps saying out loud, and the reason testing exists. A horse with four white legs and a bald face may be a heavily marked ordinary horse or a minimally marked pattern carrier, and only its genotype settles it.

In the mod that distinction is real and checkable: the info panel and a research paper name the locus, so two horses that look alike can be told apart by what they will actually throw.

What needs verifying here

Ordinary markings get a locus at priority 65 — the highest-consequence visual change so far — so the rates come first: whether founders get any white at all, whether per-leg sock rolls vary, and whether a horse marks identically across a reload.

Ordinary markings get a locus (2026-09-17, NOT played)

Markings ships at priority 65, and in the same change SW1 falls from 55% of founders to 6%. This changes what a large fraction of horses look like, so it is the highest-consequence visual change in the project so far — but most of what can go wrong here is still invisible, so the looking comes last.

  • Markings reaches founders at all. Spawn a large batch of wild horses. Most should carry some white — a star, a coronet, a sock. A herd of entirely clean-faced horses means the locus is registered but its founder table is not reaching the draw.
  • Sock counts vary. Each leg rolls independently against a threshold, so three socks and one clean leg should be common. If every marked horse has exactly four matching socks, or exactly none, the per-leg threshold is broken and the leg rolls are not being read per leg.
  • The same horse marks identically every time. The gene consumes no nextFloat() — every decision is stored epigenetics. Note a marked horse, leave and rejoin the world, and confirm the markings are identical. A horse whose blaze moves between reloads means something is rolling at paint time.
  • Pattern genes stack rather than fight. Spawn markings=M2/M2 with kit=SB1/N: the sabino should widen an existing blaze, not overwrite or double-draw it. Same with mitf=SW1/N beside it. Both are in the coat golden now, so a mismatch here means the game and the golden disagree.
  • Foals inherit the tendency. Breed two heavily marked horses and confirm the foals trend marked, and that two clean m/m parents never throw a marked foal at all.
  • Lowest priority, purely visual: whether a broad blaze and high stockings actually read as a normal marked riding horse rather than as a pinto, and whether a markings blaze is distinguishable from a minimally-expressed sabino — which it deliberately is not, so the test is really “does it look plausible”, not “can I tell them apart”.
