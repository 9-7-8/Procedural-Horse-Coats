Gameplay / non-genetic

Rescuing braid

A one-use piece of mane or tail gear that turns a killing blow into a trip home instead of just a reprieve. The instant the horse would die the braid breaks, and the horse — with you on it, if you were riding — is standing in its own stall, bolting. It is the first thing in the game that goes in the mane or the tail.

What it is

Craft a braid from any wool, a horse hair and an ender pearl, work it into a horse’s mane or tail on the Gear tab, and forget about it. It does nothing at all until the day something is about to kill that horse, and then it spends itself: the blow is refused, the braid is gone, and the horse is at home.

It is the second thing in the mod that saves a horse without being used, and the two are deliberately different instruments. An emergency stasis chamber is yours — carried, covering every horse you own, and it answers by taking the animal out of the world into a bottle you then have to find and open. A braid is the horse’s — worn, covering only the animal wearing it, and it answers by putting it somewhere you will walk past tomorrow, still an animal, still saddled, standing where it lives.

Where it sends a horse

To that horse’s own stall. Failing that — no stall, or the stall has been built over, filled up or pulled down since — to your holding pen. Those are the same two destinations, picked in the same order, that the browser’s Send home button already uses, and it lands the horse by the same rules a ticket does: it will not put an animal down inside a wall, and it reaches across worlds.

A braid is not bound to anywhere. It works out where home is at the moment it breaks, which means one in a chest works for whichever horse you put it on, a braid can never be pointing at a stall you took down last week, and moving a horse to a new stall needs nothing done to the braid. The price is that the item cannot tell you in advance where it will send a horse — and that a horse with no stall and no holding pen is wearing one for nothing. That last case is not silent: you get one line saying so, and the braid is not spent. It is still in the hair the day you hang a sign up.

What actually happens, in order

Three separate things try to keep a horse alive now, and they run in a fixed order so they add up rather than fight:

  1. Last stand goes first. A horse’s free once-per-recovery save refuses the blow, leaves it on half a heart and gives it its few seconds of immunity, with the totem particles.
  2. Then the braid. It sees a horse standing on that sliver and takes it home — so the horse arrives inside the immunity window, which is the whole point of doing it in this order. A horse teleported out of a fight and then killed by the first thing it lands beside has been moved rather than saved.
  3. Then the emergency chamber, if you are carrying one, as the net behind both. It never sees a horse a braid has already sent home, so nothing is wasted by the ordering — the bottle you did not spend is still in your pocket for the next horse.

A braid still works when the last stand has nothing left to give — a horse that has not healed back up since the last time it was saved — which is the case it matters most in.

You come with it

If you are riding when the braid breaks, you arrive too. That is the one thing this feature must not get wrong: being separated from your horse by the item that was supposed to save you both is worse than the blow. Within one world you simply travel with it; across worlds the horse is re-created on the far side, so you are sent after it and put back on.

And it comes up bolting. A horse that has just been held on half a heart is well under the health it runs for its life at, and whatever hurt it counts as a fresh threat for a few more seconds, so the ordinary escape behaviour takes over the moment it lands — reins thrown, at a gallop, around its own stall. It settles as soon as the fright wears off.

Two slots, two lives

A horse has a mane slot and a tail slot, and a braid in each is two independent saves. The mane one goes first; the tail one is still there for the next time. That is the same answer the emergency chamber gives to wanting a second save — carry two — and it is what stops the second hair slot being a slot that exists and cannot be used for the thing it is for.

The colour

A braid is drawn as a plait worked into the mane or the tail, in whatever colour it has been dyed, and it is dyed exactly like leather armour: put it in a crafting grid with any dye, or several, and wash it out in a cauldron if you change your mind. An undyed one is the colour of horse hair.

The colour does nothing. It is the one place on a piece of tack this mod allows pure decoration, because the item’s job is already paid for — see utility, not decoration, and the same split the Tack Dyeing Bench draws between what a saddle does and what colour it is.

What it refuses

  • A foal wears nothing, braids included — every gear slot is closed until a horse grows up.
  • Only horses. A donkey or a mule gets the last stand like everything else, but stalls are a horse’s, so a braid has nowhere to send one.
  • A wild horse is nobody’s, so there is no holding pen to fall back to and no stall to look up.

Three subscribers on one event

RescuingBraidHandler is the third thing in the mod hanging off LivingDamageEvent.Pre to keep a horse alive, and the priorities are the composition rather than tidiness:

  • HorseLastStandHandler at NORMAL — refuses the blow, holds the horse at LastStand.healthLeft, draws the particles.
  • RescuingBraidHandler at LOW — sees a horse on that floor and moves it.
  • EmergencyStasisHandler at LOWEST — dropped from LOW to make room. Its own comment used to explain LOW as “after the last stand”; it now also means “after the braid”, and the reason is the same both times.

The braid goes before the chamber because it is the more specific instrument (owner’s call): worn on one horse on purpose, where a chamber covers every horse its carrier owns. Nothing is wasted by that ordering, which is what makes it safe — a horse the braid has moved is out of the fight and takes no further blows, so the chamber never gets asked and is never spent.

Why it tests a state, not a killing blow

This is the trap in the whole feature and it would have shipped green. By the time the braid is asked, the last stand above it has usually already set the damage to zero — so damage >= health, the obvious condition, answers no on precisely the horses a braid exists for. Every test passes, the item crafts, the layer draws, and the braid never fires.

So RescuingBraid.fires asks where the horse is: the blow is fatal, or the horse is already sitting on the last stand’s floor. That is the same conclusion StasisRescue reached from the other end of the same event, for the same reason, and RescuingBraidTest pins both halves — including one assertion that is really about two features at once: a horse on that floor must still be below Escape.DEFAULT_THRESHOLD, or the promise that it arrives bolting is not kept by anything.

The item carries nothing

RescuingBraidItem has no data component, no durability and no state beyond vanilla’s dyed_color, because the destination is resolved when it breaks, not when it is made (owner’s call, against the original plan, which wanted a binding step and a bound-stall reference like BoundHorse).

What that buys: no binding UI to design, no component to sync, no braid pointing at a stall that has been pulled down, and a spare in a chest that works for whichever horse you put it on. What it costs is that the item cannot describe its own destination before it fires, which is why the no-destination case is a chat line rather than a refusal at the point of wearing.

resolve asks StallData.forHorse then StallData.penOf, and TicketHandler.landingSpot is what says yes — it re-reads the sign, re-runs the stall detection live and finds a spot the horse fits in, so a stall somebody has filled with hay bales refuses here rather than suffocating an animal. That is StallRecall’s rule verbatim: the Send home button makes exactly this choice, and a braid making a different one would be a second answer to one question.

The move, and the rider

TicketHandler.arrive is called rather than copied, so the braid gets the portal particles, the enderman sound and HorseLeads.untieFor for free — and a future change to how a horse travels reaches this without anyone remembering it exists.

The rider is the part that is genuinely new. Within one level, teleportTo carries an entity’s passengers — the property GeneReactionHandler’s blink already relies on. Across levels it does not: the entity is re-created in the target and its passengers are put down. So reseat looks the horse up again by id in the world it landed in, sends the rider after it, and remounts. Every other teleport in the mod dodges this by refusing a ridden horse outright (TicketHandler, StallRecall, EnderWhistleCalls); a braid cannot, since being ridden is the situation it is bought for.

Nothing forces the escape behaviour, and nothing should. HorseEscapeGoal reads the health bar and whether anything has hurt the horse lately on its own canUse tick, and both are still true on the far side of the teleport — Escape.THREAT_LINGERS_TICKS outlasts the trip by a wide margin. So the horse comes up bolting through the goal’s own door, which is the one that has been played, rather than through a forceStart written for this.

The streak is a mask, and the mask is baked

BraidLayer is the structural twin of EmissiveCoatLayer: one submitModel of the whole horse through a texture that is transparent everywhere it must not paint. What differs is that the texture is a mask — flat white where the mane or the tail is — and the colour is passed as the submit’s own tint, so one pair of files covers every colour a player can mix rather than sixteen baked sheets.

The masks are baked from HorseSkinGeometry, not painted. The two patches are at texOffs(56, 36) and texOffs(42, 36), doubled onto the sheet. Hand-painting two rectangles at those coordinates would work, and would silently paint a horse’s neck the day somebody moves a cube. HairMaskTool asks the mesh instead and hard-fails on a part that covers no texel, because a mask nothing draws through is a braid nobody can see and it would ship green. Re-run :common:bakeHairMasks when the mesh moves.

Gear is a synced attachment, so extractRenderState reads the worn stacks off the horse with no packet of this layer’s own, and the two colours land on GeneticHorseRenderState as plain ints — zero for nothing worn, which is the answer for almost every horse on screen almost all the time.

Why vanilla’s dye and not the mod’s tint

The mod already has a tack colour component, and the braid deliberately does not use it. tack_tint is SaddleTint(seat, bridle, metal) — three zones, because a saddle has a seat, a bridle and its fittings. A braid is one plait of one colour, and forcing it through a three-zone record would mean a zone-names case, a tooltip that says the same word three times, and a bench screen with two slots that do nothing.

So it is in #minecraft:dyeable and carries vanilla’s dyed_color, which buys the whole colour story for one tag file: dyeing in a crafting grid, mixing several dyes, washing it out in a cauldron, the tooltip line and the tinted inventory icon are all vanilla’s code doing what it already does. The item definition’s default tint and GeneticHorseRenderer.BRAID_UNDYED are a pair and must stay equal — a braid that looked one colour in the hand and another on the horse would read as a bug.

What is waiting to be checked

Nothing about the braid has been seen in a running game. It compiles, the rule is unit-tested, the recipe bakes and a dedicated server loads it — but no horse has worn one, no braid has broken, no rider has been carried across a world boundary, and nobody has looked at the streak or the item icon, both of which were generated rather than drawn.

It fires at all, and at the right moment (NOT played)

The whole feature turns on RescuingBraid.fires being asked after the last stand has zeroed the damage — see the Coding tab. The test pins the arithmetic; it cannot pin the event order in a running server.

  • Wear one and get a horse killed. Tame a horse, give it a stall, braid its mane, and hit it down past zero. Expect: totem particles, then the horse gone, then a chat line naming the stall, then the horse standing in it at a gallop.
  • Do it again with the last stand already spent — hurt the horse, get it saved once, and kill it again before it heals back up. The braid should still fire. This is the case it matters most in and the one the ordering makes least obvious.
  • Check the chamber is not also spent. Carry an empty emergency chamber while a braided horse is killed. The braid should go and the chamber should still be empty in your inventory.

The rider arrives with the horse (NOT played)

reseat is the one genuinely new mechanism here and the one nothing in the repository corroborates — every other teleport in the mod refuses a ridden horse rather than solving this.

  • Ride a braided horse to death in the same world. You should land in the stall still mounted, and immediately be thrown off by the escape behaviour — which is correct, not a bug.
  • Then do it across a world boundary. Stall in the overworld, horse killed in the Nether. This is the path EnderWhistleCalls also flags as unverified: watch for being left behind, for the horse arriving riderless, or for the remount throwing.

The streak, and the icon (NOT looked at)

  • Does a braid draw on the horse at all? The masks cover 544 texels of mane and 880 of tail, which is the right order of magnitude, but nobody has seen the result. A mask that is subtly off shows as colour on the neck or the rump.
  • Does the tint reach it? Dye two braids different colours and put one in each slot. The submit’s colour parameter is used here for the first time in this mod.
  • Is the item icon recognisable as a braid? It was generated, not drawn — a plait of two strands with a tie at the bottom, in greys so the dye multiplies cleanly. If it reads as a rope or as noise it wants redrawing by hand.
  • Does an undyed braid match itself? The icon’s default and the renderer’s BRAID_UNDYED are written in two places and must agree.

The refusal, and that it does not spend the braid (NOT played)

  • Braid a horse with no stall and no holding pen and get it killed. Expect one red line, no teleport, and the braid still in the slot afterwards.
  • Then hang a holding pen sign up and do it again. It should work, with the pen named rather than the stall.
  • Fill the stall with hay bales and try again — it should fall through to the pen rather than refusing outright.
What is still planned here

Dyeing a braid at the Tack Dyeing Bench rather than only in a crafting grid, and a crafting recipe that takes its colour from the wool that went into it.

The bench does not dye one

The original plan had the braid dyeable at the Tack Dyeing Bench and crafted in the colour of whatever wool went in. Neither shipped, and both were dropped for the same reason: the bench is built around tack_tint, which is three zones, and the braid is one. Making it work means a zone-names case, a branch in EquestrianBenchMenu.plan, and a screen that visibly does nothing with two of its three material slots — against a braid you can already dye any colour in a crafting grid. It is worth doing when somebody is next in that menu for another reason, not on its own.

The wool-decides-the-colour half needs a custom recipe for one reason only, which is to copy the wool’s dye onto the result. That is cheap; what is not cheap is that a CustomRecipe is invisible to JEI and the recipe book unless both defaults are overridden — the trap the stasis chamber recipes spent a day in. The plain JSON recipe ships instead, and the dye does the colour.
