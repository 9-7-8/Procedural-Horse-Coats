Part built / one part ships, the rest planned

Attached model parts

Meshes hung on the horse model, driven by a gene or by a breed — a unicorn horn, a row of dorsal spines, a pair of wings, feathered hooves. They need no animation of their own, because they are parented to a part of the horse that is already animated. It is the mod’s first visual that is not a texture, which is why this is a page rather than a line on a list.

The foundation is built and three parts use it: the unicorn horn, antlers and ram horns, which an antlered breed, the Rimehart, carries on every horse. Spines, crystals, hoof feathering and wings are the same machinery plus a generator each, and are still on the Roadmap tab.

Built, and not yet seen in a running game

The layer, the mesh cache, the horn and the antlers are unit-tested and compile. A horn has been seen on screen, following the head (2026-09-30); no antler has, and its placement is computed from the horse model’s boxes rather than tuned against a live animal. The open checks are on the horn’s and the antlers’ Verification tabs, and this page’s.

What a player sees

First, a unicorn — a horse with two copies of a rare recessive grows a tapering horn on its forehead, and about one of those in eight has a horn that shines in the dark. How long it is, how thick, how tightly it spirals, how far it leans and what colour it is are inherited with the allele and drift over generations, so a line bred for length really does get longer horns. A foal born with the gene wears a small one from the day it is born.

And antlers: a rare dominant, so one copy grows a rack — on any horse with Ant, on stallions only with Antm, and never on a foal. How big it is, how many tines, how far it reaches, how thick and what shade are inherited with the allele; four more genes pick its habit (forked, brow-tined, palmate, crowned or spike) and can make its points glow, turn it to crystal, or grow moss on it. A wild rack is the same on both sides; two breeds, the Rimehart and the Greenwood, are antlered on every horse.

And ram horns, built on the antler pattern: one copy grows a pair (on any horse with Rh, on stallions only with Rhm), a shape gene makes them curled, corkscrew, four-horned or scurs, and a tip gene fades the points into a colour of their own. The Mirrorgazer and the Not A Sheep carry them.

And dragon horns, the first of the head parts: two copies of a rare recessive grow a pair behind the ears, swept back over the neck — swept, straight or curled, and as long, thick, swept back and splayed as the allele says — on mares and stallions alike, never on a foal. Their colour is a gene of its own with the horn colour’s rules, so a horse’s dragon horns and unicorn horn are bred for colour separately.

And dorsal spines, the first of the body parts and the first part off the head: one copy of a rare dominant grows a row of spines along the back, from the withers to the croup. They are uniform, graduated or alternating, and as tall, thick, sharp and many as the allele says. Mares and stallions grow them alike, foals never. Their colour is a gene of its own. A saddle or a rider hides the spines under the saddle and leaves the rest.

And a back sail: two copies of a rare recessive grow spines along the back with a see-through membrane between them. A tall sail peaks over the middle of the back, a low one runs the whole length, and a scalloped one sags between its spines. Its height, arch, reach, spine count and how see-through the skin is are on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own: two different copies make the membrane one colour and the spines the other. A saddle hides the part under it, as with the spines.

And shoulder and hip plates: one copy of a rare dominant grows overlapping plates of bone over the shoulders and the hips, stepped down the flank like armour, the same on both sides. They are smooth, ridged or spiked, and their size, how many each cluster has, how far they overlap and how far they flare are on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own: two different copies give every plate an edge in the second colour. They sit in front of and behind the saddle, so a saddle hides none of them.

And a narwhal horn: one copy of a very rare dominant grows one long spiral tusk straight forward out of the front of the muzzle. Its length, thickness, twist and lift are on the allele, and its colour is a gene of its own, ivory by default. It is the first form of the tusks gene; boar tusks and sabre fangs are planned. Every part stacks: one horse can wear all eight.

What these parts are not is tack. Nothing here is put on a horse by a player, and nothing here can be taken off: a grown part is a piece of the animal, inherited like its coat. Things you buckle on are horse gear, and they will eventually be drawn by this same layer without becoming the same kind of object.

They do nothing

Deliberately, and this is worth saying plainly because a horn looks like it ought to matter. A grown part moves no stat, grants no ability, changes no hitbox and does not change how the horse is ridden or fed. If it ever did, turning the drawing off would change the world for one player and not another, which is a thing the settings below must never be able to do.

If they cost you frames

A part is real geometry — a horn is roughly as much again as the horse carrying it — so a large herd of them is the case worth a setting. Three live in this client’s own config file, and every one of them gates a drawing and nothing else:

  • parts.enabled — draw grown parts at all.
  • parts.glow — draw the extra full-bright pass a luminous part needs. Off, it still draws, lit like the rest of the horse. The first thing to try if a shader pack makes parts look wrong.
  • parts.detailDistance — how close a horse must be for its parts to be drawn. The horse itself is unaffected at any distance.

A horse with a horn and horns switched off still has the horn: it breeds it, passes it on, and lists it in the horse screen and the browser. Two players on one server may disagree about all three settings and still be looking at the same horses.

The shape of it: two sources, one renderer

A part has two possible origins and exactly one destination. Grown parts come from the genotype, in common/; worn ones will come from the gear attachment, on the client. Both reduce to the same object before they reach the layer — an AttachedPart: a shape, a transform, a colour — and the layer does not know which it is drawing. That is the whole point of the design, and what it buys is that horse shoes, a saddle pad and a set of dorsal spines cannot become three render layers with three different opinions about draw order.

What each piece does

ClassModuleJob
PartNodecommon One box: a parent, a point along it, a rotation, a length, a girth, a sheet region. A tree of these is a part.
PartKindcommon What a gene asks for. Carries the anchor and the default sheet region. One value per part, and one per side for a pair (the antlers, ram’s horns and dragon horns); a kind arrives with its geometry, never ahead of it.
PartAnchorcommon Where it roots, as an enum and never a bone name — so common/ imports nothing from Minecraft and retargeting an anchor is a client edit.
PartShapecommon The identity of a baked mesh: kind, style, size bucket. The cache key, and the thing that bounds the cost.
PartGeneratorscommon The one door from a shape to a box list. Deterministic and game-free, so a test walks every mesh the mod can build with no game running.
AttachedPartcommon One part on one horse, resolved: a shape plus the numbers applied to it at draw time — including a base and a tip colour, blended by tintAt.
GrownPartscommon Every part this genotype grows, in one call, dressed by the loci that finish it — the horn’s colour and glow are other genes. The client asks here and nowhere else, so the second granting gene is a line in this file and no change at all on the Minecraft side.
SaddleZonecommon Which elements of a part along the back a saddle hides, and when: a mask of groups by where each roots, applied while a saddle is drawn or the horse is ridden. See below.
PartMeshesneoforge Bakes a shape into a ModelPart tree, once, and keeps it.
PartModelneoforge A baked mesh with no animation of its own, typed on a Slice of the part rather than the horse, so a two-tone part can be drawn a segment at a time — see the deferred-submit note below.
AttachedPartLayerneoforge Draws them. The only place in the mod that knows a horse bone’s name.

Adding another part

  1. A value on PartKind, with its anchor and sheet region, and cases in styles(), sizeBuckets(), showsOnFoal() and glowRegions() — every one a switch expression or a comparison, so the compiler or a test catches a kind left out. A part that lies along the back also answers saddleZoned() and scalesPerElement() (the body parts).
  2. A generator beside HornGenerator, emitting PartNodes with every parent before its children — PartMeshes bakes in one pass and PartGeneratorTest is what stops a violation reaching a player as a crash report.
  3. A case in PartGenerators.build and in PartShape.nominalLength / stretchTo.
  4. If it needs a new anchor: a value on PartAnchor and a case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor, with one offset per horse model — the adult and the foal share bone names and nothing else.
  5. A region in PartSheet and a paint() call in bake-part-sheet.mjs. Keep it a uniform grain; see below.
  6. A gene that returns one, and a line in GrownParts.of — and the granting locus added to the every-part baseline in GrownParts.shapes, or its modifier loci look invisible to the editors.
  7. Its colour, if it has one of its own (owner, 2026-10-01: colour is its own gene per part): a short subclass of AbstractPartColourGene — a key, a name, a priority and the Words its outcomes are written in. The alleles, the two-tone rule, the per-copy hue/tone/chaos numbers and the founders come with it. GrownParts.of asks its tintsFor and dressed puts the base and tip on. Dragon horn colour is the worked example.
  8. A soft part (a beard, a ruff, feathering) samples PartSheet.HAIR, a fine lengthwise fibre grain laid down ahead of them; a hard one HORN or BONE.

What is not on that list is a new render layer, a new render state field, a new config key, or a texture per colour. That was the goal.

The antlers were the test of that claim, and it held (2026-10-01, in the code): no new layer, no render-state field and no config key. What they did need was two things the horn never asked for, and both landed in the shared machinery rather than beside it — a count (tines shown out of the most the mesh holds, through PartModel.Slice.shown and PartNode.group) and passes by region (a crystal antler's see-through shafts, the bloom clumps and the glowing points are each one submit drawing only some PartSheet regions, through Slice.regions). Plus a foal gate, as data on the kind. The antlers are also two kinds, one per side, rather than one mirrored mesh: a negative scale would turn a culled box inside out, and an asymmetric rack wants the sides to differ anyway.

The dragon horns were the head-parts proof (2026-10-02): a new pair of anchors (NAPE_RIGHT/LEFT, behind the ears), a generator and size ladder beside the ram horn’s, and the first part to take its colour from a subclass of the shared colour base. Nothing else moved.

The dorsal spines were the body-parts proof (2026-10-05): the first anchor off the head, and two things no head part needed, a saddle zone and a row that scales each element on its own. Both went into the shared slice rather than beside it (below). Still no new layer, render-state field or config key.

The rule that keeps a herd cheap

Everything a part can vary by is sorted by what it costs to change, not by what it means:

  • Topology — segment counts, branching, twist — goes in the PartShape and means baking a mesh. Quantised, and the set of shapes is therefore finite and small: PartKind.styles() × PartKind.sizeBuckets() per kind, for the whole game, forever. The bound on cost is structural rather than a budget somebody has to enforce, which is why PartMeshes needs neither eviction nor a time budget where GeneticCoatTextureFactory needs both.
  • Proportion and pose — length, girth, lean — is a pose-stack transform. Free, continuous, and safe under a deferred draw because a pose is copied into the submit node. This is also what closes the seam the size buckets would otherwise leave: the scale lands a part on its exact epigenetic length, so nothing pops as a lineage grows through a bucket boundary.
  • Counts — an antler's tines — sit in between. The mesh is baked with the most it can carry, numbered base to tip, and a horse shows the first k with the next one scaled by the fraction. Bounded by the mesh, so a drifted count can never draw more boxes than were baked; continuous, so a line bred for more tines grows the next one in.
  • Colour and glow are arguments to the submit. One greyscale sheet therefore serves every colour, which is the same trick a dyed braid uses.
The deferred-submit trap

submitModel stores a copy of the pose and a reference to the model, and the batch renderer calls setupAnim(state) immediately before drawing each submit. So the obvious way to vary one shared mesh per horse — set visible or xScale on its parts and then submit — is a trap: the second horse in a frame overwrites the first horse’s mesh before either is drawn, and the bug is one horse wearing another’s antlers.

The escape is that submitModel is generic in its state, so PartModel is a Model<PartModel.Slice>: anything that must be applied by mutation goes in setupAnim, where it runs per submit with that submit’s own slice. Three things go through it: which segment of a two-tone horn, which sheet regions a pass draws, and how many of an antler’s tines show — and it sets every field on every box every time, because the last submit of that shared mesh may have been another horse’s. (That setupAnim runs per submit and immediately before its draw is read from the 26.1.2 sources; two horses with different tine counts in one frame is the check that would show it.)

Anchoring, and the root transform

The layer walks the chain from the root part down, where CutieMarkLayer reaches straight for body and hand-tunes its offsets to compensate. That is not tidiness: HdHorseModel.createHdLayer applies MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F), and that transformer rewrites the root part’s own pose — a 1.1 scale and a −2.4016 y offset — so a layer that skips the root works in a space 10% too small and two and a half units too high. Walking from the root keeps the number out of the layer entirely, and makes the same code correct for the foal, whose layer carries no such transform.

Seen working, 2026-09-30. This is the claim the page rests on — that a mesh hung on an already-animated bone inherits its animation and needs none of its own — and the owner watched it hold: a unicorn's horn stayed on the head through the horse's poses in the dev yard, and sat in the right place on an adult. The computed foal offset put the horn in the right place too. So antlers, spines, crystals and wings are unblocked on this point, and the plain-layer-over-GeckoLib decision stands.

After the chain, a model unit is 1⁄16 of a block — the same units the boxes themselves use — because each translateAndRotate divides its translation by sixteen as it goes.

Never add a child to body or head in createHdBodyMesh for a part. That would move the mesh the HD coat UVs are baked against, which is the one thing on this renderer that cannot quietly shift. A separate LayerDefinition, a separate layer, always.

Meshes are baked at run time

Every other model in the mod is registered during EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterLayerDefinitions, which would have forced parts into a fixed set declared at startup — and that would have made a horn’s length one of five presets instead of a real number. It is not so constrained: LayerDefinition.bakeRoot() is public and pure (mesh.getRoot().bake(xTexSize, yTexSize), touching no registry, no event and no resource manager). Read out of the 26.1.2 sources rather than assumed; see API notes.

One greyscale sheet, and why its regions are featureless

part_sheet.png is 64×64, cut into a 4×4 grid of 16×16 regions, baked by node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-part-sheet.mjs. Eight regions are painted — horn and horn tip, antler bone and antler point, the bloom clumps, ram horn and ram tip, and hair — and the rest are room for the parts that have not been written. The baker repeats every region id by value; PartGeneratorTest.theSheetBakerAgreesWithPartSheet reads it and fails if an id, a paint() call or a PAINTED entry disagrees with PartSheet.

A box reads the corner of its region rather than all of it, because a cube’s UV rectangle is its own unwrap — 2*(girth+girth) wide and girth+length tall — laid down at the region’s origin, and the largest box any part generates is about 10×4 texels. That is deliberate: it means a part whose proportions change with an epigenetic number needs no new sheet. The price is that a region must look right at any sub-rectangle of itself, so the baker writes a tileable grain with a direction and no composition at all. A hand-painted or pack-sourced sheet is an upgrade path under the same constraint, and anything vendored that way needs its licence checked first — which is why the shipped default is procedural and owes nothing to anyone.

What the genetic size locus does to a part

Nothing, explicitly: a part inherits the horse’s render scale because that scale is already on the pose stack when the layer runs, not because the layer applies it. That is a claim from reading and not from watching, and a tiny and a huge horse are on the checks.

It is a flag rather than a sixth shows category because viewable() lists the categories it cannot draw, and a new category would read there as drawable (open check 254).
  • Both editors' rows were wrong, and are fixed in one place. A part gene's outcomes are all wild types, because they paint nothing. Both editors' "is it expressing" rule was not a wild type, so an Ant/Ant horse's antlers row said no effect. EditorRules.expressing is now the rule for both editors: a part gene expresses when its outcome is not the one its default alleles give. GrownPartsListedTest holds the listing, the sex gate, the foal flag and the row rule.

The body parts: an anchor off the head, a saddle zone, a row

Shipped with the dorsal spines, 2026-10-05. Everything here was read from the 26.1.2 sources and is unwatched: see the spines’ checks.

The spine anchor

PartAnchor.SPINE is the withers on the midline of the back. Its case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor walks the root, then body, not head_parts. The body’s own pose is the rear, so the row tilts with it. The vanilla body box is (-5,-8,-17) sized (10,10,22) on a pivot of (0,11,5), and HdHorseModel.createHdBodyMesh copies it exactly. The neck’s back edge and the mane meet the top of the box at about z -10..-12, so the anchor is body-local (0, -7.6, -11), just inside the top. The foal’s value is the adult’s and is never drawn: no body part shows on a foal, and the switch must still answer for both models.

The saddle zone

A part along the back runs through where the saddle and the rider sit. The owner’s rule: unsaddled, the whole length shows; with a saddle, only the elements inside the saddle zone are hidden. The gate is a saddle that is drawn, or a rider (EquineRenderState.isRidden, which vanilla fills from entity.isVehicle()). It is not keyed on whether a saddle item is present. A phantom saddle (how a top-bond horse is steered bare) is present and not drawn, and its rider still sits on the back. Keying on the item would put that rider inside the spines and hide them on a bare horse nobody rides. SaddleZone.covers is the rule.

Which elements is a mask of PartNode groups whose root lies in a range along the row (SaddleZone.groupsWithin). It is worked out once per mesh when PartMeshes bakes it, and carried per submit in PartModel.Slice.hidden, because a saddled and an unsaddled horse in one frame share the mesh (the deferred-submit trap again). The range is the vanilla saddle, EquineSaddleModel’s box (-5,-8,-9) sized (10,9,9) inflated by 0.5: body z -9.5..0.5. A one-unit margin each side covers a spine’s own thickness and rake. Those are named constants in AttachedPartLayer. With that range, a full row keeps one spine at the withers and two over the croup. The saddle pad is a tack slot nothing draws yet, so it cannot reach past the saddle today.

The layer order is unchanged: parts before vanilla’s armour and saddle. The zone is why that still works, because the saddle is never drawn over a spine. Body armour is still drawn over whatever stands proud.

A row scales each element on its own

Every other part is scaled as a whole on the pose stack: (girth, stretch, girth) in the anchor’s frame. That is right for a part that grows away from its anchor. A row lies along the body, and across a row that scale is also along it: a thicker row would be a longer one, past the croup. A kind that answers PartKind.scalesPerElement() skips the pose-stack scale. The layer puts the part’s stretch and girth on the slice instead, and PartModel.setupAnim applies them to each group’s first box, about its own root, beside the count’s shrink. So every element of such a kind must be a group (SaddleZoneTest holds the spines to one rooted chain per spine). It is also the one kind whose mesh is many trees rather than one, which PartGeneratorTest allows for that kind alone.

See-through regions are data on the kind

Shipped with the back sail, 2026-10-05. A see-through part is drawn in two passes split by sheet region: the regions PartKind.translucentRegions() names go through entityTranslucent at the part’s AttachedPart.opacity (times the horse’s fade), and the rest of PartSheet.SOLID is drawn solid. The base colour takes the see-through pass, the tip colour the solid one. A crystal antler names BONE, so its shafts are glass and its points solid, as before. A sail names MEMBRANE, the new sheet region, so the blended pass draws its skin and nothing else. Opacity CRYSTAL_ALPHA is a crystal antler, the copy’s number is a sail, and 0 skips the blended pass entirely: a bone sail is its bare rays. A kind with no see-through region is never translucent(), whatever its opacity says. The renderer no longer names a region, so the crystal growths are one more case in that switch.

A membrane is the first flat box: PartNode.width is its extent across x, and is the girth for every square box. PartGeneratorTest holds that only a MEMBRANE box or a plate’s slab (below) is flat, and nothing is thinner than a quarter unit.

Two rows to a mesh, and the flank anchors

Shipped with the shoulder and hip plates, 2026-10-05. PartAnchor.BODY_RIGHT and BODY_LEFT are the flanks: body-local (±5, -7.2, -6), the side wall under a unit below the line of the back and halfway along it. Their case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor walks root → body, as SPINE does, so the plates rear with the body. Read from the 26.1.2 body box, not seen. Like the antlers they are two kinds, one per side, mirrored box for box with ox and rz negated, never with a negative scale.

A side is two rows in one mesh: a cluster 7.5 units forward of the anchor (the shoulder, body z -13.5) and one 9.2 back (the hip, z 3.2), both clear of the saddle’s z -9.5..0.5. So the kind is not saddleZoned; where a saddle flap or barding meets them is the treatment’s open look, decided once seen. It does scalesPerElement, because a whole-part scale would pull the two clusters apart along the body. Slab j of the shoulder and slab j of the hip are both group j, so the count is per cluster with no new machinery: the groups are numbered top down, and PartModel’s count and per-group scale already act on every box whose parent is outside its group, which is both roots. A group with two roots is the one new shape, and PartGeneratorTest counts two trees per element for a plate kind. A slab is the second kind of flat box: a sheet of BONE hung from its top edge (rz of half a turn plus the flare), so a per-element scale grows it down the flank and never up over the back.

The snout anchor, and a part that grows forward

Shipped with the narwhal horn, 2026-10-05. PartAnchor.SNOUT is the middle of the front of the muzzle: head-local (0, -8.5, -6.5), half a unit inside the muzzle box’s front face (x -2..2, y -11..-6, z -7..-2). Its case walks root → head_parts → head, as FOREHEAD does. That is exact for the muzzle: in 26.1.2 upper_mouth and head are both PartPose.ZERO children of head_parts, and AbstractEquineModel.setupAnim animates no jaw, so a part on the head cannot be left behind by a mouth that moves.

It is the one anchor whose part does not stand up from it. Every mesh grows up its own -y, so after the translate the layer turns a SNOUT part a quarter-turn about +x (SNOUT_PITCH), which takes -y to -z: forward along the head’s axis. The per-horse tilt applies after that, so a negative tilt lifts the point. With the neck’s rest pitch the head’s axis points below level. PartKind.NARWHAL is the horn’s generator on its own ladder (NarwhalSize), so the anchor and the pitch were the only new machinery.

A row can be drawn shorter: span

AttachedPart.span is a scale along a row, on the pose stack along its own z, from the anchor. The sail’s coverage number drives it, and it is 1 for every other part. A shorter row roots its elements nearer the withers, so a different run of them is under the saddle. PartModel.saddleGroups(span) asks SaddleZone.groupsWithin(nodes, from, to, span), which divides the zone by the span rather than moving the mesh. The baked mask still serves every horse at span 1.

Every part with bone in it has a bone version

The owner’s rule (2026-10-02): a grown part that realistically has bone in it ships with a bone version — the alleles or bands that draw it as bare bone on a skeleton horse: bone-coloured, unlit, and with nothing soft on it. A wing’s bone version is the bones alone, with no membrane. It is built on the part’s own loci, never as a special case in the renderer, so a bred skeleton can lose it and an ordinary horse can carry it. procedures/add-a-gene.txt (question dd, step 10b) and write-intake-document.txt (5b) ask for it.

What is still planned here

The parts the built machinery has no generator for yet — the five head parts after the dragon horns, the three body parts after the dorsal spines (back sail, shoulder and hip plates, crystalline growths), then hoof feathering, tufted tails and wings, and the horns the antlered beasts of the bestiaries lack — plus the two hooks that would let something other than one gene ask for a part: a breed file, and the worn side, so a horse shoe draws through this layer rather than a bespoke one.

The owner wants a batch of these — “a HUGE win for how cool they look”

Meshes attached to the horse model, gene- or breed-driven, with no independent animation needed. High priority: spikes / dorsal spines and crystalline growths — small models, head- or spine-parented, big visual payoff. The unicorn horn was the first of that batch and antlers the second; both have shipped, and so have dorsal spines (2026-10-05). Lower: pegasus wings (body-parented), lion and tufted tails, hoof feathering (already wanted by the draught breeds), tusks (the narwhal horn shipped 2026-10-05; boar tusks and sabre fangs are its next slice).

Two of this page’s standing questions are answered

Plain render layer or GeckoLib — the plain layer, and it is now built rather than argued: AttachedPartLayer hangs a mesh off head_parts → head and inherits the head’s animation with no dependency and no animation asset. The wings made the argument; the horn settled it. Does the owner need Blockbench — no. The horse itself is built from CubeListBuilder boxes in Java, and so is the horn; a Blockbench export is a nicer way to author the same list of rotated boxes. What the restriction costs is smooth curves, not possibility.

  • The rest of the body parts. The dorsal spines shipped as slice A and settled the spine anchor, the saddle zone and the per-element scale. The back sail shipped as slice B and made see-through regions data on the kind, and the shoulder and hip plates shipped as slice C with the flank anchors. One slice is left, with its own colour locus: crystalline growths along the spine (dominant; seeded clusters through the sail’s translucent path). Crystal geometry is easy (crossed prisms read as eight-sided), and the render type is known: crystal antlers already draw their shafts through entityTranslucent and their points solid, by region. If that reads muddy, opaque emissive crystals are still a good look. A parts.translucent setting joins the config block if a translucent pass is measured to cost. Scoped in GitHub issue #151 (sub-issue #155).
  • Hoof feathering. Four anchors at once, and the first part that should take its colour from the horse’s own coat texture rather than a tint — a sampled mane patch comes out the right colour on every horse, the way real feathering matches the mane. Wanted by the draught breeds, so it pairs with the breed hook.
  • Tufted and lion tails. Rigid tuft on the tail bone first; a tail that curves along its length needs a segment chain with a rotation rule off its parent, which is a formula rather than an animation asset.

What the antlered beasts of the bestiaries lack

Five mythic beasts are scoped as breeds, each carrying the nearest horn that has shipped: the Shadawar, the Yale, the Calopus (with the heraldic antelope folded in), the Tarandrus and the Leucrocotta. Each breed’s book entry will say what is not drawn. The parts below are what each one lacks. Each is its own piece of work and none is scoped yet.

  • A branching hollow horn (the Shadawar, whose horn has dozens of hollow branches the wind plays through). It needs a new part kind. Antlers branch, but they are tines on a rack, not a single horn that forks. The music stays lore.
  • A saw-edged horn (the Calopus and the heraldic antelope). It is either a new part kind or a serrated form on the antler generator. First, find out whether any of the five antler forms already reads as saw-edged.
  • Swivelling horns (the Yale, whose two horns turn independently). This is the first part that would move on its own. Today a part has no motion beyond the bone it hangs from: PartModel.setupAnim sets which boxes show and never a rotation. It would need a rotation rule computed from the horse’s state, like the curving tail above. It would not need an animation asset.
  • Boar tusks and a nose tusk (the Yale, the heraldic antelope). These belong to the tusks gene. The nose tusk is its narwhal horn kept short, which has shipped on the SNOUT anchor; boar tusks on new jaw anchors are its next slice.
  • A lion’s neck and chest, a stag’s hindquarters, a mouth to the ears (the Leucrocotta). Adding a part is not enough here, because part of the vanilla horse has to be hidden first. That is the same capability the Hippocampus and the Hippalektryon wait on. Its lion tail is the tufted-tail item above.

The two hooks

  • A breed may declare parts. Touches the breed format, BreedSpecWriter, the breed-file bakes and the breed designer, which is why it is its own slice rather than a line in an existing one. Less urgent than it was: a part that a gene grows is already a breed's to give, with an ordinary gene pool and bands on the gene's numbers — the Rimehart is antlered and brow-tined that way, with no new format. This hook is for a part with no gene behind it.
  • The worn side. Point horse shoes at this layer rather than a bespoke one; gear stacks resolve to the same AttachedPart list. Saddle pads follow. Not settled: whether a worn item may ever replace a grown part — a helmet over a horn.
The wing’s bone version (owner, 2026-10-02)

When the wing part is built it ships a bone version: the wing bones alone, no membrane, in bone and unlit (the rule). A membrane drawn on the elytra mesh is a texture, so “no membrane” is a locus that turns the membrane off and a bone sheet region for what is left — to settle with the owner, with whether the skeleton breeds carry wings at all.

Wings off the elytra — what transfers and what does not

The owner asked whether the vanilla elytra can be repurposed wholesale: its model hung on a horse, so that any elytra retexture pack becomes a horse-wing texture for free. The answer splits cleanly in two, and it is worth having written down before the wing part is built, because the half that works is the half that is normally the expensive one. The texture side transfers completely. The model and layer classes do not transfer at all — but the mesh they wrap is a dozen lines, and copying it is what keeps every elytra texture lining up.

The elytra texture is no longer a hard-coded path — and that is what makes this work

There is no assets/minecraft/textures/entity/elytra.png in 26.1.2; nothing in the client sources mentions that path any more. The only elytra art in the jar is textures/entity/equipment/wings/elytra.png, reached through the equipment-asset system: assets/minecraft/equipment/elytra.json is just {"layers":{"wings":[{"texture":"minecraft:elytra","use_player_texture":true}]}}, and EquipmentClientInfo.Layer#getTextureLocation assembles the file name from the layer type — "textures/entity/equipment/" + type.getSerializedName() + "/" + path + ".png", with LayerType.WINGS serialising to wings.

So the wing texture is an ordinary Identifier handed to a render type. Nothing about it is player-specific or elytra-specific, and a wing layer that points at Minecraft's own minecraft:textures/entity/equipment/wings/elytra.png inherits whatever the player has installed over it, automatically, with no wing art in this mod's jar and so no third-party licence to settle. That is the cheapest possible version of what was asked for.

  • A retexture pack built for this era ships assets/minecraft/textures/entity/equipment/wings/<name>.png, at 64×32. It drops in as-is.
  • A pack built for before the equipment-asset move ships assets/minecraft/textures/entity/elytra.png — the same 64×32 layout, at the old path. Only the path moved, so such a pack needs a file moved and nothing redrawn.
  • What does not come across is a pack's own equipment/*.json, because a horse-wing layer draws a mod model against a texture id of its own choosing rather than going through an EquipmentAsset. That is a feature, not a limitation: it is what lets a horse's wings be chosen per horse.

Why the two vanilla classes cannot just be reused

Both are typed to the player, not to a generality: ElytraModel (now in net.minecraft.client.model.object.equipment) is an EntityModel<HumanoidRenderState>, and WingsLayer is declared <S extends HumanoidRenderState, M extends EntityModel<S>>. A horse's state is HorseRenderState → EquineRenderState → LivingEntityRenderState, which is not a HumanoidRenderState, so neither type will accept it. WingsLayer additionally reads state.chestEquipment, and an EquineRenderState carries only saddle and bodyArmorItem. Both are dead ends by the signature, not by some subtlety.

That costs less than it sounds, because ElytraModel's animation is no use here either — setupAnim reads elytraRotX/Y/Z and isCrouching off the humanoid state, none of which a horse has. Wing pose was always going to be ours to write. The server already knows the answer it needs: the glide path sets shared flag 7 (isFallFlying) in GeneAbilityHandler, so the render state needs one boolean plus whatever folded/spread poses we choose.

The part that must be copied verbatim is the mesh, since it is what every elytra texture is drawn against: left_wing and right_wing, 10×20×2 boxes with CubeDeformation(1.0F) at texOffs(22, 0), the right one mirrored, baked as LayerDefinition.create(mesh, 64, 32). Copy those UVs and third-party wing art lines up; change them and it does not. ModelLayers.ELYTRA is registered by vanilla whether or not we use its model class, so context.getModelSet().bakeLayer(ModelLayers.ELYTRA) is available for free if the vanilla mesh is wanted unaltered.

Where it hangs on the horse

CutieMarkLayer is already the worked example of a body-parented layer on this renderer: it takes getParentModel().root().getChild("body"), calls body.translateAndRotate(poseStack) to inherit the body's animated transform, and then works in body-local space (x in [-5, 5], y in [-8, 2], z in [-17, 5], front is −z). A wing layer is the same move with a withers-height offset and a mirrored pair.

The wing gets its own layer definition — it is not folded into the HD mesh

HdHorseModel is a 128px, per-part-UV mesh; an elytra texture is 64×32. Adding left_wing/right_wing as children of "body" inside createHdBodyMesh would work structurally — that is how "tail" is attached — but it would move the mesh the HD coat UVs are baked against, which is the one thing on this renderer that cannot quietly shift. Bake the wing as its own LayerDefinition and render it as a separate layer.

What this settles for the rest of the page

It answers the page's standing question — plain render layer or GeckoLib — in the plain layer's favour, for the case that looked most likely to need an animation engine. A wing that only has to fold and spread, parented to a body part that is already animated, needs a boolean and two poses; it does not need a dependency. If wings do not justify GeckoLib, horns and dorsal spines certainly do not, and the batch of head-parented parts above should be written the same way.

Open questions, left open on purpose

What drives the wings. Flying paints nothing today and a gliding horse is visually identical to any other, so wings are the obvious partner — but a visible wing that also announces a recessive is a genetics decision, not a rendering one, and a purely cosmetic wing gene or a breed-driven wing is the alternative. One texture or many. Pointing at Minecraft's own elytra id gives every winged horse the player's single installed retexture; a mod-side id per allele or per breed instead makes a pack's several wing textures into a variety, at the cost of the player having to place files by our names. An unverified constraint. Comments in HorseTackSlot and HorseGear state that EquipmentClientInfo.LayerType is not extensible; in this build it is an IExtensibleEnum whose getExtensionInfo() returns nonExtended, which says only that nothing has extended it here. That is worth re-checking — but only if wings are ever routed through the equipment system as a real tack slot, which none of the above requires.

What needs verifying here

The machinery has drawn one part, a horn, and it follows the head and sits right on an adult; what is still unwatched is the draw order against the tack and the first body part's saddle zone, the antlers on screen, any cost against a herd, and the editors' new part rows and the designer's parts panel.

Draw order has been argued, not observed

AttachedPartLayer sits after the coat passes and the braid and before the vanilla armour and saddle layers. On the head that is safe because nothing there competes with the tack. The first body part, the dorsal spines, shipped on 2026-10-05 with a saddle zone instead of a new order: the spines under a drawn saddle or a rider are hidden, so the saddle should never be drawn over one. Body armour has no zone and is drawn over whatever stands proud. Check a horned horse and a spined horse with body armour and a saddle on. The spine states are listed on the spines’ tab. The back sail is the first large blended part, drawn through entityTranslucent at the same submit order as a crystal antler’s shafts. How it sorts against the coat, armour and other horses is its own check. Treat the first drawn saddle pad as a reason to re-test, not to assume.

One layer, many parts — built, not watched

The antlers answered the code half (see Adding another part): the second part needed no new layer, render-state field or config key. What is unwatched is the runtime half — a horn and a rack of antlers on one horse, and two antlered horses with different tine counts in one frame, which is the first time setupAnim carries something per horse that a wrong order would visibly swap. Nothing has been measured

The parts.detailDistance default was chosen by reasoning about how far off a silhouette is noticeable, not from a frame-time measurement, and the decision that PartMeshes needs no bake budget rests on an estimate of how cheap a mesh bake is rather than a reading.
