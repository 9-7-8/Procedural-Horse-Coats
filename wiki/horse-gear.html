Gameplay / non-genetic

Horse gear: the nineteen slots

Everything a horse wears, in one place. A Gear tab on the horse information screen holds nineteen slots laid out on a picture of the horse itself — head, mane and tail, body, chest, a slot per leg and a slot per hoof, and two for the chests it carries. Two of them are real vanilla equipment slots and the other seventeen are a data attachment of our own, which is the constraint the whole system is shaped by. The slots exist; almost nothing to put in them does yet. The roster, the storage and the screen shipped first on purpose — every piece that follows is one row in a tag. What is still planned is on this page’s own Roadmap tab, which is the single home for gear plans.

Built, and not yet played

The Gear tab, the nineteen-slot roster and the attachment behind it landed on 2026-09-21 and have not been opened in a game. What to look at, in the order worth looking, is on this page’s own Verification tab. The two slots that came before it — saddle and barding — are the only ones with anything to put in them, and they worked on the old flat row.

Where it is

Open a horse’s inventory as you always have, and press the small i button beside it. That is this mod’s own horse information screen — the one with Overview, Genes, Health and the rest — and Gear is the second tab along. The H key is a different screen: that is the horse browser, your roster and reference, and it has no slots in it.

Overview used to carry the two tack slots in a row under the stats. It now says how many pieces the horse is wearing and sends you here, because a row nineteen slots wide would have pushed the rest of Overview off the page. That line is the first thing on Overview, above the sex and the stats: what a horse has on is the one fact on that page that changes between two looks at it.

The nineteen slots

They are arranged on a small three-quarter view of the horse, in the place the gear actually goes: the near fore boot is under the near fore leg, the tail slot is behind the tail. You find a slot by looking at the horse rather than by reading a list.

Drag the horse to turn it. Press anywhere on the animal itself and move the mouse: side to side spins it all the way round, up and down tips it as far as a steep look from above or below. The three-quarter view it opens on shows one side of the horse properly and the other at an angle, so the off-side boots and the tail are worth turning to — and a horse in full tack is worth walking round anyway. Let go and it stays where you put it; reopen the screen and it is back to the opening pose.

The slots take the click first, so a drag that starts on a boot moves nothing and equips as it always did. Start the drag on the horse and you get the horse.

A bridle and a halter share one slot on purpose

They are genuinely different pieces — a haltered horse can be led and lunged, a bridled one can be steered, and neither substitutes for the other — but a horse cannot wear both at once, so they are two items competing for one slot rather than two slots. This reverses an earlier plan that gave them a slot each; the distinction survives intact, it just lives in the items. What having a halter lets you do is ground work, still on the main roadmap because it is a riding mechanic rather than a piece of gear.

Four legs and four hooves, not a set of four

A horse can wear unevenly, and a roster that could only say “hoof equipment: yes” could never say which hoof is occupied. Eight leg and hoof slots is the price of being able to.

Putting something on

  • Or skip the screen entirely. Hold a piece of gear and right-click your horse, and it goes into the first empty slot that takes it — the same gesture that has always put a saddle or a barding on. The screen is what you want when you are choosing between things or taking something off; the click is what you want when you are holding the one piece and standing next to the horse. It is written up under rider comfort, with the rules it follows and the switch that turns it off.
  • Click a slot and it tells you what fits. A short list drops under it: everything in your pack that this slot accepts, each with its picture, and a Take off line at the top for whatever is in there now. Click a line to put it on, click anywhere else to close the list.
  • Or press Dress and drag. On your own horse, within eight blocks, the Gear tab has a Dress button in its top corner. It opens the same doll in a window of its own with your inventory down the right, and there it works like any chest: pick a piece up and put it on a slot, drag it back off, shift-click it either way. Shift-clicking a stack of something every hoof takes puts one on each hoof. The horse still turns if you drag it. Escape takes you back to the Gear tab; walking away from the horse just closes the window.
  • An empty list still answers the question. “Nothing you are carrying fits here” and “nothing takes this slot yet” are different sentences, and which one you get is the difference between going to find the item and not bothering. Most slots say the second — see the box below.
  • Nothing is ever deleted. What comes off goes to your inventory, or on the floor at your feet if there is no room.
  • Your horse, and within eight blocks. The screen opens on any horse — a stranger’s, a cowboy’s string — so you can see what one is wearing and no more. A slot you cannot use is drawn dim rather than hidden, because a gap where a slot should be reads as a bug and a greyed slot reads as the rule it is.
  • A foal wears nothing. Every slot is closed until it grows up. Tack sized for an adult is the one thing a growing horse should not be carrying.
Thirteen of the nineteen are empty by design

The saddle and the barding have had something that fits them all along, and the mane and tail now take the rescuing braid — the first piece of gear of this mod's own to fill a slot of this mod's own. The two packs take anything that stores items. The other thirteen accept nothing: not because they are broken, but because the gear that fills them has not been made. The tab says so on screen, so it reads as unfinished rather than faulty.

Each piece joins its slot as it is made, and nothing on this page needs changing when one does. The Roadmap tab is what is coming.

Gear does something, or it is not made

Cosmetic-only equipment is the thing this mod does not add. Breeding is the one place pure aesthetics are allowed; everything else must provide utility, so tack, barding and hoof equipment carry real effects. That included the mane and tail slots, and the first thing to go in one is the test case: a rescuing braid looks exactly like a decorative plait and is in fact the strongest save in the game, with the colour — the only part of it that is decoration — riding on top of a mechanic that had already paid for the slot. The rule is philosophy § utility, not decoration, and it reversed an earlier “tack gets no stats” call — see design decisions.

Chests on a horse

A horse carries one container on each flank. Not a saddlebag of this mod's making: the chest, barrel or shulker box you already have. Anything you can place in the world and store items in, you can hang on the side of a horse — other mods' chests and crates included, with nothing to configure.

You want toDo this
Hang one Hold it and right-click your horse. It goes on the flank you are standing at, or the other one if that is taken. Or put it in a Pack slot on the Gear tab or in the Dress window. An adult horse of your own; a foal carries nothing.
Open one Right-click the chest itself, where it hangs. A click anywhere else on the horse still does what it always did, which is usually to get on. From the saddle, where there is no clicking it: open the horse's screen, go to Gear, click the Pack slot and choose Open.
Take one off Empty it first. A chest with anything in it will not come off, so nothing is ever spilled by a stray click. A shulker box is the exception: it comes off full, with its contents inside it, because that is what a shulker box is — and it goes on full the same way.

The chest keeps its own screen and its own size. A chest or a barrel opens as the ordinary twenty-seven slots, a shulker box as a shulker box, a shelf as its three, a modded crate as however many it has. Nothing is locked and nothing is shrunk to fit the horse — what the horse limits is below.

An ender chest opens your ender chest — whoever opens it — exactly as one standing on the ground does. Nothing in it is on the horse.

What a load costs

The more a horse carries, the slower it goes. Every item weighs the same, whatever it is: a stack of feathers and a stack of anvils are both sixty-four items. How many a horse can carry is its pulling ability — the same score a cart reads — so the horse you bred to haul a wagon is also the one that carries the most.

  • It is a straight line. Half a load is half the slowdown, and an empty chest costs nothing at all.
  • There is a most a horse can carry, and a horse loaded that far stands still until something is taken out. Nothing is refused at the chest — you can always put the item in; the horse answers for it. For an ordinary horse the default is twice what two full vanilla chests hold, so two full chests cost it half its speed and a weak horse cannot move them.
  • An ender chest weighs nothing, however full. What is in it is somewhere else.
  • The Gear tab says how many items the horse is carrying and how much of its speed it keeps.

A server can turn the weight off, set the maximum, give every horse a free allowance, bend the curve and let an overloaded horse crawl rather than stop: the packs.* keys on the config page.

What happens to it

  • The horse dies: both chests and everything in them drop where it fell, as a donkey's do. A horse brought back afterwards comes back without them.
  • You sell it or turn it loose: the chests and their contents are handed back to you first.
  • You sign it over with a transfer paper: it goes as it stands, luggage and all.
  • A stasis chamber, a ticket, a whistle: the chests travel with the horse.
  • Only the owner can open, hang or take off a chest, and only from within eight blocks.
What counts as storage

Nobody keeps a list. A thing is storage if placing it makes a block that holds items, which is how another mod's chest works the day it is installed. Machines are left out — hoppers, droppers, dispensers, crafters, furnaces, brewing stands and jukeboxes hold items too, and none of them is somewhere you keep things. Item tags let a datapack adjust it: horsegenetics:horse_storage/denied (never), horsegenetics:gear/saddlebag_left and _right (always), horsegenetics:horse_storage/weightless (weighs nothing, like the ender chest) and horsegenetics:horse_storage/keeps_contents (comes off full, like a shulker box).

Only the number of slots comes from the block. A modded drawer that holds thousands of one item in the world is that many ordinary slots on a horse.

The two-slot ceiling

This is the constraint the whole system is shaped by. EquipmentSlot has exactly two animal entries — BODY and SADDLE — and EquipmentClientInfo.LayerType is not extensible. Nineteen slots were therefore never going to be nineteen equipment slots. The two real ones are spent on the saddle and the barding, which are the two vanilla already draws for free; every other piece is a key in a synced data attachment.

The roster is the layout

entity/HorseTackSlot is the whole table. Each constant carries its backing (an EquipmentSlot, or null for a gear slot), its zone, its label, its one-line hint, and its position on the paper doll as a fraction of the doll’s box. That last part is what keeps the screen out of it: adding a slot is a line in the enum plus a tag, and the screen is not touched.

  • isVanilla() is the difference between the two backings, and nothing outside the enum should need to ask — on() and set() cover both.
  • usableOn(horse) is vanilla’s canUseSlot for the two real slots and “not a foal” for the rest.
  • accepts(horse, stack) asks vanilla for the two real slots and asks the slot’s item tag for the rest.

What a slot accepts is a tag

Every gear slot reads an item tag named for it — horsegenetics:gear/mane, one tag per non-vanilla slot, seventeen of them. Two of them have an entry — gear/mane and gear/tail, both holding the rescuing braid — and the other fifteen are empty. A slot whose tag is empty correctly refuses everything rather than accepting dirt, which is exactly the behaviour wanted while the pieces do not exist. Shipping a piece means adding it to its tag, and a datapack can do the same without touching Java: the braid went in without a line of Java in HorseTackSlot changing.

The two storage slots are the exception, and the only one. What they take is not a list anyone could keep, so HorseTackSlot.fits asks HorseStorage instead and their tags are only an addition to what it detects — see chests below. Every caller asks fits or accepts, never the tag directly: TackEquipHandler read the tag itself until 2026-10-08, which was correct for seventeen slots and would have made a chest unwearable by hand.

Why an attachment and not a container

data/HorseGear is the store: an immutable map of slot name to ItemStack, registered as HORSE_GEAR in ModAttachments, serialized, synced and copyOnDeath. A container was the other option and is worse on two counts:

  • A widened container is reachable by hoppers. An attachment is not.
  • AbstractMountInventoryMenu.quickMoveStack is shared with donkeys, mules, llamas and nautiluses, and computes every shift-click range from hardcoded indices. A slot inserted anywhere in that arithmetic is how items duplicate or vanish, and it is the failure that looks fine until somebody shift-clicks with a chest on the horse.

This is also the shape vanilla itself uses: the saddle and armour slots are not backed by the horse’s container either — Mob.createEquipmentSlotContainer fronts the equipment array with a view that owns no storage.

One authority, and every stack is copied

The attachment is the only place a worn piece lives and is never mirrored into a container. Every stack crossing the boundary is copied, so a caller holding the stack it passed in cannot mutate what the horse is wearing, and the map is unmodifiable — every change returns a new HorseGear.

Keyed by name, not by ordinal

The same reason TackSlotPayload sends the name: an ordinal in saved data silently re-points at a different slot the moment the enum gains a constant in the middle, and this enum is expected to keep growing. An unknown key is kept rather than dropped, so a piece parked behind a removed slot is still there if the slot comes back.

The click path

Still one packet. HorseInfoScreen.clickTack opens the picker; picking a line sends TackSlotPayload(entityId, slotName, inventorySlot); ModNetworking.handleTackSlot checks the horse is the player’s and within eight blocks, then calls HorseTackSlot.set — the one place that knows which backing a slot has. A vanilla slot also gets setGuaranteedDrop, so tack a player put on is tack they get back.

The index is re-checked, not trusted. The payload used to mean the hand implicitly, which needed no validation because the server could read the hand itself. An index into the player’s inventory is a claim about a container the server is the authority on, so handleTackSlot bounds-checks it and runs the same HorseTackSlot.accepts the picker filtered on before equipping anything. A bad index is dropped in silence — no honest client can produce one, so there is nobody to tell.

The picker is the browser’s gene dropdown, field for field — a null slot means closed, the height comes from the contents, it is drawn last so it covers what it hangs over, it owns the wheel, and it eats the next click wherever that click lands. Two dropdowns in one mod that behave differently is worse than either being slightly wrong. It is not a stand-in for the Dress window below, and the two live side by side: the picker is one click on any slot, the window is a real container. A list of what fits is not a picture of an inventory — it is a menu, and it says the one thing a grid of thirty-six squares could not.

The Dress window — a real menu beside the doll

Asked for on 2026-09-25 (“the player's inventory must be visible on the right side of the gear screen, so they can equip/unequip items”) and built on 2026-09-30. menu/HorseGearMenu is an AbstractContainerMenu with the nineteen tack slots and the player's thirty-six; client/HorseGearScreen draws it, doll on the left and inventory on the right.

It is a separate window, not the information screen converted. The owner's call, from two options. HorseInfoScreen shows horses that are not loaded entities at all — a foal from the Offspring tab, a name in the family tree — and a container menu needs a live entity on the server, so a menu under that screen could never be a precondition of it opening. It is also opened by the client from four places, and a menu exists only if the server opens it. So the tab keeps its read-only doll and the click picker, and gains a Dress button. That sends OpenHorseGearPayload; ModNetworking.handleOpenHorseGear checks the same three things handleTackSlot does — a horse, the player's, within eight blocks — and opens the menu through NeoForge's extra-data factory with the entity id, exactly as JumpMenu is opened with a block position. ModMenus' class comment used to say a menu was for a block; it now says the rule is slots, which is what it always meant.

The menu keeps nothing. On the server the tack slots are a GearView — a Container whose reads are HorseTackSlot.on and whose writes are HorseTackSlot.set, so there is no third copy of the gear to drift from vanilla's equipment and the HORSE_GEAR attachment. On the client they are a plain mirror the menu sync fills, like every vanilla client menu.

Every rule of the old packet became slot behaviour, which is the wider surface, so each one is written once and delegated to:

  • mayPlace is HorseTackSlot.accepts, which carries usableOn — so the fifteen empty tags refuse everything and a foal's slots refuse too.
  • One piece per slot (getMaxStackSize on the slot and the view). That also shuts every vanilla merge path, the only paths that grow a slot's stack in place without writing it back. GearView hands out copies anyway, so a missed path would lose a count on screen rather than edit the horse behind set's back.
  • stillValid re-checks alive, owned and within eight blocks every tick, so walking off closes it. Whatever is on the cursor then goes back through vanilla's removed — into the inventory, or at the player's feet.
  • canTakeItemForPickAll is false for the tack slots: a double-click on a spare saddle in the bag would otherwise gather the worn one off the horse.
  • GearView.clearContent does nothing on purpose — it is the one method that could strip a horse with no player to hand the pieces to.

The layout is the roster, again. Slots sit at HorseTackSlot.anchorX/anchorY of a 216×160 box, the smallest the anchors survive: the tightest pairs are 0.12 and 0.16 of the box apart, which needs at least 168×131 before two slots overlap. Escape returns to the information screen it came from through a one-shot hand-off keyed by the horse's id; a close that comes from the server (reach, death) does not go through onClose, so it drops the player into the world rather than into a screen about a horse they have left.

The paper doll

Cursor.gear draws the horse first and the slots over it, each at anchorX/anchorY of the doll box, and records where each landed in tackHits rather than working the geometry out twice — which is what keeps a scrolled page’s slots clickable in the right place.

The horse is HorsePortrait.drawPosed, a new fixed-angle companion to the mouse-following draw. The doll’s horse has to hold still: its slots are positioned anatomically, so a portrait that swung to follow the cursor would put the near fore leg somewhere different from the boot slot that belongs to it. The pose is a three-quarter view because face-on overlaps the near and off legs, and four boot slots would then point at one visible leg.

The roster checks itself at boot

HorseTackSlot.verify() runs from the mod constructor, beside DietFoods.verify and for the same reason: every way this table can be wrong fails silently in the game. Two slots given the same anchor draw exactly on top of each other, so one is unclickable and the tab merely looks like it has one slot fewer. An anchor outside 0..1 puts a slot off the doll, invisible but still holding its click box. A third constant handed a real EquipmentSlot would take one the saddle or the barding needs. None of that throws on its own and none of it logs.

Why it is not a unit test

It cannot be. This module’s test source set has no Minecraft on its classpath on purpose — see MaterialScanTest, which imports none — and HorseTackSlot cannot even be loaded without one, since its fields are EquipmentSlot and TagKey. A boot-time throw is the cheapest check that actually runs.

Chests: a worn piece, a store, and a load

Shipped 2026-10-08, to four calls the owner made that day (recorded on design decisions): any placeable storage rather than an item of our own; the chest's own slots, never locked; weight by item count against pulling ability, server-configurable, ender chests exempt; a full chest does not come off. Three things, each with one home:

WhatWhereSynced?
The chest — the stack hung on a flank HorseGear, under SADDLEBAG_LEFT / SADDLEBAG_RIGHT, like any worn piece Yes, with the rest of the gear
The contents data/HorsePacks, the HORSE_PACKS attachment, keyed by the same slot name No. Saved only; a client sees them through the menu it has open
The load — two item counts and which side is weightless data/HorsePackLoad, the HORSE_PACK_LOAD attachment Yes, and not saved: recounted on join and on every change

The slot names say saddlebag because they are keys in saved worlds and were chosen before the owner settled what hangs there. They are not to be renamed.

What is storage: entity/HorseStorage

slots(stack, level, near) is the whole question, and zero means not storage. The item must be a BlockItem whose block is an EntityBlock; one block entity is built — never added to a level — and asked how many slots it has: Container.getContainerSize if it is a Container, otherwise the size of NeoForge's item capability (Capabilities.Item.BLOCK). The answer is cached per item, on each side separately. horse_storage/denied wins over everything; a slot tag makes an item storage that the probe finds nothing in, at UNKNOWN_SIZE — which is how the ender chest gets in, since its block entity holds nothing.

Unverified against another mod's chest

The probe was written against vanilla's block entities. BlockCapability.getCapability handed a block entity that is in no level is @ApiStatus.Internal in NeoForge, and a provider that looks at the world around near sees whatever is really there. Every failure is caught, logged once per item and read as not storage. It is an open check on the Verification tab.

Only the slot count is taken; the storage is ours. Running somebody else's block entity with no block under it — so that their own screen opened — was the other design, and it fails in the one place nobody can test from here: a modded menu's client side usually looks its block entity up by position, and on a horse there is none. So a chest whose size vanilla has a screen for opens vanilla's (ChestMenu for nine-by-N up to six rows, ShulkerBoxMenu for a keeps-its-contents chest of twenty-seven), and any other size opens menu/HorsePackMenu, a plain grid: nine across up to fifty-four slots, then wider rather than scrolling, to HorsePacks.MAX_SLOTS.

The store is mutable, on purpose

HorseGear is immutable and copies every stack across its boundary. HorsePacks cannot be: a container hands out its live stacks and vanilla's menus grow and shrink them in place, so a store that copied on the way out would turn every merge into a silent loss. The attachment's value is therefore a mutable object, one per horse (builder(HorsePacks::new) — a shared default would be one chest for every horse on the server), and HorsePackHandler.PackContainer is a window straight onto it. Nothing marks it dirty because nothing needs to: an entity is written with its chunk regardless.

A list grows to the highest slot written and is never cut, and a container is never smaller than what it holds — so a chest whose mod shrank it, or was removed, still shows every stack. Stacks filed under a slot whose chest has gone are kept, and unload returns them.

Putting on and taking off go through the slot

  • HorseTackSlot.set, for a storage slot, calls HorsePackHandler.unpack: a stack that arrives with a minecraft:container component — a shulker box, a chest picked with its inventory — is emptied into the store, so there is one copy. An empty component is left exactly as it was. Barrels, shelves and shulker boxes carry an empty one by default, and a barrel that came back without it would never stack with another; the gametest pins it.
  • HorseTackSlot.mayTakeOff is false for a chest with anything in it, unless HorseStorage.keepsContents. The server counts the store; a client reads the synced count, which is why HorsePackLoad exists at all.
  • HorseTackSlot.takeOff is the only way a worn piece is handed to somebody: it packs a keeps-its-contents chest's stacks back into the item (HorsePackHandler.pack) and clears the slot. GearView and handleTackSlot both used to read on() and then set(EMPTY); both now call it, and the Dress window's slot refuses through mayPickup.

The load: common/pack/PackLoad

Pure, in common/, and pinned by PackLoadTest. The load is a plain count of items; capacity is CartDraft.capacity(pull), the carts' own curve, so there is one axis of strength. The server's five packs.* keys leave ServerConfig only as one PackLoad.Curve, through packCurve(), so no reader can take one key and miss the others. HorsePackHandler.refresh recounts, writes HORSE_PACK_LOAD if it changed, and sets a transient ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL modifier on movement speed (horsegenetics:pack_load) — transient like the carts', and re-made on EntityJoinLevelEvent, so a config change is picked up on the next load or the next change to a chest rather than baked into the save. A weightless chest's items are synced in the count and left out of weighed().

HorseSpeedFloor does not undo it: that floor is on the attribute's base, and this is a modifier, exactly as the cart's is.

Clicking the chest: common/pack/PackBox

HorsePackHandler.onClickChest does not ask which face of the hitbox was struck — a horse's hitbox is a square column that does not turn with it, so “the left face” is the flank one time in four. It casts the player's own line of sight against a box per flank in the horse's frame, scaled by the horse's size. PackBox holds that box and the three centre constants, and client/PackLayer draws the chest from the same three, so what is clicked is what is seen. The event is cancelled on both sides, as TackEquipHandler's is, or the client predicts a mount. It runs at LOW: after everything that claims a click by what is in the hand, before the equip handler.

Drawing it: client/PackLayer

The layer knows nothing about chests. The worn stack is resolved to its ordinary item model in GeneticHorseRenderer.extractRenderState (ItemDisplayContext.NONE, so no display transform) and submitted on the body bone, turned so a block's front faces away from the horse and squeezed to PackBox.DRAWN_THICK. A barrel is a barrel, a dyed shulker box is its colour, and another mod's crate draws as its own item does. Photographed on 2026-10-08 with a chest, barrel, shulker box, ender chest, shelf, decorated pot and copper chest: upright, latch outward on both flanks.

Thirteen slots still draw nothing

There is no render layer for thirteen of the seventeen gear slots, because there is nothing to render. The saddle and the barding draw as they always have, through vanilla, the two packs are drawn by PackLayer as above, and the mane and tail are drawn by BraidLayer — the first of these, and the worked example of the shape: one submit of the whole model through a mask baked from the mesh, tinted. Every piece past those needs a render layer of our own — the pattern this mod already has ten instances of — and the plan for deriving that geometry rather than modelling it by hand is on the Roadmap tab.

What is still planned here

Nineteen slots ship with almost nothing to put in them, so the whole accessories system is the plan: derived models rather than drawn ones, a tack bench, dye and pattern, enchanted tack, hoof equipment, native tack, panniers that are modelled rather than squeezed blocks, and the saddle pad — with the armour materials the one piece that is only data.

Every gear plan, in one place. These sections used to be spread across the main roadmap and were moved here whole, so that the accessories system, hoof equipment, the native-tack art research and the saddlebags are read together rather than found one at a time. The dedicated saddle-pad page owns the pad design plan. The main roadmap keeps what is next to gear without being gear: ground work, the cart and jump bench, a gene that reads what the horse is wearing, and rider comfort.

Some of this has now shipped

The slot roster, the attachment behind it and the screen that draws them landed on 2026-09-21 and are documented on the Gameplay and Coding tabs above rather than here. What follows is what is still to build. Where a section below describes the roster as fifteen slots or as unbuilt, the Gameplay tab is the one that is true.

Suggested implementation plan

I would build the gear roadmap in this order, because the largest risk is not missing a single item but building the shared plumbing wrong before the roster grows. The right order is: shared slot data + network + render plumbing, then one native tack item, then the bench and decoration pipeline, then utility gear (enchantments, chests and other non-vanilla equipment), and only then the broader catalog of art variants. Add the player inventory and bulk equip controls to the menu before the catalogue grows, so players can actually move equipment into the horse. The code structure that owns each slice is the same one the project already uses: a data attachment as the authority, an item tag for valid contents, a render layer for any piece that is not vanilla, and a menu or data-model object for the final craft and tint details.

  • Phase 1: shared tack core. Add the missing slot entries to HorseTackSlot, keep the two vanilla slots as-is, and build the item-tag gate for each new slot in accepts() and usableOn(). Then extend HorseGear with the new names, serialization and copy-on-death behaviour, and make the TackSlotPayload path update the attachment through one authoritative call rather than a mirrored container. The gear tab must also show the player inventory in its normal location, with the horse slots rearranged around it so the player can see and equip available stacks.
  • Phase 2: render abstraction. Add a shared HorseGearRenderLayer or equivalent that consumes one slot + one item stack and derives a visible mesh from the horse's own bones. I would build this against the saddle-pad and boot proof before the full roster is opened, because the first render pass has to prove the ghosted layer approach works without z-fighting.
  • Phase 3: one real tack item, then one bench. I would land a halter or saddle pad first, not a whole catalogue. That means a new item class, one tag entry, one render layer and one recipe in the equestrian bench. The bench should generate recipes from material + shape rather than from object-by-object JSON so the same material scan can power horse armour and tack without a second registry.
  • Phase 4: decoration pipeline. I would add a HorseGearPattern / HorseGearDye data object, persist it on the item stack, and feed the preview and render tint through the same values. The bench screen should preview the item live on a horse and the actual render layer should use the same pattern data, not a second copy stored only on the client.
  • Phase 5: utility gear. I would implement enchanted tack, hoof equipment and horse chests as a second tier of features after the shared plumbing is proven. Hoof equipment should be a real item with an enchantment slot and a horse-side effect system. Horse chests shipped on 2026-10-08 as attachment-backed storage — see the Coding tab — and the two should share the same validation and ownership rules.
  • Phase 6: equipment actions. Add equip-all and unequip-all actions for every tack and equipment slot, using the same server-side acceptance checks as a single slot transfer. The operation should be deterministic, report slots that could not be filled, and never silently move an item that the slot would reject.
Suggested code in the repo's style

I would keep the first concrete code sketch extremely small and repo-shaped, following the existing HorseTackSlot, HorseGear, ModAttachments and ModNetworking patterns rather than inventing a second architecture. The point is to prove the design against the code the mod already relies on, before the item catalog grows.

The accessories system — everything a horse wears, made one way

This is one system, not eight features. Tack, barding, hoof equipment, saddle pads, chests and the enchantments on any of them were asked for separately over several months and kept being scoped separately; each time, the same four questions came back — where does the piece live if the horse has only two equipment slots, how is it made, how is it decorated, and how is it drawn. They get one answer here, and the sections below are that answer's parts rather than independent tickets. Everything already researched for native tack and hoof equipment is folded in and not restated. The saddle-pad design has its own page.

Tack has stats now — a settled call was reversed to allow this

“Tack gets no stats” was settled, and argued from philosophy’s “add texture, do not add chores”. The owner reversed it on 2026-09-21 and replaced the reasoning: nothing this mod adds may be purely aesthetic except breeding — everything else must provide utility, which makes cosmetic-only tack the thing to avoid rather than the thing to ship. Armour value, equipment effects and tack enchantments are therefore the point of this system rather than a ladder bolted onto a cosmetic one. The record is on design decisions and the rule is on philosophy.

The slot roster

Suggested code for this section: add the missing slot constants in HorseTackSlot, then extend the item tag roster in horsegenetics:gear/* so each slot accepts only the item families that belong to it. The implementation should be intentionally boring and data-driven: each slot owns a zone, a label, a hit-box anchor and a validity tag, with the horseInfoScreen just reading that enum rather than learning about each item one by one. This keeps the page-layout change small and makes the next gear piece a one-line slot addition rather than a UI rewrite.

The screen grows from two slots to fifteen. Asked for explicitly, and listed here because the count is what decides the architecture rather than the art:

SlotNotes
Saddle, bridleSplit apart, where vanilla has one saddle. The bridle is the minimum tack required to control a horse — which is a real gameplay rule and not a slot, and it has to reckon with BarebackSteeringHandler's phantom saddle, which writes the saddle slot every tick.
HalterHeadgear with no bit — what you put on a horse you are not riding, and the prerequisite for ground work. Deliberately distinct from the bridle above: a haltered horse can be led and lunged but not steered, so the two are not a tier and neither substitutes for the other. From Simply Equine.
BardingHorse armour as it exists now, renamed to what it is.
Saddle padSee the dedicated saddle-pad roadmap.
Boots × 4One per leg.
Hoof equipment × 4One per hoof, and a different thing from a boot. See the dedicated page.
Martingale / collar, fly maskThe two remaining real pieces of tack.
Chest × 2Storage — shipped 2026-10-08, and on the Gameplay tab now.

Fifteen slots cannot be fifteen EquipmentSlots, and this is the load-bearing constraint the whole system is shaped by: EquipmentSlot has exactly two animal entries, BODY and SADDLE, and EquipmentClientInfo.LayerType is not extensible. So every piece past the second is a synced data attachment plus a render layer of our own — the pattern this mod already has ten instances of — and the two real slots are spent on whichever two benefit most from vanilla drawing them for free. Native tack works the alpha-cutout finding out in detail and it applies to every row above.

The seam for this already exists and was built expecting it. entity/HorseTackSlot is an enum of exactly two constants — SADDLE and ARMOUR — whose own documentation says the shape is there for later and names “a bridle, a blanket and hoof equipment” as the obvious next ones — and a halter is a bridle's near neighbour. The screen that draws them is ours rather than vanilla's (HorseInfoScreen.drawTackSlot, reached by the small i button HorseScreenHooks adds beside vanilla's horse inventory), and clicks already round-trip through TackSlotPayload. So the roster is this enum growing by thirteen, and the screen it feeds — which is a much smaller statement of the work than fourteen new systems, and is the reason to do it as one change.

Per-leg is four times the geometry problem, not one-quarter of it. Boots and hoof equipment are the only rows that anchor to four animated bones rather than to the body, so they are the ones to prototype first; if four independently-rendered leg pieces do not work, the rest of the roster does not care, and if they do, everything else is easier. CutieMarkLayer is the worked precedent for geometry riding an animated bone.

Deriving the models instead of drawing them

Suggested implementation: create a single shared geometry helper that takes a named piece, a source model and an inflation value, then returns a renderable subset of the horse body or saddle mesh. The first proof should be one leg boot or one saddle pad on a single horse pose; once that passes, the rest of the roster is a table of model names and inflation values rather than a new file per item. In code terms, this should live near the existing render-layer plumbing rather than in the item classes themselves: a utility such as HorseGearGeometry.fromHorsePart() with a tiny material list and a preserve-alpha rule is the right shape.

The owner's proposal, and it is the difference between this being affordable and not: clone the existing horse model and erase every part the piece does not touch, rather than modelling fourteen pieces by hand. That is exactly what the alpha-cutout finding already establishes for equipment — a cube whose texels are transparent does not appear, so one mesh is many pieces purely by which part of the sheet is painted — and generalising it from “three pieces off the saddle mesh” to “a piece is a named subset of the horse's own parts” is the actual feature.

The open question is inflation, not erasure. A piece derived from HORSE_BODY has to clear the skin or it z-fights, which the saddle-pad research already found the hard way; a per-piece inflation value is probably the whole of what the derivation needs to take as a parameter. Worth proving on one leg boot before any of the roster is committed to.

The tack bench, and what a bench buys

Suggested implementation: put a new menu class beside the existing equestrian-bench work, with a recipe factory that takes a material, a shape and a slot and emits the output item stack. This should be the one-and-only place that crafts horse armour, bridles, halters and saddle pads; the item tags stay as the final gate for what can be worn, while the bench owns the creation path. A good first proof is a single leather barding and a single hoof item, because each one exercises a different branch: vanilla materials, modded materials and a non-standard slot.

All of it is made at a bench of our own, and the reasoning is as much about recipes as about fiction. The owner's three arguments, which are all good ones:

  • It removes the recipe-collision problem entirely. A bench recipe is in our own menu type and cannot collide with vanilla's crafting table or with another mod's — which is the standing worry that check-recipes.mjs and the BHAR check exist to manage.
  • It is one switch. Disable the bench in config and horse armour cannot be crafted, with no per-recipe gating anywhere.
  • It takes a formula, not a list. The bench generates its recipes from a shape × a material, so all horse armour including modded metals is craftable without a file per combination — which is the same trick the generated-materials layer already plays, and should share its material roster rather than build a second one.

Saddles and bridles are made here too, so the bench is the single place tack comes from. Note it is a third bench: the dyeing bench ships and the cart and jump bench is proposed, and three benches with overlapping jobs wants a deliberate answer about what distinguishes them before the second one is built.

Dye, pattern, banner, and a preview

Suggested implementation: add a small data object such as HorseGearStyle carrying baseColor, trimColor, patternId, and a bannerSeed or transform state. Store it on the item stack or on the attachment, and have both the bench preview and the render layer read the same object; do not let the client preview create a second source of truth. The first pass is a single pattern on the saddle pad and one trim colour on one boot, because those prove the layered tint and transform path without the full catalog of item families.

  • The dyeing bench grows to cover every new piece, not just the saddle and leather barding it dyes today.
  • Patterns, not just colours — stripes and dots were named. That is a second tint layer and a pattern id per piece, which IClientItemExtensions.getArmorLayerTintColor already makes cheap for two colours on one stack (see native tack), and expensive past that.
  • Banner patterns as an alternative to the mod's own. Already designed for the saddle pad as a banner-to-saddlepad editor; generalising it to every piece is the ask, and the feasibility question flagged there is unanswered and gates all of it.
  • A 3D preview in the bench, on any horse you own. The horse browser already renders live horses, so the renderer exists; what does not is a preview that shows an unmade piece, which means the model derivation above has to run client-side on a configuration that no item carries yet.

Enchanted tack — and which of these are really genes

Every piece in the roster is enchantable. Horse armour enchantments were asked for from Wonderful Enchantments, to be applied “with logic” to any tack or hoof item — meaning an enchantment has to declare which slots it is legal on, since a fly mask and a hoof item do not want the same list.

The owner's own caveat is the interesting part: some of these are better as genes. That is a real fork and it should be decided per effect rather than wholesale, because the two are opposites in this mod — a gene is inherited, breedable and the point of the project; an enchantment is bought with levels and transfers with an item. The rule that falls out: if you would want to breed for it, it is a gene; if you would want to move it between horses, it is an enchantment. Worth writing as a decision before the first one is built.

Nothing in this mod is enchantable today, and one near-miss is worth knowing about. There is no enchantment registered, no enchantable component on any item, and no enchantable/ item tag shipped. compat/ModdedArmour does build each generated armour as a real ArmorMaterial carrying iron's enchantmentValue() — but with no enchantable component that value is inert, so a generated barding is not enchantable either. The mod's only enchantment code runs the other way: GeneDeathHandler.enchantedSword() rolls one onto a dropped sword. So the first enchantable piece pays for the whole path.

A ready-made roster, from a mod that built it as saddle upgrades

Horse Tweaks (MIT, Minecraft 1.12.2, abandoned 2019) put seven abilities on a single Enhanced Saddle as NBT flags, each costing saddle durability while it was doing something: swimming, easy jump, thorns, frost walker, leaf walker, feather fall and fire resistance. That is this section's roster already written by somebody else, and the owner's call on 2026-09-21 was to take it as enchantments rather than as a saddle — which is also the answer the rule above gives, since every one of them is a thing you would want to move between horses.

Two are already ours by another route and should not become enchantments: swimming is shipped through HorseWaterRidingHandler and HorseSwimMixin, and easy jump is shipped as HorseInstantJumpMixin behind ride.instant_jump. Feather fall is the one to think hardest about — we cancel fall damage today only while a horse is genuinely flying (server/FlightFallGuard), and an enchantment that does it for any horse is a larger change to how riding works than its five neighbours.

The durability-per-use idea is worth keeping even though the saddle is not: it is what stopped seven permanent abilities being strictly free, and an enchanted piece of tack has the same problem.

The armour materials still missing

Named from two mods and grouped because they are the same work: netherite (from Simple Netherite Horse Armor) and the bone, ender, emerald, obsidian and blaze set (from Special Armor & Tool Sets).

Netherite is not an oversight, and that is what makes it a decision rather than a task. The generated-materials layer derives an armour per modded metal or gem from c:ingots/* and c:gems/*, and MaterialScan.collectTagged deliberately skips every tag entry in the minecraft: namespace — because vanilla's own armours are left to BHAR on purpose. A netherite_horse_armor therefore means crossing that line knowingly. It is a defensible crossing, since vanilla ships no netherite horse armour for BHAR to have a recipe for, but it should be recorded as a call rather than slipped in as a material.

The other five are outside the scan by shape, not by policy. Emerald is the near-miss — it is a gem, so the machinery fits it exactly, and it is excluded only because minecraft:emerald is vanilla; bone, ender, obsidian and blaze are not ingots or gems at all and no c: tag will ever surface them. So this row is really one question asked twice: should the scan be widened to name vanilla materials deliberately, and if so, five or six armours fall out of one change rather than five hand-written ones. Answer that before writing any of them.

The armour stand, and the tack pack

From Horse Armor Stand, and expanded well past it. A stand that wears anything a horse can wear — which makes it the display case for the roster above and, not incidentally, the test rig for it.

  • Right-click opens a screen with scale, wood and paint (through the same wood-and-paint system the carts and jumps use), and a pose chosen as a frozen frame of any animation a horse actually has — which is the one genuinely novel piece, since it means sampling the animation state machine at an arbitrary point rather than playing it.
  • The tack pack is the part with gameplay in it: right-click the stand to load every piece on it into one bag, right-click a naked horse to dress it, right-click a dressed horse to strip it back into the bag. This is the answer to fourteen slots being tedious, and it is worth building early for that reason rather than late as a convenience — a fourteen-slot screen without it is a chore, and philosophy says not to add those.

Curios, if it is there — and never a dependency

Wanted, and explicitly not a hard dependency. The shape that satisfies both: the attachment is the authority and the slots are ours, and a Curios integration is a view onto them registered only when the mod is present — the same fail-soft pattern neoforge/compat/ already uses to read other mods' jars without depending on them.

Two things to check before promising it. Curios is built around slots on a player; whether its API models slots on an arbitrary entity on 26.1.2, and whether a Curios-owned slot can be the authority for something a render layer of ours draws, are both unverified here. If the answer is that Curios cannot hold horse slots, the integration inverts — a rider's Curios slot holding tack for the horse they are on — which is a different and smaller feature, and should be recognised as a substitute rather than shipped as if it were the thing asked for.

Hoof equipment has its own page because the material tier, stat bonuses, powered-snow trait, independent item stacks and enchantment rules are specific to that equipment family. The shared slot, attachment, rendering and bench architecture remains documented here; the shoe-specific plan is on Horse shoes.

Native tack — cheap in code, expensive in art

Suggested implementation: land the smallest possible native-tack slice first: a single halter or blanket item built from an existing horse mesh with alpha-cutout geometry, one item tag, one render layer and one networked attachment entry. That proves the rule the horse's own mesh is the art source without forcing the team to author a four-piece harness set or to rework the rest of the gear system in the same pass. After that, the broader pack (bridle, blanket, western and racing variants) is a material-effort problem, not a code architecture problem.

Assessed against this codebase rather than in the abstract. Every seam already exists: CutieMarkLayer is a worked precedent for geometry anchored to an animated bone (body.translateAndRotate(poseStack)), EquineSaddleModel shows the cube layout for head strapwork, a new item is one line in ModItems, and the synced-attachment pattern has ten instances already.

  • Geometry is hidden with alpha, not with JSON — and that is the finding that changes this section. Equipment draws through an alpha-cutout pass, so any cube whose texels are left transparent simply does not appear. One existing mesh therefore becomes many pieces purely by which part of the sheet is painted. A halter is head_saddle + mouth_saddle_wrap with everything else erased; a bridle adds the bits and reins; a blanket is the horse-armour body cube. No modeling, and for those three, no Java at all.
  • A blanket needs no minecraft: override. Equipment assets are keyed by the file's own namespace, so assets/horsegenetics/equipment/blanket.json registers horsegenetics:blanket and its texture resolves under our namespace too. Equipping is not tag-gated: Horse.canUseSlot returns true unconditionally, so any item with Equippable(slot=BODY) equips and renders. Cost per piece: one JSON, one or two PNGs, three lines of registration.
  • The two-slot ceiling is real but less binding than it looked. EquipmentSlot has exactly two animal slots and EquipmentClientInfo.LayerType is non-extended, so three simultaneous pieces still needs a synced attachment and a bespoke layer. But two pieces — one on BODY, one on SADDLE — are free today and carry independent dye colours, because they are two stacks.
  • Two colours on ONE item: use NeoForge's hook, not a custom layer. Vanilla reads the dye once, outside the layer loop, so a single stack gives one colour however many layers it declares. NeoForge's IClientItemExtensions.getArmorLayerTintColor exists for exactly this and hands you the layer index; returning 0 skips a layer entirely, which is free per-piece toggling. About twenty lines of client Java and a two-int component — no new geometry, no custom render layer. This supersedes the draw-the-parts-twice idea, which additionally does not work as written: rendering is deferred, so toggling part.visible between two submits mutates one shared model and both draw the final state.
  • Two traps worth writing down. A saddle pad must use the HORSE_BODY mesh (inflated 0.1, so it clears the skin) and not the HORSE_SADDLE body cube, which is exactly coincident with the horse and z-fights. And the reins' visibility is hardwired to isRidden in EquineSaddleModel.setupAnim, so a bridle inherits that and cannot change it without Java.
  • The four riding sets are not four features, they are four art styles. English, western, racing and jumping share identical code and differ only in textures — and Horse Tack's are All Rights Reserved even though sekelsta's code is MIT, so not one may be copied. This project ships four tickets sharing one texture and four whistles sharing another; it cannot afford seven sets of sixteen PNGs. A draft harness is the only category needing genuinely new geometry.
  • There is no third-party code to start from — checked, not assumed. Horse Tack's source was read in full: two Java files, a long list of literal registrations, no rendering and no equip logic, on APIs 26.1.2 has removed. The taxonomy is the only transferable part — saddle pad, halter, english / jumping / western / racing tack, cart harness, and blankets (solid, trimmed, novelty) — and its two lessons are both cautionary: do not pre-register colour variants (dyeable layers collapse each ×16 run to one item), and do not pre-register combinations (that was a one-slot workaround; we have two slots).
  • Minimum viable version: one saddle pad and one halter, dyeable through vanilla dyed_color, on a new synced attachment, drawn by one render layer. No stats (settled), no sets, no foal support — vanilla already draws no equipment on babies.
  • It contradicts the current positioning. Mod compatibility says in as many words that tack is what this mod deliberately leaves to others and that the two are “almost perfectly complementary”. Shipping native tack means rewriting that claim in the same change.

Chests on a horse: what is left

Chests shipped on 2026-10-08 and are on the Gameplay and Coding tabs. Three things from the original plan did not:

  • The look of a loaded horse. A chest is drawn as its own block, halved and squeezed flat against the flank. That is honest and it works for any mod's block, but it is still a box clipped to a horse — the complaint about vanilla's donkey. Panniers that sit on the animal are the same feature as deriving equipment geometry from the horse's own parts and should be built with it.
  • Other mods' interdimensional storage. The ender chest is handled by name. Another mod's equivalent is only weightless if a datapack tags it, and only works at all if its block entity holds items — one that fronts a global inventory keyed by a frequency would be a plain box here. Doing better means running the block's own logic, which is the design the Coding tab explains turning down.
  • A key to open them from the saddle. Today it is the horse screen, the Gear tab and the slot's menu: three clicks for something a rider does often.
What needs verifying here

The tack row became a nineteen-slot Gear tab that no client has opened, so the saddle and barding still working through their new write path is the regression to check first, and the seventeen empty slots must refuse an item rather than eat one. Since 2026-09-26 a click opens a picker instead of swapping with the hand, and the packet carries an inventory index rather than meaning “the hand” — so the write path changed again, underneath a screen nobody had confirmed the first version of. Since 2026-09-30 there is also a Dress window, a real container menu with the player's inventory beside the doll, and nothing about it has been opened in a game. Since 2026-10-08 a horse carries a chest on each flank, proven by a gametest and a photo shoot and never opened, loaded or ridden by a player.

Chests on a horse (2026-10-08, NOT played)

Built from the owner's request the same day; see the Coding tab. The gametest horse_carries_chests and a clean server boot cover the rules a mock player can reach. No menu has been opened — the harness has no real player — so everything a hand does inside a chest is unproven. In the order they can be wrong without showing:

  • Put things in, log out, log in. The stacks are where they were, in the slots they were in. Then the same across a server restart, and through a stasis chamber and back.
  • Shift-click a full inventory into a chest and back, and double-click to gather a stack: nothing duplicated, nothing short. The store hands vanilla its live stacks, and this is the path that proves that was right.
  • Try to take a chest off with one item in it — in the Dress window (it should not lift) and from the picker's Take off line (it should say to empty it). Then a shulker box, which should come off full and go back on full, with the same items in the same places.
  • Kill a loaded horse. Two chests and everything in them on the ground, once. Then resurrect it: it comes back with no chests. On a horse that turns to something when it dies (a gene with a death drop), the luggage must still be there.
  • Sell a loaded horse to a cowboy, and turn one loose: the chests and contents are in your inventory or at your feet.
  • Load it and ride it. Two full chests should halve an ordinary horse's speed, the Gear tab's Carrying line should say so, and emptying them should give the speed back at once. A horse at its maximum should not walk. Then packs.weight = false on the server and a relog.
  • An ender chest opens yours, a second player's opens theirs, and filling it does not slow the horse.
