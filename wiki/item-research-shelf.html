Block / genes

Equine Research Shelf

A bookshelf that files research papers — one of each gene — and copies any of them onto blank books for as long as the original stays in it. It is what turns knowing a gene into owning it.

What it does

Recipe (shapeless): 1 bookshelf + 2 horse hair.

Right-click it and you get two tabs.

  • Store — put a research paper in the slot and the shelf files it: the paper is gone and the gene is now on the shelf’s list. Click any row in that list to take that paper back out. A shelf holds one paper per gene and any number of genes, so filing a duplicate does nothing and the paper is left in the slot rather than eaten.
  • Craft — pick a gene from the same list, put a blank book in, and take a copy of that gene’s paper out. The book is spent. The shelf’s own paper never is, so you can do it as many times as you have books.

    A copy takes time — one iron ingot’s smelt per rarity tier, so about ten seconds for a common gene and about a minute for a mythic one. The arrow between the slots fills as it goes and the line underneath counts down. Pulling the book out abandons the copy rather than pausing it.

Taking a paper back stops the copying

The list is the shelf’s contents, not your gene database. Withdraw the original and that gene leaves the list, and the shelf can no longer copy it. That is the whole point: a shelf you have filled is a collection that exists in the world, and it can be emptied.

Breaking it gives everything back — the block and every filed paper drop together. Moving a shelf costs nothing.

Where the papers come from

The shelf makes papers plentiful; it does not make them appear. The ways in are on Research papers, and the one that pairs with this is a book on a horse: right-click a horse holding a book and you get a paper for one gene that horse carries, chosen at random. You cannot pick, which is what makes a paper something you found.

So the loop is: meet horses, take what they will tell you, file it, and copy it from then on. A gene you have filed is a gene you can hand to somebody else as often as you like — which is what makes knowledge shareable rather than hoardable.

It stores a set of keys, not items

EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity keeps a Set<String> of gene keys. A research paper is completely described by the one gene it names, and the shelf holds at most one of each — so the rule is structural rather than enforced. There is no way to express “two papers for flaxen” in the data, so no code has to remember not to, and a shelf can hold every gene in the mod without an item stack per gene.

Keys become items again at exactly two moments: a withdrawal, and affectNeighborsAfterRemoval. A key for a gene this build no longer has is kept and shown by its raw key rather than dropped — losing somebody’s collection because they removed a gene pack would be worse than a row that reads oddly.

Three slots, two tabs

ResearchShelfMenu has a book slot, a result slot and a filing slot. Which are live is the tab, and the mechanism is Slot.isActive() rather than moving them: Slot.x and Slot.y are final, and isActive() is what vanilla consults for drawing and for hit-testing — the pair that must never disagree, because a slot you cannot see but can still click is the worst of both.

The result is recomputed, never remembered: it exists only while a book is in the slot and the shelf still holds the selected gene, so pulling the book out or withdrawing the original clears it on its own.

The shelf is the authority

The contents are not items in slots, so none of it reaches the client through the ordinary container sync. ShelfSyncPayload pushes the gene list on every change, and server/ShelfOpenSync pushes it once when the menu opens — that one has no change behind it, and without it a freshly opened shelf draws empty until you touch something. It is an event rather than a line in the menu’s constructor because openMenu builds the menu before it sends the open packet, so a constructor push would arrive first and be dropped.

ShelfActionPayload carries the two things a player can do — select a gene, withdraw a paper — and both are re-checked against the block entity. A forged packet can at worst ask for a gene the shelf does not hold, and get nothing.
