Equine Research Shelf

A bookshelf that files research papers — one of each allele pair — and copies any of them onto blank books for as long as the original stays in it. It is what turns knowing a gene into owning it.

What it does

Recipe (shapeless): 1 bookshelf + 2 horse hair.

Right-click it and you get two tabs.

  • Store — opens first, and it is a chest: six rows of nine slots for research papers. Put a paper in, take it out, whenever you like — nothing takes time and nothing is used up. Shift-clicking a paper from your inventory files it. A shelf holds one paper per allele pair, so a second paper for a pair that is already there will not go in — but Agouti: A/a and Agouti: A/A are two pairs and both file, which is the point, since they craft different carrots.
  • Copy — pick a pair from the papers on the shelf and put blank books in. It works like a furnace: each book becomes a copy of that pair’s paper in the result slot, one after another, whether or not you have the shelf open - set it going and walk away. Copies of the same pair stack. The shelf’s own paper is never used up.

    A copy takes time — one iron ingot’s smelt per rarity tier, so about ten seconds for a common gene and about a minute for a mythic one. The arrow between the slots fills as it goes. Running out of books, picking a different pair, or taking the original off the shelf starts the clock again rather than pausing it.

The shelf opens on whichever tab you used last.

Taking a paper back stops the copying

The Copy list is what is on the shelf, not your gene database. Take the original out of its slot and that pair leaves the list, and the shelf can no longer copy it. That is the whole point: a shelf you have filled is a collection that exists in the world, and it can be emptied.

Breaking it gives everything back — the block and every paper on it drop together, the way a chest does. Moving a shelf costs nothing.

Where the papers come from

The shelf makes papers plentiful; it does not make them appear. The ways in are on Research papers, and the one that pairs with this is a book on a horse: right-click a horse holding a book and you get a paper for one pair that horse carries, chosen at random. You cannot pick, which is what makes a paper something you found.

So the loop is: meet horses, take what they will tell you, file it, and copy it from then on.

Filing a paper teaches you its gene. Put a paper for a gene you have never met onto the shelf with your own hands and that gene is discovered for you, as if you had tamed a horse carrying it: it shows in your gene database and its carrot recipe opens up. A paper for the plain, wild pair teaches nothing, because the ordinary horse tells you nothing new.

A pair you have filed is a pair you can hand to somebody else as often as you like — which is what makes knowledge shareable rather than hoardable.

Real slots, one paper per allele pair

EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity holds a 54-slot container (SLOTS, a double chest) saved with ContainerHelper. It was a Set<String> of keys fed through one filing slot and read back through a clickable list; in play that read as a shelf that did nothing, and the owner asked for it to be “just a UI like a chest” (2026-09-10). The one-per-pair rule the set used to make structural is now a slot rule: PaperSlot.mayPlace refuses a paper whose pair another slot already holds, and every slot takes one item.

The key is the pair, and that is the whole change

Every static on this block entity used to take a gene key and now takes a ResearchTopic.token() — topicOf, topicsIn, holdsElsewhere, stores, displayName, copyTicks, the saved selected string, and ShelfActionPayload. Nothing about the shelf's shape moved; what moved is what counts as “the same paper”, since a paper names a pair now.

Two consequences worth knowing. The Copy list is much longer — a locus you have studied contributes several rows, and the flat Gene: pair list sorted by gene name was chosen over grouping rows under a gene heading because it needed no new row-type handling in the screen or the menu sync (owner's call, 2026-09-24). And copyTicks is still the gene's rarity, not the pair's: what is rare is the locus, and a carrier paper is no easier to transcribe than a true-breeding one.

A pick saved before the change is a bare gene key, which no longer matches any filed paper, so the shelf simply has nothing selected and the player picks again. A filed paper is migrated properly — see the first piece of back-compat.

Breaking it drops the contents from the block entity’s preRemoveSideEffects, where vanilla’s chests do it. The old shelf dropped from the block’s affectNeighborsAfterRemoval, which runs after the block entity is already gone — so as far as the code shows, breaking a filled shelf used to lose the lot.

Two tabs, one set of slots each

ResearchShelfMenu has the 54 paper slots (Store) and a book slot and a result slot (Copy). Which are live is the tab, through Slot.isActive() rather than moving them: Slot.x and Slot.y are final, and isActive() is what vanilla consults for drawing and hit-testing. The tab is only known on the client, so on the server every slot is live — the client never sends a click on a slot it is not drawing.

Because the papers are real slots the ordinary container sync carries them, and the Copy list is read from those slots on both sides (topicsIn). The only packet left is ShelfActionPayload’s pair pick; the old ShelfSyncPayload and the withdraw action are gone.

The copy runs on the block, like a furnace

The book slot, the result slot, the picked pair and the progress all live on EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity, and its ticker advances them whoever is looking. The menu only wraps them - its slots are the block’s containers and its progress bar reads the block’s ContainerData, the furnace’s dataAccess pattern. The copy used to live on the menu, which only exists while a player has the screen open, so it stopped the moment they clicked off (owner-reported 2026-09-10). A finished copy spends one book; the pick reaches the client as an index into the Copy list (DATA_SELECTED), since a ContainerData can only carry ints.

Its labels are window-relative. AbstractContainerScreen translates to (leftPos, topPos) before calling extractLabels, and the shelf used to add them again, which threw the title and the inventory label off the window. The last tab is a static on the screen, so it holds for the session.

The block finally looks like itself

Until 2026-09-29 this block was vanilla’s bookshelf on its four sides with oak planks on the ends — which for a block whose whole job is filing papers was close enough to be actively confusing, since a shelf and a bookshelf in the same room were indistinguishable. It now carries its own filed papers, some of them written on, with one left out on the lid.

Drawn by tools/bake-block-art.mjs over the same plank grain as the dyeing bench, and in the same dark frame, so the two workstations read as a matched pair of furniture rather than two unrelated blocks.

Filing a paper discovers its gene (owner, 2026-10-01; built 2026-10-02)

Settled: the shelf accepts a paper for a gene the player has not discovered, and filing it counts as discovering that gene. Discovery never refuses a paper. Filing is one more way to learn a gene, alongside taming and breeding.

Built: the menu’s PaperSlot.setByPlayer calls GeneDiscoveryHandler.discoverFromPaper(player, topic), which marks the gene discovered through GeneDatabaseData.discover — the same call taming and breeding make, so the carrot recipe unlocks and the database syncs to the client — and ticks the DISCOVER_GENE (and, for a magical gene, MAGICAL_GENE_DISCOVERED) progress tasks. What a paper teaches is decided in common/: ResearchTopic.discoveryTokens() is the paper’s two allele tokens, or nothing for the wild pair (taming’s rule: the ordinary horse teaches nothing) and for a paper this build cannot read. ResearchTopicTest pins that every paper a chest or a villager can hand out teaches its pair.

The two questions left open by the decision, settled while building (not the owner’s calls, so on the session log):

  • Automation discovers nothing. Every way a hand places a paper — click, swap, shift-click, drag, number key — goes through Slot.setByPlayer in vanilla’s 26.1.2 sources. A hopper cannot reach the shelf today (it registers no item capability), and if one is ever added it will write to the container directly and discover nothing. A FakePlayer (another mod’s automation, or the test yard’s own) is refused too, so it never fills the saved database with entries for a player who does not exist.
  • The player is told the way the other routes tell them: the gene database and the recipe update, and the progress tasks tick. There is no chat line of its own, because taming and breeding have none.

Does filing a paper discover its gene? (built 2026-10-02, NOT played)

Open, shipped 2026-10-02 with filing discovers. To check by hand: in a fresh world, open the gene database and note a gene you have not discovered; get a research paper for it (/give, or the supplier); file it on a shelf by clicking it in, then by shift-clicking a second pair of a second undiscovered gene. PASS: both genes now show discovered in the database and their carrot recipes show, without reopening anything.
