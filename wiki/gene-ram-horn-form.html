Magical gene / dominance series / shapes the ram horns, paints nothing

Ram horn form

The shape of a horned horse’s ram horns: curled, corkscrew, four-horned, horizontal twist or scurs. One shape on both sides. On a polled horse it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

The five shapes

AlleleShapeLooks like
FhnFour-hornedA short upright pair and a curled pair sweeping down — a Jacob sheep
CrlCurledBack, down past the ear and forward again — the ordinary ram’s horn
CrkCorkscrewLong, straight and twisted, rising up and out — a Racka or a markhor
HztHorizontal twistFlat, wide horns straight out to the sides in a shallow twist — the old Egyptian ram, and Khnum
ScrScursLoose stubs, the half-horned state

Each hides the ones below it: four-horned shows over anything, curl over corkscrew, corkscrew over the horizontal twist, and everything over scurs. So scurs need two copies, and two curled horses can throw a scurred foal. The horizontal twist is the rarest shape in the wild and shows only beside another copy of itself or a scurs allele. How far it twists is the horn’s curl number, the one that says how far round a curled horn goes.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill — it only shapes horns it might have.

Allele order is generator order

Crl, Crk, Fhn, Scr, Hzt is the declaration order, the stored order, and RamHornGenerator’s shape indices, so shapeOf(pair) is the dominant allele’s order() and the first factor of the mesh style. Dominance is its own list. Append, never reorder: Hzt came later and went on the end, so no saved horse changed shape, and RamHornsGeneTest pins the order.

The horizontal twist is a flat corkscrew laid level

Hzt (2026-10-09, #125) is RamHornGenerator.HORIZONTAL: the corkscrew’s chain — one unbranched run of boxes that roll and never bend — with its root leaned about 83° off upright, a little back, and every box flat (PartNode.width at 0.6 of its girth), so the roll reads as a turning ribbon. The curl bucket picks the roll per segment (8° to 26°, shallower than a corkscrew’s), and the horn is 0.8 of its class length where a corkscrew is 0.9. A horn is at most twelve boxes against MAX_NODES. It took 0.04 of the founder table from Crl. Neither ram-horned breed’s pool names it, so RamHornBreedsTest checks the four shapes the pools do name. It cost the box budget one raise.

One root for four horns

A part mesh is one tree with one root (PartGeneratorTest), so the four-horned shape grows its curled horn from the base of the upright one (t = 0), not as a second root. The dominance order — four-horned on top, scurs at the bottom — was this build’s call: real polycerate sheep carry a dominant trait, and scurs as the recessive makes a scurred line something you breed for.

Analogous, not measured

The shapes are real sheep and goat horn types — the ram’s spiral, the straight twisted horn of the Hungarian Racka, the two pairs of a Jacob, the level corkscrew horns of the extinct Egyptian Ovis longipes palaeoaegyptiacus that the god Khnum wears, and scurs — but in real animals they are breed characters and several loci, not one allele series. Polycerate (multi-horned) sheep do carry a dominant trait; the rest of the series is a gameplay rule.

What needs verifying here

The horizontal twist has been photographed standing still and the other four shapes have not been looked at side by side; none has been watched on a moving horse, a foal or under tack.

Each shape, side by side

A horned horse of each shape at standard and massive: the curl should read as a ram, the corkscrew as a Racka, four-horned as a Jacob (the lower horn should clear the jaw), scurs as stubs. The four-horned lower horn starting at the upper one’s base is the likeliest to look wrong.

The horizontal twist in play

Still pictures (2026-10-09, the photo stage, head-on and from behind): on a stallion and a mare the horns leave the poll level and a little back, below and outside the ears, flat and stepped by the twist, about a head’s width a side at the heavy class. Nobody has seen them on a walking or grazing horse, on a stallion with Rhm, at the massive class beside a doorway or a second horse, or with a halter or a saddle on (the horns should not be hidden inside a halter). Foals grow no horns, so a foal should show none. Whether the twist is visible at play distance, rather than only the flat taper, is a matter of taste for the owner.
