Magical gene / codominant / colours the dragon horns, paints nothing

Dragon horn colour

What colour a horse’s dragon horns are. The same colours and the same rules as horn colour — white, the rainbow, pink, black, grey or chaos, and two different copies make a two-tone pair — on a locus of its own, so a unicorn with dragon horns can wear a white horn and black dragon horns. On a horse without dragon horns it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

The colours, and two-tone pairs

Exactly the horn colour table: white in most copies, a few percent each of red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet, pink, black and grey, and chaos rarest. Two copies of one colour give a pair of that colour; two different ones give horns of the earlier colour in that list with tips of the other, fading between. Both horns of a pair are coloured alike. Every copy carries a little shade of its own, so no two red pairs are quite the same red.

Its own gene, not the horn’s

Horn colour colours only a unicorn horn, and this colours only dragon horns. A horse with both carries both genes, and you breed each colour separately.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all. It is only the colour of the horns.

One short subclass

The whole gene is a key, a name, a priority and the words its outcomes are written in. The alleles, the two-tone rule, the per-copy hue, tone and chaos numbers and the founders are AbstractPartColourGene’s, which was cut out of horn colour for this; see model parts on how a part gets its colour locus. GrownParts.of asks tintsFor once and dresses both sides with the same base and tip.

Invented

Dragon horns are not real, so neither is their colour. It is a separate locus from the unicorn horn’s because the owner chose one colour gene per part: a shared colour gene would force every part on a horse to match, and a breeder who wants a white horn over black dragon horns would have nothing to select.

What needs verifying here

No coloured dragon horn has been seen in a running game: the colours and the two-tone fade along a swept or curled horn are unit-tested, not watched.

The two-tone fade along a curve

Check a Wht/Red and a Red/Blu pair in each form: the root colour should hold for the lower part and the tips be the second colour. A curled horn has the most segments and the smoothest fade; a budding one the coarsest.
