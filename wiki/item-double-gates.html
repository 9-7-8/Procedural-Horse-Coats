Block / the yard

Double gates

A fence gate two blocks wide. One item places both halves, one click swings both leaves, and there is no post down the middle to catch a horse on. One per wood, and it is a stall’s boundary in every state exactly as a vanilla gate is.

What it is for

A horse is about 1.4 blocks wide and a one-block gateway is a thing you shove a horse through. A double gate is the doorway you actually lead one through — and because both leaves are driven by one click, opening it is one action rather than two.

Recipe (shapeless): 2 vanilla fence gates of that wood. That is the whole recipe — no horse hair.

It took a braided rope for the hinge until the rope was removed, which priced every gate in a fence line at twelve horse hair. It is the one recipe in the mod carrying no modded ingredient at all.

The rope was what kept these off everyone else’s recipe space: every recipe in this mod carries at least one modded ingredient so that none of them can collide with a vanilla or third-party one (the house rules). Dropping it gives that up for this one recipe. The trade was judged worth it because a fence line is the one thing you build dozens of, and because nothing in vanilla consumes a fence gate — every vanilla recipe that mentions one produces it — so there is no collision to have unless another mod ships its own two-gate recipe. check-recipes.mjs reads only this mod’s folder and would not see that, so a modded pack reporting a double gate that will not craft should be traced here first.

One per wood, filed beside its own gate

Every wood vanilla has a fence gate for: oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia, dark oak, pale oak, mangrove, cherry, bamboo, crimson and warped. They live in the vanilla Building Blocks tab, each one directly after the single gate of its own wood, because that is where somebody reaching for a gate looks rather than in a tab about horses.

And one per wood any other mod brought

Install a mod that adds a wood and you get a double gate in it, with no patch and nothing to switch on. The recipe is the same shape — two of that mod’s own fence gates and nothing else — and the gate is filed in the Building Blocks tab directly after that mod’s single gate, exactly as the twelve are filed after vanilla’s.

It wears that mod’s own plank texture. Nothing is drawn for it, because a double gate has never had art of its own — only a pointer at a plank texture — so a modded wood needs that mod’s planks and nothing else. The list is read out of the other mods’ jars before anything registers; how, and what else comes with it, is on the compatibility page.

Two things about a modded gate that are not true of the twelve

Two mods that both add “maple” give you two gates both called “Maple Double Fence Gate” — distinct items with distinct ids, identical names. The mod’s name is left out on purpose, because “Somebody’s Big Biome Mod Maple Double Fence Gate” is worse to read in the far commoner case where there is only one maple.

And a wood whose mod declares a gate it never actually registers gets an uncraftable gate: the block exists and places, but the recipe is skipped rather than written, with a line in the log naming the mod and the id. That is deliberate — a recipe naming an item nothing answers to does not quietly do nothing, it fails to parse in three walls of red.

Placing and breaking

  • One item, two blocks. You place the half you are pointing at and the other appears beside it, on whichever side is free.
  • It refuses rather than leaving half a gate. If neither side has room, nothing is placed and the item stays in your hand. There is no such thing as half a double gate standing on its own.
  • Breaking either half takes both, and you get your one item back. The half you did not break is removed rather than dropped, so a gate is never a way to duplicate anything.

Opening it

Click either half and both swing. Like a vanilla gate it turns to open away from you if you are on the wrong side of it, and both halves turn together. Redstone works on the pair rather than on a block: a signal reaching either half opens both, so a lever at one end of a two-wide gate does what it looks like it should.

It closes a stall, like any other gate

A stall sign treats a door, a trapdoor and a fence gate as the edge of a room whatever state it is in, open or shut. A double gate is a fence gate for that purpose, so a stall shut with one binds exactly as a stall shut with a vanilla gate does — and the checklist has a husbandry step for building one.

It extends FenceGateBlock on purpose

Not for the inherited behaviour — most of that is overridden — but because StallDetector.isDoorway asks instanceof FenceGateBlock, by block identity, in every state. Extending it makes a stall shut with one of these a closed room for free, and nothing in the stall detector had to learn this class exists. A gate that did not extend it would have read as a hole in the wall, and the floor fill would have walked out through it into the yard — which is the bug that class’s header is mostly about.

Which half is which

Each block carries a half of left or right, and finds its partner by turning from FACING — counter-clockwise for the left half, clockwise for the right. A half whose partner is gone returns air from updateShape, the way BedBlock does it. Doing it there rather than in a break hook means the pair also survives being taken apart by an explosion, a piston or /setblock.

One item out, and the loot table is what enforces it

This was written as though returning air from updateShape dropped nothing, and it does not. Block.updateOrDestroy calls destroyBlock(pos, (flags & 32) == 0), and an ordinary neighbour update carries no UPDATE_SUPPRESS_DROPS — so the struck half paid out, the orphaned partner paid out again, and breaking a double gate gave you two. It shipped that way and the owner found it in play.

Vanilla’s doors and beds have the same two-block problem and solve it in the data, not in Java: every gate’s loot table is conditioned on half=left, so whichever half is struck, exactly one of the two removals pays. Compare data/minecraft/loot_table/blocks/oak_door.json, conditioned on half=lower. The condition is written by both generators and a gate that duplicates again means one of them lost it.

playerWillDestroy is the other end of the same rule. Because left is the half that pays, a break that should drop nothing — creative, or one day a tool requirement — still leaked one item when the right half was the one struck: creative suppresses your own block, but the partner is removed by updateShape, which knows nothing about who swung. So the paying half is taken out first with flag 35, leaving the shape update nothing to pay for. DoorBlock does exactly this through DoublePlantBlock.preventDropFromBottomPart.

The partner takes whichever side is free

The first version always put it on the counter-clockwise side and refused outright when that cell was occupied. Because the side is decided by the way the player happens to be facing, that silently did nothing roughly half the time — and the fix a player finds for that is to click the neighbouring block instead, which works, and teaches them the gate takes two clicks. Owner: “clicking and nothing happens, then you have to click again on the other block, is not an intuitive gameplay design”. Placement now tries both sides and only refuses when neither has room. setPlacedBy writes the partner as the opposite of whatever was placed rather than a hardcoded right half, or both blocks would claim the same half and each delete the other.

Opening can flip FACING, and that swaps which side the partner is on

Vanilla turns a gate to swing away from whoever opened it. Applied here, that same turn reverses the direction each half must look in, so a flip swaps both halves’ half value as well, pointing each back at the block it was already paired with. This is the whole reason the open/close path is written out rather than delegated to super.

The two writes must not be observed separately

A pair is two blocks and cannot move in one setBlock. Vanilla’s gate flag 10 does not carry UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE, so shape updates run between the two writes — harmless while only OPEN changes, because the halves still differ, and not harmless when the flip has momentarily made both blocks read as the same half. Each then sees no partner opposite it and one deletes itself. Both writes therefore set UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE; nothing is lost, because neither write changes what the gate is adjacent to and so there is no IN_WALL to recompute.

And the first half is re-asserted after the second exists

BlockItem.placeBlock writes the first half with flag 11, which also does not suppress neighbour shape updates — so updateShape ran while the partner cell was still air, saw an orphan and removed it, all before setPlacedBy had a chance to place the partner. The server recovered and the client was never told, so the first half of a brand-new pair rendered as nothing until something forced a chunk update. setPlacedBy now writes the first half again once the partner is down.

The assets are generated, and one wood is irregular

Twelve blocks, each with a 32-variant blockstate, eight models, an item definition, a recipe and a loot table — over a hundred and fifty files differing only in a wood name and a texture. neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-double-gates.mjs derives all of them from one table; re-run it after touching a wood or any of the geometry. It only ever writes, so removing a wood means deleting that wood’s files by hand as well.

The models could not simply parent to vanilla’s. template_fence_gate draws a post at both ends, so two of them side by side show a double post down the middle — exactly what a double gate is meant not to look like. Each half here keeps its outer post, drops the inner one, and runs its rails the full way to the block boundary, so the pair reads as one opening with a post at each end.

Bamboo is textured with bamboo_planks, not vanilla’s bespoke block/bamboo_fence_gate sheet. That sheet is drawn for vanilla’s geometry, whose rails sit at x 2–6 and 10–14; these run one rail the full 2–14 and so sample the wrong regions of it, and the gate came out visibly scrambled. A planks texture tiles and has no alignment to lose, which is why the other eleven were fine either way. The modded path makes the same call in the same order — ModdedMaterials.plankTexture takes <name>_planks.png if the mod ships one and only falls back to reading a texture id out of that mod’s own gate model when it does not, because a mod whose gate sheet is as bespoke as bamboo’s would scramble in exactly the same way.

The same contract is now written twice, and it can drift

Change one, change the other

neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-double-gates.mjs writes the nine kinds of file for vanilla’s twelve woods at author time, in JavaScript. compat/GeneratedGates writes the same nine kinds for modded woods at run time, in Java. They are one contract in two languages. Change the shape of the blockstate, a model, the item definition, the loot table or the recipe in one and you must change it in the other.

The symptom of missing is a modded gate that is a purple chequerboard while every vanilla one is fine, with nothing logged anywhere. That is this layer’s signature silent failure: an item definition or a blockstate pointing at a model nobody wrote does not warn, it just draws the missing texture. Nothing checks the two agree — there is no parity test for this pair — so the only protection is knowing they are a pair. CLAUDE.md carries the rule as well.

The reverse-direction trap is the item icon. There is no bare <id> model to point an item definition at, because a double gate is two blocks and neither is “the” block; both writers point at the left half, which is the one the template gives vanilla’s gate gui transform to.

Where it came from, and the style that is not here

Written from scratch. The mod that prompted it is Fabric-only, publishes no source, and is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — so there was nothing to port even had that been wanted, and a ShareAlike term would not have sat with this repo’s CC BY-NC 4.0 anyway. Only the idea is borrowed, which is not a thing copyright covers.

That listing also advertised “farm-style variant gates”, and a second style shipped on the strength of those four words: the same block with four rails instead of two. With no screenshot and no way to run the original, its appearance was a guess rather than a reproduction, and it was removed the same evening on the owner’s verdict — “what is the double farm gate? It looks weird”. Two gates whose difference nobody can justify is worse than one gate. Both the generator and DoubleGates say so in a comment, so the idea is not rediscovered and re-added on the same evidence.

Verified

Two vanilla fence gates really do craft one (2026-09-19, by test). Losing the braided rope took this recipe outside the modded-ingredient house rule, and that rule was the only guard against another mod claiming the same inputs — a collision Minecraft does not report, it just makes one of the two items uncraftable. horsegenetics:double_gate_crafts puts two oak fence gates in a CraftingInput and asks the server’s own recipe manager what comes out, so it tests the resolution a player gets rather than whether the JSON parsed. Checked by mutation: adding a stick to the recipe fails it at tick 0 with “two oak fence gates craft nothing”.

It covers oak only, and only the twelve hand-baked woods exist at test time — a modded wood’s gate is generated at run time and is still on the checklist.

Breaking a double gate gives back one gate, not two (2026-09-19, by test). The owner reported the duplication in play — “breaking a double fence drops two” — and horsegenetics:double_gate_drops_one is the tripwire that followed. It places a pair, breaks it with level.destroyBlock(pos, true), and asserts exactly one gate item within two blocks; then does it again striking the other half, because left is the half the loot table pays from and breaking only one of the two would pass with the condition on the wrong half. Deliberately not helper.destroyBlock, which passes dropBlock=false and would have passed however wrong the table was.

Checked by mutation, not trusted: reverting oak’s loot table to the unconditional one fails it at tick 0 with “Expected 1 items of type Oak Double Fence Gate: found 2” — the reported bug reproduced exactly — while the other six tests go on passing. The creative half of the fix is not covered by it: the test breaks blocks without a player, so playerWillDestroy never runs. Breaking either half in creative and getting nothing is still unseen.

Redstone moves the pair, not a half (2026-09-18, by test rather than by hand). horsegenetics:double_gate_redstone places both halves, powers a redstone block beside the left one only, and asserts that the right half reports open and powered. Vanilla's rule asks only about its own block, so a pair inheriting it unchanged would swing the powered half and leave the other shut — which is why the assertion is on the far half. Run it with ./gradlew :neoforge-26.1.2:runGameTest; it is headless and takes about sixteen seconds. Checked by mutation, not trusted: moving the redstone out of range fails it at tick 102 while the other test goes on passing.

Played 2026-09-17, and the pair renders correctly. Confirmed in a world by the owner across several woods: once both halves are down they look, in her words, “GREAT”, and the inventory icons are right.

Bamboo confirmed the same day, after the texture change — “bamboo gate now looks correct”. It had been sampling vanilla's bespoke block/bamboo_fence_gate sheet, which is drawn for vanilla's rail positions rather than these, and came out scrambled; it uses bamboo_planks like the other eleven now.

The pair survives being worked as fast as a player can click, confirmed the same day: “I can open it as fast as I want with no issues”. That is the check for the UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE change above — before it, opening from the wrong side flipped FACING between the two halves' writes, both blocks briefly read as the same half, and one deleted itself: “half broken, other half invisible but still changing the model of the fence post next to it”.

One-click placement confirmed the same day too — “placement works great” — after the partner was changed to take whichever side is free.

Still unseen: the item names, their placement in the Building Blocks tab, the in-wall variants, redstone on a pair, and the stall step ticking. See verification. A gate for a modded wood has never been drawn at all — not in a world, not in a slot, and not against a real third-party mod, only a synthetic test one (§0-FK).

What needs verifying here

Placement is the half that felt broken and every fix for it is unseen: each click with room must lay both halves at once, a refusal must leave nothing standing and say why while staying quiet on ordinary misclicks, and another mod’s wood must draw rather than a purple chequerboard this layer logs nothing about.

A gate in another mod's wood draws (2026-09-18, nothing drawn)

The mod reads every other mod's jar at startup and generates a double gate per wood they added, into a pack at .minecraft/phc/generated/. It works against a synthetic test mod on a dedicated server, where nothing draws — so none of the drawing below has ever been seen. It needs a mod that adds a wood installed in neoforge-26.1.2/run/mods/; the compatibility page's checklist covers picking one, the scan, the generated pack and the recipes.

  • A modded gate draws. Place one. Pass: it looks like the vanilla twelve do, in that mod's wood, both halves, open and shut and in a wall. Fail: a purple-and-black chequerboard — the signature silent failure of this layer, which logs nothing whatever, and which means bake-double-gates.mjs and compat/GeneratedGates have drifted apart. If it happens, say which is purple: the block, the held item, or the inventory icon, because those are three different files.
  • Its inventory icon draws, and its name is right. Pass: the left half's model in the slot, and “<Wood> Double Fence Gate” with the wood capitalised properly (a blue_oak reads “Blue Oak”). Fail: a raw translation key block.horsegenetics.... means the merged lang file did not land.

What the double gates still need looking at (2026-09-17, NOT played)

The double gates were played on the day they were built and the pair itself is confirmed — both halves down, several woods, “they look fine… once both are placed, they look GREAT”. Everything below was found by that session and fixed after it. Two of those fixes — the bamboo texture, and working the gate as fast as a player can click — were confirmed in the same sitting and now live on the gate's own page; nothing still listed here has been seen working. The first item is the one that matters, because it is the one that made the gates feel broken to place rather than merely wrong to look at.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • One click places both halves, wherever you are standing. Place gates along a wall, into a corner, and out in the open, facing each way in turn. Pass: every click that has room for two blocks places two blocks — never a click that does nothing followed by a second click on the neighbouring block. The partner now takes whichever side is free rather than always the same one.
  • The first half of a new pair is visible immediately. Place a gate on open ground and do not touch anything else. Pass: both blocks are drawn the instant it is placed, with no need to walk away and back or re-log. It used to delete itself client-side before its partner existed.
  • They are named. Open the Horse Genetics creative tab and hover them. Pass: “Oak Double Fence Gate”, “Oak Double Farm Gate” and so on for all twelve woods — not a raw item.horsegenetics.… key. BlockItem does not defer to its block's name in 26.1.2; the items now ask for the block prefix explicitly.

In the game, with your hands

  • A double gate crafts from two vanilla gates and nothing else. Put two oak fence gates in the grid. Pass: an Oak Double Fence Gate comes out, with no horse hair anywhere in the recipe. Then check a modded wood the same way if you have one installed — that recipe is written at run time by a separate generator and drifts silently.
  • Breaking one in creative gives you nothing. In creative, break a double gate — once striking each half, since they are not symmetric. Pass: both blocks go and no item drops either time. The survival case is covered by a test and drops exactly one; this half of that fix is playerWillDestroy, which the test cannot reach because it breaks blocks with no player. Before it, breaking the right half in creative gave you a free gate out of the partner's removal.
  • Placement refuses cleanly with no room, and says why. Try to place one into a one-block gap. Pass: nothing is placed, the item stays in your hand — never a single half left standing — and you are told “No room for a gate two blocks wide…”. Also check it stays quiet when it should: clicking the face of a solid block, or somewhere out of reach, must produce no message at all. The refusal is told apart from every ordinary placement failure by asking whether the clicked position itself could have been replaced, and a message on all of them would be noise.

Visual, and therefore last

  • The in-wall variants sit right. Put a double gate in a line of walls rather than fences, both styles. Pass: it drops to the lower position the way a vanilla gate does, on both halves, at every facing.
  • They are filed beside the vanilla gates. Open the vanilla Building Blocks tab. Pass: each double gate sits directly after the single gate of its own wood, all twelve of them, and they are still findable by searching too.

Two more things that session left open live on the pages they belong to: shutting a stall with a double gate is on the stall signs page, and the deleted hair chain on the horse hair page and the villagers page.
