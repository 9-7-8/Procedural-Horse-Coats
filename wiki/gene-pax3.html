Two loci, one pattern

SW1 sits on MITF and SW2 on PAX3. They are different genes on different chromosomes, they produce the same-looking pattern — which is exactly why they were long taken for one gene — and a horse carrying one copy of each has considerably more white than a horse carrying either alone.

In this model that falls out for free. Both genes paint, one after the other, and white finds white: each painter raises its own strength by how much of the horse is already de-pigmented. There is no interaction rule anywhere, and the measured result is 70% white for SW1/N + SW2/N against 45% and 42% for the two on their own.

Painted blindly, this would be wrong

Before the stacking rule, two waterlines drawn at roughly the same height came out at 45% versus 43.7% — the two-gene split would have been an architectural gesture with nothing visible behind it. Reading the coat is what makes the split mean something on the horse.

Deafness is not modelled

SW2/SW2 carries a real hearing risk in live horses. The mod has no hearing, and inventing a penalty the player cannot perceive would be flavour text pretending to be a mechanic. It is recorded in the outcome’s description instead — which is where the gene dictionary will read it from when that lands.

The common one

Nine wild horses in ten carry one copy of SW2, which makes this the only locus in the mod whose variant is the ordinary horse rather than the exception. That is deliberate, and it is how minimal splash works in life: a mild splash allele is near-ubiquitous, and what one copy buys a horse — clean-edged leg white, a little belly white, a blaze — is roughly what most horses look like. The pattern people notice is the doubled one.

Why the table is written out

Two reasons, and either one on its own rules out FounderTable.hardyWeinberg:

  • 90% heterozygous is arithmetically unreachable under Hardy-Weinberg. The heterozygote share is 2pq, which peaks at 50% at p = q = 0.5. No allele frequency anywhere makes nine horses in ten heterozygous in a randomly-mating population. A table that says so has to say so directly.
  • The doubled combinations are the reward for breeding. SW2/SW2 is the bold outcome and must not turn up in a wild-caught horse — the same rule the health loci and magic body size follow. Left to Hardy-Weinberg, one copy on nine horses in ten would have made eighty-one per cent of wild horses bold splash, which is not a pattern any more; it is the base coat.

Both consequences are the point rather than side effects. Bold splash becomes the commonest thing a player breeds by accident and the first white pattern they meet. And because both splash loci read the coat they are handed (white finds white), a horse that is also MITF splash is now the usual case rather than a rarity — which is the interaction the two-locus split exists to show.

The face

The same shared the shared face-marking vocabulary that MITF uses, at the same jag of 0.11 and at this locus’s own strengths. That is the point of it being shared: a horse carrying one copy at each splash locus draws two markings from one vocabulary, and they compound the way the body white does, instead of two hand-written blazes landing on top of each other and reading as one.

Deafness

A homozygote at this locus (no N copy) is deaf, for the same reason as at MITF: the pigment cells that never reached the coat never reached the inner ear either. The description on this page used to say deafness was “described, not modelled”. It is modelled now — as a named, INFORMATIONAL condition that costs the horse nothing, because the mod has no hearing to take away.

It is the same Condition object MITF declares, so a horse homozygous at both loci is told once. See the horse’s body.
