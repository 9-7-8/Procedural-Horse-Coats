Tiger eye (SLC24A5)

A bright amber iris in a horse whose coat is entirely ordinary. It is the first gene in the mod that changes only the eyes, and the reason the eye-colour channel exists at all. Essentially confined to the Puerto Rican Paso Fino, in life and here.

The iris colour is asserted through the real pipeline in EyeColorTest — a bay's black iris comes out #C8811E and its sclera stays white. What is unknown is whether a two-texel amber iris reads as an amber eye on a horse at riding distance, or whether it needs to be brighter. Checklist on To be verified.

The combination table

CombinationOutcomeEffect
N/NwildOrdinary dark eyes

TE2/TE2tiger-eye-yellowThe paler, more yellow shade, #D6B341

Recessive: one wild-type copy and nothing shows, so two carriers bred together is the only way amber eyes appear. The two variants are the same trait a stop apart in shade — which is what a combination table is for, and cheaper than inventing two genes.

It is not a coat pattern

Worth saying, because the breed reference this mod was built from files tiger eye under coat patterns and it is not one. The coat is completely unaffected; only the iris changes. That is precisely why it could not be built until the eye-colour channel existed — there was nowhere for it to write.

It does still affect the baked coat texture, because the eyes are drawn into it. So unlike the mod's other "paints nothing" genes, its expressing outcomes are real markers rather than wild types: they stay in Genotype.coatCode(), and two horses that differ only here do not share a cached texture. They are deterministic — every amber eye is the same amber — so the cache pays one entry per outcome and not one per horse.

The eye-colour channel

A horse has one iris colour, so "what colour are its eyes" has exactly one answer and several genes have a claim on it. That makes it a channel with a single owner and a contribution hook, like the LUT and the cutie mark — not a gene that other genes fight with. A gene implements EyeColorContribution and returns an EyeColor claim; the composer collects them in code order during the overlay phase and applies the highest-ranked one once.

RankClaimantsWhat kind of claim
20 — depigmentedMITF, PAX3, EDNRB, KIT (broad white and up)The white loci take the pigment out of the iris the same way they take it out of the coat — the blue eye those patterns are known for
10 — pigmentTiger eyeChanges what colour the iris is

Blue beats amber, and it has to: a depigmented iris has no pigment left for a pigment gene to recolour. So a splashed white horse that also carries tiger eye has blue eyes. This is the one ordering the ranking exists to settle.

Note the blue eye is not a new gene and never should be. It is a property the white loci already have — the same failure of pigment cells to reach the skin leaves them out of the iris — and a "blue eyes" locus that mysteriously only ever appeared on white horses would be a worse model of the same fact. See below for who qualifies.

The iris is the dark texels, not the sclera

On the coat sheet an adult eye is a 2×2 block of pure black — the iris and pupil together, there being no room for both at 128px — beside a 2×2 block of near-white sclera. A foal's is the black block alone. So eye colour means colouring the black texels and leaving the white ones white, which is what CoatOverlay.tintIris does: it weights each texel by how dark it already is (1 - luma), so black takes the colour outright, white is untouched, and the antialiased grey between them takes a share, which keeps the eye's shape.

The obvious tool is the wrong one

shadeToward scales its target by the texel's own brightness, so that a gold hoof keeps the template's shading. Pointed at an eye it colours the sclera and leaves the iris black — exactly backwards. It is the right blend for light's glowing gold eye and the leopard complex's white sclera rim, and it must not be used for an iris.

All of this runs in the overlay phase, after CoatRegions.redrawEyes has copied the template's eyes back — which is the only point in the bake where an eye survives being written, and is exactly why that phase exists.

Who gets a blue eye

Two ways a horse qualifies, and it needs only one:

  • Its own allele says so. Splash is the blue-eyed pattern, diagnostic even when the white itself is modest, so any expressing MITF or PAX3 combination qualifies. Frame does too. KIT qualifies only from broad-white upward — a sabino with four socks and a blaze has ordinary dark eyes, because the white on it never reached the head.
  • The horse is broadly white however it got there. Past 55% of the coat depigmented, the eyes go with it. This covers the case no per-locus test can see: a horse white from two mild alleles stacking has exactly the same claim as one white from a single bold allele.

Where to find it

SLC24A5 is a Puerto Rican Paso Fino gene, essentially confined to that breed in life, and it is distributed that way here: a quarter of Paso Finos carry a copy and about one in twenty-five has the amber eyes, against roughly one wild horse in five thousand. It is the clearest case in the mod of a gene you find by knowing which breed to look in — and since the breed is RARE and spawns in jungle, savanna, beach and mangrove swamp, finding one is a trip rather than a wait.
