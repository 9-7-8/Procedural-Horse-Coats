Natural gene / phase 1

Dun

Real-horse TBX3, and the mod’s second three-allele locus. Two effects that come apart: a mild, hue-keeping body dilution, and primitive markings — a full-length dorsal stripe, plus faint leg barring — that read dark because everything around them got lighter. The third allele is the one that carries the markings without the dilution.

Two dominance orders over one locus

This is the gene that most obviously needs a table rather than a label. Dilution reads D > d1 = d2; marking reads D = d1 > d2. Two orders over the same three alleles, and no single word covers both — which is exactly why there is no dominance property in this model, only an outcome per combination.

So d2 is the only allele that draws nothing, and it — not the old catch-all d — is the gene’s default allele: the token a code with no dun segment reads as. A horse can now carry a dorsal stripe and not be a dun, which is what d1 is for.

How the markings survive the dilution

Phase 1 is downward-only — a gene can never add pigment back. So dun computes, per texel, a marking strength and lerps its dilution off where a marking is: a dorsal-stripe texel skips the dilution entirely and keeps the base colour, a leg-bar texel keeps extra black only.

keepRed is not a constant. A black horse still carries red = 1 (its blackness is all eumelanin), so keeping any of that red lands grullo in the gradient’s warm browns instead of on its neutral column. So keepRed is interpolated by the texel’s black content: a chestnut keeps almost all its red and comes out a pale red dun, a black one loses nearly all of it and comes out a mouse-grey grullo, a bay body sits between. A leg bar keeps black rather than re-introducing red, or a bar on a black leg reads as a warm patch, not a dark band.

common/genetics/genes/DunGene.java
float keepRed   = lerp(keepRedChestnut, keepRedBlack, b);   // b = this texel's black
float keepBlack = keepBlackBody;

float dorsal = (float) CoatRegions.dorsalStripe(skin, part, point, DORSAL_HALF_WIDTH);
keepRed   = lerp(keepRed,   1f, dorsal);   // dorsal: skip the dilution, keep the base
keepBlack = lerp(keepBlack, 1f, dorsal);

if (legBars && isLeg(part)) {
    float bar = (float) CoatRegions.legBar(skin, part, point, BAR_SPACING, BAR_DUTY, BAR_REACH);
    keepBlack = Math.max(keepBlack, lerp(keepBlackBody, BAR_KEEP_BLACK, bar));   // black only
}
f.setRed(px, py, r * keepRed);
f.setBlack(px, py, b * keepBlack);

d1: a marking with no dilution

A non-dun horse is by definition undiluted, so d1 cannot paint a dark line on it — phase 1 has no way to add pigment. It does not need to. A primitive marking is countershading, and “a stripe darker than the body” and “a body lighter than the stripe” are the same picture. So both marked outcomes run the same painter, differing only in their constants, and in both cases the marking is simply the region the dilution is lerped back off.

d1’s two constants are chosen to move as little as possible:

  • Black is never touched (keepBlack = 1). The whole black = 1 row of the gradient is pure black, and the composer gives a texel resolving to pure black 80% opacity (PURE_BLACK_ALPHA). Nudging a black texel off that row therefore makes it fully opaque — and so darker. A body darker than its own dorsal stripe is worse than no stripe.
  • Red is taken only where there is red to take. d1’s keepRed runs up to 1 as the texel’s black rises — the mirror of D’s ramp down to 0. A chestnut lightens a little, a bay body less, a true black or a bay’s points not at all.
A no-op is the right answer here

A d1 horse on a solid black base is composed byte-identically to a plain black one — there is no red to take and black is untouched. That is not a gap: a real non-dun black shows no visible primitive markings either. The marking appears on the bases that have red to give up — chestnut, bay, and everything downstream of them. Founder frequencies

The six weights are written out rather than computed, so one row can be retuned without disturbing the others. They are Hardy-Weinberg at p(D) = 1/24 and p(d1) = 1/10. The three D rows still sum to 8.16% — exactly what the old two-allele table gave — so adding d1 split the non-dun population rather than making duns rarer.

Still simplified

Leg barring is a hand-rolled Y-phase rather than a reuse of BodyStripes (which runs on X) — a roadmap item. Real d1 horses can show faint leg bars and shoulder shadowing as well as the dorsal stripe; here the bars belong to D alone, since at d1’s strength a bar on a mostly-black leg would have nothing to work with anyway.
