Releases

What shipped in each tagged version, newest first. This is the player-facing record — the reasoning behind each change is in the session log, and what is known to work is in to be verified. A release is a build that ran green, not a build that has been played.

Read this first

Every release so far is a development release. The suite is green and the jar assembles, but only a small fraction of what is built has been confirmed in a play session — to be verified is the authority on which is which. Treat a world you care about accordingly.

The numbering, as of 2026-09-10

The next release is 0.5.000, and after that only the last number moves — 0.5.001, 0.5.002, and on up. It is a three-digit field on purpose: there is a lot of counting to do before anything else changes.

The middle number does not move without the owner saying so, explicitly and out loud. Not for a big feature, not for a batch of genes, not because the last number is getting long. That is the whole rule and it is the owner's call, not a judgement to make from how much shipped. The versions ran 0.2.0 to 0.4.1 in two days, which is what the rule is a reaction to.

The version itself lives in one place, gradle.properties' mod_version, and a tagged release has outside testers on it — so a number is never reused, and never reissued with different bytes.

0.5.026 — insurance you can leave at home, a braid that takes a horse home, and horses that draw where they are going

An empty emergency stasis chamber filed in a stasis bank now insures your horses, exactly as one in your pocket does. A rack of spare empties in the cabinet at home is worth having, and the day you ride out having forgotten to take one is not the day your insurance lapses. The carried one is still spent first; if it is full and a bank has an empty, the bank's is used rather than the horse being refused. A bank is one you placed — the block quietly remembers, and claims an unclaimed one the first time somebody opens it, which is all you need do to a bank you built before this.

A rescue is now a row in the H screen's Log tab as well as a chat line, under a Rescues filter of its own. A chamber fires with nobody watching by design, which is most of what you pay eight ender pearls for — so the alarm that scrolls past should not have been the only record of it. And a horse in a chamber now says “in a stasis chamber” where the browser used to say not loaded for ever; stasis is a word the filter box finds horses by. If you are riding when a chamber fires, you are set down a tick first and the horse goes from under you on the next one, rather than both inside the same instant.

The rescuing braid

A new item, and the first thing in the game that goes in a horse's mane or tail. Craft a rescuing braid from any wool, a horse hair and an ender pearl, work it into a horse's hair, and forget about it. It does nothing until the day something is about to kill that horse, and then it breaks: the blow is refused and the horse — with you on it, if you were riding — is standing in its own stall, bolting.

  • It rides on top of the last stand rather than replacing it. The free save holds the horse on half a heart with its totem particles, and the braid then takes that horse home inside the immunity window. A horse teleported out of a fight and then killed by the first thing it lands beside has been moved rather than saved.
  • It works out where home is when it breaks, not when you make it: the horse's own stall, or your holding pen. So a braid in a chest works for whichever horse you put it on and can never be pointing at a stall you took down last week. A horse with neither gets one line saying so, and the braid is not spent.
  • A horse has two hair slots, and that is two saves. The mane goes first.
  • Dyed like leather armour — any dye in a crafting grid, mixed dyes, washed out in a cauldron — and drawn as a plait in that colour, worked into the mane or the tail.
  • The emergency chamber now runs after it, as the net behind both. Nothing is wasted: a horse the braid has sent home takes no further blows, so the bottle you did not spend is still in your pocket.

Also in this release

  • Every horse you own draws a line to where it is walking, and the browser grew a Send home button that spends a ticket for you — the H screen has it.
  • A cover can no longer happen through a wall. Covers turned out never to have had a wall check at all; a barn's walls had been doing the job by accident until the crowding cap went up.
  • The stasis chamber recipes became visible. All five were present, registered and working, and nothing in the game — not the recipe book, not JEI — would show anybody what they were. The three upgrades are three recipes now, one per rung. If you are on a modded server and could not craft a chamber, this is that.
  • Shift-right-clicking a horse opens this mod's screen, which it had never once done in any world.
None of the new work has been played

The bank's insurance, the braid in every one of its parts, the tick you are set down for, and the whereabouts column all compile, pass their tests and load on a dedicated server — and none of them has been watched happen. The braid's streak on the horse and its item icon were generated rather than drawn and nobody has looked at either. What to check, and in what order, is on the braid's and the stasis page's Verification tabs.

0.5.025 — a paper names a pair, the bank holds a stud farm, and your lead comes back

A research paper now names an allele pair, not just a gene — which is the half that was missing for the Known Gene Splice carrot to be worth crafting. A whole-gene paper could only ever build one pair, the first allele the locus declared, so on the forty-allele particle locus no carrot in the game could hand over a soul flame, and KIT could reach one of thirteen. Now the paper carries the pair and the carrot grants that pair. A paper read off a horse writes down what the animal actually carries, which is the only route to a compound pair; chests and the equestrian give carrier and true-breeding pairs, deliberately not all eight hundred combinations of a wide locus. The research shelf files one paper per pair, so A/a and A/A sit side by side — they craft different carrots. Research papers has the whole of it.

Your existing papers still work

The owner asked for this by name, and it is the one deliberate exception to this project’s no-back-compat rule. A paper written before this release decodes as the homozygous pair for the gene it named (or the carrier pair, on a locus whose homozygote is forbidden) rather than turning inert or vanishing. Worth hovering a few old papers before doing anything else, though — only a live save that predates the change can prove it, and the test suite proves the codec, not your world.

The Horse Stasis Bank holds two hundred and seven chambers, up from fifty-four. Six rows are on screen and the grid scrolls on the wheel, with a bar down the right-hand edge saying where in the cabinet you are; the window is the same size it was. Worth knowing what that does not change: the bank still mends one horse per turn in rotation, so a full one takes the better part of five minutes to come back round to any particular horse. That is the price of a bank costing the server the same whether it holds one chamber or all of them — a horse that actually needs mending mends faster in a field.

Three smaller things, each one a report

  • The area whistles have a tooltip at last. Three tiers, one icon, three names, and nothing anywhere said how far any of them reached — the recipe blurbs said “further than the basic one” without ever saying how far, so the only way to learn a range was to walk out and count. The radius is on the item now, along with the two outcomes players kept reporting as bugs: a ridden horse stays put, and a leashed one is untied.
  • The browser’s My horses list refreshes. It was fetched once per login and never again, so a player who bred a foal, tamed a horse or sold one saw their stable as it was when they first pressed H — until they happened to find the Refresh button. Opening the screen and switching to the tab both re-ask now, throttled to one sweep every two seconds so that flipping through the tab strip cannot make the server walk the whole ancestry table per click. The Refresh button itself is deliberately not throttled: it exists to be believed.
  • A lead comes back to you instead of being left behind. Every path that teleports a horse has to untie it first, and vanilla drops the lead where the horse was standing — right for a leash that snapped, wrong for one the mod cut. The horse lands beside you and the lead stays put: sixty-four blocks away for an echo whistle, and in another dimension for an ender whistle or an interdimensional ticket, where it is simply gone. The lead is handed to whoever caused the move now, falling back to a drop if their pack is full. The two portal paths are deliberately left alone — a horse that walked into a portal was untied next to whoever held it, so the lead is already at their feet.
One new setting

behaviour.leads_return in server.toml, default on. Turning it off restores vanilla’s behaviour exactly — the lead drops where the horse was.

Not played. A dedicated server boots clean — Done, and not one ERROR line — and the build is green. The stasis bank’s new depth is the untested surface here, and the check worth doing deliberately is the dull one: scroll to the bottom of the grid, put a chamber in a slot down there, close the bank and reopen it, and confirm it is in the same slot. Everything else about the deeper grid is cosmetic if that holds and expensive if it does not. There is also a question this release makes bigger rather than smaller: a bank sends every chamber’s whole horse to the client when you open it, and nobody has yet opened a full one over a real network connection — at two hundred chambers that may be slow, or it may be a refusal to open at all. The bank’s own check list has both. Known bugs carry over unchanged from 0.5.023 below.

0.5.024 — horses on a shelf, and a log of what happened while you were out

A horse can now be put away as an item. A stasis chamber catches one whole — its name, its bond, its tack, its pedigree, the foal it is carrying, down to the identity every stall sign and transfer deed keys on — and hands it back the same horse. A Horse Stasis Bank holds fifty-four of them, and depending on the chamber it will search them, feed and water them, collect what they produce, and breed them without a horse ever setting foot on the ground. Full page: horse stasis.

And the H menu keeps a log. Every cover that went through and every one that did not, every foal, death, taming, purchase, sale and transfer — newest first, filterable by kind. The point of it is the events nobody was there for: chat reaches whoever is standing in the paddock, once, and then scrolls away. A foal born while you were logged out is a row waiting for you when you come back.

Horse stasis, in full

  • Four chambers, a ladder of them. Basic holds a horse and nothing more — the bank cannot look inside. Intermediate can be searched and healed. Advanced also has its drops collected. Spacer can be bred in the bank. An occupied chamber upgrades in place without letting go of the horse.
  • The bank is a shelf, not a machine. Fifty-four slots, chambers only, full or empty. It drops everything when broken, it does not burn, and hoppers cannot reach the chamber grid — filing a live animal automatically is not a thing anybody asked for.
  • A Browse tab that reads as horses. Every occupied chamber as a line — name, what it is, breed, generation, tier — over a filter box that takes the Horse Browser’s own query language. A Basic chamber still shows its horse’s name but no query can reach it, and the list counts the rows a filter could not see, so a horse never quietly goes missing from a search.
  • Feed, water, and slow healing. Three supply slots and a ticker that mends the horses on the shelf under exactly the rule a live horse heals by — hurt, a diet it can eat, water, hunger as the currency. The supply slots are pipeable, so a hopper can keep them topped up and carry the empty buckets away.
  • A drop buffer. Nine slots, filled by the same production a live horse is read from. Nothing is produced until there is room for it, so a full buffer pauses rather than spilling. Milk is not in it and cannot be — milking is something you do to a horse, not something it does.
  • Breeding in the bank. Click a Browse row to put that horse to stud. A marked bank is a paddock, on the same heat cycle as on land — but the mare comes out to foal, because birth is a dozen facts about the dam and there is only one birth path in the mod. With nowhere to stand beside the block she stays in her chamber and the Supply tab says so.
  • The Emergency chamber: insurance you buy once. A Basic chamber surrounded by eight ender pearls. Carry it, and the instant any horse you own drops below a tenth of its health it is caught into the chamber, wherever the two of you are — and a horse inside a chamber cannot be hurt at all, so the catch is the save. One chamber is one life; a second horse crossing the line is refused out loud, naming the horse it could not save. Let the first one out and the chamber comes back armed. The threshold sits below the one a horse bolts at, so it always gets its own chance to run first.

What else a player gets

  • Bond fades. About a point a Minecraft day, down to a floor of 31 — the bottom of the first behaviour tier. Neglect can take back the tiers where a horse walks to you and steers bareback; it can never take back the one where it lifts its head as you walk past. A horse you raised from a foal does not become a stranger. Both numbers are settings and 0 turns it off.
  • The crowding cap is your number now, and it was far too low. A mare is not covered while too many horses stand within sixteen blocks of her, and that count was a hard-coded eight — which is a barn and its neighbours, not eight brood mares. On a busy server an ordinary stable was over the cap permanently and said so. It is fertility.nearby_horse_cap now, default 50.
  • The Enderpony. A bad little horse worth catching, because it lays ender pearls. Black under a dark shade, both irises chaos-banded into a purple box, portal motes off the spine or hooves, and an ender-echo teleport when it is hurt. Five marking strains — stars, wormholes, crescents, duskwing bands, polymoon — every one of them amethyst to violet and never pink, blue or green. Slow, flat over a fence, miserable in the rain, very rare, and from no country at all. The breed book has it.
  • Ender pearls join the egg layer. A ninth allele on horsegenetics.egg_layer, in the ordinary wild spread — so the Enderpony is the reliable source, not the only one.
  • The Cloudtouched is one step commoner — very rare to rare, so its wild herds should be founded about twice as often. Nothing else about the breed moves.
  • A filly may take her own brother. The last kinship rule in the mod is gone: a dispersing filly no longer refuses a suitor sharing her dam or sire. Nothing in this mod stops line breeding, in a wild band as much as in a paddock.
Two things were taken out

The exploding allele is gone. Magic on death is now lava, water and nothing. An explosion whose carrier looks like every other horse is a hazard the breeder’s neighbour takes without being asked. A saved horse that carried it loads at the plain allele; a splice carrot naming it is inert.

Lycanthropy is ground-dwellers only. Fifteen shapes went — the fish, the squids, the axolotl, the tadpole, the bat, the bee, the parrot, the allay, the happy ghast. A horse on this locus does not stand beside the animal, it becomes it, and those were bodies its owner could not get back: drowning in air on a hillside, or over the treeline two seconds after dusk. A companion squid is still fine; a horse inside a squid is not.

Four new settings

All in server.toml: fertility.nearby_horse_cap (50, 1 to 1,000); behaviour.bond_decay_per_day (1, and 0 restores bond that only ever goes up); behaviour.bond_floor (31); and behaviour.emergency_stasis_fraction (0.1, and 0 turns the rescue off while leaving the chambers craftable and usable by hand).

Not played. A dedicated server boots clean — Done, and not one ERROR or FATAL line — the gametests pass, and the creator parity check is green across 7,163 checks. None of it has been in front of a player, and this is a bigger untested surface than usual: an entire storage feature built in a day. To be verified is the honest list. The three worth doing deliberately are the round trip (shelve a horse with a bond, a name, tack and a pregnancy, relog, let it out), putting a mare to stud in the bank, and converting an occupied Basic chamber with the pearls — the one failure on that list that would be silent and permanent. Known bugs carry over unchanged from 0.5.023 below.

0.5.023 — the mod tells you what happened, and a horse stops dying quietly

This is the release that came out of playing on a real server. Nearly everything in it is a report from a modded survival world rather than a plan: horses leaning on a fence forever, a paddock that had silently stopped breeding, a barn-name box nobody could see, a foal called the same thing as its three sisters. Two days of those, one at a time.

Your horses now talk to you in chat. When one is hurt you are told what hurt it and what to do about that; when one dies the server is told whose it was and what killed it; and when a mare is not covered, her owner is told why — the crowding cap with both numbers, a hurt or ridden or led mare, and whether a cover took. All three are settings, all three default on.

And a horse is much harder to lose. A blow that would take one to zero takes it to half a heart instead, with six seconds of real immunity to get clear — then the save is spent until the horse has healed all the way back. Under a fifth health a horse takes itself over and runs, carrying its rider out rather than throwing them, faster and jumping higher than anyone could steer it. Full page: horse care.

What a player gets

  • Three kinds of chat notice. Hurt: names the horse, the cause, and the prevention — shade for one burning in the sun, a wall for one being blown up — plus the cure once it is down to a quarter health. Only the owner, only in the same world, never when you are the one swinging, at most one line per horse per five seconds. Death: one grey line for everybody, red in the owner’s own chat. Breeding: why she was not covered, once every five minutes per reason.
  • Last stand. Nothing kills a horse in one blow while behaviour.last_stand is on. It never applies to a genetic defect — a lethal foal still dies — nor to /kill or the void.
  • A bolting horse. At or below a fifth of its own maximum it drops any fight, stops answering the reins, and runs from whatever hurt it, jumping a fence when the way out needs one. The jump boost is sized so the weakest jumper clears a fence, so this is a horse escaping rather than a good horse escaping.
  • Horses stop shoving the fence. A hungry horse now checks it can actually walk to food before setting off, and falls back to the grass under its feet when the farm across the rail is unreachable. It also no longer eats a crop through a fence: it walks the way round it already proved exists.
  • A dropped carrot is food. Anything the horse would walk over to eat counts when it is lying on the ground too — crops, fruit, vegetables, berries, mushrooms — taking another mod’s produce with it by tag. A narrow diet is untouched.
  • A bonded horse has to be able to reach you. Staring through the rails no longer counts as following, and no longer locks the horse out of sparring, grooming and heat attraction while it does it.
  • A family overview: every horse in the world on one chart. One row per generation, founders on top, a line from each horse down to every foal it got. Drag, scroll to zoom, Fit to reset, and a click isolates one horse with its foals and whoever it had them by. Reached from the family tree’s Everyone button.
  • A horse you sold is greyed out, not gone. Any box on the tree owned by someone else draws dim and reads “sold — by X”. Still clickable, still in place.
  • The barn-name box is visible again. It had been hidden from every player, its owner included, since it was added on 2026-09-07 — the screen was asking the entity who owned the horse, which is a question only the server can answer.
  • E from the saddle opens this mod’s menu rather than vanilla’s horse inventory, and closes it again. A donkey, a mule or a horse with no record is untouched. Every other horse named on a screen is a link — companions, the rival, descendants — so you can walk a pedigree by clicking it.
  • Search by an allele pair. genotype:E/e on My horses is the question gene: could not ask: carriers only, not every horse with a red copy. Case is now significant in an allele token and nowhere else, which is what had been quietly making gene:e match every horse alive.
  • The breeding pickers filter and sort like My horses does — one box for both lists, since a mating has two sides — and the heading says “(4 of 19)” while a filter is on.
  • A stallion attempts every mare in heat he is left with. Three covers a day was a hard stop on the natural path, so the fourth mare in a paddock saw no stallion at all and nothing was said about it. It is a taper now: past three his odds halve, and a mare prefers a rested stallion over a tired one at any distance within reach.
  • Foal names stop colliding. A foal keeps one half of its name from a parent and rolls the other, always — by default the surname, down the same-sex line, so a filly carries her dam’s and a colt his sire’s. Each player sets their own rule in client.toml; nobody’s reaches another player’s herd.
  • Tack in chests, and far more of the rest. Golden carrot seeds went from 7% over eight named vanilla tables to 20% over every chest table any mod adds; research papers 18% to 30% the same way. New: a piece of horse tack per hit — saddles and leather armour dyed through this mod’s own three-zone tint, fittings from workaday metals and never gems.
  • The cowboy’s string defends him. Killing a cowboy clears the brand off his herd, and it used to cost nothing while six horses watched. They rally on whoever hit him now — and stand down the moment he falls, rather than chasing the player who came to free them.
Ten new settings

In server.toml: notices.owned_horse_damage, notices.owned_horse_death and notices.owned_horse_breeding (all true); behaviour.escape_health_fraction (0.2, and 0 turns bolting off); behaviour.last_stand (true), behaviour.last_stand_health (1.0), behaviour.last_stand_immunity_ticks (120) and behaviour.last_stand_rearm_fraction (1.0) — setting behaviour.last_stand = false restores vanilla death exactly.

In client.toml: naming.inheritedHalf (LAST) and naming.parentSource (BY_SEX), which are yours alone and are suggested to the server on login so a foal born while you are offline still follows your rule.

Not played. A dedicated server boots clean — Done, and not one ERROR or FATAL line in the log — and the creator parity check is green. None of the above has been in front of a player. To be verified is the honest list, and it is long after two days of this: the notices want two players on a dedicated server to prove “only the owner”, the last stand and the bolt want a horse taken to a sliver deliberately, and the feeding fixes want the pasture that reported them. Known bugs carry over unchanged from 0.5.022 below.

0.5.022 — the Gear tab, and the menu goes back to sneak

A horse now has nineteen tack slots, on a Gear tab of its own. They are laid out on a portrait of the horse rather than in a row: headstall, mask, mane, tail, chest, pad, saddle, barding, blanket, two saddlebags, four boots and four shoes. Two of them work today — the saddle and the barding, which is what a horse could wear before. The other seventeen accept nothing and say so on the slot, because the gear that fills them has not been made yet and a slot that quietly refuses reads as broken rather than unfinished. Full page: horse gear.

And right-click puts you in the saddle again. 0.5.021 gave the plain click to this mod's information screen; that was wrong, and it is reverted. The screen is on sneak and right-click now — empty-handed, on any horse, wild or tamed, yours or not.

What a player gets

  • The Gear tab. Hold a saddle or a set of barding and click its slot; click it empty-handed to take it back. Seventeen slots wait on gear that does not exist yet — what each one will accept is an item tag named for it, so a piece added later joins by being tagged, with no new screen.
  • Right-click mounts, as it always did. No window, no surprise.
  • Sneak-right-click with an empty hand opens the horse menu — on a tamed horse too, which is new. That gesture used to open vanilla's inventory, and everything it held for a horse is on this screen: the saddle and the barding are two of the Gear slots, and a horse has no chest. Vanilla's screen is still there on E while you are riding, with the i button on it to come back. A donkey or a mule, which does have a chest, is untouched — this mod keeps no record for one, so the click goes straight through to vanilla.
  • horseScreen.rightClickOpens is gone. There is one rule now. If you set it to false in client.toml on 0.5.021, the line is simply ignored; you already have what it was asking for.
  • Ride stays at the top right of Overview. It is no longer the only way into the saddle — it is the one for when you have just read a wild horse and decided you want it.

Not played. A dedicated server boots clean — Done, and not one ERROR line in the log. No client has opened the Gear tab, and nothing above has been in front of a player: the menu's checklist starts with the two clicks that settle the gesture, and the gear tab's starts with the saddle still going on and coming off through its new write path. Known bugs carry over unchanged from 0.5.021 below.

0.5.021 — advancements, and the horse menu on a click

The Getting Started checklist is now a hundred and three real advancements. Every box the book ticks earns one: a toast, a line in chat, a card in a Horse Genetics tab of the advancement screen, and a name /advancement can use. The mod’s own green chat line is gone — vanilla announces it now, and vanilla can be told not to. Full page: the checklist.

And a plain right-click on any horse opens this mod’s screen. No sneaking, wild or tamed, yours or not. That screen has grown a Ride button and the horse’s saddle and armour, so the click it took over has somewhere to go.

What a player gets

  • A tab in the advancement screen. A root, the eight chapters, and each chapter’s tasks under it. A chapter lights up as soon as you do anything in it rather than only when you have finished it, so the tree fills in as you wander about.
  • Challenges and goals. Identical twins, a mutation out of nowhere, a tamed dhampir, steering a horse with no tack: purple frames, with the fanfare.
  • Right-click any horse with an empty hand to read it — wild founders and a cowboy’s string included, which used to take a sneak, and your own tamed horses, which used to take two clicks through the vanilla inventory. Anything in your hand still does what it always did.
  • Ride, top right of Overview. It is how you get on now — and how you tame a wild one, by being thrown off it until you are not.
  • Saddle and barding on Overview. Hold the tack, click the slot; click it empty-handed to take it back. Two slots today, and the row is a list on purpose — a bridle or a blanket later is an entry in it rather than a new screen.
  • Prefer the old way? horseScreen.rightClickOpens = false in client.toml puts right-click back to mounting. Sneak-and-use is untouched either way, so vanilla’s horse inventory is always one gesture away.
Your first login will pop a lot of toasts

A world whose boxes were already ticked awards every one of those advancements at once, the first time you log in on this version. It happens once. Without that catch-up they could never be earned at all, because a task fires exactly once ever.

0.5.020 — the jumps rework

Twelve blocks and thirty-six items became one block and one item. Everything that used to be an item — the wood, the style — is now a control in the jump’s own screen, which means every style added from here is a button rather than a thirty-seventh item. The full page is jumps.

What a player gets

  • A screen on every jump. Right-click it. Two slots for the wood of the rails and the wood of the uprights, separately; a dye for either half; three styles; and a height.
  • Plank swaps are reversible, paint is not. A plank hands the displaced one back. A dye is spent for good, and the only way to strip paint is a fresh plank in that half — which pops the old plank out and takes the paint with it.
  • Ten heights, from 0.1 (a ground pole you walk over) to 2.0 (double), set for the whole fence. A jump is height + ⅛ blocks to clear, so everything under a block is walked over and everything from a block up has to be jumped. Stacking still works on top.
  • Crossrails are one crossed pair up to three blocks wide. A row is one wide X, not a row of small ones; a run of five gives a three and a two, and longer runs grow an upright where two X’s meet.
  • Breed spawn eggs have their own creative tab. The mod’s three now read Horse Genetics, Horse Breeds, Horse Carts.

Also in this range, from earlier the same day: pressing jump launches at full height with a cooldown, rather than charging a meter — so how high a horse jumps is a fact about the horse rather than about the rider’s thumb. Gliding still works. That change is also what surfaced gap 287.

Known bugs, open right now

Shipped knowingly. Known gaps has the full list; this is the subset that is an actual defect.

  • New: a painted jump run shows a visible seam at every block join (gap 288). Cosmetic, painted runs only. The suspect is the paint overlay inflating along the rail axis and doubling its own alpha where two blocks meet.
  • The Anglo-Arabian — a natural breed — jumps high enough to hurt itself landing (gap 287). Nothing is being changed until the breed sheets are audited.
  • Everything carried over from 0.5.019 below, which this release did not touch.
Four API findings, verified rather than remembered
  • BakedModel and IDynamicBakedModel do not exist; a model is a list of parts, so N woods across two halves costs N+N rather than N×N. API notes.
  • Element rotation is no longer limited to ±22.5° and ±45° — the angle is an unvalidated float, and there is a new multi-axis form. That limit is what had kept crossrails at one block wide, and the comment asserting it was wrong.
  • A ContainerData value is truncated to 16 bits, so an RGB colour cannot travel through one. The jump’s screen carries the block’s position and reads the block directly instead.
  • A block tint is a multiply, so it can only darken and cannot introduce a hue the texture lacks. Paint is a translucent overlay part rather than a tint on the wood — see jumps § paint.

Not built, deliberately: standards made of any solid block — cobblestone, brick, glass. Scoped and parked on the roadmap at the owner’s call, as not needed for a first release.

Everything above has been looked at in game except the height ladder, which is built and boots clean but has not been measured against a horse. Verification is the honest list.

0.5.019

Known bugs, open right now

Not fixed in this release. Design gaps, unbuilt work and unverified claims live on known gaps in full — this is only the subset that is an actual defect: wrong behaviour, a duplication risk, or something that silently does not work.

Duplication / exploit risk:

  • The bareback-steering “phantom saddle” is a real, working saddle item. The only thing stopping it escaping into a permanent, invisible-textured free saddle — a hopper, a dispenser, a death drop, another mod's inventory hook, a second player — is a per-tick sweep, and none of those other paths has been checked.
  • A horse's cowboy-brand is erased if the horse and its cowboy ever load in different chunks, which is a standing way to deliberately unbrand a horse.

Broken or misfiring mechanics:

  • The reaper cart destroys immature crops for one seed each instead of only ripe ones — contradicts its own “harvests mature crops” tooltip.
  • The supply cart's open-from-the-seat packet is wired up server-side and never sent — nothing lets you open one without dismounting.
  • Ringwork's two signature rings paint nothing at all, and its other two layers are inside-out.
  • Central marks on seven-plus genes (pupils, eyespots, agate eye, corolla, contour cells, and more) rarely land on the visible coat at all.
  • An aggressive horse under a rider throws the rider but then cannot chase or attack — half the mechanic never fires while mounted.
  • A breed file's jump and health ranges silently collapse to their low value instead of keeping the full spread, with no warning.
  • Auroraband's marking misses the horse entirely on roughly one seed in six.
  • Moonwing's gold band nearly vanishes on white-coated horses, from a colour-summing bug.
  • Natural and magic zebra draw their stripes from independent seeds, so a horse with both shows two misaligned patterns.
  • A glow-gene light block can be permanently orphaned lit if its chunk unloads before cleanup runs.
  • A stall sign keeps a horse's old name forever after the horse is renamed.
  • The horse browser's filters run over a capped roster, so “no horse carries gene X” can be a false result.
  • Seven “inverting” magical markings never actually invert.
  • The gene creator tool silently drops a gene's needs block on save, quietly disabling that locus.

Full detail, evidence and fix notes for every one of these are on known gaps, searchable by the gap number in each entry.

Horse hair goes in raw, and a gate you break no longer gives you two. A material chain three items deep sat between the shears and everything worth building with hair, and it multiplied out to prices nobody had ever totalled — a cowboy hitch was 68 horse hair, against a supply of one to three per horse per day. The chain is gone. Separately, every double fence gate had been duplicating itself on every break since the block was written.

  • The bundle, the braided rope and the hair cloth are deleted. Everything that wanted one now takes horse hair directly, one for one, so an equestrian post is 2 hair instead of 32, the supplier’s post 4 instead of 56, and the cowboy hitch 5 instead of 68. The old prices could not be preserved even in principle — a shapeless recipe holds nine items and 68 hair does not fit. Why there is no chain.
  • A double fence gate is two fence gates of that wood, and no hair at all. It used to want a braided rope for the hinge, which was twelve horse hair on top of the two vanilla gates — a fence line was several real days of brushing horses. It is also the one recipe in the mod without a modded ingredient, taken deliberately and documented as the exception it is. The recipe.
  • Breaking a double gate drops one gate. The block’s own header comment was wrong: returning air from updateShape does not suppress drops, so a pair being two blocks meant two removals and both paid out the loot table. Fixed the way vanilla fixes it for doors and beds — in the loot table, conditioned on the half that pays — for the twelve vanilla woods and for modded woods alike. Breaking one in creative leaked a free gate the other way round, and that is fixed too.
  • Six villager trades dealt in the removed items and are folded into the hair ladder rather than deleted. In raw-hair terms every one of them had already been worse than the supplier’s plain level-1 hair trade — 32 to 48 hair per emerald against 16 — so they were close to dead before this. The supplier now buys hair at 16/14/12/10/8 per emerald across its five levels and the leatherworker at 14 and 10. The trade tables.
  • Three progress tasks and three tutorial icons went with the items. They were about crafting things that no longer exist, and a task nothing can complete is what the progress checker exists to catch.
Upgrading

Stacks of horse hair bundles, braided ropes and hair cloths in an existing world will vanish — the mod carries no back-compat by standing rule. Nothing else in this release touches saved data.

What is guarded, and what is not

Both gate fixes are held by mutation-checked gametests — reverting oak’s loot table reproduces the original report exactly (expected 1, found 2), and adding a stick to the recipe fails the crafting test. The new trade rates are a judgement call, not the owner’s, and are flagged for retuning on the villagers’ checklist; so is the creative-mode break, which no test can reach because it needs a player.

0.5.018

A cart is as heavy as what is in it. Until now the draught model read one number per vehicle, so an empty supply cart and one packed with fifty-four stacks slowed a horse by exactly the same amount, and three chests bolted to a wagon were a free upgrade. Loading a cart now costs you, and the wagon’s box seat finally drives the horse — it never did, in any version, while three separate places in the game said it would.

  • Cargo counts. A vehicle’s load is what it weighs empty plus what is in it: a full supply cart asks half again what a bare one does, a full wagon sixty per cent more. It is linear in how full the thing is, so half a cart is half the penalty — no threshold to learn. Measured by goods rather than by slots touched, so a slot holding one arrow is a sixty-fourth of a slot and nine loose torches do not make a horse labour. The rule.
  • The three field implements are deliberately flat. A plow’s load is the blade in the soil, not the tools racked on it. Fitting a second hoe does not make it harder to drag, because a player choosing between fitting the tool and pulling the plow is choosing between a working plow and a useless one.
  • Chests on a wagon are half its extra weight on their own, empty or not, and they already cost seats — three chests leave none. What is in them is the other half. An animal cart is loaded by its passengers: one cow is half a load.
  • The load is live. Fill a cart that is standing hitched and the horse is slower within a second, without unhitching; empty it and the pace comes back. The same as a chest goes on, or an animal climbs in.
  • A train of carts costs what its parts cost. A cart hitched behind a cart was previously hauled for nothing — the horse paid for the first vehicle only, which got cheaper the more you loaded the rest.
  • The wagon’s box seat drives. Only the animal cart and the reaper ever spawned the hidden rider that steers; the wagon had the seat, the tooltip and the wiki page, and did nothing. It also gains the steering statistic the other two already had.
  • Two numbers where there was one. The cart tooltip shows what an ordinary horse keeps empty and full; the horse’s info screen shows the same pair for a wagon. A horse that keeps its speed empty and collapses loaded is a carriage horse, not a carthorse, and that is now visible before the trade run rather than during it.
  • The carts page is largely rewritten — recipes for all six, hitching and trains, a section per vehicle, and what the game counts. Carts.
Two things found while writing it down

The reaper does not check whether a crop is ripe — it cuts crop blocks at any age, so a field planted this morning is harvested as seedlings. And a supply cart cannot be opened from its own seat: the packet exists, the keybind was never ported. Both are recorded in known gaps and neither is fixed here.

0.5.016

A bug-report night. Six faults reported from play, five of them things that only go wrong once a horse is in front of you: every ancestor in a family tree drawn as the same black horse, a barn-name box that renamed the horse but not the screen, and a glowing horse that mostly failed to glow. Plus two readouts that now say something a player can use, and the mod’s first integration with another mod.

  • Every horse in every GUI was the same black horse. The family tree drew each ancestor in the plain wild-type coat. The genome was never missing — a cache added to ration coat baking resolved a texture id before the screen handed over the real coat, and the texture won. The same defect was in the foal preview, the horse browser and the custom spawn screen; all four now go through one call that moves the coat and its textures together. Glowing horses also lost their glow in every GUI, from the same field.
  • Suntouched now actually lights the ground. It had one candidate block and it had to be air, so a horse standing in grass, flowers, snow or shallow water lit nothing — the common case outdoors. It tries three positions now, and re-places a light that something else removed instead of trusting its own bookkeeping. The three faults. Singleplayer was not working better, it was littering: the cleanup ran on the client too and abandoned every light permanently.
  • The barn name updated the horse but not the screen. Pressing Set renamed the horse and its nameplate while the screen in front of you kept showing the old name — it held the record it opened with and never re-read it. Reported, reasonably, as there being no way to change a barn name at all.
  • Speed and jump are in real units. The information screen printed 0.188 and 0.50; it now says 8.1 m/s and 1.6 m — the height the horse can actually get onto, which is a cubic in jump strength rather than a straight line. Both conversions.
  • A bound ender whistle says whose it is. All four whistles share one texture, so a bound one and an unbound one were indistinguishable without blowing it. The horse’s name was already on the item; nothing drew it.
  • Jade support. Point at a mare and her state is in the tooltip: Pregnant, In heat, Nursing, or nothing. No timings and nothing for a mare between heats — a tooltip is on screen whenever you glance at a horse. Twins are still the vet’s kit’s alone. Jade is optional; without it nothing changes. Why this one is allowed to be a real dependency.
  • The Equestrian Bench is now the Tack Dyeing Bench, which is what it does. Name only — placed benches and anything referring to the block id keep working.

Known and not fixed: a stall sign keeps the name the horse had when it was hung (gap 283), and a glow light block is orphaned if its chunk unloads before the cleanup runs (gap 284). Existing worlds already carry abandoned light blocks from the singleplayer bug above; the fix stops new ones rather than sweeping up old ones.

0.5.012

Horses that pull things, and a fifth stat that decides how well. Six horse-drawn vehicles — a covered wagon you drive from the box seat, a plow, a seed drill, a reaper, a supply cart and an animal cart — in every wood your modpack has. How fast a horse pulls one is not a setting: pulling ability sets a ceiling and the horse's own speed decides how close it gets, so a balanced horse beats both the strongest and the fastest horse you own. Also in this one: a flying breed, a crop that grows golden carrots, and a Getting Started chapter to teach all of it.

Carts

  • Six vehicles, every one horse-drawn. The wagon seats four, grows from 36 to 108 slots as you feed it chests, takes a coloured roof from five carpets, and a passenger on the box seat drives the horse. The plow tills, paths or strips logs depending on which tool is inside it. The seed drill plants and the reaper harvests as the horse walks. The supply cart holds 54 stacks and shows you what is in it. The animal cart seats two, and small animals climb in by themselves.
  • In every wood, including modded ones. Vanilla's twelve plus one for every wood any other mod you have installed adds, discovered the same way the double fence gates discover theirs. A modded cart crafts from that mod's own planks. No cart texture ships at all — each is assembled at load time from the wood you built it out of, so a resource pack that retextures oak planks retextures oak carts for free.
  • Your horse is the whole difference. A weak horse is capped on a heavy cart however fast you breed it; a very strong slow horse never reaches the ceiling its shoulders could carry. Neither stat can be dumped, and the tooltip on any cart tells you what an ordinary horse keeps pulling it.
  • A horse at pulling 6 or better carries two riders — which, with the wagon's box seat, is what lets a carriage have a driver and somebody on the horse.
  • How far a horse has hauled is on its information screen, and belongs to the horse: it survives being sold, given away or inherited. Pull now sits beside Jump with the other body stats, with a word for what the number means.
  • The cart system is derived from AstikorCarts by MennoMax, by way of NiftyCarts by jmb19905 and the UsefulCarts port, used under the MIT Licence and maintained here from now on. Full lineage in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md, which ships inside the jar.

Pulling ability, the gene behind it

  • A fifth body stat, scored 1–10 like a breed sheet writes its stats, inherited on the allele copies and drifting like every other epigenetic number. Five is an ordinary horse.
  • It is a real breeding target now rather than a number nothing read: carts, the second rider, and the work rate any animal-labour mod can pick up.

Cloudtouched

  • A very rare magical breed built as a reservoir of true flight — no founder flies, but two of them can throw a foal that does. Bird-boned is roughly one in ten rather than one in two, and it pays for the sky with health and pulling strength.

Golden carrots, grown

  • Golden carrot seeds grow golden carrots on ordinary farmland. They cannot be crafted and a golden carrot will not plant — the equestrian supplier sells seeds at his top rank, and dungeon-grade chests have them. A mature plant gives more seeds than you put in, so the first one is the only hard part.
  • No gold ever comes out of it. It is a way to grow horse food, not a gold farm.

Getting Started

  • A new chapter, Farming and haulage, after Husbandry — eleven sub-chapters covering hay bales (the actual point of growing wheat), golden carrots, the cart wheel, pulling power taught from scratch, and one per vehicle. Each vehicle's box ticks when you actually drive it.

Fixes and smaller things

  • The wiki's popups close on a phone, and the designers say what they are doing while they boot instead of showing a blank page.
  • The homestead is counted rather than guessed at, and turned out never to have been rare; the village decides which way it faces.
  • The path-canvas guard reports its failures instead of throwing.
  • Four stale checks fixed — and in each case the thing the stale check was hiding.
None of the carts has been played

The build is green and a dedicated server boots clean, which is what catches the errors that matter at load — one mixin in this release did fail to boot and was found that way. But no cart in this release has been hitched to a horse by a human being. If something in it is obviously wrong, that is why, and the carts’ own checklist lists what to look at first.

0.5.011

Four villagers, and other people's materials. One horseman had been selling tack, carrots, armour, papers, eggs and tickets across five tiers, which made him a shop rather than a tradesman and left nothing to get better as he did. He is four people now. Separately and underneath: install a mod that adds a wood or a metal, and this mod now makes content for it — read off that mod's own files, with nobody editing a list.

The four equestrians

  • The Leatherworker sells the saddle and the leather horse armour, dyed by a ladder you can see: bone meal and flower colours at Novice, lapis with diamond fittings at Master. The dyed saddle is back after being withdrawn once for being invisible — a saddle takes three colours now, so the trade sells something you can look at.
  • The Scientist deals in breeding carrots, the vet's kit and breed spawn eggs, and buys filled seed jars. His Master carrots name both alleles, so the foal is certain to carry the gene rather than getting a coin flip — and he will not sell one for a gene whose homozygote cannot occur, because that carrot would do nothing and say nothing.
  • The Supplier is the general store — carrots, leads, whistles, tickets, papers, the empty seed jar — and the one who buys horse hair from his first day, which is still the easiest early trade in the mod.
  • The Metalsmith sells horse armour in metal and crystal and nothing else.
  • The horseman is retired outright. No alias and no transitional profession: a villager who held that job in an existing world comes back unemployed.
  • A post each, because a job site hands out exactly one profession. Five posts down the homestead's frontage now — the cowboy's hitch, the front door, the four trades, and the bench at the end.
  • The house is three rows deeper, with five beds, one per sleeper. A villager who claims a job and has nowhere to sleep never restocks, so the bed count is the other half of the post count rather than decoration.
  • Four hat colours, none of them purple. Purple is the arcane dealer's and he has to stay recognisable in a crowd.
  • All four take the same family name as the cowboy, the way the horseman did: whoever is hired first founds it and everyone after joins.

Gates and armour for other mods' materials

  • A double fence gate for every wood another mod adds, crafted from two of that mod's own gates and a braided rope, wearing that mod's own plank texture. No new art was needed for any of it — a double gate has never had a texture of its own, only a pointer at one.
  • A horse armour for every modded ingot or gem, coloured from that item's own picture, because nothing in a mod's files states what colour tin is except the art. How much it protects is read from that mod's own chestplate where there is one to read, and guessed from a ladder where there is not — the guess is labelled as one in the code.
  • The Equestrian Bench takes them too: modded ingots and gems as fittings beside the hand-picked fifteen, and modded dyes whose colour vanilla has no name for. A dye carrying vanilla's own component always worked and is untouched.
  • The Metalsmith stocks the new armours, filed by how much each one protects, so his tiers are no longer three items long.
  • It is all written to .minecraft/phc/generated/ and handed back to the game as a pack. The folder records which mods it was built for: change nothing and nothing is rewritten, add or remove a mod and it is wiped and built again. The files stay on disk on purpose — a generated model that fails is otherwise a purple cube with nothing logged anywhere.

Smaller

  • A cowboy hitch dropped nothing when you broke it. It had shipped with no loot table at all, which is not a default — it is a warning in the log and a block you cannot get back.
  • A test had been red on a clean tree for a day, asserting the uncapped harem size that a fix had already replaced. It now pins the cap from the inside and reads the constant rather than the number, so moving the cap moves the test with it.

Verification

A dedicated server boots clean on all of it. The compatibility layer was exercised with a synthetic mod written to be found: the scan finds its wood and its metals, the pack generates, an unchanged mod list is left alone, and removing the mod wipes the folder. Twenty unit tests cover the scan itself, including both texture fallbacks and the four kinds of thing that must not be picked up, and they were checked by mutation rather than trusted.

What that does not cover, and it is a lot. Nothing has been run against a real third-party mod. No generated recipe has ever successfully parsed — the test mod declares items it never registers, so what was proven is that we skip the recipe rather than that we write a good one. And nothing generated has been seen rendering at all: not a gate, not a tinted armour, not an icon, not a hat. Nor has the deepened homestead been confirmed to still generate in a village, which is the thing that gates every other villager check. See the villagers’ own checklist, and gaps 270-275.

0.5.010

The horse wizard. Magical genes sit near a 0.1% founder weight, which means most of the mod's registry has been something you could read about and never own. This release adds the man who sells them — a cowboy who deals in magic instead of a breed — and the three things that turned out to be needed before buying from him was possible at all: a way to read a horse you do not own, a way to search his string, and a way to find him.

The arcane dealer

  • About one village barn in twelve is founded by a dealer who keeps no breed and no country. His whole string is Mixed horses, each carrying one showing gene from every magical family — eleven, plus a colour modifier on a quarter of them — and no two horses in his string share an allele pair.
  • He never keeps a gelding. What you are buying is the genes, and a gelding cannot pass them on.
  • Twelve to twenty-eight emeralds, against one to three for an ordinary horse. The markup is a fact about the dealer, not about the horse: nothing in the model scores an animal, so a horse with eleven magical loci that you bred yourself is still worth what any horse is worth.
  • Sometimes he is out on the road instead, away from any village, and after a day and a half he moves on again with whatever he did not sell. Hostile mobs now ignore every cowboy, which they did not before — a dealer alone on a hillside was surviving the night while his string did not.
  • What you buy is a gene bomb. He breeds willy-nilly: a dozen unrelated loci on one animal, and almost never the single thing you went there for. Isolating it is a breeding project, and that is the intended shape of him — he sells raw material, not finished horses.
  • His stock skips the five genes that make a noise. A paddock of ten horses all meowing is one you cannot shop in.

Reading a horse before you buy it

  • Sneak and use, empty-handed, on any horse you have not tamed opens the horse information screen — wild horses, a stable's stock, and the cowboy's branded string, which refuses every other interaction. Tamed horses are unchanged, because that is how the saddle and armour slots open.
  • A filter box above every cowboy's offers. Type a breed, a gene or an allele and the list narrows; several words mean all of them. It matches gene names and registry keys, allele tokens and labels, and the name of the expression a pair actually produces, so dapple finds every horse showing one whatever gene put it there.
  • Reading is not owning. You can inspect a stranger's horse, but the barn-name box is hidden on one you do not own and a name tag refuses — a gap that had never come up, because until now nothing could read a horse you did not own.

Smaller

  • A checklist entry for buying from him, and the tutorial step that claimed a cowboy's string only ever gives you ordinary horses of a breed — which he had just made untrue.
  • The cowboy's founding announcement had been reporting one fewer horse than he owns since the mounted cowboy was deleted; it was still subtracting a mount that no longer exists.

0.5.009

Nothing in this one changes a horse. It is a release of tests, and of the machinery that runs them: four checks that could not fail, a yard warning that never said what was wrong, the first in-game tests this mod has ever had, and a reader for an unattended run. If you are playing rather than building, 0.5.008 and 0.5.009 are the same game — no gene, coat, item, breed or behaviour differs between them.

Four tests that were not testing

  • The broadest coat test had been checking 35 of 291 loci for months. Its gene mask was a long, and Java masks a shift distance to the low six bits, so 1L << 291 silently meant 1L << 35. Every locus past the 36th sat pinned to one allele and nothing went red, because the test only asserts that a bake returns a full sheet.
  • Two more were red on main and unrecorded: one asserted an all-white horse from a genotype that stopped being one three days earlier, and one reported that the magic zebra's stripes had vanished when all 11,902 of them were present — it was counting against a threshold the stripes no longer cross.
  • The creator round-trip fixture was stale, missing a verb the creator itself has always had. The alarming half of that entry — whether a paid epigenetic splice was rerolling a foal's alleles — turns out to be fine.

The first in-game tests

  • runGameTest runs the mod inside a headless Minecraft in about sixteen seconds and exits non-zero on a failure. Two tests so far, one of them a real answer to whether redstone opens both halves of a double gate.
  • 26.1.2 has no @GameTest annotation at all — the whole annotation API is gone — and the property this build had been setting to enable them is read by nothing in this version. Both are written up in the API notes.
  • The creator parity check is a test now, and one that cannot be skipped: it re-bakes the fixture and fails if the committed one was stale, then runs the comparison. It had been red for a whole release because nothing ran it but a person deciding to.

The test yard tells the truth again

  • Its soundness warning named the plot centre rather than the obstruction, which is why it was set aside as noise four sessions running. Made to name the block, it answered itself on the first boot — a water cauldron that was always meant to be there.
  • A pen could kill its neighbour's animals. Harmless while no shifter could leave its pen; a bat can, and did, within two seconds of dusk.

Brindle only shows on mares now

  • It takes two copies. A stallion has one X and therefore one copy, so he can never qualify: every stallion carrying brindle is a carrier, and every brindle horse in the world is a mare. It used to show on any stallion that carried it.
  • This settles the gene against its own explanation. Brindle is a record of which X each patch of skin silenced — which is why the two sides of the horse never match — and a horse with one X has nothing to silence.
  • The crosses invert. No colt is ever brindle, whatever the pairing. A brindle filly needs the allele from both sides; a brindle mare put to a carrier sire throws a brindle filly every time. The allele is five times commoner than it was, because one sex showing p² is a far smaller number than the old one-copy rate.

  • Fifty new coat markings, and a black form for forty-two existing ones. Cleave became two genes: the marking, and which half it is on.
  • Fireproof and bird-boned protect horse and rider properly; molten hooves leaves glowing hoofprints; size is shown as a multiple of an ordinary horse, and a breed's size range decides whether its horses carry one size gene or two.
  • Hovering a gene in the custom spawn egg shows its picture beside the description. The egg's breed list starts at Feral Mixed and includes your own breeds.
  • The research shelf is a chest, stalls land a horse dead centre, a holding pen, and a run of fixes from three play sessions.

Three new shapes came with it, each because some marking needed a measurement nothing could make: FAN, bars that radiate from a point and widen as they go (everything periodic before this ran in parallel lines); NORMAL, which way the surface faces, for a rim light or a shell’s curvature sheen; and six new parameters covering veins that pool at their junctions, per-cell radial fades, tiled fields clipped into shingles, off-centre glints, and colour that wanders instead of sweeping.

What this does to horses you already have

Nothing changes colour. All 1056 composed coats in the golden file are byte-identical to 0.4.0’s, so no gene that existed before this release paints differently. An older save loads and every horse in it keeps its coat, with the twenty-two new loci filled in at wild type — which also means no horse in an old world can be carrying one, so the new markings reach it only through newly spawned wild horses.

Forty-three existing genes did get rarer in the wild. Founder rates are budgeted across the whole set from each gene’s declared rarity tier, and adding twenty-two genes re-derived all of them — which also flattened a number of tables that had drifted above their tier. Banded Socks, Ocular, Tron and Picasso Marking each dropped by about a factor of seven. The full table, and the argument about whether those are the right numbers, is on the gene-authoring verification tab.

Not played yet

Every one of the twenty-two markings has been rendered and looked at as an icon — that is how four defects in them were caught — but none has been seen on a horse in the game, and nor has the drawing engine. The gene-authoring verification tab is the checklist.

0.3.2

A one-line fix for the bug that made 0.3.0 and 0.3.1 unplayable: every client was kicked off the moment a horse came into view.

Replace 0.3.0 or 0.3.1

Both of those releases disconnect you on entering a world with Failed to encode packet 'clientbound/minecraft:custom_payload'. There is nothing wrong with a world made under them — the fault is in what the server sends, not in what it saved — so the same save works once the jar is swapped. 0.2.0 is unaffected.

  • An epigenome code outgrew the wire. A horse’s coat is sent to the client as its genotype and epigenome text, and the epigenome stores literal values for every gene that varies — so the genes added across 0.3.0 pushed a full code past 32 767 characters, which is the length Minecraft’s default string writer silently caps at. It throws rather than truncating, and a throw while encoding drops the connection. The coat packet now declares a cap with real room in it, as the other genome-carrying packets already did.
  • The same latent fault was in three other places — the stored genome on a preset spawn egg and a genome jar, which would have kicked whoever held one, and the genotype fields on the roster and spawn packets, which were already two thirds of the way to the same crash. All the caps now come from one place, so raising them is one edit.
  • The test that was meant to catch this now can. It was watching the one cap that had a number written beside it and could not see the implicit one next to it; it now guards every genome-code cap, and it fails while there is still half the budget left.

0.3.1

Two genes that make a horse frightening, nine coat genes repainted, and the categories reorganised so they mean something.

The night loci

  • Magic night temper — eight variants in two families. Four hunt and four flee, and each names who: riders, passive animals, monsters, or everything. By day every one of them is an ordinary horse. The monster-hunter is the only one most people would keep on purpose; the one that flees riders is catchable, tamable and rideable by day and will not let you near it after dark.
  • Magic night watch — five variants that are a ladder rather than a list. It stares from anywhere through walls; or closes to ten blocks first; or watches only while it can see you; or moves to where you cannot see it; or stands directly behind you. Any of them makes the horse silent on its feet at night.
  • The temper locus supersedes the watch locus whenever it has something to act on, so a horse never tries to stalk you and flee from you at once.
  • Both are entirely heterozygous in the wild: every feral horse carries exactly one variant and none of them shows it, so the plainest outcome each locus has is the one that cannot be caught.

Coat genes

  • Three genes stopped painting a ground over the whole horse — coccinella’s black, shieldback’s green, tribal ward’s pale. All three now mark the horse’s own colour, so two carriers are different animals.
  • Koi’s orange and dark varieties lay patches on the coat instead of tiling the whole horse. The white variety still covers, because that is the one whose point is the hard boundary between two fields.
  • Snow cloud has bare drifts the coat shows through, which is what its name always described.
  • Cleave’s detached spears are spear-shaped and close to the line, instead of splotches loose on the far half.
  • Opossum’s rear edge is dithered — the dots get rarer — rather than a gradient fade.
  • Nyxborn stars only what is genuinely black, so a bay keeps its brown shoulder unstarred.
  • Stained glass gained a grisaille palette — grey and white only, and the one palette that reads as glass on any base coat.
  • Shieldback and tribal ward’s “coloured” alleles now colour the marking. They previously varied only the ground, so removing it left them identical to their first allele.

Elsewhere

  • The magical categories were reorganised. They were derived from priority bands, which is a real fact about a gene that paints and an accident about one that does not — so one category held coat markings, milk buckets, spawn eggs and a horse that stares through walls. The non-painting genes are grouped by what they do now: yield and death, behaviour, trails and emissions, body stats.
  • Shadowcreature’s page has its preview back. It never had one — the page was written from the template for a gene that paints nothing.
  • An inverted mask no longer escapes its own parts, which corrects twelve genes (carried over from the unreleased work at 0.3.0 — see the notes there).

Notes

  • Not save-compatible with 0.3.0. Two new loci change the genotype code and every horse’s epigenetic slots. Start a new world.
  • The night loci have never run in a game. Two AI goals, a supersede rule and a sound suppression, all written against the sources and none of it seen. The night watch’s own checklist carries it.

0.3.0

Genes that decide what a horse does. Eight new loci change nothing about how a horse looks and everything about how it behaves — how fast it swims, how long it lasts underwater, what it hits for, what it leaves behind when it dies, whether mobs avoid it or fight over it, how much meat it drops and how many times a day it can be milked. Six new coat genes come with them, and one new mask that asks the gradient chart what colour it actually made.

Mechanical genes

Eight loci that paint nothing at all. They exist so that a breed can be described by what its horses do rather than only by what colour they are.

  • Magic swim speed — how fast the horse moves in water, and nothing else. Completely unrelated to magic speed: a horse can be the slowest thing in the paddock and still cross a river faster than a boat.
  • Magic water breathing — how long before it starts to drown. Independent of swimming speed, so a ferry and a diver are two separate breeding projects.
  • Magic fighter — what it hits for. Wild type is 3 points; the gladiator allele adds a percentage and the wimp allele takes one off, and the two are added with opposite signs, so a wimp copy really does cancel a gladiator copy. Breeding a fighter means breeding the wimp allele out as much as breeding the gladiator in. No ceiling, but a wild gladiator carrier is only worth one to three extra points — every genuinely dangerous horse is one somebody made.
  • Magic on death — a lava source, a water source, or a creeper-sized explosion where the horse died. No feral horse has this: it is the one locus in the mod whose wild population is carriers only, so a horse that leaves a crater is always somebody's fault. Two different variants cancel each other, because “what happens here” cannot have two answers.
  • Magic item drop — 2–5 diamonds, an enchanted sword with the enchantment rolled at the moment of death, or a spawn egg that puts this horse back. The egg carries the genotype and the epigenome, so what comes out is a clone rather than a horse with the same alleles — the only thing in the mod that undoes a death. Nothing about the living animal shows any of it.
  • Magic mob aura — warding keeps hostiles about ten blocks off; baiting makes every hostile that can see the horse pick it over anything else, including its rider. One of each cancels outright. The baiting allele is the one that makes health and fighting worth stacking.
  • Magic meat — how much meat the horse leaves, added to whatever else it drops. So a diamond horse can also be a meat horse.
  • Magic milk volume — how many times a day it can be filled from. Unlike most of these a single copy shows, so a milky horse can be found rather than only bred. It governs the milk locus's water and lava too.

Coat genes

  • Nyxborn — every white marking the horse has is painted out in black, and then the whole of that black is dusted with tiny white points. A pinto goes in and a starfield comes out; the pattern underneath is only recoverable from the genotype.
  • Tron — neon run along every edge of the horse, full-bright so it holds its colour in the dark. It paints later than anything else in the mod, so whatever else the horse is wearing, the lines are on top. Two alleles: one solid colour, or a hue ramp running nose to tail.
  • Shadowcreature — the head, muzzle, ears and whole neck go flat black, the eyes burn gold with no white around them, and tapering tentacles run out of the join and down over the shoulder. The horse gives off a dim light.
  • Stained glass — black leading in straight lines with a quarter-strength wash of colour in each pane. The palette is the allele rather than an epigenetic value: one allele is blue, pink and purple, the other green, yellow and white, and a horse with one of each gets a window with all six in the tray.
  • Ocular — open eyes down the neck, each with a white, an iris in the line's own colour and a pupil. One copy gives a handful, two crowd the neck.
  • Bloodstained — red gathers along the bottom edge of the horse's markings, inside the white rather than around it, as though something had run down and settled where the white stops.

What a gene can now say

  • A gene can ask what colour the horse actually is. The new LUMA mask reads the colour the gradient chart produced, rather than the pigment behind it. That is a real difference and not a nicety: the LUT locus can hand a horse a chart whose black corner is violet, and such a horse has identical pigment everywhere and is not black anywhere. A gene that means “cover the black parts” has to ask the question a viewer asks. Fielded, yalia, voided and opalized now find the horse's white this way, so they look different from 0.2.0 — noticeably so on cream coats, where a perlino used to read as white and now reads as cream.
  • A gene can outline the horse. The new EDGE mask draws the rim of each body part's box — a wireframe. It is what Tron is built from.
  • A marking can have a bottom. spreadFrom lets a spread grow from one side only, which is the only way to ask for the lower edge of a marking rather than its whole rim.
  • Six new effect verbs — charges, breath, on_death, item_drop, mob_aura and combat — all available to a data-driven gene, not just to the built-in ones. See the effects block.
  • The attribute verb actually does something now. It had been parsed and ignored since it was written. Fixing that surfaced that its list named swim_speed, an attribute Minecraft has never had — the name never had to resolve to anything while the verb did nothing.

Notes

  • Not save-compatible with 0.2.0. Fourteen new loci change the genotype code and every horse's epigenetic slots. Start a new world.
  • An ordinary horse now kicks for 3 rather than 4. The baseline moved onto the fighter locus, so there is one number in one place instead of two that could disagree.
  • The mechanical genes have never run in a game. They compile and the suite is green; that is all. To be verified §0-AR is the checklist, and the riskiest items are the attribute add/remove cycle, the drowning accumulator and whether the mob-repel shove actually overcomes a pathfinding mob.
  • Still NeoForge, Minecraft 26.1.2 only. Both sides need the mod.
