Releases

What shipped in each tagged version, newest first. This is the player-facing record — the reasoning behind each change is in the session log, and what is known to work is in to be verified. A release is a build that ran green, not a build that has been played.

Read this first

Every release so far is a development release. The suite is green and the jar assembles, but only a small fraction of what is built has been confirmed in a play session — to be verified is the authority on which is which. Treat a world you care about accordingly.

The numbering, as of 2026-09-10

The next release is 0.5.000, and after that only the last number moves — 0.5.001, 0.5.002, and on up. It is a three-digit field on purpose: there is a lot of counting to do before anything else changes.

The middle number does not move without the owner saying so, explicitly and out loud. Not for a big feature, not for a batch of genes, not because the last number is getting long. That is the whole rule and it is the owner's call, not a judgement to make from how much shipped. The versions ran 0.2.0 to 0.4.1 in two days, which is what the rule is a reaction to.

The version itself lives in one place, gradle.properties' mod_version, and a tagged release has outside testers on it — so a number is never reused, and never reissued with different bytes.

0.5.006

A gate wide enough to lead a horse through, a stall that accepts an ordinary doorway, and temperament rebuilt onto two loci you can actually read. Four strands, only one of which was today’s work — the rest had been sitting on main behind the last tag.

Double gates

  • A fence gate two blocks wide, one per wood. All twelve vanilla woods. One item places both halves, one click swings both leaves, and there is no post down the middle — so it is a doorway you lead a horse through rather than shove one through. Crafted from two vanilla gates of that wood plus a braided rope.
  • They file into vanilla’s Building Blocks tab, each directly after the single fence gate of its own wood, rather than into the mod’s own tab.
  • A stall shut with one binds like any other, and there is a new husbandry checklist step for building one. A door, trapdoor or gate is a stall’s edge in every state, and these count.
  • Breaking either half takes both and returns the one item; redstone reaching either half opens the pair; and a gate with no room to its side now says so instead of failing silently.

A doorway is a hole, and a hole leaks

  • A stall you walk into through a plain gap can be bound. It could not before: the sign refused unless you hung a gate in the gap or bricked it up. When the floor runs out through a doorway, the wall above it is searched for a ring that closes instead — a doorway has a lintel. The entrance square is correctly not counted as part of the room.

Temperament: two loci instead of four

  • magic_night_temper, magic_day_temper and gladiator are gone. Between them a breeder had to read three loci to answer one question, and they still could not express “hunts monsters, shy of people”. They are replaced by aggression and skittishness — the same grid of three times crossed with five targets, one for what a horse goes for and one for what it runs from — with passification new beside them as the way back.
  • A saddle is no longer a gate. A horse aggressive toward riders bucks them off and comes for them. A horse aggressive toward anything else is meant to ignore its rider and does not yet — a ridden horse's movement belongs to its passenger, so the melee goal cannot walk it anywhere. That half is written down as a known gap rather than left to be found.
  • Some aggression expresses in the wild, rarer the nastier the target, because passification needs something to rescue.

Reference

  • A gene census — one page listing what every combination of every gene’s alleles actually does, and which breeds carry it. Written as a plain table so it can be handed to a machine as readily as read.
  • Getting started leads with the wild, because every horse a player meets is wild until they tame one, and magic is now its own chapter rather than a corner of genetics.

Almost none of this has been played. The double gates were, on the day they were built — the pair, the bamboo texture, fast opening and one-click placement are confirmed — and verification lists what is still unseen, here and everywhere else.

0.5.005

Five things the testers hit in 0.5.004, and the colour work that was already sitting on main behind them. The horse dimension crashed on the way in, the rename window could not be typed into or left, and a stall had a height requirement nobody had asked for.

The five reports

  • The horse dimension crashed the moment you stepped through. The new coat-check corridor asks every pen whether its combination belongs to a mare or a stallion, and that question went to a method which throws on an ordinary autosomal gene rather than answering “no”. Every pen in the plan is autosomal, so the first one threw while you were still being teleported in. All 438 pens now build, and a test walks every one of them so this cannot come back quietly.
  • The rename window works. It was drawing its background panel over its own two name boxes and both buttons — they were still there and still taking your keystrokes, just painted over at 94% opacity, which is why it looked like a dead rectangle you could neither type into nor leave.
  • A stall is its floor and the fence, gate, wall or door around it — it no longer has a height at all. It used to demand two clear blocks above every tile, which refused roofed stalls and low doorways outright and shoved a dismounting rider up into the ceiling of the ones it did accept. Where a horse may actually be put is unchanged: that is still checked against the horse’s real size when something tries to stand there.
  • Wild horses stop being mostly mares. Every adult joining a band that already existed was made female, with no limit and no re-check, so a band near a spawn collected mares for ever and never gained a second stallion. A harem now fills to four and the surplus goes elsewhere.
  • Four new checklist entries — ride a horse bareback and steer a horse with no saddle under Bonding, own a gelding and bond a gelding to approaching under Gelding.

Coat work that ships alongside it

  • A colour op can paint a different colour per region, and a ramp or a palette takes its colour source per region too.
  • A glow can be seen without starting the game — it is something you can look at now, not only read about.
  • A layer can say it paints over the ones above it, and eleven genes stop faking that with subtraction.
  • A colour op is one control, and Random stops lying about its ranges.

If you are testing

The three tests that changed are Stall signs and tickets (now including a roofed stall), Renaming a horse with a name tag, and The hay portal and the horse pens — which nobody could get past in 0.5.004.

0.5.004

Five markings that were flat now draw their structure, four more health loci, and the horse dimension turned into a checklist you can walk. The coat half is the visible one; the dimension half is what makes the next release’s coat half checkable at all.

Markings that were a wash now have a shape

  • Silver draws its dapples. It had the colour and none of the structure, so a silver dapple read as a flat lightening of the body.
  • Roan grows corn spots — and does it by suppressing white rather than painting coloured spots over the top, which is why they now appear in the places a real roan keeps its colour.
  • Flaxen fades from root to tip down the mane and tail instead of recolouring the whole hair evenly. It reuses grey’s existing hair axis rather than inventing a second one.
  • Dun’s d1 draws the leg bars and the shoulder bar, not the dorsal stripe alone — the marks that tell a dun from a horse with a stripe down its back.
  • Ordinary white markings get their own locus — stars, snips, socks and stockings — and SW1 gives back a job it should never have had. Splash white had been standing in for plain markings, so it turned up about six times more often than it should; it is now down to roughly a sixth of what it was.

Health

  • Four new health loci, plus ACAN D5, and silver’s MCOA is dosed — one copy and two copies are no longer the same eye.
  • WFFS had shipped all along and was missing only from the documentation, which is now corrected.
  • Three genes had no description at all. They do now, and the check that catches a missing one is no longer the project’s one failing test.

The horse pens are a checklist now

Step through a hay-bale portal and the corridor has a different question down each side instead of two thousand random horses.

  • On your right: every marking nobody has confirmed by eye. One pen per combination, worst first — the most recently written genes are at the near end, because that is where a drawing bug actually is. Each pen holds four horses carrying the same marking on a black, a bay, a chestnut and a white base, so a marking that only works on one of them is obvious rather than something you have to go hunting for. The white horse is the interesting one: it has no pigment at all, so anything drawn on it is a bug.
  • On your left: every breed in the game, alphabetically, a mare and a stallion each, looping round when it runs out.
  • Signs in front of each pen say what it should look like — the gene, the outcome’s name and its own description, rather than the bare genotype code the old signs carried.
  • It gets shorter as the mod gets tested. The corridor’s length is the number of unconfirmed markings, so confirming one removes its pens. It is already under half the length of 0.5.003’s corridor.
  • The pens still build as you walk, and there is still a way home every twenty-five pens.

If you are testing, walk the horse pens in For Testers is the short version, and it asks you to report the pen number from the first sign.

What it does to an existing world

  • Horses you already have will look different where they carry silver, roan, flaxen or dun — those markings draw differently now. Their genetics have not changed; the drawing has.
  • Splash white becomes rarer in newly generated horses. Horses that already exist keep the alleles they have.
  • The horse dimension is rebuilt on each visit, as always, so nothing there is lost.

0.5.003

A breeding bug that quietly sterilised mares, and the Getting started tab rebuilt as a book. A small release next to 0.5.002, and the first half of it matters considerably more than the second.

A mare who lost a pregnancy was never covered again

  • The bug. A natural cover required exactly full health, a miscarriage costs the dam half a heart, and a horse only heals beside water. Any mare who lost a pregnancy somewhere without water was permanently barren: she went on cycling, still read In heat, and stallions simply ignored her.
  • It was silent, which is why it survived three sessions looking for it. That particular refusal was the one verdict the handler passed over without a log line.
  • The fix. Covering now asks for nine-tenths health rather than all of it, so a scratch no longer stops breeding, and the refusal logs itself once a minute. The same equality check meant a wolf bite or a lost stallion fight sterilised any horse until it next found water — so this was always wider than the mare it was noticed on.

Getting started is a book now

  • Six chapters — husbandry, basic breeding, genetics, breeding projects, horses in the wild, the villagers — each split into sub-chapters, in place of one long run of eighteen sections.
  • The checklist moved into the prose. Every task now sits under the sub-chapter that teaches it, so the box for hanging a stall sign is beneath the paragraphs about hanging one. A sub-chapter you have finished is lit in gold in the contents list, and a Checklist section at the end keeps the running total and lists only what is still outstanding.
  • Dyeing tack is a new sub-chapter, covering the Equestrian Bench's three independent zones on both a saddle and leather horse armour.
  • The checklist went from 36 tasks to 62, so that every item in the mod is named by at least one of them. Nothing already ticked is lost.

Four corrections to the wiki

Writing the tab from the source rather than the documentation turned up four stale claims, all now fixed: grooming is worth two bond and not five; riding earns a point every forty seconds rather than every minute, because proximity always stacks with it; a stall sign refuses an unenclosed room instead of falling back to the ground in front of it; and the breeding page no longer says heat and pregnancy are unbuilt, or that there are no sex-linked genes.

What it does to an existing world

Nothing. No checklist task changed its id, so progress carries over untouched, and there is no save migration.

0.5.002

Five days of work, not one feature. Horses breed, eat and band together on their own; the breed list nearly trebled; and tack takes dye.

Seventy-three new breeds

  • Forty-nine breeds became a hundred and twenty-two — each built from its own breed sheet rather than a template. The breed book has the entries.
  • The mod has an extinct breed. The Tarpan never spawns wild — it is only ever reached by breeding for it.
  • Some breeds never run wild at all — Budyonny, Croatian Coldblood and Danubian are stud and sport breeds with no feral population, so they arrive through cowboys and stables only.
  • Visually fixed breeds: every Sorraia, Riwoche and Konik founder is dun, every Murgese is black, and no Pony of the Americas is ever without the leopard-complex allele.
  • The Criollo is the first breed to carry manchado, which had been reachable only from the wild founder table.
  • Vyatka carries PSSM1 heavily — about a third of the breed is genetically affected rather than a silent carrier.

A wild herd might be magical

One wild herd in twenty is the magical version of its breed. The whole herd shares one magical gene and reads as Magical (Breed); bred back into its own breed a foal stays Magical, crossed elsewhere it is the ordinary cross. Switched off, or re-weighted, under [magical_herds] in breed-spawning.toml, and a breed file may opt out. Breeds.

Horses breed on their own

  • Mares come into heat, carry a pregnancy and foal with no carrot involved — a cover needs both horses within three blocks, at full health, neither ridden nor leashed, after a real courtship. A stallion manages three covers a day at most. Twins happen, and not every twin survives.
  • A pregnancy runs one Minecraft day by default (fertility.gestation_days), with every other stage scaled from it. Plain golden carrots still give a foal at once.
  • Gelding, and the vet's kit that does it — and which is the only way to learn a mare is carrying twins before birth. The vet's kit.
  • Breeding carrots wait on the horse until a conception uses them, rather than opening a thirty-second window.
  • Fertility and gestation, breeding.

Hunger, and bands

  • Horses carry a hunger you cannot see, which drains faster when they heal. A starving horse cannot heal; hunger never injures one directly. Vanilla's free passive healing is switched off — hunger is what pays for healing now. Grazing wears grass to dirt; carnivorous horses hunt.
  • Wild horses live in bands — relationships, rank, grooming partners and rivalry, a lead mare the band follows so a stallion's death no longer scatters it, bachelor bands, dispersal, sparring, takeovers that stop short of a kill, and a dam defending her foal. Horse care.

Genes and coats

  • KIT carries every published W allele, W1 through W39, and W8, W18 and W21 gained a new speckled white. SW9 and SW10 joined splashed white, and DMRT3 the gait keeper ships at measured breed frequencies — the genotype is real, the gait itself is not implemented yet.
  • Heat and cold tolerance, two magical loci carried by thirty-eight breeds. Heat, cold.
  • The dhampir is a breed now, not a gene — one gene doing five jobs became magic white, sun sensitivity, blood diet, a vampiric allele and a red iris, reassembled as the night-only Dhampir breed.
  • The dryad became nine alleles, carrying mushrooms, flowers and bone meal, with the tree species on the allele — which retires the dark-oak horse whose saplings could never grow.
  • Glow is a level, not a switch: around fifty glowing layers fade at their edges instead of stopping at a hard line. Magic zebra is codominant. Markings can come out smaller rather than fainter.
  • Rainbow Drip is now Ribbon Drip — no horse changed, the name simply argued with the horse in front of you.
  • Fixed: every white painter and every magical gene had been painting over the whites of a foal's eyes. Fixed: a particle horse could be a homozygote or a carrier, identical to look at — non-partner pairs now show nothing.

Dyeable tack

  • A saddle dyes in three parts — seat, bridle and metal fittings, independently. Leather horse armour dyes the same way: blanket, trim and fittings.
  • The Equestrian Bench does the dyeing and names the saddle. Craft it, or find the one at any cowboy's homestead. You are charged only for the parts that changed. The bench.
  • Fifteen fittings — the hardware row takes a material rather than a dye, and iron is the way back to plain steel.
  • Nothing already owned changed. An undyed saddle is pixel-identical to the one players know, so donkeys, mules, skeleton horses and zombie horses look exactly as they did, and crafting-grid dyeing still works.

Items, cowboys and screens

  • The ender whistle binds to one horse for good and calls it from anywhere, across dimensions; it crumbles when that horse dies.
  • A cowboy comes from somewhere — his whole string of horses and his own name are drawn from one of ten regional pools, keyed off the country on each breed. The horseman takes his region from the nearest cowboy and stocks the whole tack ladder. Villagers.
  • Your own name tables drop into phc/names/, adding to the shipped ones rather than replacing them.
  • The family tree runs both ways, and My horses gained gene and allele pickers listing only what your roster holds.
  • F8 highlights nearby horses and marks the herd lead in red. F asks a ridden horse to dive, which is the only way to use Ocean-born's underwater breathing — it shares a key with vanilla's swap-hands by deliberate choice, and both are rebindable.
  • Fixed: Make egg did nothing (a widget sat on the button, eating the click), and a name tag put you on the horse while opening the rename window.

Performance

Walking toward a herd could freeze the whole machine: every newly-seen horse was baked twice inside the frame that first drew it. It is one bake per horse now, against a time budget, with a shared stand-in coat while a bake is pending and a 32-block detail distance — both knobs under coats. in client.toml.

What this does to a world you already have

  • It should load, and no world has been carried across yet. A gene that no longer exists is dropped and that locus returns to its default rather than breaking the save.
  • Spontaneous Breeding is gone as a gene — every horse breeds now — and the old dhampir gene with it; the Dhampir breed is how you get one.
  • Some breeds changed. Cream, pearl and champagne were rebuilt from per-breed frequencies, so pearl is on the Iberian breeds, Gypsy Vanner, Paint and Quarter Horse only, and the American Cream Draft is gold champagne rather than cremello. The Paint's HYPP rate rose. Anglo-Arabian, Australian Stock Horse, Azteca and Budyonny were each shorter than their own table row claimed. Horses already owned keep what they carry.
  • The new breeds are added to an existing breed-spawning.toml — a missing section is written back with its defaults, so the file need not be deleted.
  • Every horse renders about 10% bigger, because the body had been the undersized one all along — that is the tack-fit fix.
  • Horses no longer heal for free.
The testing tools ship, switched off

/testkit, /bond, the debug pens and the stall overlay are in the jar rather than compiled out, behind debug.tools in server.toml and client.toml. Off in a normal install.

Known rough edges

A mare that loses a pregnancy early may never be covered again — she reads as in heat and the stallion as having covers left, and nothing happens. Hand-feeding a foal for bond also ages it, since vanilla's feed on a baby ages it and the mod does not cancel that.

None of this has had a proper play session, and the last fourteen breeds have never been seen in a world. To be verified is the honest account of what has actually been watched working.

0.5.001

One folder for everything. Drop-in genes move into .minecraft/phc/ with the breeds and the settings, so nothing of the mod's is in config/ any more. 

.minecraft/phc/
  genes/                 your genes, from the gene creator
  breeds/                your breeds, from the breed designer
  breed-spawning.toml    which breeds your world has
  server.toml
  client.toml
Before you open a world

If you use drop-in genes, move them first. Take every .json out of .minecraft/config/horsegenetics/genes/ and put it in .minecraft/phc/genes/, then start the game. The old folder is not read. A world opened without the gene loaded loses it: every horse that carried it comes back without it, and moving the file afterwards does not bring it back. If you have no drop-in genes, there is nothing to do.

Everything else is 0.5.000, unchanged — replace that jar with this one.

0.5.000

Your world, your horses. Design a breed in the browser, drop the file into a folder, and the game spawns it; then decide which of the mod's own breeds your world keeps — down to none of them, so the only horses in it are the ones you made.

Make your own breeds

  • The breed designer walks through a breed one decision at a time — base colour, size, dilutions, white, eyes, magical markings, health, disorders, diet, temper, abilities, speed, jump, where it lives, price, name — with a horse of the breed on the right that you can reroll, and six more under it. Every number it shows comes from the mod itself, compiled for the browser. Export at any step; import to carry on.
  • Drop the file into .minecraft/phc/breeds/ and restart. It heads wild herds in the biomes you picked, and the cowboy, stables and breed spawn eggs know it too. The folder is made the first time the game runs, and the H menu's Breeds tab has a button that opens it.
  • A breed is exactly what you made it. Its horses carry the magic and the disorders you gave it and nothing else — no stray magical genes, no background rate of disease. That is true of the mod's own breeds now as well; the nine whose real-world sheets name disorders list them. Random magic turns up in one place: Feral Mixed, the unbred wild horse.
  • The idea underneath, on the philosophy page: a zombie horse is a zombie breed — rotted markings, a bad temper, a low speed score — not one indivisible zombie gene.

Decide which horses your world has

Settings now live in .minecraft/phc/, beside the breeds: server.toml, client.toml, and the new breed-spawning.toml, which lists every breed the mod ships with its biomes, how common it is and its hours — change any of them, or switch the breed off. One line switches all of them off at once, and Feral Mixed has its own section. A wild horse with nothing it is allowed to be simply does not spawn. How it works.

Horses

  • Eyes are inherited. Thirteen eye genes — iris, sclera and a heterochromia wedge for each eye, glowing irises and sclera, and a third eye. A splashed or champagne horse writes its eye colour onto itself, so the colour passes to foals that do not inherit the splash. Eye colour.
  • Twenty-seven behaviour genes — horses that sing, guard, lay eggs, ward off monsters, fear water, breed on their own, and more, each with its hazards written on its page.
  • Fifty new coat markings, and a black form for forty-two existing ones. Cleave became two genes: the marking, and which half it is on.
  • Fireproof and bird-boned protect horse and rider properly; molten hooves leaves glowing hoofprints; size is shown as a multiple of an ordinary horse, and a breed's size range decides whether its horses carry one size gene or two.
  • Hovering a gene in the custom spawn egg shows its picture beside the description. The egg's breed list starts at Feral Mixed and includes your own breeds.
  • The research shelf is a chest, stalls land a horse dead centre, a holding pen, and a run of fixes from three play sessions.

What this does to a world you already have

  • Your settings are not carried over. config/horsegenetics-server.toml and -client.toml are no longer read; copy any value you changed into the new files in .minecraft/phc/.
  • A 0.4.1 world should load, but none has been tried. The one gene whose alleles changed is Cleave: a horse carrying its old alleles comes back without the marking (the log says which) rather than breaking the world. Horses made before eye colour was inherited are expected to show brown eyes, blue-eyed splashed horses included — their eye genes did not exist when they were made.
  • New wild horses of a breed no longer carry stray magic or unlisted disorders. Horses you already have keep whatever they carry.
Not played yet

The breed spawn settings were checked on a dedicated server — the files are written and re-read live — but no horse has been watched spawning, or failing to, under them. The designer has been driven in a browser, not by a player. §0-CC, §0-CB and §0-CA are the checklist.

Twenty-two new markings, an engine that can read an SVG, and everything that piled up behind 0.3.7 — 0.3.8, 0.3.9 and 0.4.0 were built and never tagged, so their work ships here too.

Most ship as a pair — a pale dominant, and a fully recessive coloured form drawing the same shape in a colour that line of horses carries and passes to its foals. All of them are rare.

The engine can read an SVG

A gene could already carry a shape somebody drew, but only as control points typed in by hand. It now takes a real SVG path: every command including elliptical arcs, subpaths so a letter O has a hole, the fill rule, the transform list, the viewBox, and a stroke with caps, joins and dashes. Moonwing is the first marking in the mod that is an actual drawing rather than a rule for making one.

Three new shapes came with it, each because some marking needed a measurement nothing could make: FAN, bars that radiate from a point and widen as they go (everything periodic before this ran in parallel lines); NORMAL, which way the surface faces, for a rim light or a shell’s curvature sheen; and six new parameters covering veins that pool at their junctions, per-cell radial fades, tiled fields clipped into shingles, off-centre glints, and colour that wanders instead of sweeping.

Behind 0.3.7

  • A stall that killed horses. The detector leaked over its own front rail into the aisle, decided the room was not a room, and landed the horse inside the wall the sign was on. Both halves fixed.
  • Taming by hand, and a research shelf that accepts papers.
  • A Breeds tab — all of them, searchable, each with an entry showing a horse that breed can actually produce, where it lives, its build and height band, its disposition and what health to watch for. A breed sits under question marks until you have owned one, and the count keeps the honest denominator so you can see how much is left.
  • Bareback riding at the top bond tier — rewritten onto a real but invisible saddle, so jump and sprint work, which the first version could not do.
  • The cowboy and the horseman are they/them.

What this does to horses you already have

Nothing changes colour. All 1056 composed coats in the golden file are byte-identical to 0.4.0’s, so no gene that existed before this release paints differently. An older save loads and every horse in it keeps its coat, with the twenty-two new loci filled in at wild type — which also means no horse in an old world can be carrying one, so the new markings reach it only through newly spawned wild horses.

Forty-three existing genes did get rarer in the wild. Founder rates are budgeted across the whole set from each gene’s declared rarity tier, and adding twenty-two genes re-derived all of them — which also flattened a number of tables that had drifted above their tier. Banded Socks, Ocular, Tron and Picasso Marking each dropped by about a factor of seven. The full table, and the argument about whether those are the right numbers, is on to be verified §0-BM.

Not played yet

Every one of the twenty-two markings has been rendered and looked at as an icon — that is how four defects in them were caught — but none has been seen on a horse in the game, and nor has the drawing engine. §0-BL is the checklist.

0.3.6

Something to aim at: a thirty-task checklist that walks you through the whole mod, and a collection of every allele in it.

The checklist

Under the Getting started tab. Thirty-one tasks in seven groups — tame a mare, breed a foal, take a gene off a horse with a book, light a hay portal, bring a horse back from the dimension, use each whistle, bond with a horse, sign a transfer paper — and every unticked one tells you what to do. Ticking one says so in chat, with a running count.

It gates nothing. Build a research shelf on your first day and you tick two boxes at once; nothing is locked behind it.

The collection

A new Alleles tab: every allele of every gene, with the ones you have never met still under question marks. Every allele on a horse you tame or breed joins it, baseline alleles included — which is a different and much gentler thing than discovering a gene. See the checklist & the collection.

Papers are components, not lessons

Research papers are no longer read. Right-clicking one does nothing. A gene joins your database only when you have owned a living horse that carries it — tamed, bred, bought or given — and that same moment unlocks its gene carrot. Papers exist to be copied on a shelf and spent on a carrot.

The old route made a paper a shortcut past the animals: find one in a chest and you knew a gene you had never met.

Fixed

  • The Equine Research Shelf's window had its list drawn straight through its slots, and looked nothing like a Minecraft container. It is laid out off one set of constants now, and drawn in vanilla's own bevelled grey.

Getting started

Thirteen sections in the order you need them — tame one, notice you need a mare and a stallion, feed them both a golden carrot, take a gene off a horse with a book, file it on a shelf, buy one from the horseman instead if you would rather, splice it into a foal, and repeat. Then the hay portal, because a door made of hay is worth knowing about.

The pictures are the game’s own: item icons through the item renderer, and the horseman and the cowboy drawn as live entities, so you can recognise the villager you are looking for rather than read a sentence about his hat.

Also

  • The browser reopens where you left it — tab, scroll, search, sort and selection, all of it. It used to come back to the top of the first tab however deep you had been reading.
  • Copying a gene on the shelf takes time: one iron ingot’s smelt per rarity tier, so about ten seconds for a common gene and a minute for a mythic one. The arrow fills as it goes and the line underneath counts down.
  • Stables stopped generating inside mountains. They carve their whole footprint clear now instead of laying a thin skirt underneath. Needs a new world — an already-generated stable will not move.
One thing the tab does not say

A gene carrot is often described as “a 25% chance”, and that is not what the code does — it makes the fed parent pass the gene on with certainty, and what is uncertain is whether the foal shows it. The tab is written to the code. Which behaviour is actually wanted is gap 145.

The H menu stops crafting and becomes a reference, a bookshelf starts remembering genes, and five things a play session found get fixed.

Fixed

  • Two mares said nothing. A same-sex pair fed breeding carrots courted, spent the carrots and produced nothing, silently. They still try, and now they tell you why it did not work.
  • 121 error lines on every launch that meant nothing. Gone.

The Horse Browser makes nothing

The Crafting tab is now Recipes: every recipe the mod adds, drawn as its grid and its result, with a line saying what the thing is for. You read it there and you craft at a crafting table, like anything else. A category dropdown keeps the per-gene splice carrots from burying everything else.

Nothing became unobtainable — every one of those recipes already worked at a crafting table. The one thing the grid could uniquely do has moved into the world, below.

Research papers, and a shelf to keep them on

  • A book on a horse gives you a research paper for one gene that horse carries, chosen at random. You do not pick — which is what makes a paper something you found rather than paperwork. An ordinary horse has nothing to teach and costs you no book.
  • The Equine Research Shelf (1 bookshelf + 2 horse hair) files papers — one per gene, any number of genes — and copies any of them onto blank books for as long as the original stays in it. Take the original back and the copying stops. Breaking the shelf gives every paper back.

Smaller

  • Hover a gene in the custom spawn egg and it tells you what the gene does. Every gene has a summary now; seventeen did not before.
  • ♀ and ♂ after a horse’s name, coloured, so you can read sex across a paddock. Turn it off with nameplate.sexSymbol in the client config. It is drawn, not stored — the horse’s actual name is unchanged.
  • The spawn egg’s right-hand buttons are wider, and stop overflowing their labels.
Most of this has not been played

The shelf in particular has never been placed — block, menu and screen were all written in one pass. To be verified lists what to look at, and gap 143 says which parts are most likely to be wrong.

0.3.2

A one-line fix for the bug that made 0.3.0 and 0.3.1 unplayable: every client was kicked off the moment a horse came into view.

Replace 0.3.0 or 0.3.1

Both of those releases disconnect you on entering a world with Failed to encode packet 'clientbound/minecraft:custom_payload'. There is nothing wrong with a world made under them — the fault is in what the server sends, not in what it saved — so the same save works once the jar is swapped. 0.2.0 is unaffected.

  • An epigenome code outgrew the wire. A horse’s coat is sent to the client as its genotype and epigenome text, and the epigenome stores literal values for every gene that varies — so the genes added across 0.3.0 pushed a full code past 32 767 characters, which is the length Minecraft’s default string writer silently caps at. It throws rather than truncating, and a throw while encoding drops the connection. The coat packet now declares a cap with real room in it, as the other genome-carrying packets already did.
  • The same latent fault was in three other places — the stored genome on a preset spawn egg and a genome jar, which would have kicked whoever held one, and the genotype fields on the roster and spawn packets, which were already two thirds of the way to the same crash. All the caps now come from one place, so raising them is one edit.
  • The test that was meant to catch this now can. It was watching the one cap that had a number written beside it and could not see the implicit one next to it; it now guards every genome-code cap, and it fails while there is still half the budget left.

Confirmed 2026-09-09: 0.3.2 launches and makes a world (the owner), which closed the disconnect this release was for.

0.3.1

Two genes that make a horse frightening, nine coat genes repainted, and the categories reorganised so they mean something.

The night loci

  • Magic night temper — eight variants in two families. Four hunt and four flee, and each names who: riders, passive animals, monsters, or everything. By day every one of them is an ordinary horse. The monster-hunter is the only one most people would keep on purpose; the one that flees riders is catchable, tamable and rideable by day and will not let you near it after dark.
  • Magic night watch — five variants that are a ladder rather than a list. It stares from anywhere through walls; or closes to ten blocks first; or watches only while it can see you; or moves to where you cannot see it; or stands directly behind you. Any of them makes the horse silent on its feet at night.
  • The temper locus supersedes the watch locus whenever it has something to act on, so a horse never tries to stalk you and flee from you at once.
  • Both are entirely heterozygous in the wild: every feral horse carries exactly one variant and none of them shows it, so the plainest outcome each locus has is the one that cannot be caught.

Coat genes

  • Three genes stopped painting a ground over the whole horse — coccinella’s black, shieldback’s green, tribal ward’s pale. All three now mark the horse’s own colour, so two carriers are different animals.
  • Koi’s orange and dark varieties lay patches on the coat instead of tiling the whole horse. The white variety still covers, because that is the one whose point is the hard boundary between two fields.
  • Snow cloud has bare drifts the coat shows through, which is what its name always described.
  • Cleave’s detached spears are spear-shaped and close to the line, instead of splotches loose on the far half.
  • Opossum’s rear edge is dithered — the dots get rarer — rather than a gradient fade.
  • Nyxborn stars only what is genuinely black, so a bay keeps its brown shoulder unstarred.
  • Stained glass gained a grisaille palette — grey and white only, and the one palette that reads as glass on any base coat.
  • Shieldback and tribal ward’s “coloured” alleles now colour the marking. They previously varied only the ground, so removing it left them identical to their first allele.

Elsewhere

  • The magical categories were reorganised. They were derived from priority bands, which is a real fact about a gene that paints and an accident about one that does not — so one category held coat markings, milk buckets, spawn eggs and a horse that stares through walls. The non-painting genes are grouped by what they do now: yield and death, behaviour, trails and emissions, body stats.
  • Shadowcreature’s page has its preview back. It never had one — the page was written from the template for a gene that paints nothing.
  • An inverted mask no longer escapes its own parts, which corrects twelve genes (carried over from the unreleased work at 0.3.0 — see the notes there).

Notes

  • Not save-compatible with 0.3.0. Two new loci change the genotype code and every horse’s epigenetic slots. Start a new world.
  • The night loci have never run in a game. Two AI goals, a supersede rule and a sound suppression, all written against the sources and none of it seen. To be verified §0-AT is the checklist.
  • Still NeoForge, Minecraft 26.1.2 only. Both sides need the mod.

0.3.0

Genes that decide what a horse does. Eight new loci change nothing about how a horse looks and everything about how it behaves — how fast it swims, how long it lasts underwater, what it hits for, what it leaves behind when it dies, whether mobs avoid it or fight over it, how much meat it drops and how many times a day it can be milked. Six new coat genes come with them, and one new mask that asks the gradient chart what colour it actually made.

Mechanical genes

Eight loci that paint nothing at all. They exist so that a breed can be described by what its horses do rather than only by what colour they are.

  • Magic swim speed — how fast the horse moves in water, and nothing else. Completely unrelated to magic speed: a horse can be the slowest thing in the paddock and still cross a river faster than a boat.
  • Magic water breathing — how long before it starts to drown. Independent of swimming speed, so a ferry and a diver are two separate breeding projects.
  • Magic fighter — what it hits for. Wild type is 3 points; the gladiator allele adds a percentage and the wimp allele takes one off, and the two are added with opposite signs, so a wimp copy really does cancel a gladiator copy. Breeding a fighter means breeding the wimp allele out as much as breeding the gladiator in. No ceiling, but a wild gladiator carrier is only worth one to three extra points — every genuinely dangerous horse is one somebody made.
  • Magic on death — a lava source, a water source, or a creeper-sized explosion where the horse died. No feral horse has this: it is the one locus in the mod whose wild population is carriers only, so a horse that leaves a crater is always somebody's fault. Two different variants cancel each other, because “what happens here” cannot have two answers.
  • Magic item drop — 2–5 diamonds, an enchanted sword with the enchantment rolled at the moment of death, or a spawn egg that puts this horse back. The egg carries the genotype and the epigenome, so what comes out is a clone rather than a horse with the same alleles — the only thing in the mod that undoes a death. Nothing about the living animal shows any of it.
  • Magic mob aura — warding keeps hostiles about ten blocks off; baiting makes every hostile that can see the horse pick it over anything else, including its rider. One of each cancels outright. The baiting allele is the one that makes health and fighting worth stacking.
  • Magic meat — how much meat the horse leaves, added to whatever else it drops. So a diamond horse can also be a meat horse.
  • Magic milk volume — how many times a day it can be filled from. Unlike most of these a single copy shows, so a milky horse can be found rather than only bred. It governs the milk locus's water and lava too.

Coat genes

  • Nyxborn — every white marking the horse has is painted out in black, and then the whole of that black is dusted with tiny white points. A pinto goes in and a starfield comes out; the pattern underneath is only recoverable from the genotype.
  • Tron — neon run along every edge of the horse, full-bright so it holds its colour in the dark. It paints later than anything else in the mod, so whatever else the horse is wearing, the lines are on top. Two alleles: one solid colour, or a hue ramp running nose to tail.
  • Shadowcreature — the head, muzzle, ears and whole neck go flat black, the eyes burn gold with no white around them, and tapering tentacles run out of the join and down over the shoulder. The horse gives off a dim light.
  • Stained glass — black leading in straight lines with a quarter-strength wash of colour in each pane. The palette is the allele rather than an epigenetic value: one allele is blue, pink and purple, the other green, yellow and white, and a horse with one of each gets a window with all six in the tray.
  • Ocular — open eyes down the neck, each with a white, an iris in the line's own colour and a pupil. One copy gives a handful, two crowd the neck.
  • Bloodstained — red gathers along the bottom edge of the horse's markings, inside the white rather than around it, as though something had run down and settled where the white stops.

What a gene can now say

  • A gene can ask what colour the horse actually is. The new LUMA mask reads the colour the gradient chart produced, rather than the pigment behind it. That is a real difference and not a nicety: the LUT locus can hand a horse a chart whose black corner is violet, and such a horse has identical pigment everywhere and is not black anywhere. A gene that means “cover the black parts” has to ask the question a viewer asks. Fielded, yalia, voided and opalized now find the horse's white this way, so they look different from 0.2.0 — noticeably so on cream coats, where a perlino used to read as white and now reads as cream.
  • A gene can outline the horse. The new EDGE mask draws the rim of each body part's box — a wireframe. It is what Tron is built from.
  • A marking can have a bottom. spreadFrom lets a spread grow from one side only, which is the only way to ask for the lower edge of a marking rather than its whole rim.
  • Six new effect verbs — charges, breath, on_death, item_drop, mob_aura and combat — all available to a data-driven gene, not just to the built-in ones. See the effects block.
  • The attribute verb actually does something now. It had been parsed and ignored since it was written. Fixing that surfaced that its list named swim_speed, an attribute Minecraft has never had — the name never had to resolve to anything while the verb did nothing.

Notes

  • Not save-compatible with 0.2.0. Fourteen new loci change the genotype code and every horse's epigenetic slots. Start a new world.
  • An ordinary horse now kicks for 3 rather than 4. The baseline moved onto the fighter locus, so there is one number in one place instead of two that could disagree.
  • The mechanical genes have never run in a game. They compile and the suite is green; that is all. To be verified §0-AR is the checklist, and the riskiest items are the attribute add/remove cycle, the drowning accumulator and whether the mob-repel shove actually overcomes a pathfinding mob.
  • Still NeoForge, Minecraft 26.1.2 only. Both sides need the mod.

0.2.0

The gene registry roughly triples, a gene becomes a file rather than a Java class, the H menu grows from two tabs to four and gains a whole second screen, breeds become data, and two villagers move into the world.

Checked when the tag was cut (2026-09-08): the built jar was listed, and holds the common/ classes, all 85 gene files (suit.json present, collar.json and waves.json gone), the 49 breed files, the name tables, the four structure NBTs and the assets. ExtremeWhiteDominantGene and WhiteLockContribution were compiled in and PinkHairGene was not, so it was the current tree. The packaging bug that produced 0.1.0 had not come back.

Genes

The registry holds 156 loci at this tag, of which 85 are data-driven — a gene is now a JSON file with a mask stack, not a Java class, and 83 of those files are new since 0.1.0. They are grouped into nine magical families (core, body, ground, fields, spots, speckle, lines, hair, modifiers), which is the grouping the wiki and both gene editors offer.

  • New natural loci. Flaxen (a dosage, not folklore), sooty, pangare, rabicano, manchado, the Camarillo white W4, natural zebra (split off from brindle, which had been sharing its stripe field), a diet locus with twelve narrow appetites, and the dhampir — a recessive whose carrier is the advertisement.
  • Loci rebuilt. Bay is a shade continuum now (blood, bay, liver and seal off one score) rather than four names. Grey is a copy number with four rates instead of one. Dun was re-derived from a field guide — grullo goes blue and the leg bars stopped being bracelets — and its mane and tail gained the midtstol. MATP gained sunshine and snowdrop; KIT gained the booster group (W35, W32, W34); and the two splash loci swapped roles, because the common allele had been sitting on the wrong gene.
  • Disorders. The rest of the reference's disorder genes, including the first dominant ones, each with its own contribution to the body numbers.
  • Eye colour became its own channel: green and hazel eyes, both heterochromias, and a magic locus that only exists in the heterozygote. See eye colour.
  • Sex-linked genes, and the sex locus itself.
  • Genes that do something other than paint. A werewolf locus that swaps the entity at dusk, rainbow dust, a recessive particle locus, cutie marks, and extreme white dominant — the first gene here that paints nothing at all, and instead makes white final wherever it already is, so a horse carrying a tobiano and something loud reads as both rather than as the loud thing alone.
  • Removed. Pink hair (redundant beside mane colour, tail colour and the hair genes the magical import brought), and Waves — which was not a gene but Cleave backwards, and is an allele of it now.

The horse browser — the H menu

The browser had two tabs at 0.1.0, both of them reference material. It has four now, two of which are about your horses, and it is no longer the only screen: an “i” button on the horse inventory opens a second, six-tab screen about the one horse in front of you.

  • My horses (new) — every horse you own as one sortable, filterable table with clickable column headings. The filter box takes a real query language that reaches every piece of metadata a horse has, genes included: gene:SB1 finds carriers, not just the horses that show it. Each row draws the horse.
  • Breeding preview (new) — pick one of your mares and one of your stallions and read what they could produce at every locus, and how often. It is a Punnett square per gene rather than a rolled foal, grouped so the loci that pull on one trait arrive together.
  • Gene database and Crafting carry over, and the crafting tab's own button is visible again — the screen paints near-opaque panels across the window, and it had been painting them after the widgets. Chrome under, widgets and slots over.

The horse information screen behind the “i” is six tabs: Overview (name, barn name, sex, generation, breed, the four live body numbers, bond, disorders, and who bred, tamed and owns it), Genes (one row per locus, filtered by default to the loci this horse is not baseline at), Health (each gene's own contribution to speed, health, jump and size rather than the total, plus every disorder), Coat, Other genes (abilities, diet, eye colour, cutie marks), and Offspring — this horse's foals, then their foals, each generation a row of little horses.

Breeds, breeding and inheritance

  • A breed is a JSON file now, not Java — 49 of them at this tag — with a founder table, a stat curve, breed spawn eggs, a splice mark, and a browser-based breed designer.
  • Epigenetics became literal stored numbers carried on each allele copy, inherited with the allele and drifted slightly each breeding, rather than a derived quantity.
  • A mare can miscarry, and a breed lets go of horses that have bred far enough away from it.
  • Five themed splice carrots, and every gene page now shows how to craft that gene's own carrot.

The world

  • Two villagers. The cowboy is a village horse trader with a paddock, a restocking string and cheap horses; the horseman comes with the barn. They have a barn and a house between them, a work post each, and doors they can actually open. Nothing hostile targets the cowboy.
  • Transfer papers — sign one and it sells a named horse, pedigree and all.
  • Three generated stables that spawn with rare horses already standing in them. See generated stables for who built each one.

The coat engine

  • A new default gradient (horselut.png), which changed the coat palette under every gene at once, and four dilutions retuned to measured restriction targets afterwards.
  • A shadow pass at the end of phase 3, so no magical gene paints true black.
  • The paint order was rebuilt. Nine genes moved: the four modifiers that read somebody else's marking (fielded, yalia, voided, opalized) went above the markings they read, invert above those, and dhampir — which paints the whole horse white and sat above every data-driven gene — went to the bottom with suit and hood. The paint order is a new page that draws the whole stack from the registry at run time.
  • Fixes to patterns that were drawing the wrong horse: frame overo was drawing a dipped horse for three separate reasons; the two roans had been the same pattern at two strengths; white fading was unmasking the red a black horse hides, browning every marking on one; and both tobiano's and frame's coverage knobs were lying about what they controlled.

The browser tools and the wiki

  • The horse designer — common/ compiled to WebAssembly by TeaVM, so the browser runs the real genetics rather than a port of it. It is the spawn egg screen's twin, with names, JSON export and import, and a scale figure.
  • Both gene editors gained a randomize menu, per-gene locks and a family filter.
  • A wiki page per gene — 155 of them — each with its alleles, a Punnett square you choose the parents for, a spinnable preview of the gene on a coat it can actually be seen on, and its carrot recipe. Every gene also has a baked icon, photographed off the posed mesh.
  • The wiki split into three views — Gameplay, Coding, Science — with a real search across every page.
  • The LUT lab: try a gradient on the real pipeline before shipping it, click the chart to read the pigment at a point, and see which region of it a given coat actually reads.

Notes

  • Not save-compatible with 0.1.0. This is a development mod with no back-compatibility layer: genotype codes, attachment fields and the gene registry all changed. Start a new world.
  • Still NeoForge, Minecraft 26.1.2 only. Both sides need the mod.
