Natural gene / health

SLC5A3 (foal immunodeficiency syndrome)

A recessive lethal that gives you nothing to see. The foal is born looking perfectly healthy, runs with its dam for weeks, and then fails as the antibodies it borrowed from her run out and its own never arrive.

Crossing two of them

The one disorder that is common

Every other recessive lethal in this mod is a rarity you meet by accident. This one is not — not inside the breed it belongs to. Roughly two Fell Ponies in five carry a copy, so breeding two Fells together without checking is not unlucky, it is the ordinary case. The Dales Pony carries the same mutation.

That makes it the clearest argument in the mod for reading a horse’s papers before pairing it. With a gene database entry for both parents the cross is a decision; without one it is a coin you flip twice.

No founder is ever affected

The founder table lists only the clear horse and the carrier. A wild-caught horse is an adult that survived, so it can carry this and never have it, and the homozygote’s weight is simply absent from the table. The only way to produce an affected foal is to breed two carriers.

Where you will actually meet it

An unaffiliated wild horse carries it at well under one in a hundred. A Fell Pony carries it at thirty-eight in a hundred. That gap is deliberate and it is the whole point of per-breed pools: a disorder can be vanishing globally and commonplace inside one population, and a mod that only had one global number could not say so.

It paints nothing, and that is what makes it free

Every combination of this gene is an expression marked wildType. That reads oddly for a gene that kills a foal, and it is exactly right: wildType means changes nothing about the coat. Because none of the non-coat genes paints, Gene.affectsCoat() is false for all of them and they are left out of a horse’s texture key. What the gene actually does travels on the trait system instead.

Where the mod and the pathology disagree

The real disorder kills over weeks, not seconds: an affected foal nurses, looks well, and declines as maternal antibody wanes. The mod has no age model and no immune model, so it uses the ordinary LETHAL_AT_BIRTH path and the foal dies shortly after being born. That is a real simplification and it loses the cruellest part of the condition — that the foal appears healthy long enough to be named and hoped for. Flagged here rather than hidden.

What breaks

Two things fail together, which is what made the condition confusing before the causal variant was found. Affected foals cannot maintain a B-lymphocyte population, so they never build antibodies of their own; and they develop a progressive anaemia that does not respond to treatment. The combination is what kills them, and neither alone would.

The reason a foal looks well at first is maternal: it absorbs antibody from its dam’s colostrum in the first day of life and lives on that borrowed immunity. As it decays over the following weeks, nothing replaces it. The foal then meets ordinary environmental organisms with no defence, and dies of an infection that would not trouble a normal foal.

Why a fatal recessive is this common

It is the standard answer for a numerically small, closed, geographically isolated breed: a fatal allele that is completely silent in the heterozygote pays no penalty. Selection never sees a carrier, and in a small population drift and a few influential sires do the rest. A foundational screen found 82 carriers in 214 Fell Ponies — about 38% — and UC Davis has reported a figure near 40%.

That is also why the breed societies were able to act on it. A test has existed since 2010, and because carriers are ordinary healthy ponies the sensible management is not to cull them — in a breed this small that would be its own disaster — but to test and never pair two. The mod reproduces exactly that situation: the Fell Pony ships carrying it at the real rate, and the tool for dealing with it is information rather than avoidance.

Why the locus is named SLC5A3 and not “FIS”

Because there is a confirmed causal variant to name it after. Two loci in this mod — CVM and megaesophagus — are keyed on the condition instead, precisely because they have none, and both pages say so. This one does not need that hedge.

What needs verifying here

Breed rates carry the risk here: roughly two Fell Ponies in five and one Dales in five should carry slc5a3, two Fell carriers must give a foal that is born and then dies naming foal immunodeficiency syndrome, and no wild horse may ever be born affected.

FIS at a real breed rate (2026-09-17, NOT played)
