Item / stabling

Tickets

A ticket sends a horse home. Right-click one of your horses with one and it goes to its stall; the ticket is spent. The tier decides how far apart the two are allowed to be.

The mirror of a whistle

A whistle brings a horse to you; a ticket sends a horse to its stall. Two directions, two items, no overlap — which is how the two were settled against each other.

Using one

Right-click your horse with the ticket. It has to be your horse, it has to have a stall, and you have to be off its back. One use; nothing is spent unless the horse actually moved, and every refusal says which of those it was.

A ticket is not bound to a horse. The horse you click is the horse that travels, so one in your pocket covers whichever animal has got itself stuck on the wrong side of a river. The horse arrives standing in its stall — the middle of the floor if that is standing room, otherwise the first spot in the stall that is.

The four tiers

ItemReachRecipe (shapeless)
Blank ticket None — it is the base the others are made from, and says so if you try it on a horse. paper + horse hair
Basic ticket Within the overworld. Both the horse and its stall have to be there. blank ticket + horse hair
Bound ticket Within any one world, the horse dimension included — but the stall must be in the same world as the horse. basic ticket + horse hair + ender pearl
Interdimensional ticket Anywhere to anywhere. The only ticket that crosses between worlds. bound ticket + horse hair + eye of ender + blaze powder

“Bound” is a name from when the tickets were going to be bound to one horse each. They are not; the tier is reach, and the recipe ladder is what says so — an ender pearl to leave the overworld, an eye and blaze powder to leave the world you are in.

All four share one texture.

The holding pen ticket, and its sign

A horse you tamed a minute ago has no stall, so no ticket above can send it anywhere. The holding pen is for that horse. Hang a holding pen sign (a spruce sign and horse hair) on the wall of a pen - it measures the room behind it the way a stall sign does - and that pen is yours. Then right-click any horse you own with a holding pen ticket (a blank ticket and wheat) and it arrives in the middle of your pen, from any world.

  • One pen per player. Hanging a second sign moves your pen there; the old sign stays up as an ordinary sign.
  • Breaking the sign releases the pen.
  • Like every ticket it is one use, and it refuses - and is not spent - when the horse is not yours, you have no pen yet, or the pen has no room to stand.

The turnout ticket - the one that does not bring a horse home

A turnout ticket (a blank ticket, horse hair and a feather) sends a horse you own to the horse realm and gives it up there. It arrives at the realm's one entrance and is wild from that moment: untamed, unowned, healed, its tack handed back to you, and marked as a wild spawn so a band will take it in. It works from any world, and it is the only ticket with a destination you did not build.

It is the retirement route. A freedom stick already turns a horse out, but only while you are standing in the realm with it - so retiring a horse honestly meant leading it through a portal first. This is that trip in one right-click.

  • It is not reversible. Nothing fetches the horse back; if you want it again you catch and tame it like any wild horse.
  • Its stall is freed. A stall sign bound to it is unbound, so the stall can take another horse. No other release path does this, because no other one can reach a horse that still has a stall.
  • An ender whistle bound to it stops answering. That binding is permanent by design and is not refunded - see the whistles.
  • The horse loses nothing of itself. Name, genotype, epigenome, breed, pedigree, generation and bond all stay, which is what makes turning one out into a shared field a gift rather than a deletion - whoever tames it next gets the animal and its papers.
  • One use, and it refuses - unspent - when the horse is not yours, when it is being ridden, or when the realm is not loaded.

Where it lives

item/TicketItem is the item and its Tier — the tier and the tooltip words, nothing else. The interaction is server/TicketHandler, on EntityInteract, following the same shape as every other horse interaction in the mod: cancelled on both sides so the client does not predict a mount, and everything done server-side (see API notes).

The destination comes from StallData.forHorse, but the stall is measured again, live, from the sign rather than read back from the record. Two reasons, and the first one cost a horse: the stored span is a bounding box, and a box around any room that is not a plain cuboid contains the walls inside it. The second is that people rebuild stalls, so re-running the detector means a stall that has been widened or re-fenced since the sign went up is the stall the horse arrives in. The chunk is pulled in first, because a horse teleported into unloaded terrain is the failure that looks exactly like a horse that was deleted.

If there is nowhere to stand, the ticket refuses and is not spent — and "somewhere to stand" means somewhere this horse's whole box fits. It used to settle for dead centre when nothing fitted, which is a horse placed inside a block; now it refuses, and every ticket writes a [trace] ticket line giving the room it found and the spot it chose. The version before this scanned the bounding volume and, finding nothing, fell through to the sign’s own block — flush against a wall, with wall material at head height, and the one position in the routine never checked for anything. The horse suffocated. A refusal the player can read beats a horse in a wall.

The holding pen is the same machinery pointed at a player instead of a horse: item/HoldingPenSignItem writes a PenRecord (owner, dimension, sign) into StallData, keyed by the player so a second sign replaces the first, and item/HoldingPenTicketItem is handled in TicketHandler.sendToPen - the same live measure, the same dead-centre StallDetector.landingSpot, the same arrive. Breaking the sign goes through StallSignHandler.onSignBroken, which now releases a pen as well as a stall. The ticket’s texture is the ticket’s art hue-rotated, as a placeholder.

item/TurnoutTicketItem is handled in TicketHandler.turnOut, and is deliberately two existing pieces bolted together rather than a third implementation of either: arrive for the travel (the same particles, sound and lead handling as every other ticket) and server/HorseRelease.makeWild for the release, which is the single definition of what wild means and the only thing that remembers the temper reset and the wild-spawn mark. The order is load-bearing - move first, release second - so the tack is handed to a player still standing in the world the horse left, rather than dropped on the realm floor where they cannot reach it. It is also the only release that clears the horse’s StallRecord: the freedom stick works only inside the realm and HorseRealmFeral only fires on a horse already there, so neither can ever meet a horse that still has a stall. It has no destination to measure - the realm has one entrance at HorseRealm.arrivalSpot() on ground the dimension guarantees - so it is the one ticket with nothing that can fail late. It shares the ticket texture too.

What needs verifying here

Only the awkward cases are left open — a ticket spent on a horse whose bound stall is in an unloaded chunk, and an interdimensional one used from the horse dimension — where the failure is a ticket that is consumed and delivers nothing rather than refusing out loud — and now a second way to spend one, from the horse browser, which picks the ticket for you.

Spending a ticket from the browser (2026-09-25, NOT played)

The My horses table has a Send home button per row, and it spends these items without them ever being held. It does not restate the rules — the tier check, the live re-measure of the stall and the refuse-without-spending guarantee are all still TicketHandler's, called into rather than copied, because a second copy would drift into teleporting a horse inside a wall.

What is new is which ticket goes. The button spends the cheapest one in the inventory that can actually make the trip, preferring a holding-pen ticket for a pen trip. That ordering rides on TicketItem.Tier being declared cheapest-first; reorder the enum and it would quietly start spending the dearest ticket in a player's pocket.

  • Cheapest first. Hold a basic and an interdimensional, send an overworld horse to an overworld stall. Pass: the basic is the one that goes.
  • The tier still bites. Hold only a basic, with the horse in the Nether. Pass: a refusal naming the interdimensional ticket, and nothing spent.
  • The pen prefers its own ticket. Hold a holding-pen ticket and a written one, send a stall-less horse. Pass: the holding-pen one goes, and the horse lands in the pen.
