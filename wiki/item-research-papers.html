Item / knowledge

Research papers

A paper naming one gene and one allele pair — Agouti: A/a, not just Agouti. Spend it on a Known Gene Splice carrot and that is the pair the carrot hands on, which is the only way to breed for a specific combination on purpose. It is not something you read.

What it does

A paper is the write-up of one combination — one gene, and the two alleles you are being told about. Spend it on a Known Gene Splice carrot and the carrot hands that exact pair on to a foal.

The tooltip names the pair, says whether it is homozygous or heterozygous, gives the gene’s rarity, and says what that particular combination does to a horse — not what the locus is about in general.

A pair, not a gene

This is the change worth understanding, because it is what makes papers worth collecting rather than ticking off. Two papers for the same gene are two different items.

  • Cream: Cr/n is a carrier paper. Its carrot hands the foal one copy, so the foal carries cream and may not show it.
  • Cream: Cr/Cr is a true-breeding paper. Its carrot hands on two, so the foal is cream whatever the other parent brought.
  • Particle: Sflm/Gflm is a compound pair — two different variants, neither of them the ordinary one. Only a horse can teach you one of these.

A/a and a/A are the same paper. Which parent contributed which copy is not a fact about the horse, so it is not a fact about the paper either: the two normalise to one thing, stack in a slot, and take one place on a shelf.

Why this replaced the one-paper-per-gene version

A paper used to name a whole locus, and the carrot built from it took whichever pair convention had picked. That was wrong twice over. A breeder standing in a field is asking “what does A/a do here?”, not “what is agouti?” — and on a wide locus the convention was a wall. The particle gene has around forty alleles, and the old carrot could only ever hand over the first one, so no amount of playing would get you a soul flame on purpose. Now every allele has a paper and every paper has a carrot.

A paper is not something you read

Right-clicking one does nothing. It used to add its gene to your database and unlock that gene's carrot — which made a paper a shortcut past the animals: find one in a chest and you knew a gene you had never met. Your gene database is now earned only by owning a living horse that carries the gene (tamed, bred, bought, given), and that same moment is what unlocks the carrot.

So a paper is a component. It goes into an Equine Research Shelf to be copied, and it goes into a gene carrot to be spent.

Four ways to get one

  • Find it. Research papers turn up in any chest, vanilla or another mod’s, in about three chests in ten. Which gene a found paper names is weighted by rarity, so the common genes come up far more often. It used to be eight kinds of vanilla chest at not quite one in five, which on a large modded server meant almost none — see every chest, any mod. A chest gives you a carrier or a true-breeding paper, never a compound one.
  • Take it off a horse. Right-click a horse while holding a book and you get a paper for one pair that horse actually carries, at random. You do not choose which — that is the point, and it is why a paper is a discovery rather than paperwork. A completely ordinary horse has nothing to teach and costs you no book. This is the only route to a compound pair, so an odd horse is worth a book even when you already know the gene.
  • Copy one you already have. File a paper into an Equine Research Shelf and that shelf can copy it onto blank books for as long as the original stays in it. One slot per pair, so a locus you have studied properly takes several.
  • Buy or trade for it. The equestrian supplier sells them at its last two tiers, on the same carrier-or-true-breeding terms as a chest. Papers can also be handed to other players — which is the point of being able to write them. Knowledge is shareable rather than hoardable.
How many papers is that?

Two per variant allele per locus, plus every compound pair a horse can show you. The exact number is not written down here on purpose — it moves with every gene anybody adds. Each gene’s own page carries the count for that locus on its gene-carrot card, asked of the mod rather than typed.

The component and the routes

research_paper carries a research_gene data component holding a common/genetics/ResearchTopic — a gene key and two allele tokens. Right-clicking it does nothing; the gene database is earned by owning a living horse (server/GeneDiscoveryHandler), never by reading. A paper is an ingredient.

Why it is strings, and where it normalises

ResearchTopic holds tokens rather than Allele objects because a paper outlives the gene it names: a datapack gene removed, a world opened without the mod that added it. Every resolution — gene(), pair(), splice() — answers null instead of throwing, because the alternative is an exception inside somebody’s inventory.

The pair is put in canonical order in the record’s constructor, by handing it to AllelePair, which has always sorted its two slots by declaration order. So A/a and a/A are equal, produce the same token() — <geneKey>|<a>|<b> — and stack. That token is the key the research shelf, the menu sync and the block entity’s saved pick all use, which is why it is a flat string and not a record in three more places.

The one piece of back-compat in this repo

research_gene used to be a bare String gene key, and there are papers in chests, shelves and villager windows holding one. CLAUDE.md hard rule 6 refuses legacy shapes outright; this is the deliberate exception, asked for by name. A stored string still decodes, through ResearchTopic.wholeGene, as the homozygous non-wild pair for that gene — the strongest thing a whole-gene paper could have meant. It falls back to the carrier pair on a locus whose homozygote the gene forbids, because a paper naming an impossible pair would craft an inert carrot.

The component keeps its old registry id for the same reason. Renaming it to research_topic would be tidier and would silently drop the component off every existing paper, leaving blanks. The whole of the compatibility is one Codec.either(record, string) in data/ResearchTopicCodecs; encoding always writes the map form, so a world loaded once never writes a bare string again.

It is covered by a gametest, old_papers_still_read, and not by JUnit: a component whose persistent codec refuses its stored value is dropped on load with a warning nobody reads and no crash, and the NeoForge module’s test classpath deliberately carries no Minecraft, so NbtOps and a real ItemStack only exist inside a booted game.

  • Chest loot — injected at 30% into every chest table in every namespace by a global loot modifier, with the gene weighted by its rarity tier (data/loot/AddResearchPaperModifier). It was 18% across eight named vanilla tables until 2026-09-24; what replaced the list is a regex condition. The owner calls these “gene books”, after what the shelf copies them onto.
  • Off a horse — server/GeneBookFromHorse, on EntityInteract with a book. It collects every locus the horse is not homozygous-baseline at (the same test discovery uses) and picks one uniformly, writing down the pair the horse actually carries. No JSON recipe: the result depends on the animal, and this is the only source of a compound pair.
  • The equestrian supplier — data/loot/SetRandomGeneFunction with target: "paper", run as a trade’s given_item_modifiers so the offer is rolled once per restock rather than under the player’s cursor. Its homozygous flag narrows the pool to the true-breeding half.
  • Traded from another player — which is the point of the second route: knowledge is shareable rather than hoardable.

Which pairs a source may hand out

A gene is drawn weighted by rarity, as it always was; the pair inside it then comes from ResearchTopic.lootPool — for each variant allele, the carrier pair variant/wild and the true-breeding pair variant/variant, each filtered through canOccur and sexConsistent.

Deliberately not every combination the locus allows (owner’s call). A forty-allele locus has eight hundred pairs, and one of them being in this chest is not a find, it is noise; eighty is a collection. The compound pairs still exist and still make a carrot — a horse hands you one, which is the route that is supposed to be interesting.

The recipe reads the pair off the paper

server/recipe/KnownGeneSpliceRecipe resolves to a ResearchTopic rather than a Gene, and emits topic.splice(). It refuses a grid whose paper does not isResolved() — a retired gene, or a pair the locus says cannot occur — because refusing to resolve is visible and an inert carrot is not.

What the plan expected to die, and did not

This page’s Roadmap tab predicted that Gene.geneCarrotHomozygous() and CarrotEffect.defaultSpliceFor would both stop being needed once the player picked both alleles. They survived, because that plan had the shelf binding two single-allele papers into a book, and the shipped shape puts the pair on the paper instead — which leaves three callers that have a gene and no paper in hand: the Horse Browser’s recipe ghost (menu/SpliceRecipeDisplay), each gene page’s gene-carrot card through web/DesignerApi.geneCarrotJson, and the scientist’s carrot trades, which draw a gene and never see a paper at all. For those it is now ResearchTopic.defaultFor: an example pair, not a claim about every carrot for the locus. Both the card and the browser say so in those words.

The shelf keys on the pair

EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity files one paper per pair, not per gene, so Agouti: A/A and Agouti: A/a sit side by side — which is the whole reason to file them, since those two craft different carrots. topicOf, topicsIn and holdsElsewhere all key on ResearchTopic.token(), the Copy tab’s rows read Agouti: A/a, and copyTicks is still the gene’s rarity — what is rare is the locus, and a carrier paper is no easier to transcribe than a true-breeding one.

The carrot says which pair it carries

A known_gene_splice_carrot has always carried the right data and never shown it, so every one of them looked identical in the inventory. That was tolerable while the pair was a fixed convention per gene and is not now that a player can hold two carrots for one locus that do different things. All eleven breeding carrots therefore share one class, item/BreedingCarrotItem, whose only job is a tooltip built from the carrot_effects tokens — so a combination carrot lists every effect it merged, and a carrot for a gene this build no longer has says so in red rather than being quietly inert.

Verified

A research paper off a book works (owner-confirmed in play, 2026-09-10).

The Gameplay and Coding tabs agree that a paper is not read (settled 2026-09-24 against ResearchPaperItem, which has no use override at all, and GeneDiscoveryHandler, which is the only writer of the database). The Coding tab was the stale one. This was open check 302.

The pair swap, in play (NOT played)

Open, shipped 2026-09-24: research papers now name one allele pair, and the whole loop is unplayed. Every piece of it is green in tests and in a gametest, which covers the parts that fail invisibly and covers nothing about whether it reads right. In the order that can be wrong without saying so:
  • An existing paper in the owner’s live world. This is the one that matters most and the only one the owner can check and nobody else can: the server at neoforge-26.1.2/run/ and her own save have papers written before the change. Each should now read Gene: X/X rather than Blank, file into a shelf, and craft a carrot. The gametest proves the codec; it cannot prove her actual save.
  • Take a book to an odd horse. The paper should name the pair that horse carries — including a compound pair such as Particle: Sflm/Gflm, which no chest can produce. Chat says which; the tooltip should agree.
  • Craft two carrots for one locus from a carrier paper and a true-breeding paper, and check the tooltips differ. This is new (item/BreedingCarrotItem): before today every Known Gene Splice carrot looked identical in the inventory. Then feed each and confirm the foals differ the way the pairs say they should.
  • File both into an Equine Research Shelf. Two rows, not one, reading Gene: X/n and Gene: X/X; each copies onto a blank book; a duplicate of either still refuses the slot.
  • A chest and the supplier. Found and bought papers should only ever be carrier or true-breeding — a compound pair out of a chest means ResearchTopic.lootPool is not what the draw is reading.
  • Does a shelf full of pairs still read? Lowest priority, and the one the flat-list decision was made on: the Copy tab is five rows and the list is now much longer than it was. If it is unusable, grouping the rows under a gene heading was the rejected alternative.
