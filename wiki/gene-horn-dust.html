Magical gene / recessive / sheds from the horn, paints nothing

Horn dust

Two copies and a unicorn's horn sheds a fine dust that falls past its face, all the time, in the horn's own colours — a two-tone horn's dust fades from one of its colours to the other as it falls. On a horse without a horn this gene does nothing at all.

Crossing two of them

Finding one

Recessive: one copy shows nothing. In the wild a horse carries two copies or none — about one in twelve has two — so roughly one wild unicorn in twelve sheds dust. As with horn glow, most horses with two copies have no horn, so an ordinary horse can be the parent that gives your unicorn its dust.

What it looks like

A steady, fine fall of coloured motes from along the horn, standing or moving, drifting down about a block and a half to two blocks before they fade. The colour is always the horn's: whatever horn colour makes it, shade and chaos included.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill. The dust is only a particle; it leaves nothing on the ground.

An emitter that asks the horn

abilitiesFor returns one GeneAbility.Emitter, and only when the horse is Dst/Dst and GrownParts.of returns a horn. Its two colours are that horn's baseTint and tipTint, so the dust cannot disagree with the horn that is drawn. That needs the whole horse's epigenome, not this locus's (it has no numbers of its own), which is what GeneEpigenetics.epigenome() was added for; it is null when the question is asked of a genotype rather than a horse, and GrownParts then answers with midpoints.

The particle falls

Vanilla's coloured dust hangs where it appears: it randomises its own direction on spawn and multiplies its speed by a tenth, so no velocity sent with it survives. horsegenetics:horn_dust (ModParticles.HORN_DUST, HornDustOptions) is a type of the mod's own whose client particle, HornDustParticle, is vanilla's DustColorTransitionParticle with gravity set and a 30–50 tick life. The sprites are vanilla's generic_*, referenced, not copied.

The horn anchor

GeneAbilityHandler.anchorPoint gained a "horn" case: the head point, lifted by a random share of a horn's height. Like every anchor there it is estimated from the live bounding box — the server does not know the head's animated pose — so a grazing unicorn's dust starts where its horn would be if it looked up. It is not in AbilityType.EMITTER_ANCHORS, the list gene files may name: adding it there would make it part of the data format and the creator's parity surface, and no file gene has a horn.

There is no real locus here

A magical gene with no real counterpart. It asks the horn for its colour instead of having alleles of its own for it because the owner's brief was dust “that matches the horn's color”: a second colour system would be a second thing to keep in step, and would fail silently the first time the two disagreed.

What needs verifying here

No horn dust has been seen in a running game: the particle's fall, its density and where the horn anchor puts it are all tuned by arithmetic rather than by eye.

Does it fall from the horn?

Spawn a Horn/Horn Dst/Dst horse and watch it standing and walking. The motes should start along the horn, not at the eyes or above the ears, and fall rather than hang. The anchor lift (0.12–0.30 of the box height above eye level) and HornDustParticle.GRAVITY are the two numbers to tune. Check also on a tiny and a huge horse, and on a grazing one.

Its colours

A red horn's dust should be red, a two-tone horn's should fade from base to tip colour, a chaos horn's should match the horn exactly. The particle randomises each mote's brightness a little, as vanilla dust does.

Is it too much, or too little?

EMIT_CHANCE of 0.35 a tick is about seven motes a second per horse. It is meant to be a fine steady fall, not a cloud; judge it on one horse and on a pen.
