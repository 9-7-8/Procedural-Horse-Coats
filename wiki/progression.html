Gameplay / progression

The checklist & the collection

Two answers to “what do I do now?” — a checklist that walks you through everything the mod can do, and a collection of every allele in it with the ones you have never met still under fog.

The checklist

Not a page of its own any more. Every task sits under the sub-chapter of Getting started that teaches it — the box for hanging a stall sign is directly beneath the paragraphs about hanging one — and each unticked one carries a line saying what to actually do. The count is ProgressTask.values().length, across the chapters below, from watching a wild band to signing a transfer paper.

A Checklist section at the end of the contents is the overview: the running total, a bar, and only what is still outstanding, grouped by chapter. A chapter with every box ticked drops out of it entirely, so that section shrinks as you play.

A finished sub-chapter goes gold

A sub-chapter whose every checklist item is ticked is painted amber in the contents list down the left. It is the one place the page says “you are finished here” without your having to open it and count boxes, and it is the reason every sub-chapter carries at least one task: one with nothing to do could never be finished, so an empty task list is deliberately never coloured.

It gates nothing. Nothing in the mod asks whether a task is done. A player who reads this wiki and builds a research shelf on their first day ticks two boxes at once and is not stopped — the moment a checklist gates content it stops being advice and starts being homework.

Every task is also an advancement

Ticking a box off is how this mod earns you a real Minecraft advancement — the toast in the corner, the line in chat, a card in the advancement screen under a Horse Genetics tab of its own, and a name /advancement and any datapack can use. There is one per task, plus one per chapter and a root that holds the tab, and nothing about them is hand-written: they are baked off the same enum the book’s boxes are drawn from, so the two lists cannot drift.

The tree is one tab, eight branches. The root is earned by ticking anything at all; each chapter node by ticking anything in that chapter, so the tree lights up as you wander into a subject rather than only when you have exhausted it; and each task hangs off its chapter. A handful are purple challenge frames — identical twins, a mutation, a dhampir tamed, steering a horse bare — and a handful gold goals, for the moments something opens up. The card’s description is the first sentence of the same hint the book gives you.

The mod no longer says anything itself

It used to print its own green [✓] line with a running count. That is gone: the advancement announces itself, in vanilla’s words, through vanilla’s settings — so a player who wants silence can turn toasts or the announceAdvancements gamerule off and be obeyed, which a hand-rolled chat line never let them do. Two announcements of one event is one too many.

A save from before this pops them all at once

The first login on a world whose boxes were ticked before the advancements existed awards every one of them in one go, so expect a run of toasts. It happens once: an advancement already held is never re-awarded. The catch-up is on purpose — a task fires exactly once, ever, so without it those advancements could never be earned at all.

The chapters are:

  • Horses in the wild — first, because every horse a player meets is wild until they tame one. Where the breeds are and founding one from a spawn egg; a wild horse drifting over to food and galloping for its favourite; taming from the saddle and from the ground; being kicked by one, and watching one bolt; reading a wild horse's place in its band. Then the band itself — sparring, a dam fetching her foal, a stallion driving a stray mare back in and later losing the band to a challenger, rank settled at the water, grooming partners standing head to tail, grazing, and a meat-eater hunting.
  • Husbandry — what a horse is and naming it, feeding and watering and healing, then the whole injury-to-mended thread — a horse of yours getting hurt, putting food into a hungry one, and seeing it the rest of the way back to full — grooming and what horse hair becomes, bonding to each of its three tiers and the two things riding bare teaches you (that it bonds exactly as well as a saddled ride, and that at the top tier the horse steers without tack), stalls and holding pens and the four tickets — including hanging a stall on a double gate, the doorway wide enough to lead a horse through — the four whistles, dyeing your tack, and milking a mare. Nothing about breeding at all: keeping a horse is a whole game before genetics is.
  • Basic breeding — heat and natural covering, reading a mare with a vet's kit, pregnancy and foaling and twins, filling and using a seed jar, and gelding — having one done, owning one however he got that way, and feeling him settle to you faster than a stallion would. No golden carrots: the first breeding a player does is the one the horses arrange themselves.
  • Genetics — taking a gene off a horse with a book, earning one into your database, the research shelf, the gene carrot, the splice carrots, and the hay portal as a showroom for genes you have not bred yet.
  • Magical horses — its own chapter rather than a corner of genetics, because a magical gene paints after the coat has resolved instead of restricting pigment on the way, and it arrives by its own routes. What one is and the magical splice carrot; finding and taming a dhampir of either strain, the seal brown nine in ten and the white one in ten; a magical herd, where every horse carries and shows the same single magical gene; and the two traits that change how you keep a horse at all — burning in daylight, and drinking blood.
  • Breeding projects — a foal carrying something it does not show, one that breeds true, and line breeding for a recessive.
  • The villagers — trade with an equestrian, meet a cowboy, sign a transfer paper. The trade task takes any of the four, on purpose: which post a player happens to put down first should not be the difference between a ticked box and an unticked one.
Some boxes tick because you watched

Most of the wild chapter is horses doing things to each other, which a player cannot perform. Those tasks complete through HorseProgress.completeForWatcher — the nearest player within twenty-four blocks, with line of sight, is credited. Their titles say watch for that reason: an unticked box phrased as an instruction, for something nobody can instruct, reads as a bug. The line-of-sight test is deliberate too — a box that ticked for a fight behind a hill would be claiming you had seen something you had not.

Every item in the mod has a task

Deliberately, and it is worth keeping true as items are added: every registered item a player can obtain in survival is named by at least one checklist task. The only items without one are the custom and preset horse spawn eggs, which have no recipe and no trade and exist for creative and debugging.

The collection

The Alleles tab is every allele of every gene in the mod. The ones you have met are named and lit; the ones you have not are ??? and a row of question marks the right length. Each gene shows how many of its alleles you have, and the header counts the lot.

Meeting an allele is not the same as discovering a gene

Every allele on any horse you tame or breed joins the collection — both copies, at every locus, baseline alleles included. That is a record of what you have laid eyes on, and the plain wild-type alleles count exactly as much as the strange ones.

Discovering a gene is the other thing and is deliberately harder: it takes owning a living horse that carries something other than the plain baseline there — tamed, bred, bought or given — and that is also what unlocks the gene's carrot recipe. A research paper will not do it; papers are components, not lessons. An ordinary horse fills in a lot of your collection and discovers nothing.

So the two tabs answer different questions. The gene database is what can I make?; the collection is what is left out there?

Tasks are an enum in common/

common/progress/ProgressTask is the vocabulary: an id, a title, a hint and a group, with no Minecraft in it, so it is testable without a game and it survives the version port. The hooks that complete a task are necessarily Minecraft-side and live next to whatever event already existed — taming in the tame handler, foals in the breeding handler, whistles in the whistle item, crafting in ProgressHooks.

ProgressTask.Group is the chapters of the book, not a filing system of its own, and it is only used for grouping the summary section. Which sub-chapter a task belongs to is held the other way round, in TutorialPage.Step.tasks() — the page owns the mapping because the page is what draws it, and that is what ClientProgress.allDone reads to decide whether a contents row is painted gold. A task in the enum but in no Step would show only in the summary and could never finish a sub-chapter, so the two lists are kept in step by hand.

Every task is wired, and a check says so

A checklist with an item nobody can tick is worse than a shorter checklist: it reads as a bug and the player cannot tell which one. Because the enum is in one module and the hooks are scattered across another by design, nothing but a sweep can prove they are all reachable — node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/check-progress-tasks.mjs is that sweep, and it fails by name.

HorseProgress.complete(player, task) is the whole API a handler uses. It swallows a client-side player, a null and a server-less world, because it is called from a dozen files and the twelfth call site will be careless. HorseProgressData is the SavedData behind it, keyed by player UUID beside the gene database, and completing is idempotent — the first time is the only time anything happens.

One trigger, and a hundred baked advancements

What happens on that first time is ProgressTaskTrigger, this mod’s single CriterionTrigger (horsegenetics:progress_task, registered in ModTriggers). A trigger per task would be a hundred classes saying the same thing; instead the condition names the task, and three shapes of it cover the whole tree:

ConditionMatchesUsed by
{"task": "tame_mare"}that one taskevery leaf
{"chapter": "wild"}anything in that chapterthe eight chapter nodes
{}any task at allthe tab’s root

The JSON under data/horsegenetics/advancement/ is generated by node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-advancements.mjs, which reads the enum and rewrites the whole directory — so a deleted task loses its advancement too. Titles and descriptions are literal text components rather than translation keys, because the English already lives in the enum and a lang key would be a second copy of it kept in step by hand. The one thing the bake cannot derive is the icon: those are a hand-written map in the script, validated against the mod’s own item definitions and against vanilla’s item list read out of the patched jar — an icon naming an item that does not exist makes the advancement fail to load silently, with nothing in the log.

Add a task, re-run the bake

check-progress-tasks.mjs now fails on a task with no advancement and on an advancement no task backs, alongside its original sweep for a task nothing can complete. A new checklist item is therefore three things: the enum line, the hook, and an icon in the bake — and the check names whichever you forgot.

HorseProgressData.awardEverythingDone, called from ProgressHooks.onLogin, fires the trigger for everything a player has already ticked. It is what lets advancements arrive after the fact on a save that was played without them; awarding one a player holds does nothing, so it is safe every login rather than once.

The collection is a second set, on purpose

GeneDatabaseData holds two things per player now: the book of discovered genes, and a flat set of geneKey|token for alleles seen. They are separate because sharing one map would have made one behaviour quietly become the other — an entry in the book is what knows() tests, so recording baseline alleles there would have unlocked every gene in the mod off one ordinary horse.

Both ride the same GeneDatabaseSyncPayload, which grew a third field, and both are mirrored client-side in ClientGeneDatabase. collect() is called from GeneDiscoveryHandler.discoverFrom, which is already the one place that sees a genotype the player has earned.

Getting started, now seven chapters (2026-09-17, NOT played)

Thirteen tasks now tick because you were watching, and that is the whole risk

Most of the new wild chapter is horses doing things to each other, which a player cannot perform, so those tasks are credited by HorseProgress.completeForWatcher: the nearest player within WATCH_RADIUS (24 blocks) who has line of sight. Nothing else in the mod is credited that way, and it can fail in both directions at once — never firing because the chosen nearest player was the one behind a tree, or firing for something that happened off-screen.

The one test that covers the mechanism: stand in the open beside a wild band and wait for a spar or a grooming pair, and confirm a chat line arrives. Then put a solid wall between you and a band at the same distance and confirm nothing arrives. If the second one ticks, hasLineOfSight is not doing what the comment in HorseProgress says it does — it is flagged there as unverified on this NeoForge version.

A dhampir biting you also ticks Get kicked by a wild horse

Known and deliberate, and the cheaper of two bad options. Get kicked is credited from the damage event rather than from HorseMeleeGoal, because the goal knows it swung and not that anything was hit — a creative player gets kicked at and takes nothing, and ticking that box would be a lie. But a blood-drinker's bite is also a mob_attack from an untamed horse and is indistinguishable at that event. Excluding it needs a marker on the bite's damage source (gap 264).

The tab is seven chapters of sub-chapters now — Horses in the wild, Husbandry, Basic breeding, Genetics, Magical horses, Breeding projects, The villagers — and the checklist is no longer a section of its own. Every task sits under the sub-chapter that teaches it, and a Checklist section at the end carries the running total and only what is still outstanding. Pass: the contents list shows seven chapter headings with their sub-chapters indented beneath, every sub-chapter opens, and nothing runs off the panel at GUI scale 4 — which is a real risk now that one chapter carries eleven sub-chapters where the longest used to carry eight.

The wild chapter moved to the front, and taming moved into it. That is a teaching-order change with one visible consequence: Tame a mare and Tame a stallion are in the Horses in the wild group now, so they appear under that heading in the Checklist summary rather than under Husbandry. Their ids did not change, so an existing save keeps its ticks — confirm that on a world that already had them ticked, because a task id is what is saved and a mistaken rename here would silently blank somebody's progress.

A sub-chapter whose every box is ticked is painted amber in the contents list. The easy thing to get subtly wrong is the overlap: the selected row is drawn over the amber, so check that a finished sub-chapter still reads as selected when you click it, and that the hover still shows. Milk a mare is the cheapest one to finish — it carries a single task.

Twenty-four tasks are new (the count itself is ProgressTask.values().length), and tools/check-progress-tasks.mjs proves only that something can reach each one, never that it fires at the right moment (gap 148). In rough order of how quietly each could be wrong:

  • The four hooks that needed a firing guard. Each fires once per event by a condition rather than by the event existing, so each can stick on or off. Watch two horses groom each other hangs off a new settled flag in GroomAndRest, because that goal re-sets the pair's rotation every tick for the whole 6–12 second rest; Watch a horse hunt sits inside the existing kill branch, because hunt() runs once a swing; Put food into a hungry horse is gated on Hunger.seeksFood being true beforehand at all three feeding sites, so an idle nibble at full does not count; and Mend a horse all the way back up is gated on health having reached max. The grooming one is the one to watch: if it never ticks, the flag is never being reset.
  • Watch two horses spar completes twice per bout, because both horses run their own Spar goal. Completing is idempotent so only the first says anything in chat — confirm you get one chat line, not two.
  • The two find tasks ride the 30-tick care tick — Find a dhampir and Find a magical herd. Both test a breed label (lineage().components().contains("dhampir"), and BreedLineage.isMagical()) before doing any player scan. The dhampir test is a string spelled in two files with no shared constant (gap 265), so a breed-file rename breaks it silently. Confirm both tick, and that a spliced or magical-variant dhampir line still counts.
  • A wild-chapter box must not tick for your own horse. Have a wild horse come to you for food and Have one run to you for its favourite food are gated on !horse.isTamed() because FoodTemptGoal serves tamed horses too. Hold wheat near a tamed horse of yours and confirm neither ticks.
  • Read a wild horse's place in its band rides the inspect lease, once a second per viewer, and is gated to untamed horses. Opening one of your own must tick nothing.
  • The slow-tick husbandry pair — Heal a hurt horse and Let your horses form a herd — are credited to horse.getOwner() when that owner happens to be loaded, so they can fail by never firing (owner out of range or offline) or by firing when any horse anywhere is scratched. Hurt a horse of yours beside water, stand next to it, watch for the one chat line; then walk a long way off, hurt another, and confirm nothing arrives.
  • The three rarest things to see at all. Watch a stallion lose his band needs a bachelor challenge (0.35 per band per day), Watch one horse push another off water needs a rank gap of 0.15 at water or hay and never at plain grass, and Watch a colt disperse-shaped behaviour needs 1–3 adult days. These are the ones most likely to read as broken when they are merely rare — check the log before concluding anything.
  • Dyeing tack ticks only when a colour actually changed. Put a saddle in the bench, type a name and change no row, and take it: the box must not tick. Change one row and take it: it must.
  • A natural cover is credited to the mare's owner, so a cover in a wild band must tick nothing at all.
  • The four themed splice carrots tick twice — their own box and Use an unknown gene splice carrot, because a themed splice is a narrowed one and shares its effect token. Feed a dilution carrot and confirm both. The magical splice is in the Magical horses chapter now rather than beside them.
  • The three written tickets tick per tier. A basic ticket ticks only Send a horse to its stall; a bound and an interdimensional one each also tick their own.
  • The crafted tasks — the blank ticket and the gene carrot — tick at a crafting table, including when the result is shift-clicked out as a stack. (The hair-bundle, braided-rope and hair-cloth tasks were deleted with their items.)
  • Feeding by hand now ticks two boxes at once (Bond with a horse and Feed a horse by hand), and a favourite food ticks a third.
  • The vet's kit, gelding, and a seed jar used on a mare each tick.
  • Founding a breed from a spawn egg ticks, as does taming a horse of any named breed (Meet a breed).

Every item in the mod is now named by at least one task, bar the custom and preset horse spawn eggs, which have no recipe and no trade. Worth re-checking whenever an item is added.

The prose is unread as well as unplayed. Eleven new sub-chapters of wild behaviour, a magic chapter of four, and a rewritten husbandry opener were all written from the code rather than from the wiki — deliberately, because breeds and horse care both carry claims the code contradicts (gap 266). Read the new chapters once in game at the width they actually render: a claim that is wrong in a tutorial is worse than one that is wrong in a wiki, because the tutorial is what a new player believes.

The checklist becomes advancements (2026-09-20, NOT played)

Every checklist task is now a real advancement. A dedicated server boots clean and loads all 112 advancement files — 1617 vanilla + 112 ours = the 1729 the log reports — so nothing here is about whether they parse. None of it has been in front of a player.

The advancements, which need a player who has ticked something

  • The first login is the flood. A save with boxes already ticked awards all of them at once. Expect a run of toasts and chat lines, once. If it happens on every login, that is a bug and the catch-up in ProgressHooks.onLogin is where to look.
  • One fresh tick. Do something not yet done — milk a mare, say — and watch for the toast, the chat line and the box in the book all agreeing. The mod's own green [✓] line should be gone.
  • Turn it off. /gamerule announceAdvancements false should silence the chat half, which is the whole argument for having dropped the hand-rolled line.
  • The tab. Open the advancement screen: there should be a Horse Genetics tab with a root, eight chapter nodes off it, and each chapter's tasks fanned out from its own node. Check the tree is readable at that width — the wild chapter has nineteen children.
  • The icons. A hundred and twelve of them were hand-assigned and machine-checked for existence, not for sense. Any that reads as plainly wrong is a line in bake-advancements.mjs.
