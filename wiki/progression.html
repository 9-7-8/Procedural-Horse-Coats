Gameplay / progression

The checklist & the collection

Two answers to “what do I do now?” — a checklist that walks you through everything the mod can do, and a collection of every allele in it with the ones you have never met still under fog.

The checklist

Not a page of its own any more. Every task sits under the sub-chapter of Getting started that teaches it — the box for hanging a stall sign is directly beneath the paragraphs about hanging one — and each unticked one carries a line saying what to actually do. The count is ProgressTask.values().length, across the chapters below, from watching a wild band to signing a transfer paper.

A Checklist section at the end of the contents is the overview: the running total, a bar, and only what is still outstanding, grouped by chapter. A chapter with every box ticked drops out of it entirely, so that section shrinks as you play.

A finished sub-chapter goes gold

A sub-chapter whose every checklist item is ticked is painted amber in the contents list down the left. It is the one place the page says “you are finished here” without your having to open it and count boxes, and it is the reason every sub-chapter carries at least one task: one with nothing to do could never be finished, so an empty task list is deliberately never coloured.

It gates nothing. Nothing in the mod asks whether a task is done. A player who reads this wiki and builds a research shelf on their first day ticks two boxes at once and is not stopped — the moment a checklist gates content it stops being advice and starts being homework.

Ticking one says so in chat, once, with a running count. The chapters are:

  • Horses in the wild — first, because every horse a player meets is wild until they tame one. Where the breeds are and founding one from a spawn egg; a wild horse drifting over to food and galloping for its favourite; taming from the saddle and from the ground; being kicked by one, and watching one bolt; reading a wild horse's place in its band. Then the band itself — sparring, a dam fetching her foal, a stallion driving a stray mare back in and later losing the band to a challenger, rank settled at the water, grooming partners standing head to tail, grazing, and a meat-eater hunting.
  • Husbandry — what a horse is and naming it, feeding and watering and healing, then the whole injury-to-mended thread — a horse of yours getting hurt, putting food into a hungry one, and seeing it the rest of the way back to full — grooming and what horse hair becomes, bonding to each of its three tiers and the two things riding bare teaches you (that it bonds exactly as well as a saddled ride, and that at the top tier the horse steers without tack), stalls and holding pens and the four tickets, the four whistles, dyeing your tack, and milking a mare. Nothing about breeding at all: keeping a horse is a whole game before genetics is.
  • Basic breeding — heat and natural covering, reading a mare with a vet's kit, pregnancy and foaling and twins, filling and using a seed jar, and gelding — having one done, owning one however he got that way, and feeling him settle to you faster than a stallion would. No golden carrots: the first breeding a player does is the one the horses arrange themselves.
  • Genetics — taking a gene off a horse with a book, earning one into your database, the research shelf, the gene carrot, the splice carrots, and the hay portal as a showroom for genes you have not bred yet.
  • Magical horses — its own chapter rather than a corner of genetics, because a magical gene paints after the coat has resolved instead of restricting pigment on the way, and it arrives by its own routes. What one is and the magical splice carrot; finding and taming a dhampir of either strain, the seal brown nine in ten and the white one in ten; a magical herd, where every horse carries and shows the same single magical gene; and the two traits that change how you keep a horse at all — burning in daylight, and drinking blood.
  • Breeding projects — a foal carrying something it does not show, one that breeds true, and line breeding for a recessive.
  • The villagers — trade with a horseman, meet a cowboy, sign a transfer paper.
Some boxes tick because you watched

Most of the wild chapter is horses doing things to each other, which a player cannot perform. Those tasks complete through HorseProgress.completeForWatcher — the nearest player within twenty-four blocks, with line of sight, is credited. Their titles say watch for that reason: an unticked box phrased as an instruction, for something nobody can instruct, reads as a bug. The line-of-sight test is deliberate too — a box that ticked for a fight behind a hill would be claiming you had seen something you had not.

Every item in the mod has a task

Deliberately, and it is worth keeping true as items are added: every registered item a player can obtain in survival is named by at least one checklist task. The only items without one are the custom and preset horse spawn eggs, which have no recipe and no trade and exist for creative and debugging.

The collection

The Alleles tab is every allele of every gene in the mod. The ones you have met are named and lit; the ones you have not are ??? and a row of question marks the right length. Each gene shows how many of its alleles you have, and the header counts the lot.

Meeting an allele is not the same as discovering a gene

Every allele on any horse you tame or breed joins the collection — both copies, at every locus, baseline alleles included. That is a record of what you have laid eyes on, and the plain wild-type alleles count exactly as much as the strange ones.

Discovering a gene is the other thing and is deliberately harder: it takes owning a living horse that carries something other than the plain baseline there — tamed, bred, bought or given — and that is also what unlocks the gene's carrot recipe. A research paper will not do it; papers are components, not lessons. An ordinary horse fills in a lot of your collection and discovers nothing.

So the two tabs answer different questions. The gene database is what can I make?; the collection is what is left out there?

Tasks are an enum in common/

common/progress/ProgressTask is the vocabulary: an id, a title, a hint and a group, with no Minecraft in it, so it is testable without a game and it survives the version port. The hooks that complete a task are necessarily Minecraft-side and live next to whatever event already existed — taming in the tame handler, foals in the breeding handler, whistles in the whistle item, crafting in ProgressHooks.

ProgressTask.Group is the chapters of the book, not a filing system of its own, and it is only used for grouping the summary section. Which sub-chapter a task belongs to is held the other way round, in TutorialPage.Step.tasks() — the page owns the mapping because the page is what draws it, and that is what ClientProgress.allDone reads to decide whether a contents row is painted gold. A task in the enum but in no Step would show only in the summary and could never finish a sub-chapter, so the two lists are kept in step by hand.

Every task is wired, and a check says so

A checklist with an item nobody can tick is worse than a shorter checklist: it reads as a bug and the player cannot tell which one. Because the enum is in one module and the hooks are scattered across another by design, nothing but a sweep can prove they are all reachable — node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/check-progress-tasks.mjs is that sweep, and it fails by name.

HorseProgress.complete(player, task) is the whole API a handler uses. It swallows a client-side player, a null and a server-less world, because it is called from a dozen files and the twelfth call site will be careless. HorseProgressData is the {@code SavedData} behind it, keyed by player UUID beside the gene database, and completing is idempotent - it says something in chat exactly once.

The collection is a second set, on purpose

GeneDatabaseData holds two things per player now: the book of discovered genes, and a flat set of geneKey|token for alleles seen. They are separate because sharing one map would have made one behaviour quietly become the other — an entry in the book is what knows() tests, so recording baseline alleles there would have unlocked every gene in the mod off one ordinary horse.

Both ride the same GeneDatabaseSyncPayload, which grew a third field, and both are mirrored client-side in ClientGeneDatabase. collect() is called from GeneDiscoveryHandler.discoverFrom, which is already the one place that sees a genotype the player has earned.
