Horse gear: the slots
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Everything a horse wears, in one place. A Gear tab on the horse information screen holds twenty slots laid out on a picture of the horse itself — head, mane and tail, body, chest, a slot per leg and a slot per hoof, and two for the chests it carries. Two of them are real vanilla equipment slots and the other eighteen are a data attachment of our own, which is the constraint the whole system is shaped by. The slots exist; almost nothing to put in them does yet. The roster, the storage and the screen shipped first on purpose — every piece that follows is one row in a tag. What is still planned is on this page’s own Roadmap tab, which is the single home for gear plans.

The two-slot ceiling This is the constraint the whole system is shaped by. EquipmentSlot has exactly two animal entries — BODY and SADDLE — and EquipmentClientInfo.LayerType is not extensible. Twenty slots were therefore never going to be twenty equipment slots. The two real ones are spent on the saddle and the barding, which are the two vanilla already draws for free; every other piece is a key in a synced data attachment. The roster is the layout entity/HorseTackSlot is the whole table. Each constant carries its backing (an EquipmentSlot , or null for a gear slot), its zone, its label, its one-line hint, and its position on the paper doll as a fraction of the doll’s box. That last part is what keeps the screen out of it: adding a slot is a line in the enum plus a tag, and the screen is not touched. isVanilla() is the difference between the two backings, and nothing outside the enum should need to ask — on() and set() cover both. usableOn(horse) is vanilla’s canUseSlot for the two real slots and “not a foal” for the rest. accepts(horse, stack) asks vanilla for the two real slots and asks the slot’s item tag for the rest. What a slot accepts is a tag Every gear slot reads an item tag named for it — horsegenetics:gear/mane , one tag per non-vanilla slot. gear/mane and gear/tail hold the rescuing braid and gear/harness the four storage harnesses ; most of the rest are empty. A slot whose tag is empty correctly refuses everything rather than accepting dirt, which is exactly the behaviour wanted while the pieces do not exist. Shipping a piece means adding it to its tag, and a datapack can do the same without touching Java: the braid went in without a line of Java in HorseTackSlot changing. The two storage slots are the exception, and the only one. What they take is not a list anyone could keep, so HorseTackSlot.fits asks HorseStorage instead and their tags are only an addition to what it detects — see chests below. Every caller asks fits or accepts , never the tag directly: TackEquipHandler read the tag itself until 2026-10-08, which was correct for eighteen slots and would have made a chest unwearable by hand. Why an attachment and not a container data/HorseGear is the store: an immutable map of slot name to ItemStack , registered as HORSE_GEAR in ModAttachments , serialized, synced and copyOnDeath . A container was the other option and is worse on two counts: A widened container is reachable by hoppers. An attachment is not. AbstractMountInventoryMenu.quickMoveStack is shared with donkeys, mules, llamas and nautiluses, and computes every shift-click range from hardcoded indices. A slot inserted anywhere in that arithmetic is how items duplicate or vanish, and it is the failure that looks fine until somebody shift-clicks with a chest on the horse. This is also the shape vanilla itself uses: the saddle and armour slots are not backed by the horse’s container either — Mob.createEquipmentSlotContainer fronts the equipment array with a view that owns no storage. One authority, and every stack is copied The attachment is the only place a worn piece lives and is never mirrored into a container. Every stack crossing the boundary is copied, so a caller holding the stack it passed in cannot mutate what the horse is wearing, and the map is unmodifiable — every change returns a new HorseGear . Keyed by name, not by ordinal The same reason TackSlotPayload sends the name: an ordinal in saved data silently re-points at a different slot the moment the enum gains a constant in the middle, and this enum is expected to keep growing. An unknown key is kept rather than dropped, so a piece parked behind a removed slot is still there if the slot comes back. The click path Still one packet. HorseInfoScreen.clickTack opens the picker; picking a line sends TackSlotPayload(entityId, slotName, inventorySlot) ; ModNetworking.handleTackSlot checks the horse is the player’s and within eight blocks, then calls HorseTackSlot.set — the one place that knows which backing a slot has. A vanilla slot also gets setGuaranteedDrop , so tack a player put on is tack they get back. The index is re-checked, not trusted. The payload used to mean the hand implicitly, which needed no validation because the server could read the hand itself. An index into the player’s inventory is a claim about a container the server is the authority on, so handleTackSlot bounds-checks it and runs the same HorseTackSlot.accepts the picker filtered on before equipping anything. A bad index is dropped in silence — no honest client can produce one, so there is nobody to tell. The picker is the browser’s gene dropdown, field for field — a null slot means closed, the height comes from the contents, it is drawn last so it covers what it hangs over, it owns the wheel, and it eats the next click wherever that click lands. Two dropdowns in one mod that behave differently is worse than either being slightly wrong. It is not a stand-in for the Dress window below, and the two live side by side: the picker is one click on any slot, the window is a real container. A list of what fits is not a picture of an inventory — it is a menu, and it says the one thing a grid of thirty-six squares could not. The Dress window — a real menu beside the doll Asked for on 2026-09-25 ( “the player's inventory must be visible on the right side of the gear screen, so they can equip/unequip items” ) and built on 2026-09-30. menu/HorseGearMenu is an AbstractContainerMenu with the twenty tack slots and the player's thirty-six; client/HorseGearScreen draws it, doll on the left and inventory on the right. It is a separate window, not the information screen converted. The owner's call, from two options. HorseInfoScreen shows horses that are not loaded entities at all — a foal from the Offspring tab, a name in the family tree — and a container menu needs a live entity on the server, so a menu under that screen could never be a precondition of it opening. It is also opened by the client from four places, and a menu exists only if the server opens it. So the tab keeps its read-only doll and the click picker, and gains a Dress button. That sends OpenHorseGearPayload ; ModNetworking.handleOpenHorseGear checks the same three things handleTackSlot does — a horse, the player's, within eight blocks — and opens the menu through NeoForge's extra-data factory with the entity id, exactly as JumpMenu is opened with a block position. ModMenus ' class comment used to say a menu was for a block ; it now says the rule is slots, which is what it always meant. The menu keeps nothing. On the server the tack slots are a GearView — a Container whose reads are HorseTackSlot.on and whose writes are HorseTackSlot.set , so there is no third copy of the gear to drift from vanilla's equipment and the HORSE_GEAR attachment. On the client they are a plain mirror the menu sync fills, like every vanilla client menu. Every rule of the old packet became slot behaviour , which is the wider surface, so each one is written once and delegated to: mayPlace is HorseTackSlot.accepts , which carries usableOn — so the empty tags refuse everything and a foal's slots refuse too. One piece per slot ( getMaxStackSize on the slot and the view). That also shuts every vanilla merge path, the only paths that grow a slot's stack in place without writing it back. GearView hands out copies anyway, so a missed path would lose a count on screen rather than edit the horse behind set 's back. stillValid re-checks alive, owned and within eight blocks every tick, so walking off closes it. Whatever is on the cursor then goes back through vanilla's removed — into the inventory, or at the player's feet. canTakeItemForPickAll is false for the tack slots: a double-click on a spare saddle in the bag would otherwise gather the worn one off the horse. GearView.clearContent does nothing on purpose — it is the one method that could strip a horse with no player to hand the pieces to. The layout is the roster, again. Slots sit at HorseTackSlot.anchorX/anchorY of a 216×160 box, the smallest the anchors survive: the tightest pairs are 0.12 and 0.16 of the box apart, which needs at least 168×131 before two slots overlap. Escape returns to the information screen it came from through a one-shot hand-off keyed by the horse's id; a close that comes from the server (reach, death) does not go through onClose , so it drops the player into the world rather than into a screen about a horse they have left. The paper doll Cursor.gear draws the horse first and the slots over it, each at anchorX/anchorY of the doll box, and records where each landed in tackHits rather than working the geometry out twice — which is what keeps a scrolled page’s slots clickable in the right place. The horse is HorsePortrait.drawPosed , a new fixed-angle companion to the mouse-following draw . The doll’s horse has to hold still : its slots are positioned anatomically, so a portrait that swung to follow the cursor would put the near fore leg somewhere different from the boot slot that belongs to it. The pose is a three-quarter view because face-on overlaps the near and off legs, and four boot slots would then point at one visible leg. The roster checks itself at boot HorseTackSlot.verify() runs from the mod constructor, beside DietFoods.verify and for the same reason: every way this table can be wrong fails silently in the game. Two slots given the same anchor draw exactly on top of each other, so one is unclickable and the tab merely looks like it has one slot fewer. An anchor outside 0..1 puts a slot off the doll, invisible but still holding its click box. A third constant handed a real EquipmentSlot would take one the saddle or the barding needs. None of that throws on its own and none of it logs. Why it is not a unit test It cannot be. This module’s test source set has no Minecraft on its classpath on purpose — see MaterialScanTest , which imports none — and HorseTackSlot cannot even be loaded without one, since its fields are EquipmentSlot and TagKey . A boot-time throw is the cheapest check that actually runs. Chests: a worn piece, a store, and a load Shipped 2026-10-08, to four calls the owner made that day (recorded on design decisions ): any placeable storage rather than an item of our own; the chest's own slots, never locked; weight by item count against pulling ability, server-configurable, ender chests exempt; a full chest does not come off. Three things, each with one home: What Where Synced? The chest — the stack hung on a flank HorseGear , under SADDLEBAG_LEFT / SADDLEBAG_RIGHT , like any worn piece Yes, with the rest of the gear The contents data/HorsePacks , the HORSE_PACKS attachment, keyed by the same slot name No. Saved only; a client sees them through the menu it has open The load — two item counts and which side is weightless data/HorsePackLoad , the HORSE_PACK_LOAD attachment Yes, and not saved: recounted on join and on every change The slot names say saddlebag because they are keys in saved worlds and were chosen before the owner settled what hangs there. They are not to be renamed. What is storage: entity/HorseStorage slots(stack, level, near) is the whole question, and zero means not storage . The item must be a BlockItem whose block is an EntityBlock ; one block entity is built — never added to a level — and asked how many slots it has: Container.getContainerSize if it is a Container , otherwise the size of NeoForge's item capability ( Capabilities.Item.BLOCK ). The answer is cached per item, on each side separately. horse_storage/denied wins over everything; a slot tag makes an item storage that the probe finds nothing in, at UNKNOWN_SIZE — which is how the ender chest gets in, since its block entity holds nothing. Unverified against another mod's chest The probe was written against vanilla's block entities. BlockCapability.getCapability handed a block entity that is in no level is @ApiStatus.Internal in NeoForge, and a provider that looks at the world around near sees whatever is really there. Every failure is caught, logged once per item and read as not storage . It is an open check on the Verification tab . For vanilla's containers only the slot count is taken, and the storage is ours. A size vanilla has a screen for opens vanilla's ( ChestMenu for nine-by-N up to six rows, ShulkerBoxMenu for a keeps-its-contents chest of twenty-seven) over HorsePacks , and any other size opens menu/HorsePackMenu , a plain grid: nine across up to fifty-four slots, then wider rather than scrolling, to HorsePacks.MAX_SLOTS . No block is needed for any of that. Another mod's chest is a different matter — see the next section . Another mod's chest opens as its block: server/HostedPacks A modded menu finds its block entity in the world, at a position , on the server and on the client both. Read out of the jars in the owner's modpack on 2026-10-08: Sophisticated Storage's StorageContainerMenu is built from a BlockPos on both sides and throws if nothing is there, its stillValid looks the same position up again, and DimStorage's menu factory reads a position off the packet. (That mod's own author wrote a second set of menus, Sophisticated Storage In Motion , to put its storage on a minecart.) A block entity that was never placed cannot open one. So it is placed — the owner's call, from four options. While a hosted chest is open it really exists: at level.getMinY() under the player, where the bedrock is, with the block above cleared so a lid has room. It goes down the way a hand puts it ( BlockItem.updateCustomBlockEntityTag , applyComponentsFromItemStack , setPlacedBy ), is opened through the block's own useWithoutItem , and is picked up the way vanilla's pick-block-with-data does ( saveCustomOnly into minecraft:block_entity_data , plus collectComponents ). Between openings the chest is that one ItemStack , kept in HorsePacks.hold with the item count taken while it stood; what is worn in HorseGear , drawn and synced, is the same stack without its contents. What could go wrong What stops it The server dies with a chest standing The horse's copy is re-picked every tick while the chest is live, and a SavedData journal names both positions before they are touched. On the next start the journal is swept and the original blocks go back with UPDATE_SKIP_BLOCK_ENTITY_SIDEEFFECTS — no drops. A crash can lose a tick; it cannot make a second chest. Every menu asks “is the player still at my block”, and the block is a hundred blocks down mixin/HostedMenuPlayerMixin and HostedMenuPacketMixin wrap the five places vanilla asks ( ServerPlayer.tick , doTick ; the click, button and recipe packets) and hand a hosted player's question to HostedPacks.stillValid , which answers for the horse . Anybody else gets the menu's own answer after one empty-map check. The modded client builds its menu before the block arrives The block update and the block entity's data are sent to the opening player ahead of the call that opens the menu, rather than left to the end-of-tick broadcast. Somebody else finds the block Clicks and breaks at a live position are cancelled. It will not place, will not open, or throws It is lifted, the item is remembered as one that does not host until the next restart, logged once, and the chest opens as the plain grid. The rider leaves the column the block is in The chest shuts sixty-four blocks out. A galloping rider can outrun an open modded chest; a vanilla one has no such limit. HostedPacks.hosts decides which chests take this path: not the minecraft namespace, not the ender chest, not anything in horsegenetics:horse_storage/plain_screen , and nothing with packs.real_screens off. An empty hosted chest that is no different from a new one comes off the horse as a plain item, so it stacks; anything else comes off carrying its block data. The gametest hosted_chest_is_a_real_block drives the whole path with a vanilla chest and an unjoined ServerPlayer , and was seen to fail with the mixin answer removed. Unverified against another mod No modded chest is installed in the dev run, so no modded menu has been opened this way. It is the first check on the Verification tab . The store is mutable, on purpose HorseGear is immutable and copies every stack across its boundary. HorsePacks cannot be: a container hands out its live stacks and vanilla's menus grow and shrink them in place, so a store that copied on the way out would turn every merge into a silent loss. The attachment's value is therefore a mutable object, one per horse ( builder(HorsePacks::new) — a shared default would be one chest for every horse on the server), and HorsePackHandler.PackContainer is a window straight onto it. Nothing marks it dirty because nothing needs to: an entity is written with its chunk regardless. A list grows to the highest slot written and is never cut, and a container is never smaller than what it holds — so a chest whose mod shrank it, or was removed, still shows every stack. Stacks filed under a slot whose chest has gone are kept, and unload returns them. Putting on and taking off go through the slot HorseTackSlot.set , for a storage slot, calls HorsePackHandler.unpack : a stack that arrives with a minecraft:container component — a shulker box, a chest picked with its inventory — is emptied into the store, so there is one copy. An empty component is left exactly as it was. Barrels, shelves and shulker boxes carry an empty one by default, and a barrel that came back without it would never stack with another; the gametest pins it. HorseTackSlot.mayTakeOff is false for a chest with anything in it, unless HorseStorage.keepsContents . The server counts the store; a client reads the synced count, which is why HorsePackLoad exists at all. HorseTackSlot.takeOff is the only way a worn piece is handed to somebody: it packs a keeps-its-contents chest's stacks back into the item ( HorsePackHandler.pack ) and clears the slot. GearView and handleTackSlot both used to read on() and then set(EMPTY) ; both now call it, and the Dress window's slot refuses through mayPickup . The load: common/pack/PackLoad Pure, in common/ , and pinned by PackLoadTest . The load is a plain count of items; capacity is CartDraft.capacity(pull) , the carts' own curve, so there is one axis of strength. The server's five packs.* keys leave ServerConfig only as one PackLoad.Curve , through packCurve() , so no reader can take one key and miss the others. HorsePackHandler.refresh recounts, writes HORSE_PACK_LOAD if it changed, and sets a transient ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL modifier on movement speed ( horsegenetics:pack_load ) — transient like the carts', and re-made on EntityJoinLevelEvent , so a config change is picked up on the next load or the next change to a chest rather than baked into the save. A weightless chest's items are synced in the count and left out of weighed() . HorseSpeedFloor does not undo it: that floor is on the attribute's base , and this is a modifier, exactly as the cart's is. Clicking the chest: common/pack/PackBox HorsePackHandler.onClickChest does not ask which face of the hitbox was struck — a horse's hitbox is a square column that does not turn with it, so “the left face” is the flank one time in four. It casts the player's own line of sight against a box per flank in the horse's frame, scaled by the horse's size. PackBox holds that box and the three centre constants, and client/PackLayer draws the chest from the same three, so what is clicked is what is seen. The event is cancelled on both sides, as TackEquipHandler 's is, or the client predicts a mount. It runs at LOW : after everything that claims a click by what is in the hand, before the equip handler. Drawing it: client/PackLayer The layer knows nothing about chests. The worn stack is resolved to its ordinary item model in GeneticHorseRenderer.extractRenderState ( ItemDisplayContext.NONE , so no display transform) and submitted on the body bone, turned so a block's front faces away from the horse and squeezed to PackBox.DRAWN_THICK . A barrel is a barrel, a dyed shulker box is its colour, and another mod's crate draws as its own item does. Barding is drawn edged: client/gear/BardingLayer Since 2026-10-10 ( #240 , piece 2 of worn geometry ) horse armour on a horse this mod draws is not vanilla’s shell. GeneticHorseRenderer registers BardingLayer where it used to register a SimpleEquipmentLayer for HORSE_BODY , and that layer draws every suit — vanilla’s, another mod’s, a generated metal’s — a quarter unit proud of the coat with a wall where the painted plate ends. Donkeys, mules and the vanilla undead horses are not drawn by this mod and keep vanilla’s armour. It reads the armour as vanilla does. The layers come from the item’s equipment asset, each texture through ClientHooks.getArmorTexture , each colour through IClientItemExtensions.getArmorLayerTintColor with the dye as the fallback, and a colour of zero skips the layer: the body of EquipmentLayerRenderer.renderLayers . So the three-colour leather and a generated metal’s color_when_undyed arrive unchanged. Each layer is then its own WornMeshes mesh at the same lift. The mesh is cut from the bare horse. Vanilla’s HORSE_ARMOR model is the body mesh inflated by 0.1; a rim on that would stop short of the coat. The layer bakes the same mesh with no inflation ( createBodyMesh(CubeDeformation.NONE) , 64 by 64, scaled 1.1 as LayerDefinitions scales it) and lifts it the whole WornRule.FLOOR . Inflation moves no UV, so every armour texture fits. The model is the layer’s own and is posed there ( setupAnim ) before its bones are walked. The glint is the same quads a second time through RenderTypes.armorEntityGlint() , whose pipeline draws where depth is equal, so it lies on the faces and the walls alike. Once per suit, as vanilla. A fading horse fades its armour here , as HarnessLayer does: entityTranslucent and the alpha on the tint. FadingGearMixin still covers the saddle, and armour on the fallback path. When it steps aside is one pure rule, client/gear/BardingRule , pinned by BardingRuleTest : the gear.edges switch is off, another mod swapped the armour model for that item, the stack carries a trim, or any drawn layer has no mesh (an unreadable texture, or an edge over RimMesh.QUAD_LIMIT ). The whole item then goes to the vanilla layer the mod used to register, kept inside this one. Never layer by layer. Known differences from vanilla’s draw. Custom geometry carries no outline colour, so edged armour adds nothing to a glowing horse’s outline. A pack or mod that replaces the HORSE_ARMOR layer definition itself is not followed. This is a deliberate, looks-only exception to “vanilla mechanics stay intact” (owner, 2026-10-09). No behaviour of armour changes, and the client switch restores vanilla’s drawing. What the other slots do when a horse leaves Fixed 2026-10-08 (#218). A horse sold to a cowboy or the trader, or turned loose , hands back what it wears in this mod's own slots: TackEquipHandler.returnGear , called from CowboySale.returnTack and HorseRelease.returnTack beside the saddle and the barding. Until then a braid left with the horse and was deleted when a dealer's string or the wild turnover discarded it. In the same change GeneDeathHandler.onHorseDrops stopped clearing the saddle and barding out of the drops of a horse whose gene replaces its loot. On death everything drops (owner, 2026-10-09, #218). After the chests are dealt with, HorsePackHandler.onDrops adds whatever is left in this mod's own slots — the harness, a braid — to the drops through TackEquipHandler.takeGear , and HorseResurrection.revive takes the same gear off the snapshot, which was made before the drops, so a horse brought back does not wear a second set. Until then the gear stayed with the body and came back only with a resurrection. Every way a horse leaves Path What happens to the chests Death HorsePackHandler.onDrops ( LivingDropsEvent , LOWEST ) sets each chest down as a block (#221): openSpace picks the nearest replaceable, dry position within three blocks, preferring one with something under it; NeoForge's EventHooks.onBlockPlace is asked first so a claim can refuse; the block goes down through HostedPacks.setDown , the same place-as-a-hand-would steps a hosted chest uses, and a plain chest is then filled slot for slot from HorsePacks . Whatever cannot be placed, or does not fit the placed block, is added to the drops as before. Lowest because GeneDeathHandler.onHorseDrops clears the list for some genes. A hosted chest drops as its one whole item. Resurrection, a realm-backup raise The afterlife snapshot is taken before the drops, so it still holds them; HorseResurrection.revive calls strip , or two chests of anything could be copied by dying. Sold to a cowboy or the trader; turned loose CowboySale.returnTack and HorseRelease.returnTack call giveBack . Those horses can later be discarded outright. Thirteen slots still draw nothing There is no render layer for thirteen of the eighteen gear slots, because there is nothing to render. The saddle and the barding draw as they always have, through vanilla, the two packs are drawn by PackLayer as above, and the mane and tail are drawn by BraidLayer — the first of these, and the worked example of the shape: a mask baked from the mesh, tinted, and since 2026-10-10 cut into a mesh that stands a quarter unit off the hair ( worn geometry : nothing a horse wears is flat). Every piece past those needs a render layer of our own — the pattern this mod already has ten instances of — and the plan for deriving that geometry rather than modelling it by hand is on the Roadmap tab.
