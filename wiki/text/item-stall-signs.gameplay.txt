Stall signs
(Item / stabling)
A sign you bind to one horse and then hang on a wall, which marks the room behind that wall as that horse’s stall.

How to get one Recipe (shapeless): an oak sign + horse hair. That gives you a blank stall sign; binding it to a horse is the first of the two steps below, and a bound sign has no recipe of its own. How to use one Right-click a horse with a blank stall sign. The sign becomes bound to that horse and takes its name. Hang it on the stall wall , from either side. A real sign is placed with the horse’s name on it, the room it names becomes that horse’s stall, and its outline flashes with particles so you can see what was claimed. It has to find a room. It looks at both sides of the wall and takes whichever is the room — if both close, the smaller one, because the bigger is the building the stall is in. It walks the floor , so a fence across the front of a stall is a wall even though you can see over it, and a slab, a carpet or a one-block step is still the same room. A gate or a doorway is the edge of the stall whether it is open or shut. You do not have to hang anything in the gap you walk through. If the floor runs out of the stall through an open doorway, the sign looks up to three blocks above and below itself for a height where the wall makes an unbroken ring — the lintel over a doorway is one — and measures the floor inside that ring instead. The doorway’s own square is part of the ring, so the stall ends there, exactly as it would if you had hung a gate in it. A gap that is open all the way up is still not a stall: there is no ring at any height, and nothing is holding your horse in either. If nothing encloses — a sign on a fence post in an open field — the sign refuses to go up and stays in your hand , with a line asking you to close the room in first. There is no fallback box, and there deliberately is not one: a sign that bound to a made-up room would report a stall the player does not have, and they would find out about it when a horse arrived inside a wall. Breaking the sign block releases the stall. What a stall is for A stall is where a ticket sends the horse. That is the whole of what consumes one — there is no auto-return and no “assigned pen” behaviour. One horse, one sign. Hanging a second sign for a horse that already has a stall refuses and tells you where the old sign is; hang it again within ten seconds and the stall moves, with the old sign popping off its wall as a bound sign you can pick up. Only a horse you own. A sign will not bind to an untamed horse or to somebody else's, in creative mode too — a stall for a horse you cannot ticket is a stall you cannot use. Cleanup is thin: only breaking the exact sign block releases a stall, so rebuilding the wall elsewhere or removing the horse leaves a stale record behind.
