Whistles
(Item / tool)
Three area whistles call every tamed horse you own nearby, each reaching further than the last. The fourth, the ender whistle , is bound to one horse for good and brings it to you from anywhere — even another dimension. A horse close enough to simply come walks in rather than appearing, the ender whistle asks for a bond before it will bind, and one key blows the lot , and a bound ender whistle glimmers so you can pick it out of a hotbar. The fifth, the command whistle , does not call at all: hold it on a horse and a wheel of standing orders opens.

The area whistles item/WhistleItem , one class and a radius per tier. Pathing instead of teleporting and the bond gate both shipped on 2026-09-29. appendHoverText reads the radius field rather than a fourth constructor argument or a per-tier lang key, for the same reason WhistleItem.task() does: the radius is already the only thing that distinguishes the three, and a second parallel field is a second thing that can disagree with it. Added 2026-09-24 — until then the three tiers had no tooltip at all, and the numbers 16, 32 and 64 appeared nowhere a player could read them. The second line (“a ridden horse stays put; a leashed one is untied”) deliberately stops short of saying where the lead goes : that is behaviour.leads_return , a server option, and a client on a dedicated server has no copy of it to read. The lead, when a whistle moves the horse Neither whistle unties the horse itself any more. Both call server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player) , which suppresses vanilla’s ground drop and hands the lead to whoever blew the whistle — written up, with the reasoning and the gametest that guards it, on rider comfort , which owns that behaviour. Tickets go through the same helper. The ender whistle item/EnderWhistleItem — use calls, inventoryTick checks for dust once a second. Stacks to one. appendHoverText names the bound horse, reading it off the component rather than the entity: the horse may be unloaded, in another dimension, or dead, and the client cannot reach it in any of those cases. Same shape as the bound stall sign , which is where the wording came from. server/EnderWhistleCalls — binding (an EntityInteract handler using HorseOwnership.bindRefusal , the same refusal stall signs use), calling, and the dust. The binding is the existing BOUND_HORSE component. data/HorseWhereabouts — a server-global SavedData of each tamed horse’s last dimension and block, and whether it died. Written by server/HorseWhereaboutsHandler on join, on leave, every ten seconds (only when it moved more than 16 blocks or changed dimension), and on death. The ancestry database keeps a dead horse’s record for the pedigree, so it cannot answer “is it alive” — this can. How a call finds a horse Any level with the entity loaded: move it now. Otherwise the last sighting’s chunk gets a TicketType.PORTAL ticket (radius 2, which expires by itself) and the call waits on the server tick for up to five seconds for the entity section to load. Same dimension: snapTo . Another dimension: Entity.teleport(TeleportTransition) , which re-creates the entity in the target level. Ownership is checked at call time , never at bind time, which is what makes the whistle follow a sale. It moves an entity found by UUID and never creates one, so it cannot duplicate a horse. A horse deleted rather than killed — the horse dimension clearing its pens — is gone from the ancestry database, and that crumbles the whistle too. Walking a near horse in (2026-09-29) server/WhistleCalls holds the decision and every way a call can end; server/WhistleCallGoal does nothing but walk. Decided once, at blow time. tryWalk asks the horse’s own navigator for a fresh path to the player and walks only if that path exists, actually arrives ( canReach() — a path that stops short is a fence), and is within WALK_PATH_BLOCKS steps. Nothing is re-decided later: a horse either set off walking or it teleported, and there is no third state. The route is measured, not the distance , because fifteen blocks away can be eighty blocks of switchback. Since a route is never shorter than the straight line between its ends, capping the route caps the distance for free — so there is one number and not two. The cap is counted in path nodes , which are block steps, and a diagonal step covers about 1.4 blocks; that approximation is why the tooltip says “about”, and the tooltip reads the constant rather than repeating the number. Not BondFollowGoal ’s ReachCheck . That caches for two seconds, which is right for a goal asking every tick and wrong for a whistle — a call is a single event and deserves a fresh answer. It also only answers yes/no, and this needs the length. Why the state machine is not in the goal Every ending is decided in WhistleCalls ’s server-tick sweep, and the goal reads a marker. That is deliberate: a goal that never starts cannot end itself. If something at priority 0 holds MOVE , or the horse is in danger, the goal simply never runs — and a state machine living inside it would leave the horse neither walking nor teleported, which is the “the whistle ignored my horse” report with nothing in the log. Arrived — within the same three blocks the teleport path calls “already here”. Gave up — deadline passed, or no headway for two seconds. It is teleported . That fallback is the guarantee that a walked call is never worse than the old behaviour, and it is also what covers the goal never having started. In danger — dropped, and deliberately not teleported. Invalidated — player or horse gone, mounted, leashed. Dropped quietly. The marker is in memory only , keyed by horse UUID. A call lasts seconds; a half-finished one restored from disk would be a bug, so a reload mid-walk just ends it. Priority 1, and why not 2 The design note for this reasoned “below survival, above everything social” and concluded priority 2 . Reading the generated hierarchy rather than that line shows why 2 is wrong: priority 2 is vanilla’s BreedGoal , which claims MOVE , and an equal priority never interrupts — WrappedGoal.canBeReplacedBy requires a strictly lower number. At 2, a horse in love mode would refuse the whistle outright: the exact failure the note was warning about, behind a different goal than it named. There is no integer between 1 and 2, so the goal sits at 1 and gives up what arbitration was doing there — yielding to panic and sun-shade, which are also at 1. That is paid for by hand: inDanger is checked in both the goal and the sweep, and is deliberately coarser than PanicGoal ’s own trigger (on fire, freezing, or a non-zero hurtTime ) so it errs toward letting the horse look after itself. Escape and the inspect hold are at 0 and still interrupt this the ordinary way, so the two genuinely non-negotiable goals need no help. The bond gate on binding (2026-09-29) EnderWhistleCalls.bindRefusal , a new method wrapping HorseOwnership.bindRefusal and adding the tier test on top. Not added to the shared one: that is also what bound stall signs call, and a bound sign has no reason to want a bond. Putting the test there would have gated the sign silently. The threshold is BIND_BOND_TIER , a named constant rather than a literal, so the next tuning session is one line. No server config: one threshold is cheap but it is a surface, and it has not been asked for. Guarded by the whistle_needs_bond gametest — bond 60 refused, 61 accepted, the two values either side of the boundary and nothing else, since anything more would be testing behaviourTier() . It goes through bindRefusal rather than posting an EntityInteract , because that method is the seam the real handler uses and there is exactly one call to it. Confirmed to fail with BIND_BOND_TIER lowered to 1, which let bond 60 through. One key, every whistle (2026-09-29) common/care/WhistleSelection holds the rule, and it is pure: a list of radii and bound ids in, the picks out, with WhistleSelectionTest beside it. server/WhistleBlowing does what needs a world — the inventory scan, cooldowns, calling the items, one line of chat. The payload carries no fields at all. The obvious design has the client work out which whistles to blow and name them, which is the TackSlotPayload lesson again: an index into a container is a claim about that container. So the client says only that the key was pressed and the server scans the sender’s real inventory. A tampered client can at most press its own key. StreamCodec.unit does the encoding — not a new trick here, DismountPayload already used it. Both items’ use() now delegate to a shared blow() that owns the cooldown. Two implementations would have become a way round the cooldown the first time one of them forgot to set it. The selection returns the area whistle first, and that ordering is a contract. A player can hold an echo whistle and an ender whistle bound to a horse twenty blocks away; both would call it, and it would teleport twice and chime twice. So the area whistle is blown first and reports which horses it handled, and an ender whistle bound to one of those is skipped. Returning the picks in inventory order would have made that work or not depending on which pocket the whistle was in — which is what the ordering test in WhistleSelectionTest pins. The action-bar prompt uses sendOverlayMessage , not the displayClientMessage with a boolean flag that the design note assumed: 26.1.2 renamed it, as the comment on block/Jumps already recorded. The text is built from Component.keybind , so it resolves against the player’s own Controls and reads correctly after a rebind. Telling a bound whistle apart, without art (2026-09-29) EnderWhistleItem.isFoil(stack) , returning whether BOUND_HORSE is present. Three lines. This replaced a planned art job , and is a better answer than the one it replaced rather than just a cheaper one. The plan was a second sprite plus an item model conditional on the component; the owner asked for the enchantment glint instead. The reason it is better is that the glint is derived, not stored . Every whistle bound before this shipped glimmers the moment the class loads, because nothing was written to any stack — "is this bound" keeps exactly one answer, the component, and the look is computed from it. The obvious alternative, setting ENCHANTMENT_GLINT_OVERRIDE on bind, would have worked for whistles bound afterwards and left every existing one dull with no way to find them. That is the general shape of the trap: a cosmetic written into saved data needs a migration, and a cosmetic computed from saved data does not. ItemStack.hasFoil() consults isFoil unless that override component is present, and it is what CuboidItemModelWrapper and SpecialModelWrapper actually read — checked in the 26.1.2 sources rather than assumed, since overriding a hook nothing calls would look exactly like the feature failing. The whistle carries no enchantment, so the default ( isEnchanted() ) was false and nothing is being overridden away. Two things deliberately not built (2026-09-29) Both were on the Roadmap tab as open possibilities and are now closed, so that the list shrinks rather than carrying a maybe forever. They are here rather than on decisions because that page is a pointer index now and says in as many words not to add decisions to it; a settled call belongs on the Coding tab of the thing it settles. Binding donkeys, mules and llamas to an ender whistle. No. The area whistles already call any AbstractHorse and are unchanged. The ender binding stays horse-only because BoundHorse , HorseWhereabouts , the crumble-to-dust path and the ancestry lookup all key on a genome-carrying Horse . Widening it would also drag in bound stall signs, which share the same component — so the cost is not the one class it looks like. Bound modes on the three area whistles — no. The idea was a ladder: loaded chunk, then same dimension, then any dimension, by tier. The ender whistle is that ladder’s top rung built properly, and a second way to reach across a dimension would compete with it for no gain. The Roadmap tab used to hedge this with “unless someone wants the ladder back”; it does not any more. The command whistle (2026-10-03) Piece 1 of the command whistle treatment. The idea came from the Bedrock addon 1,000,000 Horses! (Cynosia; scripts by InnerTri), whose Horse Whistle cycles Stay / Follow / Wander on right-click and applies to every nearby tamed horse when sneaking. It was surveyed from its .mcaddon on 2026-10-03. Nothing is copied from it : the copy surveyed was a re-upload of a paid Marketplace pack, so no code, art or text from it is in this repository. The wheel, the bond gates and the order goals are this mod’s own. common/ : care/HorseOrder , the orders. The enum is append-only and saved by NAME, so an unknown name loads as Rejoin herd. care/HorseOrders holds the gate (lead, then cart, then bond; Rejoin herd never refused) and every word a player reads. care/CommandWheel is the slice geometry: slice 0 is centred straight up and the slices run clockwise, with a dead centre. Tests: HorseOrdersTest , CommandWheelGeometryTest . The order is the attachment horsegenetics:horse_order ( data/HorseOrderAttachment ): the order, an anchor, the dimension it was given in, and who gave it. It is synced, and not copied on death. The default is no order, so every older world loads unchanged. Clearing is lazy where it can be. server/HorseOrdering.current drops an order whose giver no longer owns the horse, or that was given in another dimension. No hook in the ownership or portal code is needed. The moves within one dimension clear it themselves: the area whistle’s recall, the ender whistle’s call, Send home ( StallRecall ), and HorseStasisHandler.swallow . Opening the wheel ( client/CommandWhistleClient ) cancels InputEvent.InteractionKeyMappingTriggered . That event fires in Minecraft.startUseItem before MultiPlayerGameMode.interact sends the interact packet (read from the 26.1.2 sources), so the server never hears the click and nothing on the server has to be kept in step. The entity-interact events would not do: they fire after the packet has gone. The aimed horse is ray-cast out to 16 blocks and stopped at the first block. Whose horse it is, on the client, is the record’s answer ( HorseOrders.whistleUse , issue #36, fixed 2026-10-04). The first build asked vanilla’s getOwnerReference() , which is always null on a client — vanilla syncs the tamed flag and never the owner — so the plain hold opened nothing on any horse, and only the sneak-hold, which asks no owner, worked. The rule now reads HorseRecord.ownedBy , the owner the server mirrors onto the record every two seconds ( HorseOwnerTrackingHandler ). So a horse tamed a moment ago may take up to two seconds to answer the whistle, and a horse whose record has not reached the client opens nothing. Picking on release ( client/CommandWheelScreen ) works two ways. Opening a screen calls KeyMapping.releaseAll() , so keyUse.isDown() is useless from then on. The screen gets the right button’s mouseReleased , and tick() also polls GLFW. The poll catches a release that came before the screen opened, which is a tap: the cursor is still in the dead centre, so a tap picks nothing. The screen does not pause single-player. The client decides nothing. CommandHorsePayload carries entity ids and the order’s name. HorseOrdering.handle re-finds the horses and re-checks ownership, reach (16 + 2 blocks of slack), lead, cart and bond. Goals : OrderStayGoal and OrderFollowGoal , both at priority 4 , not the 1 the treatment recommended. A goal at equal or worse priority is never even asked canUse while a better one runs, so at 1 a stayed horse would never start HungerFoodGoal (3), and the owner’s rule is that an order never starves a horse. At 4, every need pre-empts the order: escape (0), panic and a whistle call (1), and hunger, food, sparring and fighting (3). Idling is held off: herd life (5–7) and vanilla’s stroll (6). BondFollowGoal shares 4 and stands down under any order, and its tier-1 Watch under Wander. WildHerdGoal needs no hook, because it already refuses tamed horses and only tamed horses take orders. Stay’s anchor is snapped to the ground ( HorseOrdering.groundAt ), and “at the spot” is judged across the ground. A horse ordered mid-jump, or still landing, was otherwise anchored in the air where no path reaches. The order_stay_walks_back gametest found this, because its horse had not landed yet. The item is item/CommandWhistleItem , stack size 1, and does nothing by itself. Its art is a recolour of the basic whistle in bake-item-recolours.mjs : green, because the ender whistle already owns purple. The trade is level 3 at 5 emeralds. The checklist task is COMMAND_WHISTLE . Gametests : order_stay_walks_back : a stayed horse moved three blocks is steered back to its spot. It checks the navigation’s target, not only the distance, because the neighbouring tests’ horses pull it about. With the walk-back cut out, the horse still drifted home once. Its horse is re-founded as the wild type once founded (#40, 2026-10-04): a founding roll that hunts horses ( Ade/Ade , Aae/Aae ) goes for any horse within sixteen blocks, and the fight out-ranks Stay by design, so about one run in thirty it never stood still long enough. A forced Aae/Aae roll with a horse overhead failed 100 of 100 copies before and passed 100 of 100 after. order_yields_to_needs : the order goals sit below every need, and bond-follow stands down. order_clears : an order is dropped on a new owner, in another dimension, and in stasis. whistle_sees_the_owner (#36): the record’s owner names the taming player, follows a sale and empties when the horse goes wild, and whistleUse answers each state as the wheel would. With the owner mirror cut out it fails on the first state. The recall’s clear needs a real ServerPlayer , so it is a play check. Hunt monsters and Defend me (Piece 3, 2026-10-03) Two orders appended to HorseOrder : HUNT_MONSTERS (anchored, so it keeps a spot like Stay) and DEFEND_ME (it follows() , like Follow). Both are combat() and bond tier 2. Nothing new is saved: the order’s name and its anchor already were, and the gate is derived from the genes each time. The gate is HorseOrders.fighter(genotype, epigenome) in common/. It asks HorseAbilities.of for each of the three loci — the path the genes’ own behaviour runs on, so the gate and the gene cannot disagree — and passes a horse granted an aggressive temper towards hostile or all (the aggression h and a pairs, and guardian), or a Combat ability above the 3.0 baseline (magic fighter). The a pairs are in by the owner’s call, asked when this piece was built: the treatment recommended leaving them out, because such a horse also goes for its rider. The gate is read only through those three genes’ keys, so a data-driven gene with a temper does not open it. The new refusal NOT_A_FIGHTER is asked after the lead and the cart and before the bond: no amount of bond changes the genes, and “not bonded enough” would send a player raising the bond for an order the horse can never take. Server and client ask the same question. HorseOrdering.fighter(record) parses the horse record; the server calls it in situation() , and the wheel calls it on the record the client already has from HorseRecordSyncPayload , so no new sync was needed. A record the client has not received yet does not grey the slice: the server answers for it. server/OrderCombatGoal , priority 2 in the target selector, only ever sets or drops the target. The chase and the swing are HorseMeleeGoal ’s (3), which out-ranks the order goals at 4, so the way home is the existing OrderStayGoal (now any anchored() order) and OrderFollowGoal (any order that follows() ). It scans once a second, staggered by entity id, considering at most eight entities — the aggression locus’s beat and cap — around the leash’s centre: the anchor, or the player who gave the order. It does not start while the horse already has a live target, and drops only the target it picked, so a horse whose own genes are fighting keeps fighting. The leash is HorseOrders.Leash(radius, breakOffHealth) , built by ServerConfig.orderLeash(order) from the three config keys: mayEngage picks only within the radius and only while above the break-off; keepsFighting lets a fight drift LEASH_SLACK_BLOCKS (4) past it, for the quarry or the horse, so a monster on the edge is not picked and dropped every second. This reopens the aggression locus’s no-chase rule for orders only; see its Coding tab . What it may pick : MobGroups.isHostile , minus players, the entity-type tag horsegenetics:command_never_target (creepers; packs can add to it), anything Passification.suppresses , any owned pet, any tamed horse, and anything the horse cannot canAttack . The last two were found by the gametest. A tamed zombie horse from a neighbouring test turned up as hostile, by its category. And an invulnerable husk was picked every second and never fought, because Mob.getTarget() answers null for a target the mob cannot attack. Only what the horse can see is picked, as the aggression locus does. The treatment’s UNVERIFIED 5 is settled. The only other target goal a horse has, WildHorseForgetTargetGoal , runs for untamed horses only, so nothing clears an ordered horse’s target on its own. UNVERIFIED 2 is answered and not used: 26.1.2 has Mob.setHomeTo / isWithinHome , and MeleeAttackGoal honours it, but the home is saved by vanilla and is what a lead sets ( PathfinderMob ), so it would fight the leash code. The leash is our own. Config (server, phc/server.toml ): orders.hunt_radius (16, 8–32), orders.defend_radius (8, 4–16), orders.break_off_health (0.3, 0–1; 0 is fight to the death). The defaults live once, as HorseOrders.DEFAULT_* ; accessors fall back to them before the config loads. Read live, so an edit takes effect on the next scan. Tests : HorseOrdersTest covers the gate for every ordered aggression pair (exactly six pass: three times of day, two targets), guardian, magic fighter and a plain horse. It also covers breeding-before-bond, the leash with its slack, and break-off with its clamps. Breaking the all branch turns it red. Gametests: order_combat_gate (a plain horse refuses both combat orders on the server’s own gate, a guardian takes them, and Stay is untouched). And order_hunt_picks_a_monster : a guardian on Hunt picks a husk four blocks off, not a creeper two blocks off. It is a guardian because a guardian starts nothing by itself, so the target can only be the order’s. With the tag check removed it went for the creeper. Both wait for the horse to be founded before giving it genes, because a founding afterwards rolls a new genome. Graze nearby, Guard here and Go home (Piece 2, 2026-10-03) The last three orders of the treatment, appended to HorseOrder (so saves keep loading: an order is saved by name, and nothing new is saved). All three are bond tier 2, by the owner’s call. Two new questions on the enum say what a goal needs to know: stands() (Stay, Hunt monsters, Guard here — held still at the anchor) and oneShot() (Go home — carried out at once, never stored). Guard here is anchored() and combat() : Hunt monsters with a short reach. It needed no goal of its own. OrderStayGoal now runs for any order that stands() (it was any anchored() one, which would have pinned a grazing horse), OrderCombatGoal centres it on its anchor, and ServerConfig.orderLeash gives it orders.guard_radius (6, 3–16). The fighting gate, the creeper tag and the break-off come free. The treatment recommended “never chases beyond 8”; the shipped leash lets a fight drift the usual LEASH_SLACK_BLOCKS (4) past the radius, so 10 at the default — one rule for every combat order rather than a second slack for this one. Graze nearby is anchored() but does not stands() . server/OrderGrazeGoal , MOVE at priority 4 beside the other order goals, starts only once the horse is past orders.graze_radius (12, 4–32) of its anchor, and walks it back until it is within half of that ( HorseOrders.Tether , TETHER_RETURN_SHARE ), so a horse at the edge is not turned back every step. Inside, it holds nothing, so vanilla’s stroll and the herd goals run as usual, while BondFollowGoal stands down as under any order. Not vanilla’s setHomeTo , for the reason in Piece 3 : a lead sets it. Go home is StallRecall.goHome : the Send home trip, not a second one. StallRecall.send (the browser button) and goHome now share travel() — rider, home, its world, price, live landing spot, then TicketHandler.arrive , after clearing the order. So it goes to the stall, or the holding pen without one, as the button does; the treatment’s “refuses with has no stall” became “no stall, and you have no holding pen” to match. It pays the server’s price per horse but skips the button’s cooldown, which exists to stop a mashed button pulling far chunks in; the whistle only reaches loaded horses beside you. Its refusals are four new Refusal values only the server gives (the client does not know the stalls, so the wheel never greys Go home for them). HorseOrdering.current drops a stored one-shot, which only a hand-edited save could hold. The wheel has nine slices, clockwise from the top: Stay, Guard here, Hunt monsters, Defend me, Follow, Wander, Graze nearby, Go home, Rejoin herd — kin beside kin. Nine 78-wide labels overlap on any circle that fits a GUI-scale-4 1080p screen, so the ring is now an ellipse (118 by 86), and the cursor is squashed by the same ratio before CommandWheel.sliceAt , which puts every label inside its own slice (checked by script for all nine). Tests : HorseOrdersTest (the tier-2 calls, the one-shot, the anchored and standing sets, the tether’s two edges with its clamp, the radii inside their bounds, Go home’s summary); moving the tether’s return share to 0.6 turns it red. Gametests: order_guard_holds_its_reach — a guardian on Guard here ignores a husk eight blocks off for three scans, then picks one four off, never the creeper; given the hunt radius by mistake, it went for the far husk. order_graze_tether — a horse anchored where it stands never runs the graze goal, one whose spot is past the radius does. Go home needs a real ServerPlayer and a stall, so it is a play check. Known gaps The three area whistles share one texture. A bound ender whistle is now told apart by its glint ; the tiers are not told apart at all. Nothing gates the area whistles on bond — deliberately; they call horses you already own.
