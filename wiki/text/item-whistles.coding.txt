Whistles
(Item / tool)
Three area whistles call every tamed horse you own nearby, each reaching further than the last. The fourth, the ender whistle , is bound to one horse for good and brings it to you from anywhere — even another dimension. A horse close enough to simply come walks in rather than appearing, the ender whistle asks for a bond before it will bind, and one key blows the lot , and a bound ender whistle glimmers so you can pick it out of a hotbar.

The area whistles item/WhistleItem , one class and a radius per tier. Pathing instead of teleporting and the bond gate both shipped on 2026-09-29. appendHoverText reads the radius field rather than a fourth constructor argument or a per-tier lang key, for the same reason WhistleItem.task() does: the radius is already the only thing that distinguishes the three, and a second parallel field is a second thing that can disagree with it. Added 2026-09-24 — until then the three tiers had no tooltip at all, and the numbers 16, 32 and 64 appeared nowhere a player could read them. The second line (“a ridden horse stays put; a leashed one is untied”) deliberately stops short of saying where the lead goes : that is behaviour.leads_return , a server option, and a client on a dedicated server has no copy of it to read. The lead, when a whistle moves the horse Neither whistle unties the horse itself any more. Both call server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player) , which suppresses vanilla’s ground drop and hands the lead to whoever blew the whistle — written up, with the reasoning and the gametest that guards it, on rider comfort , which owns that behaviour. Tickets go through the same helper. The ender whistle item/EnderWhistleItem — use calls, inventoryTick checks for dust once a second. Stacks to one. appendHoverText names the bound horse, reading it off the component rather than the entity: the horse may be unloaded, in another dimension, or dead, and the client cannot reach it in any of those cases. Same shape as the bound stall sign , which is where the wording came from. server/EnderWhistleCalls — binding (an EntityInteract handler using HorseOwnership.bindRefusal , the same refusal stall signs use), calling, and the dust. The binding is the existing BOUND_HORSE component. data/HorseWhereabouts — a server-global SavedData of each tamed horse’s last dimension and block, and whether it died. Written by server/HorseWhereaboutsHandler on join, on leave, every ten seconds (only when it moved more than 16 blocks or changed dimension), and on death. The ancestry database keeps a dead horse’s record for the pedigree, so it cannot answer “is it alive” — this can. How a call finds a horse Any level with the entity loaded: move it now. Otherwise the last sighting’s chunk gets a TicketType.PORTAL ticket (radius 2, which expires by itself) and the call waits on the server tick for up to five seconds for the entity section to load. Same dimension: snapTo . Another dimension: Entity.teleport(TeleportTransition) , which re-creates the entity in the target level. Ownership is checked at call time , never at bind time, which is what makes the whistle follow a sale. It moves an entity found by UUID and never creates one, so it cannot duplicate a horse. A horse deleted rather than killed — the horse dimension clearing its pens — is gone from the ancestry database, and that crumbles the whistle too. Walking a near horse in (2026-09-29) server/WhistleCalls holds the decision and every way a call can end; server/WhistleCallGoal does nothing but walk. Decided once, at blow time. tryWalk asks the horse’s own navigator for a fresh path to the player and walks only if that path exists, actually arrives ( canReach() — a path that stops short is a fence), and is within WALK_PATH_BLOCKS steps. Nothing is re-decided later: a horse either set off walking or it teleported, and there is no third state. The route is measured, not the distance , because fifteen blocks away can be eighty blocks of switchback. Since a route is never shorter than the straight line between its ends, capping the route caps the distance for free — so there is one number and not two. The cap is counted in path nodes , which are block steps, and a diagonal step covers about 1.4 blocks; that approximation is why the tooltip says “about”, and the tooltip reads the constant rather than repeating the number. Not BondFollowGoal ’s ReachCheck . That caches for two seconds, which is right for a goal asking every tick and wrong for a whistle — a call is a single event and deserves a fresh answer. It also only answers yes/no, and this needs the length. Why the state machine is not in the goal Every ending is decided in WhistleCalls ’s server-tick sweep, and the goal reads a marker. That is deliberate: a goal that never starts cannot end itself. If something at priority 0 holds MOVE , or the horse is in danger, the goal simply never runs — and a state machine living inside it would leave the horse neither walking nor teleported, which is the “the whistle ignored my horse” report with nothing in the log. Arrived — within the same three blocks the teleport path calls “already here”. Gave up — deadline passed, or no headway for two seconds. It is teleported . That fallback is the guarantee that a walked call is never worse than the old behaviour, and it is also what covers the goal never having started. In danger — dropped, and deliberately not teleported. Invalidated — player or horse gone, mounted, leashed. Dropped quietly. The marker is in memory only , keyed by horse UUID. A call lasts seconds; a half-finished one restored from disk would be a bug, so a reload mid-walk just ends it. Priority 1, and why not 2 The design note for this reasoned “below survival, above everything social” and concluded priority 2 . Reading the generated hierarchy rather than that line shows why 2 is wrong: priority 2 is vanilla’s BreedGoal , which claims MOVE , and an equal priority never interrupts — WrappedGoal.canBeReplacedBy requires a strictly lower number. At 2, a horse in love mode would refuse the whistle outright: the exact failure the note was warning about, behind a different goal than it named. There is no integer between 1 and 2, so the goal sits at 1 and gives up what arbitration was doing there — yielding to panic and sun-shade, which are also at 1. That is paid for by hand: inDanger is checked in both the goal and the sweep, and is deliberately coarser than PanicGoal ’s own trigger (on fire, freezing, or a non-zero hurtTime ) so it errs toward letting the horse look after itself. Escape and the inspect hold are at 0 and still interrupt this the ordinary way, so the two genuinely non-negotiable goals need no help. The bond gate on binding (2026-09-29) EnderWhistleCalls.bindRefusal , a new method wrapping HorseOwnership.bindRefusal and adding the tier test on top. Not added to the shared one: that is also what bound stall signs call, and a bound sign has no reason to want a bond. Putting the test there would have gated the sign silently. The threshold is BIND_BOND_TIER , a named constant rather than a literal, so the next tuning session is one line. No server config: one threshold is cheap but it is a surface, and it has not been asked for. Guarded by the whistle_needs_bond gametest — bond 60 refused, 61 accepted, the two values either side of the boundary and nothing else, since anything more would be testing behaviourTier() . It goes through bindRefusal rather than posting an EntityInteract , because that method is the seam the real handler uses and there is exactly one call to it. Confirmed to fail with BIND_BOND_TIER lowered to 1, which let bond 60 through. One key, every whistle (2026-09-29) common/care/WhistleSelection holds the rule, and it is pure: a list of radii and bound ids in, the picks out, with WhistleSelectionTest beside it. server/WhistleBlowing does what needs a world — the inventory scan, cooldowns, calling the items, one line of chat. The payload carries no fields at all. The obvious design has the client work out which whistles to blow and name them, which is the TackSlotPayload lesson again: an index into a container is a claim about that container. So the client says only that the key was pressed and the server scans the sender’s real inventory. A tampered client can at most press its own key. StreamCodec.unit does the encoding — not a new trick here, DismountPayload already used it. Both items’ use() now delegate to a shared blow() that owns the cooldown. Two implementations would have become a way round the cooldown the first time one of them forgot to set it. The selection returns the area whistle first, and that ordering is a contract. A player can hold an echo whistle and an ender whistle bound to a horse twenty blocks away; both would call it, and it would teleport twice and chime twice. So the area whistle is blown first and reports which horses it handled, and an ender whistle bound to one of those is skipped. Returning the picks in inventory order would have made that work or not depending on which pocket the whistle was in — which is what the ordering test in WhistleSelectionTest pins. The action-bar prompt uses sendOverlayMessage , not the displayClientMessage with a boolean flag that the design note assumed: 26.1.2 renamed it, as the comment on block/Jumps already recorded. The text is built from Component.keybind , so it resolves against the player’s own Controls and reads correctly after a rebind. Telling a bound whistle apart, without art (2026-09-29) EnderWhistleItem.isFoil(stack) , returning whether BOUND_HORSE is present. Three lines. This replaced a planned art job , and is a better answer than the one it replaced rather than just a cheaper one. The plan was a second sprite plus an item model conditional on the component; the owner asked for the enchantment glint instead. The reason it is better is that the glint is derived, not stored . Every whistle bound before this shipped glimmers the moment the class loads, because nothing was written to any stack — "is this bound" keeps exactly one answer, the component, and the look is computed from it. The obvious alternative, setting ENCHANTMENT_GLINT_OVERRIDE on bind, would have worked for whistles bound afterwards and left every existing one dull with no way to find them. That is the general shape of the trap: a cosmetic written into saved data needs a migration, and a cosmetic computed from saved data does not. ItemStack.hasFoil() consults isFoil unless that override component is present, and it is what CuboidItemModelWrapper and SpecialModelWrapper actually read — checked in the 26.1.2 sources rather than assumed, since overriding a hook nothing calls would look exactly like the feature failing. The whistle carries no enchantment, so the default ( isEnchanted() ) was false and nothing is being overridden away. Two things deliberately not built (2026-09-29) Both were on the Roadmap tab as open possibilities and are now closed, so that the list shrinks rather than carrying a maybe forever. They are here rather than on decisions because that page is a pointer index now and says in as many words not to add decisions to it; a settled call belongs on the Coding tab of the thing it settles. Binding donkeys, mules and llamas to an ender whistle. No. The area whistles already call any AbstractHorse and are unchanged. The ender binding stays horse-only because BoundHorse , HorseWhereabouts , the crumble-to-dust path and the ancestry lookup all key on a genome-carrying Horse . Widening it would also drag in bound stall signs, which share the same component — so the cost is not the one class it looks like. Bound modes on the three area whistles — no. The idea was a ladder: loaded chunk, then same dimension, then any dimension, by tier. The ender whistle is that ladder’s top rung built properly, and a second way to reach across a dimension would compete with it for no gain. The Roadmap tab used to hedge this with “unless someone wants the ladder back”; it does not any more. Known gaps The three area whistles share one texture. A bound ender whistle is now told apart by its glint ; the tiers are not told apart at all. Nothing gates the area whistles on bond — deliberately; they call horses you already own. Binding an ender whistle is gated. The far-chunk wait and the cross-dimension teleport are written against 26.1.2 sources and not seen working — see the verification tab . A horse moved while its chunk was unloaded by something other than itself (a piston, another mod) is looked for where it was last seen, and not found.
