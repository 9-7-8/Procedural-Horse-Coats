Carts: wagons, plows and what a horse is worth in harness
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Six horse-drawn vehicles — a covered wagon you can drive from the box seat, a plow , a seed drill , a reaper , a supply cart and an animal cart — in every wood your modpack has , vanilla's twelve and anyone else's. How fast a horse pulls one is the first thing that ever read pulling ability , and it reads the horse's speed at the same time, on purpose — and now what is in the cart as well. Derived from AstikorCarts / NiftyCarts / UsefulCarts, MIT — full lineage below . Not play-tested ; checklist in verification .

1. The six vehicles Every one of them is pulled by one animal, and they divide cleanly into three that carry things — wagon, supply cart, animal cart — and three that do a job to the ground they are dragged over : plow, reaper, seed drill. Which group a vehicle is in decides almost everything about it, including whether filling it slows your horse down. Building one Craft the cart wheel first, because every vehicle spends two of them and the wagon spends four. It is eight sticks around one plank, of any wood; the wheel is not wood-specific, only the cart is. Every other recipe takes planks (and for the wagon, stripped logs) of one wood, and that is the wood the cart comes out in. Mixing woods in the grid crafts nothing. The recipes. That second fact is why some modded woods have five carts and not six — a mod that never shipped a stripped log cannot build a wagon, so it does not get one. See §15 . Place a cart by right-clicking the ground with it. It needs the room: if the cart would overlap a block or another entity, nothing happens and you keep the item. 3. Hitching, unhitching, and trains Press R — "Attach/Detach Cart", rebindable under Horse Carts in the controls. That is the whole interface. It hitches the nearest cart within about two blocks of you or of whatever you are riding, and pressing it again unhitches whatever you are pulling. You do not need a horse. Standing on foot and pressing R hitches the cart to you , and you can drag any of the six around yourself. It is slow and none of the genetics apply, but it is how you reposition a cart without fetching an animal. Anything saddleable can pull — horses, donkeys, mules, camels. Not pigs or striders, which are steered by a held item rather than reins. An animal that is not one of this mod's horses pulls at the baseline pulling score; a donkey is not feeble for want of a genome. A cart can pull a cart. Hitch a second vehicle to the back of the first and you have a train, and you can keep going. The horse pays for all of it — the loads of every vehicle in the chain add up on the animal at the front. It comes unhitched on its own when the cart is dragged too far from the puller, when a block comes between the two — drive between two trees and the traces part — and when the puller dies or itself becomes a passenger. There is an attach and a detach sound, so you can hear which happened without looking. A hitched cart follows at a fixed distance: about 2.5 blocks for the wagon and 1.3 for the implements, which is the room they need to swing behind a turning horse. Carts climb a one-block step without help. 4. Weight: what a full cart costs The rule A vehicle's load is what it weighs empty, plus what is in it. Filling a supply cart to the brim adds half again to what it asks of the horse; filling a wagon adds sixty per cent. The three field implements never change — a plow's load is the blade in the soil, not the tools racked on it. The cost is linear in how full the thing is , so there is no threshold to learn and no cliff to fall off: half a cart is half the penalty, and every stack you add costs the same as the last. What counts as "full" is the one thing that differs by vehicle: What "full" means Vehicle Measured by Notes Supply cart its 54 slots By goods , not by slots touched: a slot holding one arrow is a sixty-fourth of a slot, not a full one. Nine loose torches do not make a horse labour. Wagon chests fitted, then what is in them Half the wagon's extra weight is the chests themselves , empty or not — three bolted-on chests and nothing in them is already half the full penalty. Filling them is the other half. Animal cart its two seats One cow is half a load, two is a full one. No attempt to weigh a pig against a sheep — only animals of about that size fit in the first place. Plow, reaper, seed drill nothing Deliberately flat. A plow does not become half again as hard to drag because you gave it a second hoe, and a player choosing between fitting the tool and pulling the plow is choosing between a working plow and a useless one. The load is live. Load a cart while it stands hitched and the horse gets slower within a second , without unhitching; unload it and the speed comes back. The same is true of a cow climbing into the animal cart, or a chest going onto a wagon mid-journey. This is the reason to breed a strong horse rather than just a fast one for trade runs specifically. An ordinary horse can trot along with an empty wagon; the same horse with three chests packed is doing close to twice the work, and the difference between an ordinary animal and a draft one stops being cosmetic exactly there. 5. The wagon The biggest, the heaviest, and the only one with a roof. Empty, it seats four and holds nothing. Chests, and what they cost Right-click the wagon with a chest to bolt one on. Its storage grows in three stages, and so does the number of seats it loses: Chests against seats Chests Storage Seats left To fit the next one none — 4 any time 1 9×4 4 no more than 2 aboard 2 9×8 2 nobody aboard 3 12×9 none — So a full freight wagon is not a passenger wagon, and you cannot sneak the third chest on with the family sitting in the back. The chests come back as items if the wagon is destroyed, along with everything in it. The roof Right-click high on the wagon — aim at the hoops, not the bed — holding at least five wool carpets of one colour. That is the roof, in that colour. Doing it again with a different colour swaps it and hands the old five carpets back. Right-click the roof with an empty hand to furl and unfurl it: three positions, open to shut. Driving from the box seat The first person to board drives . The box seat steers the horse in front through a hidden rider, which is why it works at all — and that hidden rider occupies a seat on the horse's back, so on a horse strong enough to carry two you can have a driver on the wagon and somebody riding the horse. That is what makes a carriage read as a carriage rather than a trailer. Newly built The box seat did nothing at all until this was wired up — the wagon was the one vehicle that never spawned the hidden rider, while its tooltip claimed otherwise. It is code that has never been played. First thing to check in verification . Passengers you did not invite Small animals climb into a wagon by themselves if there is a seat, the same way they climb into the animal cart, and tame ones sit down when they do. Shift-right-click the wagon to throw every non-player passenger out. A wagon will also fly a banner . 6. The plow Three slots, three tools, three lanes : the tools sit at the left, centre and right of the blade and each works its own furrow about 1.7 blocks ahead of the cart. Fit one tool and you plow one lane; fit three and you plow three abreast. What each tool does to the ground Tool Effect Clears out of the way first Hoe tills grass and dirt into farmland grass, ferns, dead bushes, flowers, saplings Shovel makes dirt paths the same, plus snow Axe strips logs it is dragged past vines, glow lichen That clearing step is what stops a plow jamming on a dandelion: the flower is broken and dropped, then the ground underneath is tilled. Anything else with a tool component can be racked too, and does whatever that tool does when used on a block — but only those three have blocks they will clear first. Right-click the plow to toggle it on and off , so you can drive one home across a field you have already tilled. It works only while the cart is actually moving, and only while a player is driving — either pulling it on foot or riding the animal that is. A plow towed behind a wandering horse does nothing. The tools wear out. They take durability damage as they work and break with the usual sound, leaving the lane empty until you rack another. Fitting three tools does not make the plow heavier. 7. The reaper A cutting bar that clears a swath about a block and a half wide beside the cart as it is drawn along, from roughly 1.1 to 2.3 blocks out to one side. It folds up when it is not working, and unfolds with a trapdoor clunk when it is. It cuts what a scythe would: crops, grass and dry grass, ferns, bushes, dead bushes, flowers and saplings , and anything else tagged as harvestable. Everything drops on the ground where it fell — the reaper has no inventory of its own, so bring a supply cart behind it if you do not fancy walking the field twice. It does not check ripeness The reaper cuts a crop block because it is a crop, not because it is ready. Drive one over a field you planted this morning and you will harvest seedlings. Recorded in known gaps ; until it is fixed, the seed drill and the reaper want to be run in that order and not the other way round. Somebody has to be sitting on it. Right-click to climb on; shift-right-click and it tells you as much in the status bar. From that seat you steer the horse, the same way the wagon's box seat does, and the game counts the distance separately as "steering". 8. The seed drill Nine slots of seed, planted three lanes wide — one to each side of the cart and one down the middle — onto ground that is already tilled. It plants roughly one seed per step, drawn at random from whichever slots have something plantable in them, so loading it with four kinds of seed gives you a mixed field rather than stripes. It plants wheat, beetroot, melon and pumpkin seeds, potatoes, carrots, torchflower seeds, pitcher pods and small flowers — and any modded crop , without that mod knowing anything about this one: if the item places a crop block, the drill will sow it. It needs no driver. Unlike the plow and the reaper, a seed drill plants whenever it is being pulled and moving, whoever or whatever is pulling. Lead it behind a plow on the same pass and you till and sow in one trip. 9. The supply cart Fifty-four stacks — a double chest on two wheels — and the prettiest thing in the cart system: it shows you what is in it . The four commonest kinds of thing in the cart are drawn sitting in the bed, blocks stacked before loose items, with real models for tools, armour, flowers, paintings and a spare wheel. You can tell a loaded cart from an empty one at fifty blocks, and roughly what it is loaded with. Right-click to sit on it; shift-right-click to open it . If somebody is already sitting on it, right-click opens it instead. Every piece of the cargo rendering can be switched off separately in the client config if it costs you frames, and each falls back to a plain floating item rather than vanishing. It is the vehicle where weight bites hardest in practice, because it is the one you actually fill to the top. A full supply cart asks half again what an empty one does, which puts it level with a plow. It will also fly a banner . 10. The animal cart Two seats, for players or for livestock, and animals load themselves : walk a cow into the back of one, or park it where they mill about, and in they climb. Tame animals sit down once aboard. Only creatures small enough to fit will board — no cows-in-a-teacup — and water animals never do. Shift-right-click to empty it : every non-player passenger gets out. Right-click to take a seat yourself, from which you steer the horse, as on the reaper. Its passengers are its cargo, so two animals make it a full cart . It is the lightest vehicle in the game empty, and even with both seats filled it asks less of a horse than an empty reaper — though rather more than an empty seed drill, which is the thing to remember before you lead a pair of cows up a hill. 11. Banners, damage, and what drops Supply carts, animal carts and wagons will fly a banner — right-click one onto the back. Any banner, patterns and all. Putting a second banner on swaps them and hands the first one back. Carts take damage like a boat: hits accumulate and heal off over a few seconds, and enough of them in quick succession breaks the cart. A creative player breaks one in a single hit. Cactus does nothing to them, and you cannot break the cart you are sitting in . When one does break it drops itself, its banner, its contents, and — for a wagon — its chests and its carpets, so nothing is ever lost to a mis-swing except the walk back. One more thing worth knowing before you park: farm animals keep away from the working vehicles . Cows, sheep and the rest path around a plow, seed drill, reaper or supply cart standing within about three blocks. They do not avoid the wagon or the animal cart — which is exactly why those two are the ones they will climb into. 12. Why your horse matters Pulling ability sets a ceiling; speed decides how close you get to it. That is the whole rule, and both halves are real. A horse with a low pulling score is capped on a heavy cart. You can breed it faster and faster and it will not go faster, because it cannot get the wagon moving any harder than its shoulders allow. A horse with a huge pulling score and no speed never reaches the ceiling its shoulders could carry — it is simply a slow horse. So the fastest carthorse is neither specialist : Pulling an empty wagon Horse Speed Pull Keeps Actual haul speed fast and weak 0.30 3 43% 0.129 slow and strong 0.13 9 81% 0.105 balanced 0.22 7 67% 0.147 The balanced horse beats both. This is deliberate and it is the reason the model is shaped the way it is — a draft breed that dumped speed entirely would be the obvious and boring answer, so the model does not allow it. The numbers in that table are a unit test , not a claim. Loading the wagon does not change the shape of any of this; it moves every horse down the same curve, and it moves the weak ones furthest. 13. Reading it on the horse Open a horse's information screen (the i button on its inventory). On the Overview tab: Pull sits with Health, Speed, Jump and Size, because it is the fifth body stat and not a cart footnote. It shows the 1–10 score and a word for it — feeble, light, ordinary, strong, powerful, a true draft horse . A Draught block below says what the score buys: how much of its speed this horse would keep hauling a wagon empty and loaded to the roof , whether it carries a second rider, and how far it has hauled in its life . The two wagon figures are the useful pair. A horse that keeps most of its speed empty and collapses when loaded is a carriage horse, not a carthorse, and the screen is where you find that out before the trade run rather than during it. That last figure belongs to the horse , not to you. It survives being sold, given away or inherited, and it is how you tell a working animal from a fresh one at a glance. Your own side of it is on the vanilla statistics screen: distance pulling each of the six vehicles, distance riding in one, and distance steering a wagon, reaper or animal cart from its seat. 14. Two riders A horse whose pulling score is 6 or better carries two riders . Six is one point above ordinary: a modestly bred horse gets there, a wild-caught one usually will not, and you do not need a draft breed for it. Carrying and hauling are the same claim about an animal, which is why there is no separate carrying gene to breed for. This also matters for the wagon, for the reason in §5 : the box seat spends one of those two places on the horse's back. 15. Every wood, including modded ones Carts exist in vanilla's twelve woods and in every wood any other mod you have installed adds , discovered the same way the double fence gates discover theirs. A modded cart crafts from that mod's own planks and is textured from that mod's own blocks — no cart texture is shipped at all, they are assembled at load time out of the wood you built it from. Two rough edges. A modded wood with no stripped log gets five carts and no wagon , because the wagon recipe spends one — better than a wagon that cannot be crafted, which is what used to happen. And a modded wood's carts wear the oak icon in the inventory, because there are painted icons in the jar for vanilla's woods and nothing to paint a thirteenth from; the cart itself is the right wood the moment you place it. See known gaps . 16. Horse-powered machinery If you have a mod that makes a horse turn a mill or a treadmill, a good horse can drive it harder than a bad one. See compatibility for what is wired up and what still needs the other mod's cooperation. 17. What the game asks of you The carts have a chapter in Getting Started — farming and haulage. Placing a cart ticks one task, hitching one ticks another, and each of the six vehicles has its own task that completes the first time you drive it, which means the cart has actually moved with you at the reins. Upstream's eleven advancements were not ported; this is what replaced them.
