Cheek spikes
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One copy and the horse grows a short row of bone spikes along each cheek, pointing out from the head and a little back, the longest at the rear. How long and thick they are and how many each cheek has is written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, cheek spike colour .

It is two kinds (one per side), CheekSpikeGenerator and CheekSpikeSize , and one line in GrownParts.of dressed by its colour locus . The granting locus is in GrownParts.shapes ’s every-part baseline. The mesh: a row that scales per spike A spike is two boxes, a base and a narrower point, swept back about x and then laid over a quarter turn about z so it points out from the head. Four are baked, 1.3 units apart along the cheek and centred on the anchor. Spike 0 is the rearmost and the longest, so the count shows the big end of the row and grows the next one in toward the mouth. The spikes point across the anchor’s up, so the layer’s whole-part stretch (which is along that up) would move them apart and not lengthen them. The kind therefore scales per element ( PartKind.scalesPerElement ), the spine row’s rule and its first use on the head: each spike is one tree rooted on the anchor, and PartModel puts the length and girth on each root in its own frame. There is one style. Length, girth and count are all applied at draw time, so the whole part is three meshes a side. CheekSpikesGeneTest holds the numbering and the mirroring. The bone version Bon/Bon on the colour locus . A spike is all bone, so nothing is left off. Both skeleton breeds carry cheek_spikes in their horn count group as a slot of its own, with the colour pinned to bone (owner, 2026-10-09).
