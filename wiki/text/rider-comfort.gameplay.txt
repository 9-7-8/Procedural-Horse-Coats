Rider comfort
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Five small things that make riding less irritating, borrowed from other mods that already do them. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship alone. Four of the five have shipped, and one is left — a lead comes back to you now whether this mod teleported the horse out from under it or the leash simply snapped, you can mine from the saddle at the speed you would standing there, ALT + E opens your own pack from the saddle, and a piece of tack goes on with a right-click instead of a trip through the Gear tab. What remains is fading the horse out as you look down, which touches the coat itself and is the only one with a real unknown in it.

Your lead comes back A horse cannot stay leashed to a fence while it is being teleported across the world, so anything that summons one has to untie it first. It used to leave the lead lying on the ground where the horse had been standing — which, when you had just called the horse to you from sixty blocks away, meant walking back for it. With an ender whistle or an interdimensional ticket it was worse than that: the lead was left in the dimension the horse started in, and you were unlikely to ever see it again. Now the lead goes into your pack , or drops at your own feet if your pack is full. Nothing else changes: the horse still arrives, and it still arrives untied. It applies wherever this mod moves a horse on purpose — all three area whistles , the ender whistle, and both kinds of ticket . It does not apply when a horse walks into a portal on its own: the person holding the lead is standing right there in that case, so the lead lands at their feet already and vanilla’s own behaviour is the legible one. Server owners who would rather have vanilla exactly can turn it off with behaviour.leads_return , which puts the lead back on the ground where the horse was. — and so does the one that snapped The above was only half of it. Vanilla drops leads too, and on the whole it drops them in worse places: a leash snaps when the horse gets more than twelve blocks from you, and the lead lands where the horse was — which is the far end of whatever just went wrong, usually while you are chasing it. Break a fence knot with four animals tied to it and you get four leads scattered round the post. Now those come back too. A lead goes to whoever tied it on , wherever they happen to be. Whoever tied it on is meant literally, and it is not the same as “whoever is holding it”. A lead on a fence post has nobody holding it at all — that is the case worth getting right, since a horse can stand tied up for days — so the mod remembers the person who walked the horse to the fence, and the lead finds them even if they are in the Nether by the time it snaps. It never holds a lead for you. Three cases stay exactly as vanilla left them: a horse nobody was recorded as tying up (leashed by a command, or tied before this shipped), a horse tied by somebody who has since logged off, and a horse that walked into a portal with its holder standing right beside it. In all three the lead falls on the ground where it always did. The rule is that a lead is never lost , and never parked somewhere you have to go and claim it later. Same switch as the first half, behaviour.leads_return — off restores vanilla for both. Mining from the saddle Minecraft mines at a fifth speed whenever you are not standing on the ground. That rule is there to stop you tunnelling as you fall, and it is a good rule — but a player sitting on a horse is not standing on anything either, so it caught riders too. One sapling across the path, one dirt block on a ledge, and you had to get off, break it, and get back on . Now you mine from the saddle at the speed you would mine standing in the same spot. Nothing else about mining changes: the same tools, the same blocks, the same enchantments. The horse has to have its feet on the ground. Mid-jump, or on a horse that flies, the fifth-speed penalty still applies — you are genuinely in the air then, and mining out of the air is a different thing from mining from the saddle. This is the one piece on this page that changes a vanilla rule rather than adding something beside it, so the switch matters more here than elsewhere: behaviour.mounted_mining_penalty_removed is on by default, and turning it off restores vanilla exactly. It is a server setting, so on a server it is the server’s answer that counts, not each player’s. Your own inventory, from the saddle This mod takes E away while you are riding: instead of vanilla’s horse screen you get this mod’s own horse screen , which is a far better trade than it sounds — the screen it replaces held a saddle slot and a barding slot and nothing else. But it left a rider with no way at all to open their own bag without getting off the horse first. ALT + E now opens your own inventory. E on its own still opens the horse’s screen, exactly as before. It works on foot too , not only in the saddle. A key that only functions while mounted would be one more thing to remember, and when it did nothing you would have no way to tell whether the key was unbound or the game had decided you were not riding. There is no config option for this one , which makes it the exception on this page. Unbinding the key in Options Controls already switches it off, and it is listed there under Inventory , directly beneath vanilla’s own “Open/Close Inventory” — which is where you would be looking anyway if you were wondering what happened to E . Rebind either of them, or swap the two over, and the mod follows. On a donkey or a mule nothing changed to begin with: this mod keeps no record of one, so E opens vanilla’s screen with its chest grid, as it always did. The new key still works there, and gives you the same pack it gives you anywhere else. Tack goes on with a right-click A saddle has always gone on by holding it and right-clicking the horse. So has a barding. But the seventeen other things a horse can wear — the braids, and every pad, blanket, bag, boot and shoe still to be made — could only be put on through the Gear tab: open the screen, find the tab, click the slot, pick the item off a list. Now they go on the same way the saddle does. Hold the piece, right-click your horse, and it lands in the first empty slot that takes it. Today that means the rescuing braid , and only the braid , because it is the only piece of this mod’s own gear that has been made yet. It is not a special case, though: the click asks each slot what it accepts, so every piece still to come works this way from the day it exists, with nothing to add. It never swaps. If every slot that would take the piece is already full, the click is left alone and does whatever it would have done — usually putting you in the saddle. You can see the braid already in the mane, so nothing is ambiguous, and the alternative would mean being unable to mount your own horse while holding a spare. A braid goes to the mane first and the tail second; a boot would go near fore, off fore, near hind, off hind, in the order the paper doll reads. Your horse, and not a foal. Both refusals say so in a line across the bottom of the screen rather than doing nothing, because both are rules this mod invented and a silent rule looks like a bug. An untamed horse is left to vanilla, which rears up at you — a clearer answer about a wild horse than any sentence here would be. Sneaking is untouched: sneak-and-use still opens the horse’s screen whatever is in your hand. The switch is behaviour.rightclick_equips_tack , on by default; off, the Gear tab is the only way again. The saddle and the barding are vanilla’s behaviour either way and the switch does not reach them.
