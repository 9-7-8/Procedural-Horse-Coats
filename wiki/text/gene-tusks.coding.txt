Tusks
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One gene for the growths of the mouth and muzzle, with a form for each allele: a narwhal horn , one long spiral tusk straight out of the front of the muzzle; boar tusks , a pair curling up from the corners of the lower jaw; and sabre fangs , a pair of long blades hanging from the upper lip past the chin. A horse with two different forms grows both. Each form’s length, thickness, curve and shape are written on its own allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, tusk colour .

PartKind.NARWHAL is HornGenerator in its two twisted styles ( NARWHAL_STYLES : spiral and tight, never the bending smooth style) on its own ladder, NarwhalSize , from 10 to 30 model units, geometric, with the horn’s sixteen size buckets. Two alleles, two generators and four anchors; no new render path, render-state field or config key, and no change to anything saved. TuskGenerator : a tapering chain of three to five boxes. The root leaves the jaw 66° outward from upright and every later box turns back toward upright, so the tusk curls up beside the nose. Three forms ( CURL , short; SWEEP , long; HOOKED , whose last two boxes turn hard), three sweep buckets and two hook directions: TuskGenerator.styles() meshes a side. The sweep is baked in buckets, as the dragon horn’s splay is, because it is a turn about z that differs by side and the layer’s one free rotation is about x . SabreGenerator : a flat blade of two boxes (three when curved), thin across the horse through PartNode.width . It is the third flat box after the membrane and the plate’s slab, and PartGeneratorTest allows it for PartKind.sabre() . Three forms ( STRAIGHT , CURVED , SERRATED ) and three serration patterns. A pattern shows only on the serrated form, so SabreGenerator.style folds the other forms to pattern 0 and they share a mesh. The fang hangs because its root is turned over. Every mesh grows up its own -y . The fang’s root box carries a half-turn about x , in the mesh and not in the layer. That keeps the blade on the anchor’s y axis, so the layer’s stretch and tilt act on it unchanged and the two new anchor cases are only a translate. After the half-turn the mesh’s -z is the horse’s back: a positive rx curves the blade toward the throat, and the barbs sit on -z . Sizes : TuskSize (2.5 to 9 units) and SabreSize (3 to 10), four classes each, geometric. Within a class the part is scaled uniformly, the dragon horn’s rule, with girth as the nudge across it. Pairs are two kinds , one per side, mirrored box for box with rz negated and never with a negative scale. TusksGeneTest.theLeftOfAPairIsTheRightMirrored holds it. One outcome per set of forms expressionOf asks which forms the pair holds, not how many copies: narwhal , tusks , sabre , tusks_sabre , tusks_narwhal , sabre_narwhal , or wild. partsFor returns the horn, then the tusks right and left, then the fangs right and left, and GrownParts.of dresses all of them from the one colour locus. GrownParts.listed names a pair once (“Boar tusks”, “Sabre fangs”). Each form reads its own copy Every other part gene reads the expressed copy. This one reads the copy that carries the form , through TusksGene.copyFor : a Nar/Sab horse grows its fangs from the Sab copy’s numbers and its horn from the Nar copy’s, so the two are bred apart though they share a locus. Two copies of one form read the first copy (the antlers’ symmetric rule), not the higher-priority one. A copy follows its own allele because of Epigenome ’s alignment invariant: Copies.first() belongs to pair.first() , and Genome keeps them aligned when breeding. The treatment marked this unverified. TusksGeneTest breeds Nar/n with n/n both ways round and checks that every horned foal’s horn has its Nar copy’s length, never the empty wild copy’s. eachFormReadsTheCopyThatCarriesIt then sets the two copies of every mixed pair to different lengths and checks each form against its own. A one-copy boar is n/Tsk , so its tusks read the second slot while a one-copy narwhal reads the first: a mutant that always read slot 0 turned that test red (2026-10-08), as one that always read the second slot did for slice A. The numbers are generic Every copy carries the same five numbers whatever its form, and each form reads the ones it uses. style is a category of six so that each form’s own count divides it: every style equally likely, and two forms on one horse independent. The schema has been these five since slice A, so slice B changed nothing saved. A breed file may write either; AllelePair puts them in order. The skeleton breeds roll one of the three Owner, 2026-10-08. The skeleton breeds’ horn count group holds one slot for this gene, and a founder that draws the slot gets Nar/Nar , Tsk/Tsk or Sab/Sab at equal weight, in bone ( tusk_colour is pinned Bon/Bon ). The slot’s share of founders did not move; the three forms split it. A group’s locus could only name one pair, so the format grew a choice: Breed.GroupLocus now holds a weighted list of pairs. A one-pair locus draws no extra randomness, so every other grouped locus rolls exactly as it did. UndeadBreedsTest.theTusksSlotRollsOneOfThreeForms holds the thirds and the share, and went red with the draw fixed to the first pair. Why priority 334 The part loci fill 227–246, and 247 is the only slot left beside them. The next free run of two was 334/335.
