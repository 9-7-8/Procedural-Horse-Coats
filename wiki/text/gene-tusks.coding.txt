Tusks
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One gene for the growths of the mouth and muzzle, with a form for each allele. Today it has one form, the narwhal horn : one long spiral tusk growing straight forward out of the front of the muzzle. Its length, thickness, twist and lift are written on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own, tusk colour . Boar tusks and sabre fangs are the next two forms (see the Roadmap tab).

Gene key horsegenetics.tusks Class TusksGene Priority 334 (magical band), with its colour locus at 335; only a code-order slot Family Grown parts (a MAGIC_PARTS override) Alleles Nar n , dominant; Tsk and Sab come with slice B Founders Nar/n at 0.1% ( WILD_NARWHAL_PERCENT ); no invisible carriers Epigenome length (0–0.6, clamp 0–1), girth (0.85–1.15, clamp 0.7–1.6), curve (0–1), style (one of STYLES , six), seed Part NARWHAL at SNOUT ; HORN , with HORN_TIP on the last two segments Coat effect none ; every outcome is a wild type Slice A of the tusks treatment The narwhal horn is the cheapest form, so it came first and proved the gene, the new anchor and the colour locus. There is no new generator . PartKind.NARWHAL is HornGenerator in its two twisted styles ( NARWHAL_STYLES : spiral and tight, never the bending smooth style) on its own ladder, NarwhalSize , from 10 to 30 model units, geometric, with the horn’s sixteen size buckets. No new render path either: the client change is the SNOUT anchor’s case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor . One call in GrownParts.of , dressed by its colour locus ; the granting locus is in GrownParts.shapes ’s every-part baseline. Each form reads its own copy Every other part gene reads the expressed copy. This one reads the copy that carries the form , through TusksGene.copyFor . That is for slice B: a Sab/Nar horse will grow its fangs from the Sab copy’s numbers and its horn from the Nar copy’s, so the two are bred apart though they share a locus. Two copies of one form read the first copy (the antlers’ symmetric rule), not the higher-priority one. A copy follows its own allele because of Epigenome ’s alignment invariant: Copies.first() belongs to pair.first() , and Genome keeps them aligned when breeding. The treatment marked this unverified. TusksGeneTest now breeds Nar/n with n/n both ways round and checks that every horned foal’s horn has its Nar copy’s length, never the empty wild copy’s. A mutant that always read the second slot turned that test and the homozygote test red. The numbers are generic Every copy carries the same five numbers whatever its form, and each form reads the ones it uses. The narwhal horn reads four: length (its place on NarwhalSize ), girth , curve as a lift of up to NARWHAL_LIFT (0.45 rad) above the head’s axis, and style % 2 as its twist. style is a category of six so that each form’s own count (two now, three for the tusks and fangs) divides it: every style equally likely, and two forms on one horse independent. The seed is for the fangs’ serration and the tusks’ hook. Both are fixed now, so slice B changes no saved schema. Why only Nar is declared A Tsk or Sab allele declared today would grow nothing, which is a promise the game would not keep. Alleles are saved by token, and slice B appends them after n . Appending never changes the canonical order of a pair that already exists, so no saved horse’s copies move to the other allele. Why priority 334 The part loci fill 227–246, and 247 is the only slot left beside them. The next free run of two was 334/335. The MAGIC_PARTS override files the pair with the parts whatever band the number falls in, and nothing requires slots to be unique.
