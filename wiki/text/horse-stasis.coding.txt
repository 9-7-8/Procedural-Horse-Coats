Horse stasis: chambers and the bank
(Gameplay / partly built)
A cheap way to hold horses without them costing server tick time. A stasis chamber is an item a horse goes into; a Horse Stasis Bank is a block that holds many chambers and lets you read the horses inside them. The whole point is the cheapest tier, so a server with hundreds of horses can put most of them somewhere that costs nothing, instead of every horse being a real, ticking entity forever. All four chambers are built and the bank does everything they buy — it keeps them, reads them on its Browse tab, mends them from its feed and water, collects what they drop, and breeds a pair you turn out at stud. What is still planned is the self-triggering Emergency chamber.

Capture and release: it is the lycanthropy swap This page used to say that capture/release of a live, specific entity was “new work with no existing analog in the mod” . That was wrong, and finding out it was wrong is most of why stage one was cheap. LycanthropyHandler has carried a whole horse through a night spent inside a wolf since the LYCAN gene shipped, and it does it with exactly two calls: horse.saveWithoutId(TagValueOutput) a CompoundTag , then horse.discard() . EntityType.HORSE.create(level, EntitySpawnReason.LOAD) horse.load(TagValueInput.create(...)) level.addFreshEntity(horse) . On NeoForge saveWithoutId carries the data attachments , and it stores the entity's UUID . That is the whole reason this shape is right and the one this page originally sketched was not: a released horse is the same horse down to the id that the ancestry database, every pedigree, every stall sign and every transfer deed key on. The sketch — a record enumerating horse_record , horse_gear , horse_care and friends by hand — would have lost the id, lost every vanilla field nobody listed (age, custom name, leash, the saddle in the vanilla slot), and rotted the first time somebody added a fifteenth attachment without thinking of that file. Where the snapshot had to live, against the plan The plan wanted StasisSnapshot in common/ so the web designer and a future 1.12.2 port could read a chamber. A tag-based capture cannot go there: CompoundTag is a Minecraft type and common/ imports none (hard rule 1). So the split is common/…/horse/StasisTier.java — the four tiers and their capability flags, the one part with no Minecraft in it, with StasisTierTest pinning the ladder — and neoforge/…/data/StasisSnapshot.java for the capture itself. Correct restoration beat portability; the portable half is the half that had something to port. The bank is a filtered chest with a clock on it It is modelled on EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity down to the setChanged forwarding and the server-only ticker, because it is the same problem: a SimpleContainer the menu wraps on the server and the ordinary vanilla slot sync fills in on the client, plus one number that advances with nobody looking. Nothing in the chamber grid ever unpacks a snapshot. A horse in stasis is a data component on an item, and the grid moves items; only the upkeep reads inside one. The shelf's createTickerHelper shape is copied exactly, and two ContainerData values have appeared with it — the water meter and the foaling-blocked flag, both genuinely facts about the block that the screen cannot derive. Everything else on all three tabs is still a loop over slots the client already has: the horse count, every Browse row, what is in the feed slot, what is in the drop buffer, and which chambers are at stud . Every chamber carries its whole horse and its own mark, so both sides can count and read for free. Two small divergences from the chest it resembles It is not flammable. Vanilla's chest takes ignitedByLava() and this does not, which is one omitted call: mod blocks are not in FireBlock 's flammability table unless they register themselves. A chest that burns down loses its diamonds; a bank that burns down would drop fifty-four pedigreed horses into the fire that did it. It is still not a Container . The block entity holds two rather than implementing one, exactly as the research shelf does, so nothing outside can reach the chamber grid. What is reachable is the supply row, and only because it was asked for by name — see below. The upkeep: four refusals, then one horse HorseStasisBankBlockEntity.tick is deliberately almost all refusal, in cost order, and nothing past a refusal happens: Is there anything here worth doing? A cached boolean , recomputed only when a chamber slot moves: the or of the three jobs - a chamber that heals, one that collects, one at stud. A bank of Basic chambers — or of empty ones, or an empty bank — costs one field read a tick and no more. This is the owner's rule, and it is why the ticker may be attached unconditionally: the block has no blockstate properties to pick a ticker by, so the gate has to be the first line of the ticker instead. Is it this tick? One turn every StasisUpkeep.HEAL_INTERVAL , phase-staggered by block position — the same interval and the same trick HorseCareHandler uses on live horses, keyed by entity id there and by position here. Whose turn is it? A saved cursor walks the grid one slot a turn, so the work is one chamber's worth however full the bank is. Saved, or a reloaded bank would restart at slot zero every time and favour the top-left horse. Is that one hurt? Two cheap field reads, before anything decodes a record or resolves a diet. Only past all four does the bank open a bucket or take a mouthful. That ordering is what lets the feature keep its own promise: a bank must cost less than the horses it replaced , and it has to hold that at fifty-four chambers as well as at one. Stage five added two more jobs to the same turn rather than a second clock, and each refuses again on its own terms: the buffer on whether anything is owed, the stud on whether she is in heat at all. The whole turn is still one chamber's worth of work , which is the number the promise is about. Reading a stored horse's abilities costs roughly what a live horse's own tick costs — and GeneAbilityHandler pays that every tick , where a shelved horse pays it once every StasisUpkeep.HEAL_INTERVAL at its very busiest, in a bank holding exactly one chamber. A full bank is thirty times cheaper again. The economy is the field's, which is why it is so small StasisUpkeep invents nothing. A live horse heals if it is hurt, if its diet is one items can feed, and if there is water nearby, paying Hunger.COST_PER_HEALTH a point and never spending below Hunger.STARVING — and eating is what puts hunger back. The bank applies exactly that and only supplies the food and the pond. The consequences follow for free: a blood-drinker is no more feedable in a chamber than in a field, a starving horse eats first and heals out of the same turn, and food without water is banked rather than wasted. The whole of it is pure Java and unit-tested. StasisCare is the other half and does the tag surgery, because rebuilding an entity per turn is precisely the work the feature exists to avoid . release builds a real Horse and is right to; doing it once a turn for a chamber nobody is looking at would make a full bank cost more than the fifty-four ticking animals it replaced. So the arithmetic runs on four numbers and this class fetches and returns them. A saved horse's attributes list is very often empty AttributeMap materialises an instance on demand , and getValue reads the entity type's supplier without creating one — so a horse can be saved with a perfectly good maximum health and an attributes list of [] . Reading the bar out of that list alone would have made every bank decide every horse was unhurt and heal nothing, for ever, with nothing logged. So maximum health comes from the horse's own record , which is where HorseRecords.applyTraitsToEntity gets it from in the first place; the saved list is the fallback, for an unconverted vanilla horse with no papers of ours. This was not reasoned out — the stasis_tag_is_readable gametest caught it on its first run, on a 53-health horse whose saved list was empty, which is the entire argument for having written that test at all. Why the goods slots are on the wire and the chambers are not The bank exposes a NeoForge item-handler capability over the goods container only . Feed and water are bulk goods that run out, and keeping a stud farm's bank topped up by hand is exactly the chore automation is for (owner, 2026-09-24) — so a hopper, a dropper or another mod's pipework can push hay and buckets in, pull empties out, and empty the drop buffer. The chamber grid is not part of the handler at all, so the original call stands untouched: nothing outside the bank can file or fetch a live animal. The slot rules are written once. NeoForge's VanillaContainerWrapper asks Container.canPlaceItem before it inserts, so the per-slot predicates the block entity already writes for the menu are the same rules a pipe obeys — feed in the feed slot, water in the water slot, nothing at all into the empties or the buffer, which are outputs. There is no second copy to drift. The buffer is in that container on purpose, not for tidiness The drop buffer could have been a third SimpleContainer beside the other two and it is nine more slots on the existing one instead, with FIRST_DROP_SLOT telling them apart. The capability wraps one container: a second would have wanted a second registration, something to join the two handlers, and a second copy of the slot rules to keep in step. As it is, StasisBankCapability did not change at all when the buffer shipped, and a hopper under the bank pulls eggs out of it for free. The cost is that SUPPLY_SLOTS now means twelve, which is why every reader goes through HorseStasisBankMenu.supplyX / supplyY rather than doing the arithmetic twice. 26.1.2 has no IItemHandler Not verified in a running game. Written against the 26.1.2 sources: the item capability is Capabilities.Item.BLOCK and its type is ResourceHandler<ItemResource> , NeoForge's newer transfer API, not the IItemHandler every tutorial still shows. The patched HopperBlockEntity falls back to that same capability when the target is not a Container , which is what makes a vanilla hopper work here. See api-notes.html . Chambers are stacksTo(1) in ModItems already; ChamberSlot.getMaxStackSize() and the container's both say so again, so no shift-click can ever try to merge two horses into one stack. The slot predicate takes any tier, empty or occupied — a bank that refused a Basic chamber would have inverted the whole ladder, since the bottom rung is the one that has to be worth using. The Browse tab reads the stable's papers, not the chamber The design call this tab had to make was where a row's details come from, and the Roadmap tab offered two answers: decode a whole entity tag per rendered row, or cache more fields onto the component at capture time. It is neither. A horse in a chamber keeps its UUID , the stable's ancestry records are keyed on exactly that, and ClientHorseRoster already holds every one of those records as a HorseListing — the same rows the Horse Browser 's My horses table sorts. So a row is a map lookup on an id the chamber has already told the client, and the tab inherits the browser's coat description, sex label, breed, generation and whole query language for free. That is also why the filter box takes HorseQuery verbatim rather than a smaller search of its own: a second, weaker query language in the same mod would have been more code and a worse feature. The screen opens by sending HorseRosterRequestPayload , the same request the browser's Refresh button sends, because a player may never have opened the browser on this client. What that choice costs, and why it is the right side of the trade The roster is this player's horses, and it is capped at HorseRosterPayload.MAX_ENTRIES . A chamber somebody else filled and handed over, or a stable past that cap, therefore has no row to match — so the tab draws that line by name and says it has no papers, and a search never returns it. The alternative, decoding fifty-four entity tags to draw a list, buys those rows at the cost of the one thing the whole feature exists to avoid: doing work per horse that nobody is looking at. The name is never lost either way , because StasisSnapshot cached it at capture time for exactly this. StasisBrowseRow is in common/ , and holds the gate: a row is searchable only if its own chamber's StasisTier.searchable() says so and there is a listing behind it, and locked() counts the rows that fail that test so the screen can say how many a query could not reach. Both halves are StasisBrowseTest 's subject, because a horse silently disappearing from a filtered bank is the one failure this tab must not have: the player's next thought is that the bank lost it. Two things the screen does that are not about stasis at all: The list is rebuilt on a change, not per frame. A signature over which horse is in which slot, and in what tier, is taken each frame — ids and ordinals, no tag decoded — and the rows are rebuilt only when that, the roster's version, or the query moves. ClientHorseRoster makes the same point about why a table must not resolve a genotype per row per frame. A text box inside a container screen must handle Escape first. EditBox.canConsumeInput() is true for every key once the box has focus, so guarding on it alone traps the player in the window with no way out - the equestrian bench's own override says so, having been reported for it, and this copies that shape rather than rediscovering it. See api-notes.html . The drop buffer: the same ability verb, a different sink Passive production in this mod is exactly one thing — GeneAbility.Produce , an item id, an interval and a count — and HorseAbilities.activeFor(genotype, epigenome) is pure common/ . So the bank reads a shelved horse's yields off the same call the live translator reads them off , with no second table and no per-gene knowledge anywhere in the stasis code. A gene that gains a produce is collectable in a bank the day it ships. The epigenome has to be passed. EggLayerGene is an EpigeneticAbilityContribution and its interval is written on the allele copy, so activeFor(genotype) alone would have given every horse in every bank the midpoint interval instead of its own — a difference nothing would ever have reported. The cooldown had to become durable, and that is an improvement GeneAbilityHandler keeps its produce cooldowns in a static Map<String, Long> keyed by uuid/geneKey/produce , which is lost on restart and cleared wholesale past eight thousand entries. A shelved horse has no tick to carry one at all, so the bank writes its stamp into the horse's own HORSE_COOLDOWNS attachment under produce:<geneKey> — the same attachment shearing and the yield genes already use. The consequence is the pleasant one: a shelved layer keeps a real clock across restarts, where a live one lays again on every server boot. Priced, then paid: why due and stamp are two calls A live horse calls spawnAtLocation and cannot fail. A bank can: nine slots fill up. So StasisDrops.due reads what is owed and changes nothing, the block entity's roomFor runs the insert against a throwaway container, and only if all of it fits does stamp mark the cooldown spent. A full buffer therefore pauses production rather than destroying a yield, and emptying it resumes on the next turn. Half a yield is never taken either, which is why the dry run is over the whole batch rather than per stack. A condition a chamber cannot answer refuses GeneAbilityHandler.conditionHolds reads the live entity and the level for almost every flag in the vocabulary — in_water , night , raining , has_rider , near_jukebox . A horse in a chamber is in none of those, and they are not false about it so much as meaningless. StasisProduce.holdsInStasis answers only the flags that are facts about the horse itself — adult , baby , sex_male , sex_female — and refuses everything else, negation included : “not raining” is no more a fact about a bottle than “raining” is. The alternative was a gene quietly behaving differently indoors. Nothing shipped is affected: the egg-layer's condition is always , and no spec gene emits a produce yet. The Age key is the one bare literal, and it has a tripwire adult means reading a saved AgeableMob 's age, and there is no constant to import for it — vanilla writes the literal "Age" . A rename would not throw; every shelved foal would simply start producing as though it were grown. So stasis_tag_is_readable snapshots the same horse twice, once grown and once with setBaby(true) , and asserts both. In-bank breeding: the mark, the cover, and coming out to foal Three separate problems, and only the third was interesting. The mark is a Boolean data component, STASIS_AT_STUD , on the chamber item. That is the same call STASIS_SNAPSHOT made and for the same reasons: it syncs to the client with the ordinary slot sync so the Browse tab can draw it with no packet of its own, it travels with the chamber between banks, and there is no copy on a block entity to drift from the chamber it describes. A set of marked UUID s on the block would have needed syncing, pruning when a chamber left, and a rule for what happens when the same horse is in two banks. The mark alone is not permission: StasisStud.atStud asks the tier as well, because StasisUpgradeRecipe copies components and a mark can therefore outlive the rung that bought it. Toggling it is AbstractContainerMenu.clickMenuButton with the chamber slot as the id — JumpMenu 's shape, and the rule BenchNamePayload states from the other side: an int fits in a menu button and a string does not. The server re-checks the tier and the occupancy, because a menu button is a packet and a packet is whatever somebody sent. The cover is StasisMating over Conception.attempt , and it is small because it borrows everything. Reproduction 's times are all absolute game ticks and its state is derived from them ( ReproRules.stateAt ), so a mare's cycle reaches her on a shelf with nothing ticking — the single property that makes this feature possible, and it was there before stasis was. Once-per-heat is ReproRules.mayTryNaturally unchanged, the stallion's three-a-day tiredness is coversOn read off his own stored record, and the health gate is COVER_HEALTH . Nothing here is a stasis number. The pregnancy is carried in the chamber; the mare comes out to foal This is the design call of the stage. Gestation is free: a Pregnancy is two ticks and a list of embryos, and it advances on a shelf exactly as it would in a field. Birth is not free, and the reason is worth stating plainly — HorseBreedingHandler.populateFoal names the foal under the dam's owner's NamingPolicy , tames it to her owner, gives it a quarter of her bond, files it in HorseAncestryData , puts it in her wild band, writes its HORSE_SOCIAL birth record and tells everyone nearby. Every one of those is a fact about the dam , and half of them are read off her live entity. Reimplementing that against a saved tag would have been a second breeding path to keep in step for ever , which is the exact class of drift this repository keeps a table of rules about. So the bank does the cheap half and hands the expensive half back: at her due date it releases her beside the block and ReproHandler 's ordinary SCAN tick foals her within two seconds. A bank-bred foal is not like every other foal, it is one. It also means she is a ticking entity for the minutes around the birth and not for the gestation, which is the trade the whole feature is about. alreadyLoose is checked first, for the creative-copy reason release already had. It will not foal into a wall foalingSpot wants a position whose block and the one above it have an empty collision shape — two, because a horse is nearly two tall and setting one down inside a wall is how a released animal suffocates in a cellar. The four horizontal neighbours are tried first, then a small box round the block. With nothing found she stays in her chamber , foalingBlocked goes up, and the Supply tab says so; her turn comes round again. That flag is the second ContainerData value, because it is the one thing the bank can want that a player cannot read off a slot. It is not saved — it is a fact about the room, and a reloaded bank that is quiet for a minute beats one that remembers a wall somebody has knocked down. Two deliberate omissions. No carrot bias: ARMED_CARROTS lives on a live horse and is spent by a breeding somebody is watching, so a bank pairing folds GameteBias.NONE on both sides, which is what a wild cover draws. And no crowd cap: DEFAULT_NATURAL_CAP counts entities in a chunk radius, a bank holds none, and the number of chambers the player marked is already their own cap. Why two of these are Java recipes Neither is a CustomRecipe for the fun of it, and both reasons are worth keeping: The water container is not consumed. A vanilla recipe eats every input. StasisChamberRecipe overrides getRemainingItems and hands back the input's own declared crafting remainder — which is how a vanilla water bucket returns an empty bucket, the same as in a cake. The ingredient is matched on c:buckets/water ( Tags.Items.BUCKETS_WATER ), so another mod's container crafts it without anyone naming it here. An upgrade must carry the horse. A shapeless JSON recipe builds its result from scratch and copies no components, so upgrading an occupied chamber would hand back an empty one and delete the animal — silently, with nothing in the log. StasisUpgradeRecipe copies the stasis_snapshot across. The JSON version of this feature is not a cheaper version of it; it is a data-loss bug with a crafting recipe attached. Both serializers are unit codecs, and that is load-bearing Each recipe has one INSTANCE shared by MapCodec.unit(INSTANCE) (the value overload) and StreamCodec.unit(INSTANCE) . A supplier-based map codec decodes a different object, the stream codec's identity check then throws, and because NeoForge sends the whole recipe set to every joining client as one payload, that is a login kick on every dedicated server — which shipped once, in v0.5.014. Singleplayer never encodes a recipe, so nothing short of a real join catches it. See api-notes.html . The every_recipe_encodes gametest is the cheap version of that join, and both of these pass it. The component syncs the whole tag, on purpose It is tempting to give STASIS_SNAPSHOT a lossy stream codec that sends only the name and the id, since that is all a tooltip needs, and keep the heavy tag server-side. That is wrong, and the reason is creative mode: a creative pick-block sends the client's copy of a stack back to the server, so anything the client was never told is anything a creative player can quietly erase. The whole tag goes over the wire. Bandwidth is therefore a real question for the bank, where fifty-four of them sit in one container — not for an item in a hand. The bank ships syncing all of it , deliberately: sending less is how a creative player silently erases a horse, and the reverse of that trade is a number to measure rather than a bug to fix. The measurement is on this page's Verification tab. Small decisions inside release Position comes from the chamber, not the tag. The tag remembers where the horse was captured, which may be a world away; where you open it is where it wakes up, with its velocity zeroed. It starts fed. Hunger is explicitly reset to Hunger.FULL , matching the hunger attachment's own rule that a re-summoned horse is not starving. It is also the only honest answer when the horse has been outside time. A horse that will not load stays in the chamber. If load throws, nothing is consumed and the chamber is unchanged, so a later build may still be able to read it. The alternative is destroying a pedigreed animal to tidy up an exception.
