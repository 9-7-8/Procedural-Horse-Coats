Horse stasis: chambers and the bank
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
A cheap way to hold horses without them costing server tick time. A stasis chamber is an item a horse goes into; a Horse Stasis Bank is a block that holds many chambers and lets you read the horses inside them. The whole point is the cheapest tier, so a server with hundreds of horses can put most of them somewhere that costs nothing, instead of every horse being a real, ticking entity forever. The whole feature is built — five chambers, and a bank that keeps them, reads them on its Browse tab, mends them from its feed and water, collects what they drop, and breeds a pair you turn out at stud. The fifth chamber is the Emergency one, which is the only one you never use: it catches a horse of yours that is about to die, by itself, while you are carrying it.

Capture and release: it is the lycanthropy swap This page used to say that capture/release of a live, specific entity was “new work with no existing analog in the mod” . That was wrong, and finding out it was wrong is most of why stage one was cheap. LycanthropyHandler has carried a whole horse through a night spent inside a wolf since the LYCAN gene shipped, and it does it with exactly two calls: horse.saveWithoutId(TagValueOutput) a CompoundTag , then horse.discard() . EntityType.HORSE.create(level, EntitySpawnReason.LOAD) horse.load(TagValueInput.create(...)) level.addFreshEntity(horse) . On NeoForge saveWithoutId carries the data attachments , and it stores the entity's UUID . That is the whole reason this shape is right and the one this page originally sketched was not: a released horse is the same horse down to the id that the ancestry database, every pedigree, every stall sign and every transfer deed key on. The sketch — a record enumerating horse_record , horse_gear , horse_care and friends by hand — would have lost the id, lost every vanilla field nobody listed (age, custom name, leash, the saddle in the vanilla slot), and rotted the first time somebody added a fifteenth attachment without thinking of that file. Where the snapshot had to live, against the plan The plan wanted StasisSnapshot in common/ so the web designer and a future 1.12.2 port could read a chamber. A tag-based capture cannot go there: CompoundTag is a Minecraft type and common/ imports none (hard rule 1). So the split is common/…/horse/StasisTier.java — the four tiers and their capability flags, the one part with no Minecraft in it, with StasisTierTest pinning the ladder — and neoforge/…/data/StasisSnapshot.java for the capture itself. Correct restoration beat portability; the portable half is the half that had something to port. The bank is a filtered chest with a clock on it It is modelled on EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity down to the setChanged forwarding and the server-only ticker, because it is the same problem: a SimpleContainer the menu wraps on the server and the ordinary vanilla slot sync fills in on the client, plus one number that advances with nobody looking. Nothing in the chamber grid ever unpacks a snapshot. A horse in stasis is a data component on an item, and the grid moves items; only the upkeep reads inside one. The shelf's createTickerHelper shape is copied exactly, and two ContainerData values have appeared with it — the water meter and the foaling-blocked flag, both genuinely facts about the block that the screen cannot derive. Everything else on all three tabs is still a loop over slots the client already has: the horse count, every Browse row, what is in the feed slot, what is in the drop buffer, and which chambers are at stud . Every chamber carries its whole horse and its own mark, so both sides can count and read for free. Two small divergences from the chest it resembles It is not flammable. Vanilla's chest takes ignitedByLava() and this does not, which is one omitted call: mod blocks are not in FireBlock 's flammability table unless they register themselves. A chest that burns down loses its diamonds; a bank that burns down would drop a stud farm's worth of pedigreed horses into the fire that did it. It is still not a Container . The block entity holds two rather than implementing one, exactly as the research shelf does, so nothing outside can reach the chamber grid. What is reachable is the supply row, and only because it was asked for by name — see below. The upkeep: four refusals, then one horse HorseStasisBankBlockEntity.tick is deliberately almost all refusal, in cost order, and nothing past a refusal happens: Is there anything here worth doing? A cached boolean , recomputed only when a chamber slot moves: the or of the three jobs - a chamber that heals, one that collects, one at stud. A bank of Basic chambers — or of empty ones, or an empty bank — costs one field read a tick and no more. This is the owner's rule, and it is why the ticker may be attached unconditionally: the block has no blockstate properties to pick a ticker by, so the gate has to be the first line of the ticker instead. Is it this tick? One turn every StasisUpkeep.HEAL_INTERVAL , phase-staggered by block position — the same interval and the same trick HorseCareHandler uses on live horses, keyed by entity id there and by position here. Whose turn is it? A saved cursor walks the grid one slot a turn, so the work is one chamber's worth however full the bank is. Saved, or a reloaded bank would restart at slot zero every time and favour the top-left horse. Is that one hurt? Two cheap field reads, before anything decodes a record or resolves a diet. Only past all four does the bank open a bucket or take a mouthful. That ordering is what lets the feature keep its own promise: a bank must cost less than the horses it replaced , and it has to hold that at HorseStasisBankBlockEntity.SLOTS chambers as well as at one. That promise is why the grid could be made this deep at all: nothing in the turn scales with how many chambers are filed. Stage five added two more jobs to the same turn rather than a second clock, and each refuses again on its own terms: the buffer on whether anything is owed, the stud on whether she is in heat at all. The whole turn is still one chamber's worth of work , which is the number the promise is about. Reading a stored horse's abilities costs roughly what a live horse's own tick costs — and GeneAbilityHandler pays that every tick , where a shelved horse pays it once every StasisUpkeep.HEAL_INTERVAL at its very busiest, in a bank holding exactly one chamber. A full bank is thirty times cheaper again. The economy is the field's, which is why it is so small StasisUpkeep invents nothing. A live horse heals if it is hurt, if its diet is one items can feed, and if there is water nearby, paying Hunger.COST_PER_HEALTH a point and never spending below Hunger.STARVING — and eating is what puts hunger back. The bank applies exactly that and only supplies the food and the pond. The consequences follow for free: a blood-drinker is no more feedable in a chamber than in a field, a starving horse eats first and heals out of the same turn, and food without water is banked rather than wasted. The whole of it is pure Java and unit-tested. StasisCare is the other half and does the tag surgery, because rebuilding an entity per turn is precisely the work the feature exists to avoid . release builds a real Horse and is right to; doing it once a turn for a chamber nobody is looking at would make a full bank cost more than the two hundred-odd ticking animals it replaced. So the arithmetic runs on four numbers and this class fetches and returns them. A saved horse's attributes list is very often empty AttributeMap materialises an instance on demand , and getValue reads the entity type's supplier without creating one — so a horse can be saved with a perfectly good maximum health and an attributes list of [] . Reading the bar out of that list alone would have made every bank decide every horse was unhurt and heal nothing, for ever, with nothing logged. So maximum health comes from the horse's own record , which is where HorseRecords.applyTraitsToEntity gets it from in the first place; the saved list is the fallback, for an unconverted vanilla horse with no papers of ours. This was not reasoned out — the stasis_tag_is_readable gametest caught it on its first run, on a 53-health horse whose saved list was empty, which is the entire argument for having written that test at all. Why the goods slots are on the wire and the chambers are not The bank exposes a NeoForge item-handler capability over the goods container only . Feed and water are bulk goods that run out, and keeping a stud farm's bank topped up by hand is exactly the chore automation is for (owner, 2026-09-24) — so a hopper, a dropper or another mod's pipework can push hay and buckets in, pull empties out, and empty the drop buffer. The chamber grid is not part of the handler at all, so the original call stands untouched: nothing outside the bank can file or fetch a live animal. The slot rules are written once. NeoForge's VanillaContainerWrapper asks Container.canPlaceItem before it inserts, so the per-slot predicates the block entity already writes for the menu are the same rules a pipe obeys — feed in the feed slot, water in the water slot, nothing at all into the empties or the buffer, which are outputs. There is no second copy to drift. The buffer is in that container on purpose, not for tidiness The drop buffer could have been a third SimpleContainer beside the other two and it is nine more slots on the existing one instead, with FIRST_DROP_SLOT telling them apart. The capability wraps one container: a second would have wanted a second registration, something to join the two handlers, and a second copy of the slot rules to keep in step. As it is, StasisBankCapability did not change at all when the buffer shipped, and a hopper under the bank pulls eggs out of it for free. The cost is that SUPPLY_SLOTS now means twelve, which is why every reader goes through HorseStasisBankMenu.supplyX / supplyY rather than doing the arithmetic twice. 26.1.2 has no IItemHandler Not verified in a running game. Written against the 26.1.2 sources: the item capability is Capabilities.Item.BLOCK and its type is ResourceHandler<ItemResource> , NeoForge's newer transfer API, not the IItemHandler every tutorial still shows. The patched HopperBlockEntity falls back to that same capability when the target is not a Container , which is what makes a vanilla hopper work here. See api-notes.html . Occupied chambers are stacksTo(1) in ModItems ; empty ones stack , so ChamberSlot.getMaxStackSize() and the container's are no longer belt and braces but the only thing keeping a stack of empties out of a chamber slot — which matters because EmergencyStasisHandler writes a filled chamber straight back into the slot it found an empty one in, and would destroy the rest of the stack. Empties stack because a chamber with a horse in it is a different item : an empty one can never come to hold a horse, so it is about no horse at all and is interchangeable with every other empty of its tier. The one thing the code owes that is HorseStasisHandler.capture taking one off the held stack rather than replacing it — a plain setItemInHand there would turn five chambers into one. The slot predicate takes any tier, empty or occupied — a bank that refused a Basic chamber would have inverted the whole ladder, since the bottom rung is the one that has to be worth using. The Browse tab reads the stable's papers, not the chamber The design call this tab had to make was where a row's details come from, and the Roadmap tab offered two answers: decode a whole entity tag per rendered row, or cache more fields onto the component at capture time. It is neither. A horse in a chamber keeps its UUID , the stable's ancestry records are keyed on exactly that, and ClientHorseRoster already holds every one of those records as a HorseListing — the same rows the Horse Browser 's My horses table sorts. So a row is a map lookup on an id the chamber has already told the client, and the tab inherits the browser's coat description, sex label, breed, generation and whole query language for free. That is also why the filter box takes HorseQuery verbatim rather than a smaller search of its own: a second, weaker query language in the same mod would have been more code and a worse feature. The screen opens by sending HorseRosterRequestPayload , the same request the browser's Refresh button sends, because a player may never have opened the browser on this client. What that choice costs, and why it is the right side of the trade The roster is this player's horses, and it is capped at HorseRosterPayload.MAX_ENTRIES . A chamber somebody else filled and handed over, or a stable past that cap, therefore has no row to match — so the tab draws that line by name and says it has no papers, and a search never returns it. The alternative, decoding a bankful of entity tags to draw a list, buys those rows at the cost of the one thing the whole feature exists to avoid: doing work per horse that nobody is looking at. The name is never lost either way , because StasisSnapshot cached it at capture time for exactly this. StasisBrowseRow is in common/ , and holds the gate: a row is searchable only if its own chamber's StasisTier.searchable() says so and there is a listing behind it, and locked() counts the rows that fail that test so the screen can say how many a query could not reach. Both halves are StasisBrowseTest 's subject, because a horse silently disappearing from a filtered bank is the one failure this tab must not have: the player's next thought is that the bank lost it. Two things the screen does that are not about stasis at all: The list is rebuilt on a change, not per frame. A signature over which horse is in which slot, and in what tier, is taken each frame — ids and ordinals, no tag decoded — and the rows are rebuilt only when that, the roster's version, or the query moves. ClientHorseRoster makes the same point about why a table must not resolve a genotype per row per frame. A text box inside a container screen must handle Escape first. EditBox.canConsumeInput() is true for every key once the box has focus, so guarding on it alone traps the player in the window with no way out - the equestrian bench's own override says so, having been reported for it, and this copies that shape rather than rediscovering it. See api-notes.html . The drop buffer: the same ability verb, a different sink Passive production in this mod is exactly one thing — GeneAbility.Produce , an item id, an interval and a count — and HorseAbilities.activeFor(genotype, epigenome) is pure common/ . So the bank reads a shelved horse's yields off the same call the live translator reads them off , with no second table and no per-gene knowledge anywhere in the stasis code. A gene that gains a produce is collectable in a bank the day it ships. The epigenome has to be passed. EggLayerGene is an EpigeneticAbilityContribution and its interval is written on the allele copy, so activeFor(genotype) alone would have given every horse in every bank the midpoint interval instead of its own — a difference nothing would ever have reported. The cooldown had to become durable, and that is an improvement GeneAbilityHandler keeps its produce cooldowns in a static Map<String, Long> keyed by uuid/geneKey/produce , which is lost on restart and cleared wholesale past eight thousand entries. A shelved horse has no tick to carry one at all, so the bank writes its stamp into the horse's own HORSE_COOLDOWNS attachment under produce:<geneKey> — the same attachment shearing and the yield genes already use. The consequence is the pleasant one: a shelved layer keeps a real clock across restarts, where a live one lays again on every server boot. Priced, then paid: why due and stamp are two calls A live horse calls spawnAtLocation and cannot fail. A bank can: nine slots fill up. So StasisDrops.due reads what is owed and changes nothing, the block entity's roomFor runs the insert against a throwaway container, and only if all of it fits does stamp mark the cooldown spent. A full buffer therefore pauses production rather than destroying a yield, and emptying it resumes on the next turn. Half a yield is never taken either, which is why the dry run is over the whole batch rather than per stack. A condition a chamber cannot answer refuses GeneAbilityHandler.conditionHolds reads the live entity and the level for almost every flag in the vocabulary — in_water , night , raining , has_rider , near_jukebox . A horse in a chamber is in none of those, and they are not false about it so much as meaningless. StasisProduce.holdsInStasis answers only the flags that are facts about the horse itself — adult , baby , sex_male , sex_female — and refuses everything else, negation included : “not raining” is no more a fact about a bottle than “raining” is. The alternative was a gene quietly behaving differently indoors. Nothing shipped is affected: the egg-layer's condition is always , and no spec gene emits a produce yet. The Age key is the one bare literal, and it has a tripwire adult means reading a saved AgeableMob 's age, and there is no constant to import for it — vanilla writes the literal "Age" . A rename would not throw; every shelved foal would simply start producing as though it were grown. So stasis_tag_is_readable snapshots the same horse twice, once grown and once with setBaby(true) , and asserts both. In-bank breeding: the mark, the cover, and coming out to foal Three separate problems, and only the third was interesting. The mark is a Boolean data component, STASIS_AT_STUD , on the chamber item. That is the same call STASIS_SNAPSHOT made and for the same reasons: it syncs to the client with the ordinary slot sync so the Browse tab can draw it with no packet of its own, it travels with the chamber between banks, and there is no copy on a block entity to drift from the chamber it describes. A set of marked UUID s on the block would have needed syncing, pruning when a chamber left, and a rule for what happens when the same horse is in two banks. The mark alone is not permission: StasisStud.atStud asks the tier as well, because the component is a bare boolean on an arbitrary stack and nothing in the type system ties it to the rung that is allowed to set it. A chamber handed in by a /give with the component spelled out, or by another mod, or by some later recipe, arrives marked without ever having passed the menu's check — so the read asks the tier rather than trusting the flag. Corrected 2026-09-24: the reason this page gave was not true It said the tier is re-checked “because StasisUpgradeRecipe copies components and a mark can therefore outlive the rung that bought it”, and StasisStud.atStud 's own comment said the same. That cannot happen. The upgrade copies stasis_snapshot and nothing else, so it does not carry the mark at all; and only a Spacer can be marked in the first place ( clickMenuButton refuses any rung whose breedsInBank() is false), while a Spacer is the top rung and next() hands back null , so a marked chamber can never be put through an upgrade. Both halves of the story were wrong. The check is right and stays; only the reason for it has been replaced with one that happens. If a rung is ever added above Spacer, the upgrade dropping the mark becomes a real behaviour to decide on rather than a hypothetical — a mare quietly coming back in from stud — and that is the thing to remember from this, not the sentence that was here. Toggling it is AbstractContainerMenu.clickMenuButton with the chamber slot as the id — JumpMenu 's shape, and the rule BenchNamePayload states from the other side: an int fits in a menu button and a string does not. The server re-checks the tier and the occupancy, because a menu button is a packet and a packet is whatever somebody sent. The cover is StasisMating over Conception.attempt , and it is small because it borrows everything. Reproduction 's times are all absolute game ticks and its state is derived from them ( ReproRules.stateAt ), so a mare's cycle reaches her on a shelf with nothing ticking — the single property that makes this feature possible, and it was there before stasis was. Once-per-heat is ReproRules.mayTryNaturally unchanged, the stallion's three-a-day tiredness is coversOn read off his own stored record, and the health gate is COVER_HEALTH . Nothing here is a stasis number. The pregnancy is carried in the chamber; the mare comes out to foal This is the design call of the stage. Gestation is free: a Pregnancy is two ticks and a list of embryos, and it advances on a shelf exactly as it would in a field. Birth is not free, and the reason is worth stating plainly — HorseBreedingHandler.populateFoal names the foal under the dam's owner's NamingPolicy , tames it to her owner, gives it a quarter of her bond, files it in HorseAncestryData , puts it in her wild band, writes its HORSE_SOCIAL birth record and tells everyone nearby. Every one of those is a fact about the dam , and half of them are read off her live entity. Reimplementing that against a saved tag would have been a second breeding path to keep in step for ever , which is the exact class of drift this repository keeps a table of rules about. So the bank does the cheap half and hands the expensive half back: at her due date it releases her beside the block and ReproHandler 's ordinary SCAN tick foals her within two seconds. A bank-bred foal is not like every other foal, it is one. It also means she is a ticking entity for the minutes around the birth and not for the gestation, which is the trade the whole feature is about. alreadyLoose is checked first, for the creative-copy reason release already had. It will not foal into a wall foalingSpot wants a position whose block and the one above it have an empty collision shape — two, because a horse is nearly two tall and setting one down inside a wall is how a released animal suffocates in a cellar. The four horizontal neighbours are tried first, then a small box round the block. With nothing found she stays in her chamber , foalingBlocked goes up, and the Supply tab says so; her turn comes round again. That flag is the second ContainerData value, because it is the one thing the bank can want that a player cannot read off a slot. It is not saved — it is a fact about the room, and a reloaded bank that is quiet for a minute beats one that remembers a wall somebody has knocked down. Two deliberate omissions. No carrot bias: ARMED_CARROTS lives on a live horse and is spent by a breeding somebody is watching, so a bank pairing folds GameteBias.NONE on both sides, which is what a wild cover draws. And no crowd cap: DEFAULT_NATURAL_CAP counts entities in a chunk radius, a bank holds none, and the number of chambers the player marked is already their own cap. Every chamber has two recipes, and only one of them is drawn Each of the five chambers is made by a pair : a plain JSON recipe and a Java CustomRecipe . They are written so that no grid ever matches both , which is the property the whole arrangement rests on — two recipes claiming one grid are resolved by registry order, and the loser becomes unreachable with nothing red anywhere. Chamber The JSON recipe takes… …and the Java one takes Basic A minecraft:water_bucket . Any other container in c:buckets/water — the tag minus the vanilla bucket, via a DifferenceIngredient . The three upgrades , and Emergency horsegenetics:basic_stasis_chamber and friends. horsegenetics:occupied_basic_stasis_chamber and friends — different items . A chamber with a horse in it is a different item This is the load-bearing decision of the whole arrangement, and it is worth being exact about why. Each chamber is registered twice — basic_stasis_chamber and occupied_basic_stasis_chamber , and so on for all five. Putting a horse in swaps the item ; letting it out swaps it back. The stasis_snapshot component still carries the animal, but which item a stack is now tells you whether there is one. The pair of recipes above must never both claim a grid, because two that do are resolved by registry order and the loser is simply unreachable — here, with a pedigreed horse deleted on the way past. Two earlier attempts at holding them apart were both unsound: A component ingredient. NeoForge's neoforge:components takes a removal patch, and "!horsegenetics:stasis_snapshot": {} matches only a stack without that component. It works — but it makes the “simple, maximally compatible” half of each pair neither, and it fails in the worst way available: get one of its two easily-mistaken names wrong and the entire recipe file is dropped , with the error reaching only the server log and the item silently having no recipe. A priority file. NeoForge's data/neoforge/recipe_priorities.json ranks recipes, and ranking the Java halves above the JSON ones would make the Java one win whenever both matched. This does not work , and the reason is worth carrying elsewhere: when a player clicks an entry in the recipe book , the book hands that recipe back to RecipeManager.getRecipeFor as a hint , and a matching hint is honoured ahead of any priority . The book would have pulled an occupied chamber out of the player's inventory to fill the grid it had just offered them. Two item ids make the collision unrepresentable rather than guarded . Nothing has to read a component, nothing has to be ordered correctly, and every recipe in data/horsegenetics/recipe/ is plain vanilla JSON again — a bare item id or a tag, with no NeoForge extension anywhere in the folder. What it costs: a chamber can no longer be filled in place An ItemStack cannot change its own item, so HorseStasisHandler.swallow returns the filled chamber and each caller writes it back where the old one was — into the hand for a player capture, and into a container slot for the emergency walk, which carries a ChamberSlot (container plus index) rather than a bare stack for exactly this reason. Release, and a mare coming out of a bank to foal, swap the other way. A caller that forgets leaves the horse in a stack nobody is holding , which is the one new way this can go wrong. Why bother, when the Java recipe alone matched everything? Because a plain JSON recipe is the only shape proven to be drawable on a modded server — it is why the bank was the one thing in this family a player could find in JEI — and because the two halves need genuinely different things: An upgrade must carry the horse. A shapeless JSON recipe builds its result from scratch and copies no components, so upgrading an occupied chamber would hand back an empty one and delete the animal — silently, with nothing in the log. StasisUpgradeRecipe copies the stasis_snapshot across. The JSON version of that half is not a cheaper version of it; it is a data-loss bug with a crafting recipe attached. It is safe for the empty half only because an empty chamber is a different item , so no plain ingredient in that recipe can name one that has a horse in it. So must the ender pearls. EmergencyChamberRecipe is the same hazard wearing a different shape — the chamber it converts sits in the middle of the grid and may well have a horse in it — so it copies stasis_snapshot too. It is shaped rather than shapeless because the arrangement is the recipe's name: a shapeless nine-item match would also accept the chamber in a corner, which reads like a different recipe that happens to work. A tag is the only way to accept a container this mod has never heard of. StasisChamberRecipe matches c:buckets/water ( Tags.Items.BUCKETS_WATER ) so another mod's bucket-equivalent crafts a chamber without anyone naming it here. On a pack with no such mod the difference is empty and that recipe goes quiet altogether — it neither matches nor draws, rather than showing a recipe with a blank slot in it. A claim that stood here for a release and was simply false This section used to say the Basic chamber had to be a Java recipe because “a vanilla recipe eats every input” and the water container must come back. It does not. Items.WATER_BUCKET is declared craftRemainder(BUCKET) , so any recipe hands the empty bucket back by itself — it is how cake returns three of them. The custom recipe was never needed for the vanilla bucket, only for a modded container that declares no remainder, which getRemainingItems hands straight back still full. Both serializers are unit codecs, and that is load-bearing Each recipe has one INSTANCE shared by MapCodec.unit(INSTANCE) (the value overload) and StreamCodec.unit(INSTANCE) . A supplier-based map codec decodes a different object, the stream codec's identity check then throws, and because NeoForge sends the whole recipe set to every joining client as one payload, that is a login kick on every dedicated server — which shipped once, in v0.5.014. Singleplayer never encodes a recipe, so nothing short of a real join catches it. See api-notes.html . The every_recipe_encodes gametest is the cheap version of that join, and all three of these pass it. The component syncs the whole tag, on purpose It is tempting to give STASIS_SNAPSHOT a lossy stream codec that sends only the name and the id, since that is all a tooltip needs, and keep the heavy tag server-side. That is wrong, and the reason is creative mode: a creative pick-block sends the client's copy of a stack back to the server, so anything the client was never told is anything a creative player can quietly erase. The whole tag goes over the wire. Bandwidth is therefore a real question for the bank, where two hundred of them sit in one container — not for an item in a hand. The bank ships syncing all of it , deliberately: sending less is how a creative player silently erases a horse, and the reverse of that trade is a number to measure rather than a bug to fix. The measurement is on this page's Verification tab. Small decisions inside release Position comes from the chamber, not the tag. The tag remembers where the horse was captured, which may be a world away; where you open it is where it wakes up, with its velocity zeroed. It starts fed. Hunger is explicitly reset to Hunger.FULL , matching the hunger attachment's own rule that a re-summoned horse is not starving. It is also the only honest answer when the horse has been outside time. A horse that will not load stays in the chamber. If load throws, nothing is consumed and the chamber is unchanged, so a later build may still be able to read it. The alternative is destroying a pedigreed animal to tidy up an exception. It is two halves, and the seam is load-bearing. restore builds the horse and place puts it down; release is the two called together, which is what the right-click and the foaling path still use. The seam exists for Send home , which has to measure the animal before it knows where to put it — see below. Send home, out of a chamber The browser's Send home button is StallRecall , and until now it could not reach a chambered horse at all. Its two ways of finding a horse were is it loaded and pull in the chunk where it was last seen , and neither can ever work here : a horse in stasis is not unloaded, it is not an entity, so no amount of waiting will load it; and the sighting HorseWhereabouts.enteredStasis wrote is where it went in , which is precisely a place it is not. So the stasis case is asked before the sighting is read, and branches into fromChamber . Finding the jar is the index's second question The pockets first, which is an inventory walk and free. Then StasisBankIndex.bankHolding , which is the armed flag asked the other way round: every bank publishes which horse ids it is holding , on the same two occasions it publishes whether it is armed — the grid changing, and the block loading. The ids come off the snapshot component, which is the whole reason StasisSnapshot unpacks an id at all, so publishing costs a field read per slot and no tag is decoded. The flag itself is the emergency chamber's , and this is its shape reused. What that buys is the same thing the armed flag buys: the usual answer — not in any of your banks — is a walk of a short list in memory and no chunk is touched at all , and the answer yes names exactly one chunk to load. Like the flag, it is last-known and the real container is re-read rather than trusted; a stale row costs a wasted look and finds nothing, which is the right way round. Why the horse is built before the stall is measured TicketHandler.landingSpot sizes a stall against the animal's own bounding box — a shire does not fit where a Shetland does — so the horse has to exist before the destination can be checked. But it must not be in the world yet, because everything after that point can still say no. Hence the restore / place split. The order is: find the chamber, confirm the horse is not already loose, resolve home, restore the horse unplaced, check it answers to this player, measure the stall against it, choose a ticket — and only then place , swap the stack for withoutHorse , and spend the ticket. Every one of those refusals leaves the chamber as full as it was , which is the promise StasisChamberItem.useOn already makes. The alternative shape — release somewhere, then teleport — is a real horse standing in the room with the bank for a tick, and on a refusal a bottle opened for nothing. One destination, not two copies of one The stall-then-pen choice and the three sentences that go with it ( “that stall's world is not loaded” , “there is nowhere in that stall this horse fits” , “is back in its stall” ) moved into a Home record when the second caller appeared. That is the same rule this class already follows about TicketHandler : the two paths look separable and are not, and a drift between them would be a horse teleported into a wall or a player told to check a sign that was never the problem. The trip is measured from where the bottle is , not from where the horse went in, so the ticket tier has to reach from the bank's world — which is the honest question, since that is where the animal actually re-appears from. Drawing the horse in the jar An occupied chamber is a different item from an empty one already — it has to be , since recipe ingredients cannot see components — so it could simply be given a different texture, and that is the whole of the art change: occupied_*_stasis_chamber.png is the tier's own bottle with a window cut out of the upper body . The tier colour survives as the liquid in the base, deliberately, because a bank of Basic chambers still has to read blue across a grid of fifty-four. Behind that window the screen draws HorsePortrait , the browser's own row portrait, with the coat from ClientHorseCoats — which is keyed by record UUID and answered from HorseAncestryData , so it works perfectly well for an animal that does not exist anywhere. The chamber grid is drawn in the background pass, so the portrait lands under the item icon and shows through the hole without anything being drawn over the jar. Clipped, not shrunk — and the four numbers are a copy The portrait is drawn at the slot's full sixteen pixels and scissored to the window. A five-pixel portrait is a smudge; five pixels of a sixteen-pixel horse is that horse's barrel at the size the rest of the mod draws it, which is enough to say chestnut , grey , spotted at a glance. The dark well the portrait fills first is clipped with it, so the hole reads as the inside of a bottle. GLASS_X0/Y0/X1/Y1 in HorseStasisBankScreen are where the PNG is transparent , and the two must agree. Every pixel of that rectangle has to be inside the glass, or the dark fill spills onto the panel beside the jar. Redraw the bottles and those four numbers move with them. The pose is fixed at a three-quarter view rather than following the cursor, which the browser's rows do: fifty-four horses all turning together would be a wall of jars that ripples when the mouse moves, and a specimen in a bottle holds still. The Browse tab's own portraits do follow the cursor — there are four of them, and they are the ones you are looking at . The emergency chamber: a subclass, and one event at LOWEST Shipped 2026-09-24, and the smallest stage of the six by a distance — three new classes and about a hundred lines, because the only genuinely new thing in it is when to call swallow . Everything after that was already built. Why it is a subclass and not a fifth tier EmergencyStasisChamberItem extends StasisChamberItem at StasisTier.BASIC , and that one line is why nothing else in the feature had to change at all . The bank's isChamber is an instanceof on the superclass, so an emergency chamber files, counts, stacks, lists and upgrades exactly as the Basic chamber it was built from; HorseStasisBankBlockEntity , the menu, the Browse tab, the upkeep, the drop buffer and StasisStud are all untouched, and none of them knows the class exists. A fifth StasisTier was the obvious alternative and is wrong twice over. The ladder is four rungs of what the bank may do with the horse , cumulative and pinned by StasisTierTest ; self-capture is not that kind of capability at all, and the chamber is built from the cheapest rung rather than sitting above the dearest one. A rung costing eight ender pearls that bought no bank capability would be exactly the hole in the ladder that test exists to prevent. The visible consequence is the right one: a book upgrades an emergency chamber into an ordinary Intermediate , horse and all, spending the emergency for the window. That is the escape hatch rather than the intended path — releasing hands the armed chamber straight back — but it means a rescued horse is never stuck somewhere the bank cannot read it. Where the hook had to go, against the plan The Roadmap tab expected to hang this off “whatever handler ends up watching for the buck-off-below-20% escape behaviour”. There is no such handler. That behaviour is HorseEscapeGoal , an AI goal that reads the health bar on its own canUse tick, and a goal cannot see a blow coming — by the time it runs the horse is already dead or not. So the model is HorseLastStandHandler instead, which asks the same question at the same moment: is this hit about to take the horse somewhere it should not go? LivingDamageEvent.Pre , for the last stand's reason: it is the first point at which getNewDamage is the number that will really come off the health, armour and enchantments already taken out of it, so a horse in barding is not judged on a blow it was never going to take in full. LOWEST priority, and that ordering is the feature Three handlers are now on this one event, so the priority is not tidiness — it is how they compose. Running last, the rescue sees a horse the other two could not help: a killing blow is caught by the last stand , which zeroes the damage and leaves the horse on a sliver; a rescuing braid at LOW takes that horse home if it is wearing one; and only then does this see a horse still standing on a sliver and take it out of the fight. The player gets the totem particles and the rescue, in that order, which is exactly the story that happened. In the losing order the chamber would swallow a horse the last stand was about to save anyway — spending the insurance on a blow that was never going to land. It dropped from LOW to LOWEST when the braid shipped, and the reason is the same both times. A braid is the more specific instrument — worn on one horse on purpose, where a chamber covers every horse you own — so it spends itself first. Nothing is lost by that: a horse the braid has sent home is out of the fight and takes no further blows, so the chamber it did not spend is still in your pocket. The scan the roadmap was worried about does not exist The open question this stage carried was cost: watching every owned horse's health sounds like precisely the server-wide per-tick sweep the whole stasis feature exists to avoid. It is not one, and the reason is almost too simple to write down — health only moves when something moves it , so the question is asked on a damage event and nowhere else. Past the two field reads that answer “is this a horse, and is it low”, the only cost is one walk of one player's inventory, and only for a horse that is already about to die. A world with no fighting in it pays nothing. The owner is found by HorseOwnership.ownerId and then getPlayerList().getPlayer(uuid) , not horse.getOwner() , which answers only for an owner loaded in the same level — the gap HorseHurtNoticeHandler documents and accepts. A chamber that only worked when you were standing next to the horse would be worth very little, since you are usually not. Four small decisions A state, not a crossing. StasisRescue.rescues asks where the health is , not whether this blow moved it across the line. A horse already under the threshold - because its owner was offline, or carrying nothing, when it first got hurt - must still be caught by the next chamber that can reach it. Testing for a crossing would make the rescue depend on who happened to be watching the moment it first went low, which is not a thing a player can reason about. The rider is set down, not refused. An ordinary capture refuses a horse with somebody on its back, because passengers would be discarded with it. Refusing here would make the item useless in the exact situation it is bought for, so ejectPassengers runs first and the horse vanishes from under them. It is the uncommon path anyway: HorseEscapeGoal throws the rider off at twice this health. The damage is zeroed after the capture , not before, so a horse that somehow could not be stored takes its blow rather than being handed free immunity. The refusal is throttled per horse, not per player. A full chamber refuses on every blow, and a horse standing in a fire takes one every half second. Ten seconds of quiet per horse, the same shape and the same sweep as HorseHurtNoticeHandler 's - two horses in the same fire are two different pieces of news. The threshold itself lives in common/ as StasisRescue , with the two chat lines, so StasisRescueTest can pin them — including the one assertion that is really about two features at once: the rescue fraction must stay below Escape.DEFAULT_THRESHOLD , or a horse would be swallowed before it was ever given the chance to run. The bank as a second place to look, without a world search An empty emergency chamber filed in a bank insures its owner's horses too, and the obvious way to implement that is the one thing this whole feature exists to avoid: finding a block somewhere in some dimension means a search of the world, or a chunk load per bank per blow. StasisBankIndex is what makes it cheap, and the trick is its second field . A row is a bank's position and its owner — that much is only a phone book. What matters is armed : the block entity publishes, every time its chamber grid changes and again on onLoad , whether it is currently holding an empty emergency chamber . So the usual answer — no, none of your banks can help — is a walk of a short list in memory with no chunk touched at all . A chunk is loaded only for a bank that says it is holding one, which is a horse's life against one chunk load; paying that to find out the answer is no would not have been worth it. The flag is last-known, and the reader copes. It is re-checked against the real container rather than trusted, so a stale row costs one wasted chunk load and finds nothing — which is the right way round for a cache that is deciding whether a horse lives. The block, not the index, is also the authority on who owns a bank. The search shares the refusal's clock. StasisRescue.dueAgain gates the bank walk as well as the sentence, so a horse burning to death with a stale row against it cannot re-open the same chunk every half-second. A failed search starts the quiet period even though it says nothing, which is the one place the two uses of that map differ. Eight banks, at most, per rescue. A player with three never reaches MAX_BANKS_CONSULTED ; a player with two hundred does not get to turn a wolf bite into two hundred chunk loads. A bank is claimed by placing it, or by opening an unclaimed one. That second door is the entire migration story for a world that predates this, and it takes nothing from anybody: an owned bank is untouched, and owning one grants no access that opening it did not already give. The chamber is filled in place inside the bank's grid, exactly as it is in an inventory slot, and chambers().setChanged() is what re-derives the tick gate and republishes the bank as no longer armed. That one call is the only thing the bank half adds to close . A tick between the eject and the capture Owner's call: a rider is put down and the horse goes into the bottle on the next server tick, rather than both happening inside one event and the client having to work out where its feet went. The blow is refused up front in that path — unlike the immediate one, where it is refused after the capture — because a horse left standing for a tick still holding the blow that triggered all this would simply die of it. And it is held harmless for that tick. A CAUGHT set of horse ids is checked on the first line of onBlow and zeroes any damage to a horse mid-grace, so the courtesy cannot be the thing that kills the horse . The deferral only happens when there was a rider; an unridden horse goes in immediately and has no window to protect. Both failure modes leave the chamber empty and the player insured, which is the only safe way round: a horse gone before the bottle closed, and a chamber somebody filled by hand in the one tick it was reserved for, both abort with a trace line rather than spending anything. Why a rescue is a log row and not only a chat line HorseEvent.Kind.RESCUE is the eighth kind, and it is here for the reason the Log tab exists at all: a chamber fires with nobody watching by design, possibly a thousand blocks away, so the alarm that scrolls past was the only record there was. The row's line() defers to StasisRescue.saved / savedInBank rather than writing its own wording, the way a cover defers to CoverNotice — the log is where you look when you missed the chat line, and two different sentences about one event would leave the reader wondering whether they were two events. HorseLog.rescued takes ids rather than the entity, which is the exception among its writers and is the feature rather than an awkwardness: by the time anything knows a rescue happened, the horse has been discard ed and is a data component on an item. How the browser knows a horse is in a chamber HorseWhereabouts records stasis the way it already records death, and for the same reason. A horse in a chamber is neither alive in a chunk nor dead: it fires no LivingDeathEvent , it will never be sighted again, and it therefore sat in the roster reading not loaded for ever — the same wrong claim, in the same column, that a dead horse used to make before the roster started dropping them. The mark is written by swallow before the discard, while the horse still has a position, and cleared by release once the entity is really in the world. Those are the two moments that are certain; nothing is inferred, and an ordinary sighting clears the mark too, since a horse something can see is out of the bottle however it got there. The label itself is HorseListing.IN_STASIS in common/ , and the two places the browser drew this column now both call whereLabel instead of writing loaded ? where : "not loaded" by hand. That was the real defect: with the rule written out twice, the second copy was always going to be the one that got missed. Every chamber recipe was invisible, and that is not the same as broken Reported 2026-09-25 from a modded server as “the recipes for the stasis chambers never made it into the game — the bank has a recipe, but not the chambers” , with the decisive detail that clicking a chamber in JEI popped up nothing at all . Every recipe was in fact present, registered and working: the grids below crafted correctly for anyone who already knew them. What no part of the game would do was tell anyone what they were. The cause is three defaults, not two, and getting only two of them was enough to ship the bug a second time. CustomRecipe answers isSpecial() == true and placementInfo() == PlacementInfo.NOT_PLACEABLE ; and Recipe , which it implements, defaults display() to List.of() and CustomRecipe never overrides it. They answer three different questions: Method Answers isSpecial() Is this recipe listed at all? placementInfo() May the book place it into the grid for you? display() What is actually drawn? v0.5.026 overrode the first two and shipped. The owner's report came back word for word unchanged , because a recipe that produces no RecipeDisplay has nothing for the recipe book to draw however findable and placeable it claims to be. The bank was unaffected throughout for the same reason it always was: it is an ordinary shaped JSON file, and vanilla's ShapelessRecipe and ShapedRecipe build their displays for themselves. This is the trap worth remembering , because it is invisible from inside the game code and from every test that was in place. A special recipe still crafts . The gametest suite had EVERY_RECIPE_ENCODES , which proved the recipes reach a joining client, and the class comments explained at length why each had to be Java rather than JSON — all true, all beside the point. Nothing asserted the one thing a player needs, which is that the recipe can be shown to them. STASIS_CHAMBERS_CRAFT now asserts all three flags for all five grids, and the whole family additionally grew a plain JSON twin — belt and braces, since a plain JSON recipe is the only shape this mod has ever seen draw correctly on the owner's server. Why the upgrades became three recipes Overriding the two flags was enough for the Basic and Emergency chambers. It could not be enough for the upgrades, because a recipe advertises exactly one set of ingredients , and one StasisUpgradeRecipe instance was matching all three rungs by reading tier().next() off whatever chamber was in the grid. There is no placementInfo() that says “a chamber, and whichever item its own tier calls for”, so that instance could never have described itself however the flags were set. So the tier being upgraded to is now a field on the recipe, read from a "to" key in the JSON, and data/horsegenetics/recipe/ carries stasis_upgrade_intermediate , _advanced and _spacer in place of the single stasis_upgrade . Matching is unchanged in what it accepts: each file takes the rung below its own and nothing else, which is what the one-instance version already did by a different route — including an emergency chamber going up to an Intermediate, since that item is a StasisChamberItem at BASIC and the Intermediate file asks for a tier, not a class. A side benefit worth naming: the upgrade serializer now has a real codec with a real field instead of MapCodec.unit , which removes it from the shared-instance trap described on the carrot page — there is nothing left for StreamCodec.unit 's identity check to fail on.
