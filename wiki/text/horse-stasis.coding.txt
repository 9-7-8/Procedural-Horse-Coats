Horse stasis: chambers and the bank
(Gameplay / partly built)
A cheap way to hold horses without them costing server tick time. A stasis chamber is an item a horse goes into; a Horse Stasis Bank is a block that holds many chambers and lets you read the horses inside them. The whole point is the cheapest tier, so a server with hundreds of horses can put most of them somewhere that costs nothing, instead of every horse being a real, ticking entity forever. The four chambers are built, the bank keeps them, and its Browse tab reads them. What is still planned is everything that makes the bank act on a stored horse: passive healing, the drop buffer, in-bank breeding.

Capture and release: it is the lycanthropy swap This page used to say that capture/release of a live, specific entity was “new work with no existing analog in the mod” . That was wrong, and finding out it was wrong is most of why stage one was cheap. LycanthropyHandler has carried a whole horse through a night spent inside a wolf since the LYCAN gene shipped, and it does it with exactly two calls: horse.saveWithoutId(TagValueOutput) a CompoundTag , then horse.discard() . EntityType.HORSE.create(level, EntitySpawnReason.LOAD) horse.load(TagValueInput.create(...)) level.addFreshEntity(horse) . On NeoForge saveWithoutId carries the data attachments , and it stores the entity's UUID . That is the whole reason this shape is right and the one this page originally sketched was not: a released horse is the same horse down to the id that the ancestry database, every pedigree, every stall sign and every transfer deed key on. The sketch — a record enumerating horse_record , horse_gear , horse_care and friends by hand — would have lost the id, lost every vanilla field nobody listed (age, custom name, leash, the saddle in the vanilla slot), and rotted the first time somebody added a fifteenth attachment without thinking of that file. Where the snapshot had to live, against the plan The plan wanted StasisSnapshot in common/ so the web designer and a future 1.12.2 port could read a chamber. A tag-based capture cannot go there: CompoundTag is a Minecraft type and common/ imports none (hard rule 1). So the split is common/…/horse/StasisTier.java — the four tiers and their capability flags, the one part with no Minecraft in it, with StasisTierTest pinning the ladder — and neoforge/…/data/StasisSnapshot.java for the capture itself. Correct restoration beat portability; the portable half is the half that had something to port. The bank is a filtered chest, and that is the whole of stage two It is modelled on EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity down to the setChanged forwarding, because it is the same problem: a SimpleContainer the menu wraps on the server and the ordinary vanilla slot sync fills in on the client. Nothing here ever unpacks a snapshot. A horse in stasis is a data component on an item, and the bank moves items. Two things it deliberately does not have yet, each because the thing that needs it is a later stage: No ticker. Nothing advances with nobody looking. Passive healing (stage four) is what will want one, and it will want the research shelf's server-only createTickerHelper shape. No ContainerData , the Browse tab included. There is no progress and no server-side pick to sync. The horse count on the title row, and every row of the Browse list, is a loop over the slots the client already has rather than a synced number — every chamber carries its whole horse, so both sides can count and read for free. Two small divergences from the chest it resembles It is not flammable. Vanilla's chest takes ignitedByLava() and this does not, which is one omitted call: mod blocks are not in FireBlock 's flammability table unless they register themselves. A chest that burns down loses its diamonds; a bank that burns down would drop fifty-four pedigreed horses into the fire that did it. It is not a Container . The block entity holds one rather than implementing one, exactly as the research shelf does, so hoppers and pipes cannot reach it. Automating the filing of a live animal is not a thing anybody asked for, and the cheapest way to not have it is to not expose the inventory. Chambers are stacksTo(1) in ModItems already; ChamberSlot.getMaxStackSize() and the container's both say so again, so no shift-click can ever try to merge two horses into one stack. The slot predicate takes any tier, empty or occupied — a bank that refused a Basic chamber would have inverted the whole ladder, since the bottom rung is the one that has to be worth using. The Browse tab reads the stable's papers, not the chamber The design call this tab had to make was where a row's details come from, and the Roadmap tab offered two answers: decode a whole entity tag per rendered row, or cache more fields onto the component at capture time. It is neither. A horse in a chamber keeps its UUID , the stable's ancestry records are keyed on exactly that, and ClientHorseRoster already holds every one of those records as a HorseListing — the same rows the Horse Browser 's My horses table sorts. So a row is a map lookup on an id the chamber has already told the client, and the tab inherits the browser's coat description, sex label, breed, generation and whole query language for free. That is also why the filter box takes HorseQuery verbatim rather than a smaller search of its own: a second, weaker query language in the same mod would have been more code and a worse feature. The screen opens by sending HorseRosterRequestPayload , the same request the browser's Refresh button sends, because a player may never have opened the browser on this client. What that choice costs, and why it is the right side of the trade The roster is this player's horses, and it is capped at HorseRosterPayload.MAX_ENTRIES . A chamber somebody else filled and handed over, or a stable past that cap, therefore has no row to match — so the tab draws that line by name and says it has no papers, and a search never returns it. The alternative, decoding fifty-four entity tags to draw a list, buys those rows at the cost of the one thing the whole feature exists to avoid: doing work per horse that nobody is looking at. The name is never lost either way , because StasisSnapshot cached it at capture time for exactly this. StasisBrowseRow is in common/ , and holds the gate: a row is searchable only if its own chamber's StasisTier.searchable() says so and there is a listing behind it, and locked() counts the rows that fail that test so the screen can say how many a query could not reach. Both halves are StasisBrowseTest 's subject, because a horse silently disappearing from a filtered bank is the one failure this tab must not have: the player's next thought is that the bank lost it. Two things the screen does that are not about stasis at all: The list is rebuilt on a change, not per frame. A signature over which horse is in which slot, and in what tier, is taken each frame — ids and ordinals, no tag decoded — and the rows are rebuilt only when that, the roster's version, or the query moves. ClientHorseRoster makes the same point about why a table must not resolve a genotype per row per frame. A text box inside a container screen must handle Escape first. EditBox.canConsumeInput() is true for every key once the box has focus, so guarding on it alone traps the player in the window with no way out - the equestrian bench's own override says so, having been reported for it, and this copies that shape rather than rediscovering it. See api-notes.html . Why two of these are Java recipes Neither is a CustomRecipe for the fun of it, and both reasons are worth keeping: The water container is not consumed. A vanilla recipe eats every input. StasisChamberRecipe overrides getRemainingItems and hands back the input's own declared crafting remainder — which is how a vanilla water bucket returns an empty bucket, the same as in a cake. The ingredient is matched on c:buckets/water ( Tags.Items.BUCKETS_WATER ), so another mod's container crafts it without anyone naming it here. An upgrade must carry the horse. A shapeless JSON recipe builds its result from scratch and copies no components, so upgrading an occupied chamber would hand back an empty one and delete the animal — silently, with nothing in the log. StasisUpgradeRecipe copies the stasis_snapshot across. The JSON version of this feature is not a cheaper version of it; it is a data-loss bug with a crafting recipe attached. Both serializers are unit codecs, and that is load-bearing Each recipe has one INSTANCE shared by MapCodec.unit(INSTANCE) (the value overload) and StreamCodec.unit(INSTANCE) . A supplier-based map codec decodes a different object, the stream codec's identity check then throws, and because NeoForge sends the whole recipe set to every joining client as one payload, that is a login kick on every dedicated server — which shipped once, in v0.5.014. Singleplayer never encodes a recipe, so nothing short of a real join catches it. See api-notes.html . The every_recipe_encodes gametest is the cheap version of that join, and both of these pass it. The component syncs the whole tag, on purpose It is tempting to give STASIS_SNAPSHOT a lossy stream codec that sends only the name and the id, since that is all a tooltip needs, and keep the heavy tag server-side. That is wrong, and the reason is creative mode: a creative pick-block sends the client's copy of a stack back to the server, so anything the client was never told is anything a creative player can quietly erase. The whole tag goes over the wire. Bandwidth is therefore a real question for the bank, where fifty-four of them sit in one container — not for an item in a hand. The bank ships syncing all of it , deliberately: sending less is how a creative player silently erases a horse, and the reverse of that trade is a number to measure rather than a bug to fix. The measurement is on this page's Verification tab. Small decisions inside release Position comes from the chamber, not the tag. The tag remembers where the horse was captured, which may be a world away; where you open it is where it wakes up, with its velocity zeroed. It starts fed. Hunger is explicitly reset to Hunger.FULL , matching the hunger attachment's own rule that a re-summoned horse is not starving. It is also the only honest answer when the horse has been outside time. A horse that will not load stays in the chamber. If load throws, nothing is consumed and the chamber is unchanged, so a later build may still be able to read it. The alternative is destroying a pedigreed animal to tidy up an exception.
