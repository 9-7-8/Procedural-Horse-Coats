Cheek spikes
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One copy and the horse grows a short row of bone spikes along each cheek, pointing out from the head and a little back, the longest at the rear. How long and thick they are and how many each cheek has is written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, cheek spike colour .

Crossing two of them One copy, either sex Dominant: Chk/n and Chk/Chk both grow spikes, so a spiked horse bred to a plain one gives a spiked foal at least half the time. It is not sex-limited. Foals grow none; the spikes come in when the horse grows up, and stay. Where they sit In a row along the side of the head, below the eye and above the mouth, the same on both sides. The spike nearest the neck is the longest and each one toward the muzzle is shorter. Finding one Rare in the wild: about three horses in a thousand have cheek spikes, and because one copy is enough, every horse that carries the gene shows it. The skeleton horses can grow them in bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts. What varies Length : three classes, short , medium and long . Short to medium in the wild; a line bred for length reaches long and stops. Girth : how thick each spike is for its length. Count : how many each cheek has, from the back forward, two to four in the wild. A line can be bred down to a single spike per cheek, and the next one grows in gradually. There is one shape. Everything stacks: a horse can wear cheek spikes with every other part. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no damage to anything, no hitbox. It is a look.
