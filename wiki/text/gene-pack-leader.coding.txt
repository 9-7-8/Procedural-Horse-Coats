Leader of the pack
(Magical gene / no coat effect)
Something follows this horse. Which something depends on the allele — there is one for every non-hostile mob in the game — and they trail it within sixteen blocks wherever it goes. Two copies of the same allele or nothing .

Gene key horsegenetics.pack_leader Alleles one per non-hostile mob, plus n Inheritance homozygous recessive, and recessive to each other — a matched pair or nothing Reaches the game as a mob_aura effect in follow mode Coat effect none, ever Continuous pathfinding for the followers is the real cost Every follower recalculates a path on its own schedule, and pathfinding is the most expensive thing a mob does. A long interval and a low max_targets are not tuning, they are the design. Followers also drag chunks busy as they cross boundaries behind the horse. Do not inject a goal into another mod’s mob A goal added to an arbitrary mob has to be taken off again, and a horse that dies, despawns or stops expressing leaves it behind on every creature it ever met — the exact lifecycle failure mob aura already avoids by shoving rather than adding an avoidance goal. Nudge navigation per beat instead; it is stateless and it cannot leak. The allele set is free MobRoster already holds this list — token, mob id, label and habitat per entry, with the ids as plain strings resolved against the live registry. Read the roster rather than writing a second list that will drift from it. This locus takes peaceful() whole; lycan takes only ground() . The group filter must use entity type tags Five genes share this filter. A hardcoded list makes every modded creature invisible to all five at once, and the failure is silent — the aura simply never fires on anything from another mod. Build it on tags in the change that introduces it. A third mode, not a new verb mob_aura already has repel and attract . Following is follow , on the same verb, with the same cap and the same beat. A summon verb was specified early in design and folded into this instead once it became clear that spawner was the only gene that genuinely needed one. Why a matched pair Same reasoning as lycan : a locus with dozens of alleles where any one showed would be a slot machine, and a locus where you must find the same allele twice is a search with a target. Chaos (2026-10-01) Cha sits after the last vanilla allele and before n , and the copy carries a chaos seed — the locus’s first epigenetic value. The shared base, AbstractMatchedPairGene , does the work for both mob loci on it: a matched Chaos pair whose two seeds agree hands its mob_aura the subject chaos:<seed> in the ordinary mob field, a disagreeing pair hands it nothing, and foundersShareSeeds makes a fresh horse one line. The game module swaps the subject for a real id at each beat through ChaosRoster ’s PACK list — modded mobs that are not a MONSTER spawn category and not hostile on inspection. The design and the reasons it is one allele rather than one per mob are on lycan’s page ; the seed field and the info-screen line are on its Roadmap tab . ChaosMobLociTest pins the common half. Verified The retinue costs nothing measurable (2026-09-13). A pen of four leaders and sixteen cows, twenty-four mobs re-pathing on a forty-tick beat, held a flat 50.0 ms per tick with 213 entities in the dimension, which is its baseline. A stable with several pack leaders in it is fine. A retinue forms and follows, a mismatched pair leads none, and the cap holds (test yard, pen PACK LEADER, the run of 2026-10-02 03:52 on a56afcbb , three clockwork PASS lines). A Cow/Cow horse re-pathed cows to itself on 21 of 21 beats; the ten cows’ mean distance fell 9.3 4.8 while it shuffled, and the eight within 6 blocks then walked 13 blocks east after it, ending 2.9 from it against 12.3 had they stood still (03:53:55). No beat re-pathed more than 6 cows ( MAX_TARGETS ), on 21 beats with up to 10 in the radius (03:53:55). Before it, a Cow/Shp horse walked the same steps past the same cows and drew none to its block in 10 samples (03:53:07). A leader that dies takes its last order back (2026-10-08, test yard, pen PACK LEADER, CLOCKWORK PASS on three launches running: 09:41:29, 10:55:38 and 10:56:51). The pen kills a Cow/Cow leader with ten cows round it and reads each cow's navigation one and two beats later. On the third launch three cows were on their way to it at the kill, and none was a beat later. Before the fix the same check failed in eight runs of ten, with three or four cows still walking out the last few blocks to the block the leader died on: a follower's path was never renewed after the death, but nothing withdrew it either ( #213 ).
