The horse browser
(Gameplay / partly built)
The horse browser is the mod’s own index of everything you own and everything it knows — an H -key screen carrying a list of your horses, a searchable gene database, the recipes, the alleles, the breeds, a breeding preview and a log of what has been happening to your stable . Part of it ships. Most of the built half is written up on Breeding & pedigree , under client sync, the information screen and the family tree ; the Log tab is written up here, and the rest of the plan is on the Roadmap tab.

The roster refreshes on every open (2026-09-24) HorseBrowserScreen.init() and the tab-switch path both used to ask for the roster only if (!ClientHorseRoster.received()) . That reads as “only the first time” and means never again this session : ClientHorseRoster.clear() runs on disconnect and nowhere else, so a player who bred a foal, tamed a horse or sold one saw the stable as it was the first time they pressed H until they found the Refresh button. Both paths now re-ask. Throttled, though, and that is the part worth knowing. Unguarded, every click on the tab strip would make the server walk the whole ancestry table ( HorseRoster.gather , plus the allele and breed sweeps), and flipping through tabs is a casual gesture in a way pressing Refresh is not. requestRosterUnlessJustAsked() skips a sweep within two seconds of the last one — long enough that no realistic clicking costs more than one, far shorter than anything a player could do to change the stable in between. The cooldown is static , so closing and reopening the screen does not reset it, which is exactly the gesture it exists to soak up. The Refresh button is deliberately not throttled: it exists to be believed. ClientHorseRoster.received() is still read, and now only for what it was always good for — telling an empty stable from the answer has not arrived yet , which is the difference between “no horses” and “asking the server…” in three places including HorseStasisBankScreen . Birth and death pushes are still owed; see the roadmap tab . The Log tab, as built Shipped 2026-09-24. The model is game-free and the game module only translates into it, which is the usual split and matters more than usual here: what a row says is the feature, and a sentence is the easiest thing in the mod to unit-test and the hardest to notice going wrong. Where it lives common/…/log/HorseEvent — one row. Kind, tick, horse UUID, the horse’s name as it read at the time, the other party, and — for a cover only — a CoverNotice.Reason plus the crowd numbers. line() is the sentence; detail() is that sentence with the leading name trimmed off for the table’s own Horse column; when() turns the tick into Day 42, 06:30 . common/…/log/HorseEventLog — the store. One log per owner , newest-first reads, bounded at MAX_PER_OWNER , and the deduplication rule. Everything a test can check is in these two classes; HorseEventLogTest checks it. neoforge/…/data/HorseEventData — a server-global SavedData , in the same shape as HorseAncestryData , with HorseEventCodecs for disk and wire. neoforge/…/server/HorseLog — the translation layer and the death hook. HorseLogPayload / HorseLogRequestPayload , client/ClientHorseLog , and the LOG tab in HorseBrowserScreen . A refusal is a constant, not a string A row for a failed cover carries the CoverNotice.Reason itself, and renders by calling CoverNotice.line(…) — the same method the chat notice calls. So the log row and the chat line the owner missed are not merely similar, they are the same string built by the same code, and a refusal added to NaturalCover becomes a log row for free. The Horse column would have repeated the name, so detail() trims the leading name off mechanically rather than keeping a second, shorter set of phrasings that could drift from the first. Why every writer resolves a UUID and never a player HorseDeathNoticeHandler and HorseHurtNoticeHandler both need a ServerPlayer in the same level, and both are right to: a chat line for somebody who logged out an hour ago is not an alarm. But that owner still lost a horse, and the answer to what happened while I was away has to be written while they are away or it cannot exist. So every writer here goes through HorseOwnership.ownerId , and none of them is gated on the notice config. For the same reason the death hook is HorseLog ’s own LivingDeathEvent subscriber rather than a call from the notice handler, which returns early on three separate conditions — the config, the showDeathMessages game rule, and the horse not being tamed. The duplicate that risks (several handlers, one death) is caught by the same deduplication rule as everything else. One deduplication rule, in one place Two different things produce repeats: a refused cover is re-evaluated every two seconds for the whole of a mare’s heat, and a death passes through several handlers. Rather than teach each call site its own throttle — which is how one of them ends up without one — a repeat of the same kind, horse and reason within DEDUPE_TICKS is dropped in HorseEventLog.add . The window is CoverNotice.QUIET_TICKS on purpose, so the log and the chat line agree about what counts as news. Note what the rule cannot collapse: a birth is keyed by the foal , so two foals five minutes apart are two different horses and two rows. The rule is about one happening reported twice, never two happenings that look alike. One clock Every row is stamped with the overworld’s game time whatever dimension the event happened in. Each dimension keeps its own day counter, so stamping locally would interleave the horse dimension ’s day 4 with the overworld’s day 900 and newest-first would be nonsense. The whole log, coalesced HorseLogPayload carries the player’s whole log rather than a page or a delta, which rests on it already being bounded server-side: two hundred rows of a few dozen bytes is less than the roster this screen already sends. Paging would buy two more states to get wrong — a half-loaded list and a stale page — and would force the filter server-side for a list the client can filter in a frame. Because the payload is the whole log, writes mark the owner dirty and a server tick flushes once a second instead of sending on every row. A foaling morning can land a dozen rows in one tick, and sending the same two hundred rows a dozen times over is the kind of waste that only shows up on a server with people on it. A foal is not a taming HorseOwnerTrackingHandler writes the taming row at the one moment tamedBy goes from empty to filled. That normally happens only to a horse somebody roped — but a foal born to a tamed dam whose owner was offline also arrives with tamedBy empty and gets filled in later by the same poll. So the row is skipped when the record already has a mother: that foal already has its birth row, and without the guard the owner would come back to “You tamed Willow” for a horse they never touched. It needed no new tab-strip work The horizontal scrolling tab strip already existed — centred while it fits, scissored and scrolled with a bar once it does not — so adding a seventh tab cost nothing but an enum constant. The rest of that plan is still open: nothing scrolls the selected tab into view, and the tabs are drawn rather than being widgets, so there is no keyboard focus to move through them.
