Horn colour
(Magical gene / codominant / colours the horn, paints nothing)
What colour a unicorn's horn is — white, any colour of the rainbow, pink, black, grey, or chaos , a colour of its own on every copy. Nothing is dominant, so a horse with two different copies has a two-tone horn : one colour, with a tip of the other. On a horse without a horn this gene does nothing at all.

A horse with two different ones has a horn of one colour whose tip is the other, fading between them about halfway up. Which colour is the base is decided by the pair, not by which parent gave which: it is the one earlier in the list above. So a white horse carrying red has a white horn with a red tip, and a red/blue horse a red horn tipped blue — every time. Because nothing hides anything, you can read a unicorn's colour genes straight off its horn. Most wild unicorns are white, and a good share have a coloured tip. No two reds are the same red Every copy carries a little variation of its own, written on the allele and inherited with it: every red horn is clearly red, but the exact shade differs from horse to horse and drifts slowly down a family. White, black and grey vary only a touch. Chaos is the extreme of this — its whole colour is on the copy, so no two chaos horns match. On a horse without a horn Nothing. A hornless horse can carry any colours at all and show no sign of them, and passes them on to its foals like any other gene — so the colour a unicorn comes out can depend on ancestors that never had a horn.
