The four posts
(Blocks / villagers)
The four equestrians ’ workstations. Put one down near an unemployed villager and you get that trade, and only that trade — which is why there are four of them and not one.

They are POIs and nothing else Plain blocks with no behaviour of their own — everything happens because each one backs a POI of its own name ( horsegenetics:leatherworkers_post and the other three), registered in village/ModPoiTypes with maxTickets 1 the way every vanilla job site is. The professions that claim them are in village/ModVillagerProfessions ; the blocks themselves are four registerSimpleBlock calls in block/ModBlocks . A POI is only half the wiring. It has to be in the minecraft:acquirable_job_site tag before an unemployed villager will take the job, and that half is a tag file under data/minecraft/tags/point_of_interest_type/ . Tags merge across datapacks, so adding to vanilla’s is additive and safe where overriding a worldgen file would not be. The Cowboy Hitch must never appear in one : it is deliberately not a POI, and a tag naming a missing entry fails to load as a whole , taking vanilla’s own village POIs down with it — which it did, for four days ( the record ). Why four blocks and not one A POI hands out exactly one profession. Vanilla decides the job from the job site claimed, not the other way round, so a single shared post could only ever hand out a single job — and the version that tried to alternate could not undo the job-site ticket it had already granted. Four jobs therefore need four blocks, and the hitch is a fifth for the separate reason that a cowboy is an entity rather than a profession. They are identical in every mechanical respect but their name and their recipe, down to a shared cube_bottom_top model. What they no longer share is their art. Until 2026-09-29 all five drew one three-face texture set, so five different workstations were pixel-identical in world and no player could tell whose post they were standing at. Each now carries its own trade on the same barrel carpentry: a hide stretched on the leatherworker’s, a rack of filled tubes on the scientist’s, a grain sack and carrots on the supplier’s, a horseshoe on the metalsmith’s, and a hitching ring with a coiled lariat on the cowboy’s. The iron hoops are recoloured to the hat of the equestrian that post recruits — russet, teal, olive, steel — so a player who has learned the hats has already learned the posts; the hitch keeps the original black iron as the plain one of the five. The art is baked by tools/bake-villager-posts.mjs , which stamps a pixel map per trade onto pristine copies of the shared carpentry, so the barrel exists once and a motif is edited a character at a time. Only the underside is still shared, deliberately: it is the face of a block nobody sees, and five copies of a plank texture would be five things to keep in step for nothing. The five posts as they are actually built — drag to turn them, scroll to zoom, or focus the view and use the arrow keys. They are one scene rather than five, so one drag compares all five at the same angle, which is the whole question this picture answers. The fossil texture name, and the moment it was fixed The shared set was called horse_traders_post_{top,side,bottom}.png , and there has never been a block called a Horse Trader’s Post — the old block was horsemans_table , the “Horseman’s Table”, and the wiki called it a Horse Trader’s Post in prose for a while without either of them ever matching the art. This page used to argue the rename was not worth doing on its own — it means touching every model that references it and re-checking the atlas, for no change a player can see — and that the right moment was when the four stop sharing one set . That is exactly what happened: the top and side were replaced per post and deleted, the bottom was renamed post_bottom.png , and every model was being rewritten anyway. The fossil is gone. Loot tables One per post, all of them the ordinary survives_explosion single-item drop, at data/horsegenetics/loot_table/blocks/ . The four were written at the same time the hitch finally got one — the hitch had shipped with none, which in this version means the block drops nothing rather than defaulting to itself. A block registered without noLootTable() and without a file is silently unrecoverable, and nothing in the build says so.
