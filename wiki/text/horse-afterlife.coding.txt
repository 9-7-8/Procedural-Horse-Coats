Bringing a dead horse back
(Gameplay / operator tooling)
An operator's undo for the one loss nothing else can reverse. Every other way of losing a horse has a tool that finds it: sold, turned out, bottled, stabled, lost across a dimension. A horse that died had nothing, and the thing that dies is not a stack of items — it is an animal with a name somebody chose, a bond they spent weeks on and a pedigree three generations deep. So a dead horse is now kept whole for a while , and /horseresurrect spends that: the horse that stands back up is the same horse , same id, and every foal's pedigree still points at it. It is an operator's command and nothing a player can reach.

The four pieces Class Job data/HorseAfterlife SavedData , server-global, id horsegenetics:horse_afterlife . A map of horse id to Wake . Holds the snapshot, the owner, where and when it died, and the owner-online ticks spent so far. server/HorseAfterlifeHandler The writing half: LivingDeathEvent takes the snapshot, and a ServerTickEvent.Post sweep spends the window. server/HorseResurrection The one place a dead horse is put back on its feet, shared by the command and the egg so the two cannot disagree. server/HorseResurrectCommand /horseresurrect , and nothing else reads the store. It is the stasis chamber's payload, pointed at a death A Wake carries a StasisSnapshot — the dead animal's complete Entity.saveWithoutId tag, which on NeoForge takes the data attachments with it. That is the same payload a stasis chamber holds and the same one the lycan gene has been carrying a horse through the night inside since it shipped, so this is a third caller on a proven mechanism rather than a fourth way to freeze a horse. The tag is what keeps the UUID , and the UUID is the whole argument for this design. Rebuilding the horse from its HorseRecord instead would have been easier and would have produced an animal with the right coat, the right name, the right stats and the wrong identity — every foal it ever had orphaned, every stall sign pointing at a horse that no longer exists, and nothing anywhere saying so. The gametest exists because that failure is invisible. It is not a second copy of the ancestry database. HorseAncestryData keeps a dead horse's record for ever on purpose, because the pedigree needs it, and nothing here duplicates that. What is kept here is the part the ancestry data deliberately does not hold: the live entity, as it stood. The clock is the owner, and it counts up Wake.ownerTicks counts up and is compared against the setting when it is read, rather than counting a budget down from whatever the setting was at death. That is what lets a config change apply to horses that are already dead — in both directions, including to “for ever” and back. HorseAfterlife.spend is only ever called for a player who is logged in, which is what makes the clock theirs; the sweep walks the online players every 200 ticks. dropExpired runs in the same sweep and is not redundant with it: a horse belonging to somebody who never comes back would otherwise sit in the save for ever under a window it is already past, the moment an operator lowered the setting. The consequence to know about An owner who never logs in again never spends their window, so their horses are kept indefinitely. That is the design working as asked — the clock is their chance to ask — but it means the store's floor is set by departed players rather than by the setting. Nothing trims it today. Undoing the death, not just the absence A stasis snapshot is of a healthy horse somebody chose to bottle. This one is of an animal in the instant it died, and the tag is honest about that. HorseResurrection.revive runs before the horse is placed, so it never exists in the world in the state it died in: The gear is cleared , through the entity's own EquipmentSlot s rather than by deleting NBT keys — the keys move between versions, and a rename would fail silently as a duplicated saddle rather than as a crash. The snapshot is taken before vanilla drops the tack, so it is in the tag and on the ground. Effects, fire and air are reset. A horse that died of wither would otherwise stand up and die again, which reads as the command not working. The health is refilled from the genotype , not from the tag — the tag says zero. The same applyTraitsToEntity(.., true) a newborn foal gets. The corpse has to go first (2026-09-26) A horse does not leave the world the instant it dies: it falls over for twenty ticks and is removed at the end of that. For the whole of that second the corpse still holds the UUID , so addFreshEntity refuses the resurrected horse with “UUID of added entity already exists” — a warning in the log, a null return, and an operator told the horse could not be read. That is the most likely resurrection there is: the one typed while the owner is still shouting about it. HorseResurrection.clearCorpse discards any entity with that id that is not isAlive() , across every level, before restoring. Its loot has already dropped by then, so nothing is destroyed but the animation. A genuinely living horse with that id is a different matter and is refused by alreadyAlive instead. This was found by the gametest below, on its first run, and not by reading the code. The egg is the preset egg ModDataComponents.RESURRECTION is a StasisSnapshot on a preset_horse_spawn_egg , and PresetHorseSpawnEggItem.useOn checks for it before STORED_GENOME . Reusing that item rather than adding one is the same call GeneDeathHandler.cloneEgg already makes for the death drop: the two are the same thing arrived at from different directions, and one of them already has a model, a tooltip and a spawner behind it. It is a component of its own rather than STASIS_SNAPSHOT even though the type is identical, because the two mean different things to the item carrying them — a chamber holding a snapshot is a full chamber and its whole state machine turns on that. The saving would have been one registration line. One exception, written down HorseWhereabouts.seen refuses to un-kill a horse, on purpose, so that a stale sighting cannot. seenAlive is the single caller entitled to say otherwise, and exists so the browser and the ender whistle stop insisting a horse is dead while its owner is riding it.
