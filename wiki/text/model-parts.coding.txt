Attached model parts
(Part built / one part ships, the rest planned)
Meshes hung on the horse model, driven by a gene or by a breed — a unicorn horn, a row of dorsal spines, a pair of wings, feathered hooves. They need no animation of their own, because they are parented to a part of the horse that is already animated. It is the mod’s first visual that is not a texture , which is why this is a page rather than a line on a list.

The shape of it: two sources, one renderer A part has two possible origins and exactly one destination. Grown parts come from the genotype, in common/ ; worn ones will come from the gear attachment, on the client. Both reduce to the same object before they reach the layer — an AttachedPart : a shape, a transform, a colour — and the layer does not know which it is drawing . That is the whole point of the design, and what it buys is that horse shoes, a saddle pad and a set of dorsal spines cannot become three render layers with three different opinions about draw order. What each piece does Class Module Job PartNode common One box: a parent, a point along it, a rotation, a length, a girth, a sheet region. A tree of these is a part. PartKind common What a gene asks for. Carries the anchor and the default sheet region. One value today ( HORN ); a kind arrives with its geometry, never ahead of it. PartAnchor common Where it roots, as an enum and never a bone name — so common/ imports nothing from Minecraft and retargeting an anchor is a client edit. PartShape common The identity of a baked mesh: kind, style, size bucket. The cache key, and the thing that bounds the cost. PartGenerators common The one door from a shape to a box list. Deterministic and game-free, so a test walks every mesh the mod can build with no game running. AttachedPart common One part on one horse, resolved: a shape plus the numbers applied to it at draw time. GrownParts common Every part this genotype grows, in one call. The client asks here and nowhere else, so the second granting gene is a line in this file and no change at all on the Minecraft side. PartMeshes neoforge Bakes a shape into a ModelPart tree, once, and keeps it. PartModel neoforge A baked mesh with no animation of its own, typed on the part rather than the horse — see the deferred-submit note below. AttachedPartLayer neoforge Draws them. The only place in the mod that knows a horse bone’s name. Adding the second part A value on PartKind , with its anchor and sheet region, and a styles() case. A generator beside HornGenerator , emitting PartNode s with every parent before its children — PartMeshes bakes in one pass and PartGeneratorTest is what stops a violation reaching a player as a crash report. A case in PartGenerators.build and in PartShape.nominalLength / stretchTo . If it needs a new anchor: a value on PartAnchor and a case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor , with one offset per horse model — the adult and the foal share bone names and nothing else. A region in PartSheet and a paint() call in bake-part-sheet.mjs . Keep it a uniform grain; see below. A gene that returns one, and a line in GrownParts.of . What is not on that list is a new render layer, a new render state field, a new config key, or a texture per colour. That was the goal. The rule that keeps a herd cheap Everything a part can vary by is sorted by what it costs to change , not by what it means: Topology — segment counts, branching, twist — goes in the PartShape and means baking a mesh. Quantised, and the set of shapes is therefore finite and small: PartKind.styles() × PartShape.SIZE_BUCKETS per kind, for the whole game, forever. The bound on cost is structural rather than a budget somebody has to enforce , which is why PartMeshes needs neither eviction nor a time budget where GeneticCoatTextureFactory needs both. Proportion and pose — length, girth, lean — is a pose-stack transform. Free, continuous, and safe under a deferred draw because a pose is copied into the submit node. This is also what closes the seam the size buckets would otherwise leave: the scale lands a part on its exact epigenetic length, so nothing pops as a lineage grows through a bucket boundary. Colour and glow are arguments to the submit. One greyscale sheet therefore serves every colour, which is the same trick a dyed braid uses. The deferred-submit trap submitModel stores a copy of the pose and a reference to the model, and the batch renderer calls setupAnim(state) immediately before drawing each submit. So the obvious way to vary one shared mesh per horse — set visible or xScale on its parts and then submit — is a trap: the second horse in a frame overwrites the first horse’s mesh before either is drawn, and the bug is one horse wearing another’s antlers. The escape is that submitModel is generic in its state , so PartModel is a Model<AttachedPart> : anything that must be applied by mutation goes in setupAnim , where it runs per submit with that submit’s own numbers. Nothing needs it yet, so setupAnim is empty — and that is exactly where an antler’s tine count will have to go. Anchoring, and the root transform The layer walks the chain from the root part down, where CutieMarkLayer reaches straight for body and hand-tunes its offsets to compensate. That is not tidiness: HdHorseModel.createHdLayer applies MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F) , and that transformer rewrites the root part’s own pose — a 1.1 scale and a −2.4016 y offset — so a layer that skips the root works in a space 10% too small and two and a half units too high. Walking from the root keeps the number out of the layer entirely, and makes the same code correct for the foal, whose layer carries no such transform. Seen working, 2026-09-30. This is the claim the page rests on — that a mesh hung on an already-animated bone inherits its animation and needs none of its own — and the owner watched it hold: a unicorn's horn stayed on the head through the horse's poses in the dev yard, and sat in the right place on an adult. The computed foal offset put the horn in the right place too. So antlers, spines, crystals and wings are unblocked on this point, and the plain-layer-over-GeckoLib decision stands. After the chain, a model unit is 1 16 of a block — the same units the boxes themselves use — because each translateAndRotate divides its translation by sixteen as it goes. Never add a child to body or head in createHdBodyMesh for a part. That would move the mesh the HD coat UVs are baked against, which is the one thing on this renderer that cannot quietly shift. A separate LayerDefinition , a separate layer, always. Meshes are baked at run time Every other model in the mod is registered during EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterLayerDefinitions , which would have forced parts into a fixed set declared at startup — and that would have made a horn’s length one of five presets instead of a real number. It is not so constrained: LayerDefinition.bakeRoot() is public and pure ( mesh.getRoot().bake(xTexSize, yTexSize) , touching no registry, no event and no resource manager). Read out of the 26.1.2 sources rather than assumed; see API notes . One greyscale sheet, and why its regions are featureless part_sheet.png is 64×64, cut into a 4×4 grid of 16×16 regions, baked by node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-part-sheet.mjs . Two regions are painted and the rest are room for the parts that have not been written. A box reads the corner of its region rather than all of it, because a cube’s UV rectangle is its own unwrap — 2*(girth+girth) wide and girth+length tall — laid down at the region’s origin, and the largest box any part generates is about 10×4 texels. That is deliberate: it means a part whose proportions change with an epigenetic number needs no new sheet. The price is that a region must look right at any sub-rectangle of itself , so the baker writes a tileable grain with a direction and no composition at all. A hand-painted or pack-sourced sheet is an upgrade path under the same constraint, and anything vendored that way needs its licence checked first — which is why the shipped default is procedural and owes nothing to anyone. What the genetic size locus does to a part Nothing, explicitly: a part inherits the horse’s render scale because that scale is already on the pose stack when the layer runs, not because the layer applies it. That is a claim from reading and not from watching , and a tiny and a huge horse are on the checks.
