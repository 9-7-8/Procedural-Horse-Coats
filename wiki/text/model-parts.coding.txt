Attached model parts
(Part built / one part ships, the rest planned)
Meshes hung on the horse model, driven by a gene or by a breed — a unicorn horn, a row of dorsal spines, a pair of wings, feathered hooves. They need no animation of their own, because they are parented to a part of the horse that is already animated. It is the mod’s first visual that is not a texture , which is why this is a page rather than a line on a list.

The shape of it: two sources, one renderer A part has two possible origins and exactly one destination. Grown parts come from the genotype, in common/ ; worn ones will come from the gear attachment, on the client. Both reduce to the same object before they reach the layer — an AttachedPart : a shape, a transform, a colour — and the layer does not know which it is drawing . That is the whole point of the design, and what it buys is that horse shoes, a saddle pad and a set of dorsal spines cannot become three render layers with three different opinions about draw order. What each piece does Class Module Job PartNode common One box: a parent, a point along it, a rotation, a length, a girth, a sheet region. A tree of these is a part. PartKind common What a gene asks for. Carries the anchor and the default sheet region. One value per part, and one per side for a pair (the antlers, ram’s horns and dragon horns); a kind arrives with its geometry, never ahead of it. PartAnchor common Where it roots, as an enum and never a bone name — so common/ imports nothing from Minecraft and retargeting an anchor is a client edit. PartShape common The identity of a baked mesh: kind, style, size bucket. The cache key, and the thing that bounds the cost. PartGenerators common The one door from a shape to a box list. Deterministic and game-free, so a test walks every mesh the mod can build with no game running. AttachedPart common One part on one horse, resolved: a shape plus the numbers applied to it at draw time — including a base and a tip colour, blended by tintAt . GrownParts common Every part this genotype grows, in one call, dressed by the loci that finish it — the horn’s colour and glow are other genes. The client asks here and nowhere else, so the second granting gene is a line in this file and no change at all on the Minecraft side. SaddleZone common Which elements of a part along the back a saddle hides, and when: a mask of groups by where each roots, applied while a saddle is drawn or the horse is ridden. See below . PartMeshes neoforge Bakes a shape into a ModelPart tree, once, and keeps it. PartModel neoforge A baked mesh with no animation of its own, typed on a Slice of the part rather than the horse, so a two-tone part can be drawn a segment at a time — see the deferred-submit note below. AttachedPartLayer neoforge Draws them. The only place in the mod that knows a horse bone’s name. Adding another part A value on PartKind , with its anchor and sheet region, and cases in styles() , sizeBuckets() , showsOnFoal() and glowRegions() — every one a switch expression or a comparison, so the compiler or a test catches a kind left out. A part that lies along the back also answers saddleZoned() and scalesPerElement() ( the body parts ). A generator beside HornGenerator , emitting PartNode s with every parent before its children — PartMeshes bakes in one pass and PartGeneratorTest is what stops a violation reaching a player as a crash report. A case in PartGenerators.build and in PartShape.nominalLength / stretchTo . If it needs a new anchor: a value on PartAnchor and a case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor , with one offset per horse model — the adult and the foal share bone names and nothing else. A region in PartSheet and a paint() call in bake-part-sheet.mjs . Keep it a uniform grain; see below. A gene that returns one, and a line in GrownParts.of — and the granting locus added to the every-part baseline in GrownParts.shapes , or its modifier loci look invisible to the editors. Its colour, if it has one of its own (owner, 2026-10-01: colour is its own gene per part): a short subclass of AbstractPartColourGene — a key, a name, a priority and the Words its outcomes are written in. The alleles, the two-tone rule, the per-copy hue / tone / chaos numbers and the founders come with it. GrownParts.of asks its tintsFor and dressed puts the base and tip on. Dragon horn colour is the worked example. A soft part (a beard, a ruff, feathering) samples PartSheet.HAIR , a fine lengthwise fibre grain laid down ahead of them; a hard one HORN or BONE . What is not on that list is a new render layer, a new render state field, a new config key, or a texture per colour. That was the goal. The antlers were the test of that claim, and it held (2026-10-01, in the code): no new layer, no render-state field and no config key. What they did need was two things the horn never asked for, and both landed in the shared machinery rather than beside it — a count (tines shown out of the most the mesh holds, through PartModel.Slice.shown and PartNode.group ) and passes by region (a crystal antler's see-through shafts, the bloom clumps and the glowing points are each one submit drawing only some PartSheet regions, through Slice.regions ). Plus a foal gate, as data on the kind. The antlers are also two kinds, one per side, rather than one mirrored mesh: a negative scale would turn a culled box inside out, and an asymmetric rack wants the sides to differ anyway. The dragon horns were the head-parts proof (2026-10-02): a new pair of anchors ( NAPE_RIGHT / LEFT , behind the ears), a generator and size ladder beside the ram horn’s, and the first part to take its colour from a subclass of the shared colour base. Nothing else moved. The dorsal spines were the body-parts proof (2026-10-05): the first anchor off the head, and two things no head part needed, a saddle zone and a row that scales each element on its own. Both went into the shared slice rather than beside it ( below ). Still no new layer, render-state field or config key. The rule that keeps a herd cheap Everything a part can vary by is sorted by what it costs to change , not by what it means: Topology — segment counts, branching, twist — goes in the PartShape and means baking a mesh. Quantised, and the set of shapes is therefore finite and small: PartKind.styles() × PartKind.sizeBuckets() per kind, for the whole game, forever. The bound on cost is structural rather than a budget somebody has to enforce , which is why PartMeshes needs neither eviction nor a time budget where GeneticCoatTextureFactory needs both. Proportion and pose — length, girth, lean — is a pose-stack transform. Free, continuous, and safe under a deferred draw because a pose is copied into the submit node. This is also what closes the seam the size buckets would otherwise leave: the scale lands a part on its exact epigenetic length, so nothing pops as a lineage grows through a bucket boundary. Counts — an antler's tines — sit in between. The mesh is baked with the most it can carry, numbered base to tip, and a horse shows the first k with the next one scaled by the fraction. Bounded by the mesh, so a drifted count can never draw more boxes than were baked; continuous, so a line bred for more tines grows the next one in. Colour and glow are arguments to the submit. One greyscale sheet therefore serves every colour, which is the same trick a dyed braid uses. The deferred-submit trap submitModel stores a copy of the pose and a reference to the model, and the batch renderer calls setupAnim(state) immediately before drawing each submit. So the obvious way to vary one shared mesh per horse — set visible or xScale on its parts and then submit — is a trap: the second horse in a frame overwrites the first horse’s mesh before either is drawn, and the bug is one horse wearing another’s antlers. The escape is that submitModel is generic in its state , so PartModel is a Model<PartModel.Slice> : anything that must be applied by mutation goes in setupAnim , where it runs per submit with that submit’s own slice. Three things go through it: which segment of a two-tone horn, which sheet regions a pass draws, and how many of an antler’s tines show — and it sets every field on every box every time, because the last submit of that shared mesh may have been another horse’s. (That setupAnim runs per submit and immediately before its draw is read from the 26.1.2 sources; two horses with different tine counts in one frame is the check that would show it.) Anchoring, and the root transform The layer walks the chain from the root part down, where CutieMarkLayer reaches straight for body and hand-tunes its offsets to compensate. That is not tidiness: HdHorseModel.createHdLayer applies MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F) , and that transformer rewrites the root part’s own pose — a 1.1 scale and a −2.4016 y offset — so a layer that skips the root works in a space 10% too small and two and a half units too high. Walking from the root keeps the number out of the layer entirely, and makes the same code correct for the foal, whose layer carries no such transform. Seen working, 2026-09-30. This is the claim the page rests on — that a mesh hung on an already-animated bone inherits its animation and needs none of its own — and the owner watched it hold: a unicorn's horn stayed on the head through the horse's poses in the dev yard, and sat in the right place on an adult. The computed foal offset put the horn in the right place too. So antlers, spines, crystals and wings are unblocked on this point, and the plain-layer-over-GeckoLib decision stands. After the chain, a model unit is 1 16 of a block — the same units the boxes themselves use — because each translateAndRotate divides its translation by sixteen as it goes. Never add a child to body or head in createHdBodyMesh for a part. That would move the mesh the HD coat UVs are baked against, which is the one thing on this renderer that cannot quietly shift. A separate LayerDefinition , a separate layer, always. Meshes are baked at run time Every other model in the mod is registered during EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterLayerDefinitions , which would have forced parts into a fixed set declared at startup — and that would have made a horn’s length one of five presets instead of a real number. It is not so constrained: LayerDefinition.bakeRoot() is public and pure ( mesh.getRoot().bake(xTexSize, yTexSize) , touching no registry, no event and no resource manager). Read out of the 26.1.2 sources rather than assumed; see API notes . One greyscale sheet, and why its regions are featureless part_sheet.png is 64×64, cut into a 4×4 grid of 16×16 regions, baked by node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-part-sheet.mjs . Ten regions are painted — horn and horn tip, antler bone and antler point, the bloom clumps, ram horn and ram tip, hair, a sail’s membrane, and crystal — and the rest are room for the parts that have not been written. The baker repeats every region id by value; PartGeneratorTest.theSheetBakerAgreesWithPartSheet reads it and fails if an id, a paint() call or a PAINTED entry disagrees with PartSheet . A box reads the corner of its region rather than all of it, because a cube’s UV rectangle is its own unwrap — 2*(girth+girth) wide and girth+length tall — laid down at the region’s origin, and the largest box any part generates is about 10×4 texels. That is deliberate: it means a part whose proportions change with an epigenetic number needs no new sheet. The price is that a region must look right at any sub-rectangle of itself , so the baker writes a tileable grain with a direction and no composition at all. A hand-painted or pack-sourced sheet is an upgrade path under the same constraint, and anything vendored that way needs its licence checked first — which is why the shipped default is procedural and owes nothing to anyone. What the genetic size locus does to a part Nothing, explicitly: a part inherits the horse’s render scale because that scale is already on the pose stack when the layer runs, not because the layer applies it. That is a claim from reading and not from watching , and a tiny and a huge horse are on the checks. The body parts: an anchor off the head, a saddle zone, a row Shipped with the dorsal spines , 2026-10-05. Everything here was read from the 26.1.2 sources and is unwatched : see the spines’ checks . The spine anchor PartAnchor.SPINE is the withers on the midline of the back. Its case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor walks the root, then body , not head_parts . The body’s own pose is the rear, so the row tilts with it. The vanilla body box is (-5,-8,-17) sized (10,10,22) on a pivot of (0,11,5) , and HdHorseModel.createHdBodyMesh copies it exactly. The neck’s back edge and the mane meet the top of the box at about z -10..-12 , so the anchor is body-local (0, -7.6, -11) , just inside the top. The foal’s value is the adult’s and is never drawn: no body part shows on a foal, and the switch must still answer for both models. The saddle zone A part along the back runs through where the saddle and the rider sit. The owner’s rule: unsaddled, the whole length shows; with a saddle, only the elements inside the saddle zone are hidden. The gate is a saddle that is drawn , or a rider ( EquineRenderState.isRidden , which vanilla fills from entity.isVehicle() ). It is not keyed on whether a saddle item is present. A phantom saddle (how a top-bond horse is steered bare) is present and not drawn, and its rider still sits on the back. Keying on the item would put that rider inside the spines and hide them on a bare horse nobody rides. SaddleZone.covers is the rule. Which elements is a mask of PartNode groups whose root lies in a range along the row ( SaddleZone.groupsWithin ). It is worked out once per mesh when PartMeshes bakes it, and carried per submit in PartModel.Slice.hidden , because a saddled and an unsaddled horse in one frame share the mesh (the deferred-submit trap again). The range is the vanilla saddle, EquineSaddleModel ’s box (-5,-8,-9) sized (10,9,9) inflated by 0.5: body z -9.5..0.5 . A one-unit margin each side covers a spine’s own thickness and rake. Those are named constants in AttachedPartLayer . With that range, a full row keeps one spine at the withers and two over the croup. The saddle pad is a tack slot nothing draws yet, so it cannot reach past the saddle today. The layer order is unchanged: parts before vanilla’s armour and saddle. The zone is why that still works, because the saddle is never drawn over a spine. Body armour is still drawn over whatever stands proud. A row scales each element on its own Every other part is scaled as a whole on the pose stack: (girth, stretch, girth) in the anchor’s frame. That is right for a part that grows away from its anchor. A row lies along the body, and across a row that scale is also along it: a thicker row would be a longer one, past the croup. A kind that answers PartKind.scalesPerElement() skips the pose-stack scale. The layer puts the part’s stretch and girth on the slice instead, and PartModel.setupAnim applies them to each group’s first box, about its own root, beside the count’s shrink. So every element of such a kind must be a group ( SaddleZoneTest holds the spines to one rooted chain per spine). It is also the one kind whose mesh is many trees rather than one, which PartGeneratorTest allows for that kind alone. See-through regions are data on the kind Shipped with the back sail , 2026-10-05. A see-through part is drawn in two passes split by sheet region: the regions PartKind.translucentRegions() names go through entityTranslucent at the part’s AttachedPart.opacity (times the horse’s fade), and the rest of PartSheet.SOLID is drawn solid. The base colour takes the see-through pass, the tip colour the solid one. A crystal antler names BONE , so its shafts are glass and its points solid, as before. A sail names MEMBRANE , the new sheet region, so the blended pass draws its skin and nothing else. Opacity CRYSTAL_ALPHA is a crystal antler, the copy’s number is a sail, and 0 skips the blended pass entirely: a bone sail is its bare rays. A kind with no see-through region is never translucent() , whatever its opacity says. The renderer no longer names a region, so the crystal growths were one more case in that switch and no change to it. A membrane is the first flat box : PartNode.width is its extent across x , and is the girth for every square box. PartGeneratorTest holds that only a MEMBRANE box or a plate’s slab ( below ) is flat, and nothing is thinner than a quarter unit. Two rows to a mesh, and the flank anchors Shipped with the shoulder and hip plates , 2026-10-05. PartAnchor.BODY_RIGHT and BODY_LEFT are the flanks: body-local ( 5, -7.2, -6) , the side wall under a unit below the line of the back and halfway along it. Their case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor walks root body , as SPINE does, so the plates rear with the body. Read from the 26.1.2 body box, not seen. Like the antlers they are two kinds, one per side, mirrored box for box with ox and rz negated, never with a negative scale. A side is two rows in one mesh: a cluster 7.5 units forward of the anchor (the shoulder, body z -13.5) and one 9.2 back (the hip, z 3.2), both clear of the saddle’s z -9.5..0.5. So the kind is not saddleZoned ; where a saddle flap or barding meets them is the treatment’s open look, decided once seen. It does scalesPerElement , because a whole-part scale would pull the two clusters apart along the body. Slab j of the shoulder and slab j of the hip are both group j , so the count is per cluster with no new machinery: the groups are numbered top down, and PartModel ’s count and per-group scale already act on every box whose parent is outside its group, which is both roots. A group with two roots is the one new shape, and PartGeneratorTest counts two trees per element for a plate kind. A slab is the second kind of flat box: a sheet of BONE hung from its top edge ( rz of half a turn plus the flare), so a per-element scale grows it down the flank and never up over the back. Clusters as trees, and see-through prisms Shipped with the crystal growths , 2026-10-05, the last of the four body parts, and the confirmation that the machinery was finished: no client change. PartKind.CRYSTALS sits on SPINE , is saddleZoned and scalesPerElement , and names the new CRYSTAL sheet region in translucentRegions() ; the rest is CrystalGenerator . A cluster of three to seven crystals is one tree : its central crystal is the root, upright, and the others hang off it at t = 0 , leaning away from the midline. So a cluster is one group with one root, the count and the saddle zone act on clusters as they do on spines, and the per-element scale grows a whole cluster about its centre. A crystal is a shaft, the same shaft rolled /4 (two crossed squares read as an octagonal prism) and a short solid cap on BONE_TIP . The seed picks one of eight arrangements, each a fixed-length SeededRng walk, so spread and size never move where a crystal stands. Six clusters, the ends pinned to the withers and croup, so a saddled horse keeps those two. The snout anchor, and a part that grows forward Shipped with the narwhal horn , 2026-10-05. PartAnchor.SNOUT is the middle of the front of the muzzle: head-local (0, -8.5, -6.5) , half a unit inside the muzzle box’s front face ( x -2..2, y -11..-6, z -7..-2 ). Its case walks root head_parts head , as FOREHEAD does. That is exact for the muzzle: in 26.1.2 upper_mouth and head are both PartPose.ZERO children of head_parts , and AbstractEquineModel.setupAnim animates no jaw, so a part on the head cannot be left behind by a mouth that moves. It is the one anchor whose part does not stand up from it. Every mesh grows up its own -y , so after the translate the layer turns a SNOUT part a quarter-turn about +x ( SNOUT_PITCH ), which takes -y to -z : forward along the head’s axis. The per-horse tilt applies after that, so a negative tilt lifts the point. With the neck’s rest pitch the head’s axis points below level. PartKind.NARWHAL is the horn’s generator on its own ladder ( NarwhalSize ), so the anchor and the pitch were the only new machinery. The jaw and lip anchors, and a part that hangs Shipped with the boar tusks and sabre fangs , 2026-10-08. Two more pairs of anchors on the muzzle box, both walking root head_parts head like SNOUT , both read from the 26.1.2 sources and not tuned by eye. The model has no separate lower jaw, so “the corner of the jaw” is a place on that one box. JAW_RIGHT / JAW_LEFT : head-local ( 1.8, -7.2, -4.6) , just inside the side wall, a unit above the chin, halfway along the muzzle. A boar tusk leaves it sideways and curls up beside the nose. LIP_RIGHT / LIP_LEFT : head-local ( 1.55, -7.6, -6.9) , just inside the front face, so the front half of a fang’s blade stands proud of it and shows from its root down. Neither anchor turns its part, unlike SNOUT . A fang has to hang, and the half-turn that hangs it is on the mesh’s own root box ( SabreGenerator ). A half-turn about x keeps the part on the anchor’s y axis, so the layer’s stretch and per-horse tilt still act along the blade, and the two cases are a translate and nothing else. A quarter-turn (the narwhal’s) does not keep that axis, which is why that one lives in the layer. The fang’s blade is the third kind of flat box, after the membrane and the plate’s slab. The ear, cheek and brow anchors: a fan, a row on the head, a bar on its side Shipped with the ear fins , cheek spikes and brow ridge , 2026-10-09. Five more anchors on the head, all walking root head_parts head , all read from the 26.1.2 sources and not tuned by eye. The three cases in AttachedPartLayer.anchor are a translate and nothing else. EAR_RIGHT / EAR_LEFT : head-local ( 2.9, -10.4, 3.4) on the adult, just inside the side wall and outside the ear, a unit forward of the dragon horn’s NAPE root. On the head , not the ear bone. The treatment left open whether vanilla ears animate. They do not: left_ear and right_ear are PartPose.ZERO children of head , and AbstractEquineModel.setupAnim never touches them. This is the first new anchor a foal draws at since the forehead, so it has a real foal offset, ( 2.9, -3.35, 0.7) , read from BabyHorseModel . CHEEK_RIGHT / CHEEK_LEFT : head-local ( 2.7, -8.0, 0.8) , a little inside the side wall and below the middle of the head’s depth. Where the eye is painted is the coat’s, so “midway between the eye and the mouth” is for the owner’s eyes. BROW : head-local (0, -11.0, -1.2) , on the top surface of the skull two units further down the face than FOREHEAD , so a unicorn horn stands behind the ridge. Each part needed one thing the others had not. An ear fin is a fan off one knuckle ( EarFinGenerator ): the root is a short box leaned out from upright, and every ray is its child, turned about x alone. So the lean and the fan angle are on different boxes, and the rays lie in one plane. A ray is the fourth kind of flat box. A fan’s or a frill’s rays are counted groups, numbered longest first. The cheek spikes are a row that scales per element ( PartKind.scalesPerElement ), the first on the head: a spike points sideways, across the anchor’s up, so a whole-part stretch would move the spikes apart instead of lengthening them. Each spike is one tree rooted on the anchor, numbered from the back. The brow ridge is a bar laid on its side ( BrowRidgeGenerator ): a box may be thinner across x than it is deep but never wider, so a bar wider than it is tall is a box turned a quarter round z , rooted at the right end of the brow. The part’s stretch is then its height and its girth its width across the head. What stands on the bar is turned a quarter back. The box budget is nearly spent. PartGeneratorTest.theWholeSetOfMeshesIsSmall caps every mesh the cache can ever hold, in boxes. These three parts first went over it. They were trimmed to fit rather than the cap raised: a blade and a plain ridge are one style each (they ignore their bucket), and the ear fins have two size classes, not three. The next part with more than a few dozen boxes meets the cap again, and that is the owner’s call ( Verification ). A row can be drawn shorter: span AttachedPart.span is a scale along a row, on the pose stack along its own z , from the anchor. The sail’s coverage number drives it, and it is 1 for every other part. A shorter row roots its elements nearer the withers, so a different run of them is under the saddle. PartModel.saddleGroups(span) asks SaddleZone.groupsWithin(nodes, from, to, span) , which divides the zone by the span rather than moving the mesh. The baked mask still serves every horse at span 1. Every part with bone in it has a bone version The owner’s rule (2026-10-02): a grown part that realistically has bone in it ships with a bone version — the alleles or bands that draw it as bare bone on a skeleton horse : bone-coloured, unlit, and with nothing soft on it. A wing’s bone version is the bones alone, with no membrane. It is built on the part’s own loci, never as a special case in the renderer, so a bred skeleton can lose it and an ordinary horse can carry it. procedures/add-a-gene.txt (question dd, step 10b) and write-intake-document.txt (5b) ask for it.
