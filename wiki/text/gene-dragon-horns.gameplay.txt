Dragon horns
(Magical gene / recessive / grows geometry, paints nothing)
Two copies and the horse grows a pair of horns behind its ears, swept back over the neck. Whether they curve, stand straight or curl, and how long, thick, swept and splayed they are, is written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, dragon horn colour . The first of the head parts, and the fourth grown part after the unicorn horn , antlers and ram horns .

Crossing two of them Two copies, either sex Recessive, like the unicorn horn: Drg/Drg grows the pair, and a Drg/n carrier looks like any other horse. Two carriers bred together are the reliable way to get one. It is not sex-limited — mares and stallions grow them alike, unlike the stallion-only antler and ram horn alleles. Foals grow none; the pair comes in when the horse grows up, and stays. Finding them Rare in the wild — about one horse in four hundred has a pair, and about one in ten carries a copy without showing it. No breed carries them yet. What varies Form — swept (raked back, curving along the neck), straight (an angled spike) or curled (a ram-style curl that opens outward). Length — five classes, budding to great ; budding to long in the wild, and a line bred for length reaches great and stops. Sweep — how far a swept horn bends back (from still pointing up to tipping down past the neck), how far a spike leans, how far round a curl goes. Splay — how wide the pair angle out from the skull. Girth — thicker or thinner. Both horns of a pair are the same: the numbers come from one copy. Everything stacks — a horse can wear a unicorn horn, antlers, ram’s horns and dragon horns at once, each at its own place on the head. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no attack, no hitbox. It is a look. Its gene carrot
