Horse stasis: chambers and the bank
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
A cheap way to hold horses without them costing server tick time. A stasis chamber is an item a horse goes into; a Horse Stasis Bank is a block that holds many chambers and lets you read the horses inside them. The whole point is the cheapest tier, so a server with hundreds of horses can put most of them somewhere that costs nothing, instead of every horse being a real, ticking entity forever. The whole feature is built — five chambers, and a bank that keeps them, reads them on its Browse tab, mends them from its feed and water, collects what they drop, and breeds a pair you turn out at stud. The fifth chamber is the Emergency one, which is the only one you never use: it catches a horse of yours that is about to die, by itself, while you are carrying it.

The four stasis-chamber tiers Craft a Basic Horse Stasis Chamber from a glass bottle, a wheat, a horse hair and a container of water — the water container is handed straight back, so it costs you a bucket of water and not a bucket. Right-click one of your own horses with an empty chamber and the horse goes in. Right-click the ground with a full one and it comes back out, in one piece: the same horse, with its bond, its name, its tack, its pregnancy, its cooldowns and its place in every pedigree exactly as they were. While it is in there the horse is not an entity at all. It does not tick, does not get hungry, does not grow up, cannot be hurt and costs the server nothing. That is the feature: two hundred horses you are not currently using can sit in a chest instead of standing in a field being simulated. The chamber is not spent Letting a horse out hands you the same chamber back, empty and ready for another one (owner's call, 2026-09-24). One chamber holds one horse at a time — that is what “single use” meant. You never have to re-craft a Spacer chamber to get your diamond's worth twice. A shelved horse stays on your roster , and the browser now says where it is: the whereabouts column reads in a stasis chamber rather than not loaded . That was worth fixing rather than leaving — “not loaded” means somewhere you cannot see right now , which reads as a horse you could go and fetch, and no amount of walking towards a bottle will ever load it. It also means stasis is a word the filter box finds horses by, so which of my two hundred are shelved is one search. Item Recipe Unlocks, in the bank Basic Horse Stasis Chamber Glass bottle + wheat + horse hair + a container of water, in any arrangement. Any modded water container matches, by tag. The container is not consumed. Storage only. The horse is listed by name on the Browse tab and nothing else about it can be asked for. Intermediate Horse Stasis Chamber A Basic chamber + a book. The bank can see inside. All of the horse's information becomes searchable — the Browse tab shows what it is and the filter box can find it — and the bank will feed and mend it. Both built. Advanced Horse Stasis Chamber Intermediate + a gold ingot. The bank collects what this horse drops on its own — eggs, feathers, wool and the rest of the laying alleles — into its nine-slot buffer. Built. Spacer Horse Stasis Chamber Advanced + a diamond. This horse can be turned out at stud and bred without leaving the bank, against another Spacer in the same bank. She carries the pregnancy in her chamber and comes out on her due date to foal. Built. Storage is free at every tier and always will be. What the upper tiers buy is what the bank may do with a horse. Upgrading an occupied chamber carries the horse across — you do not have to let it out first, so a Basic chamber is never a dead end. There is a fifth chamber that is not a rung on this ladder at all — the Emergency one, below, which is a Basic chamber that uses itself. Why one book buys two things Searching a horse and healing it are the same capability with two uses: the bank being able to look inside the chamber . Basic's whole contract is that it cannot, and a bank that cannot look inside cannot know that the horse is hurt, what it eats, or how much of its bar is missing. So Intermediate unlocks both, and nothing above it can search without healing or heal without searching. The Emergency Horse Stasis Chamber The fifth chamber, and the only one you never use. Take a Basic chamber, put it in the middle of a crafting grid and surround it with eight ender pearls . Then carry the result. That is the whole of it: from that moment every horse you own is insured, and the instant one of them falls below a tenth of its health the horse is in the bottle — no click, no aim, nothing to remember in the middle of a fight. It works wherever you are. The chamber does not have to be in your hand, only somewhere on you, and you do not have to be anywhere near the horse : a mare being torn apart by a pack of wolves three thousand blocks away is caught the same way, and the first you hear of it is the line in your chat saying it happened. Because nothing can hurt a horse inside a chamber, being caught is not a reprieve — it is the end of the incident. One chamber is one life If it already holds a horse and a second one of yours crosses the line, it refuses and says so — one line naming the horse it could not save. That horse takes its chances. The answer to wanting two saves is to carry two chambers (owner's call, 2026-09-24): the alternatives were a queue that goes stale the moment you log out, or swapping the healthier horse back into the fight that is killing the other one. It is not spent, and the horse is not stuck. Let the horse out and you have the same emergency chamber back, empty and armed again for the next one. In every other respect it is a Basic chamber: it files in a bank, it is counted and listed by name, and the bank cannot look inside it. A book will upgrade it with the horse still in — but that trades the emergency away, since what comes out is an ordinary Intermediate chamber. Letting the horse out and filing it properly is the cheaper move. It sits below the bolt, on purpose A hurt horse already runs for its life at a fifth of its health — it throws off its rider, drops the fight and bolts. The chamber is set at half that, so the horse always gets its own chance to escape first and is only swallowed when running did not work. Both numbers are settings ( behaviour.emergency_stasis_fraction , behaviour.escape_health_fraction ); setting the first to 0 turns the self-capture off entirely without stopping the chambers working by hand. Two things worth knowing before you rely on it. You have to be logged in — an inventory that is not in the world cannot catch anything, and neither can a bank if its owner is offline, so a horse that gets into trouble overnight is on its own. And if you are riding the horse you are set down first : you land on your feet where it was standing, and the horse goes into the bottle from under you a tick later. By then it has usually thrown you off anyway, since it bolts at twice this health. Spare chambers in the bank count too You do not have to be carrying one. An empty emergency chamber filed in a stasis bank you placed insures your horses exactly as one in your pocket does — so a rack of spare empties in the cabinet at home is worth having, and the day you ride out having forgotten to take one is not the day your insurance lapses. The one in your inventory is spent first (owner's call): the bottle on your belt is the one you bought for this moment, and the bank is the backstop. If the carried one is full and a bank has an empty, the bank's is used rather than the horse being refused . The chat line tells you which happened, because the two leave you in different positions — one of them means you are a bottle short in your pocket and the other does not. It is a bank you placed A bank is otherwise unowned — anybody who can reach it can open it, file a chamber and take one out — but spending a stranger's emergency chamber would be theft, so the block quietly remembers who put it down. A bank placed before this existed is claimed the first time somebody opens it , which is the whole of what you need to do to an old one. Owning a bank grants nothing else. It writes it down as well as saying it A chamber fires with nobody watching, by design — that is most of what you are paying eight ender pearls for. So a rescue is now a row on the browser's Log tab as well as a line in chat, under a Rescues filter of its own. The chat line is the alarm and it scrolls away; the row is the answer to “what happened to my mare” an hour later, when the horse is a bottle in a cabinet with no story attached to it. The Horse Stasis Bank Craft one from five planks in a U, an ender pearl in the middle, an iron ingot in each top corner and a horse hair between them — any wood, so a plank you already have on hand will do. Place it, right-click it, and you get two hundred and seven slots that take stasis chambers and nothing else — a double chest's six rows on screen at a time, and twenty-three rows behind them. The grid scrolls : roll the wheel over it and the rows move, with a bar down the right-hand edge saying where in the cabinet you are. That is the first of its three tabs, and it is worth having on its own: two hundred bottles in an ordinary chest are two hundred identical bottles, and this is where a stud farm's shelved horses live instead. Chambers of any tier go in, full or empty — a rack of spare empties beside the occupied ones is the obvious way to use one. The title bar counts what is in there, so a glance tells you nine horses of twelve rather than nothing at all. The Browse tab: the bank as a list of horses The second tab is the same chambers read as animals . Every occupied chamber is a line: the horse's name, what it is — Mare – Bay, tobiano — its breed and generation on the right, and the tier of the chamber it is sitting in. Four fit at a time and the list scrolls. Above it is a filter box, and it takes the same query language as the Horse Browser's My horses table (the H screen) — see the browser's own page for the full language. mare , gen>2 , gene:SB1 , -lethal , a bare word for a name or a breed; terms are ANDed and a - negates one. A bank you can ask which of these two hundred are mares carrying sabino is the reason the tab exists — and the deeper the grid got, the more it is the reason, since twenty-three rows of identical bottles is not something anybody is going to read by eye. The tier gates each line, not the tab A Basic chamber is listed — with its horse's name, because the chamber's own tooltip already tells you that and a bank that hid it would be arguing with the bottle in your hand — but it says “a basic chamber cannot be looked into” where the details go, and no search will ever return it . When a filter hides rows, the list says how many it could not reach, so a horse never just vanishes from the bank. An eye of ender is what fixes that, and it upgrades the chamber with the horse still inside. The details come from your stable's own papers rather than from the chamber, which has one visible consequence: a horse the records have nothing on — a chamber somebody else filled and gave you — is listed by name and says it has no papers, rather than being hidden or guessed at. With a filter typed, the count in the corner turns into 3 of 9 . The rows are also buttons. Clicking one turns that horse out at stud , if it is in a Spacer chamber; the line under the list says how many are, or invites you to try it, and a click on a chamber that cannot be marked says which rung can. Breaking it gives everything back, and it will not burn Every chamber drops when the bank is broken, the way a chest's contents do. It is also deliberately not flammable , unlike a chest: a chest that burns down loses its diamonds, and a bank that burns down would lose two hundred pedigreed horses to a lightning strike and drop them into the fire that did it. The Supply tab: feeding and mending what is in there The third tab is feed , water , the empties the water leaves behind, and a row of nine under them that is the bank's drop buffer . Put hay or wheat in one and a bucket of water in the other and the bank slowly heals the horses it is holding . That is the first thing it does to a horse rather than shelving one. The rule is the one horses already live by out in a field, so there is nothing new to learn: a horse heals if it is hurt, if it eats something a person can hand it, and if there is water — and healing costs it hunger, which is what eating puts back. All the bank does is supply the two things a shelved horse cannot go and find. A bucket is worth about fifty hearts of healing, so one sees off several injured horses; the feed slot takes anything a horse of that diet would eat off the ground, including another mod's horse food, and a hay bale is worth far more than a carrot. It is a slow hospital, and it gets slower the fuller it is The bank mends one horse at a time , in rotation, about a heart and a half every turn. A bank with one hurt horse in it heals that horse at roughly the rate a field would; a bank with fifty gets round to each one far less often, and a full one takes the better part of five minutes to come back round to any particular horse. That is the price of the whole feature being cheap — a bank costs the server the same whether it holds one chamber or every slot it has — and a horse in no hurry is exactly what a shelved horse is. The deep grid made that trade steeper, and it was taken on purpose. Speeding the rotation up to match would mean a full bank doing four times the work of the old one every tick, which is the one thing the feature is not allowed to do. If you have a horse that actually needs mending, it mends fastest in a bank that is not also storing two hundred others — or in a field. A bank with nothing to mend spends nothing. It will not touch the feed slot or open a bucket for a horse that is not hurt, and if every chamber in it is Basic — or empty, or holds healthy horses — it does no work at all. The Supply tab says which of those is the case rather than sitting there looking broken. The whole of that tab is the one part of the bank a hopper can reach. Feed and water going in, the empty buckets coming back out, and the drop buffer being emptied can all be piped — by a hopper, a dropper, or another mod's pipework — so keeping a stud farm's bank supplied, and collecting from it, is not a chore you do by hand. The chambers are not: nothing outside the bank can file or fetch a horse, and nothing is meant to. The drop buffer: what an Advanced chamber collects Some horses make things. The egg-layer locus gives a horse two matching copies of a laying allele and it drops that thing on the ground every few hours — an egg, a feather, wool, a slime ball, ink, string, bone or leather, depending which allele it is, and how often is written on the allele copy itself. Put such a horse in an Advanced chamber and the bank catches all of it in the nine slots under the supply row instead. It is the same clock, at the same rate, on the same genetics: a shelved layer is not a better or a worse layer than one standing in a paddock, and a horse whose egg was nearly due when you shelved it does not lose it. What changes is only that nothing ends up on the floor. A full buffer stops production rather than wasting it A horse in a field drops its egg and walks off; a bank has nowhere to drop one. So if the nine slots are full the bank does not produce at all that turn, and the horse's clock is not spent either — empty the buffer and it picks up where it left off. Nothing is ever quietly destroyed, and the tab says full, so nothing more is made rather than leaving you to work that out. Not milk. Milk cannot work this way This was planned as a “milk and drops” buffer, and the milk half is not buildable. Every milk locus in the mod — milk , potion milk , the volume gene — works by you holding a bucket out to the horse . There is no passive milking anywhere in the mod for a bank to copy, so a bank that filled itself with milk buckets would be inventing a mechanic rather than shelving one, and a milkable horse would be worth more in a box than in a field. The buffer collects what a horse does on its own ; milking is still something you do with your hands. Putting a horse to stud in the bank A Spacer chamber can be turned out at stud : open the Browse tab and click the row . The right-hand corner of the row changes from Spacer to at stud , and clicking again brings it back in. Nothing else on the row moves, and a click on any lower tier says so rather than doing nothing. A bank with a mare and a stallion at stud is a paddock. Both horses stay exactly where they are, and the bank breeds them on the ordinary rules: she is covered when she comes into heat, once per heat and not twice , and a cover that does not take waits for her next one. A stallion who has already covered three times that day is less likely to catch, exactly as he would be in a field. Both of them have to be well , too — which is the other reason the feed and water slots matter. Her cycle runs while she is shelved. It does not need her to be awake: a mare's heat is worked out from the clock, so a fortnight in a chamber is a fortnight of cycles, and she is in whatever phase she would have been in standing in a field. She comes out to foal The pregnancy is carried in the chamber — you can see it on her row in the Horse Browser like any other — and on her due date the bank lets her out , standing beside the block, and she foals within a couple of seconds. So you come back to a mare and a foal next to the bank and an empty chamber where she was. The foal is named, tamed, filed and logged exactly like any other foal, because it is any other foal: the birth is the ordinary one. It will not foal into a wall. If there is nowhere beside the bank to put her down, she stays in her chamber and the Supply tab says a mare is due — clear a space beside the bank . Nothing is lost while you do. Two things a bank breeding does not do. Breeding carrots do not reach it — a carrot is armed on a live horse and spent by a breeding you are standing next to, so a bank pairing draws like a wild cover. And there is no crowd limit : the paddock rule that stops breeding in a packed pen counts animals, and a bank holds none. How many chambers you mark is the only cap, which is the point of having to mark them. What it refuses, and why it says so A chamber is a crafted item used on purpose, so nothing here fails silently. It will not take a horse that is not yours or not tamed (the same HorseOwnership rule the stall signs, seed jar and transfer papers all use, with no creative exception), it will not take a horse with a rider on its back, and a chamber that already holds a horse says so rather than swallowing a second. Releasing refuses in one more case: if the horse this chamber names is already out in the world , which only happens if the full chamber was duplicated in creative. Two entities sharing one id would corrupt the pedigree, so the copy refuses and says why.
