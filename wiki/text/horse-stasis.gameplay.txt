Horse stasis: chambers and the bank
(Gameplay / partly built)
A cheap way to hold horses without them costing server tick time. A stasis chamber is an item a horse goes into; a Horse Stasis Bank is a block that holds many chambers and lets you read the horses inside them. The whole point is the cheapest tier, so a server with hundreds of horses can put most of them somewhere that costs nothing, instead of every horse being a real, ticking entity forever. The four chambers are built, the bank keeps them, its Browse tab reads them, and with feed and water in its supply slots it slowly mends them. What is still planned is the drop buffer and in-bank breeding.

The four stasis-chamber tiers Craft a Basic Horse Stasis Chamber from a glass bottle, a wheat, a horse hair and a container of water — the water container is handed straight back, so it costs you a bucket of water and not a bucket. Right-click one of your own horses with an empty chamber and the horse goes in. Right-click the ground with a full one and it comes back out, in one piece: the same horse, with its bond, its name, its tack, its pregnancy, its cooldowns and its place in every pedigree exactly as they were. While it is in there the horse is not an entity at all. It does not tick, does not get hungry, does not grow up, cannot be hurt and costs the server nothing. That is the feature: two hundred horses you are not currently using can sit in a chest instead of standing in a field being simulated. The chamber is not spent Letting a horse out hands you the same chamber back, empty and ready for another one (owner's call, 2026-09-24). One chamber holds one horse at a time — that is what “single use” meant. You never have to re-craft a Spacer chamber to get your diamond's worth twice. Item Recipe Unlocks, in the bank Basic Horse Stasis Chamber Glass bottle + wheat + horse hair + a container of water, in any arrangement. Any modded water container matches, by tag. The container is not consumed. Storage only. The horse is listed by name on the Browse tab and nothing else about it can be asked for. Intermediate Horse Stasis Chamber Any chamber + an eye of ender. The bank can see inside. All of the horse's information becomes searchable — the Browse tab shows what it is and the filter box can find it — and the bank will feed and mend it. Both built. Advanced Horse Stasis Chamber Intermediate + a gold ingot. The bank passively collects the horse's milk/drops into its 9-slot buffer. Spacer Horse Stasis Chamber Advanced + a diamond. The horse can be bred while still inside the bank — the other parent must also be in a Spacer chamber — and the foal pops out into the world. Storage is free at every tier and always will be. What the upper tiers buy is what the bank may do with a horse. Upgrading an occupied chamber carries the horse across — you do not have to let it out first, so a Basic chamber is never a dead end. Why one eye of ender buys two things Searching a horse and healing it are the same capability with two uses: the bank being able to look inside the chamber . Basic's whole contract is that it cannot, and a bank that cannot look inside cannot know that the horse is hurt, what it eats, or how much of its bar is missing. So Intermediate unlocks both, and nothing above it can search without healing or heal without searching. The Horse Stasis Bank Craft one from five planks in a U, an ender pearl in the middle, an iron ingot in each top corner and a horse hair between them — any wood, so a plank you already have on hand will do. Place it, right-click it, and you get a double chest's worth of slots that take stasis chambers and nothing else . That is the first of its two tabs, and it is worth having on its own: fifty-four bottles in an ordinary chest are fifty-four identical bottles, and this is where a stud farm's shelved horses live instead. Chambers of any tier go in, full or empty — a rack of spare empties beside the occupied ones is the obvious way to use one. The title bar counts what is in there, so a glance tells you nine horses of twelve rather than nothing at all. The Browse tab: the bank as a list of horses The second tab is the same chambers read as animals . Every occupied chamber is a line: the horse's name, what it is — Mare – Bay, tobiano — its breed and generation on the right, and the tier of the chamber it is sitting in. Four fit at a time and the list scrolls. Above it is a filter box, and it takes the same query language as the Horse Browser's My horses table (the H screen) — see the browser's own page for the full language. mare , gen>2 , gene:SB1 , -lethal , a bare word for a name or a breed; terms are ANDed and a - negates one. A bank you can ask which of these fifty-four are mares carrying sabino is the reason the tab exists. The tier gates each line, not the tab A Basic chamber is listed — with its horse's name, because the chamber's own tooltip already tells you that and a bank that hid it would be arguing with the bottle in your hand — but it says “a basic chamber cannot be looked into” where the details go, and no search will ever return it . When a filter hides rows, the list says how many it could not reach, so a horse never just vanishes from the bank. An eye of ender is what fixes that, and it upgrades the chamber with the horse still inside. The details come from your stable's own papers rather than from the chamber, which has one visible consequence: a horse the records have nothing on — a chamber somebody else filled and gave you — is listed by name and says it has no papers, rather than being hidden or guessed at. With a filter typed, the count in the corner turns into 3 of 9 . Breaking it gives everything back, and it will not burn Every chamber drops when the bank is broken, the way a chest's contents do. It is also deliberately not flammable , unlike a chest: a chest that burns down loses its diamonds, and a bank that burns down would lose fifty-four pedigreed horses to a lightning strike and drop them into the fire that did it. The Supply tab: feeding and mending what is in there The third tab is three slots — feed , water , and the empties the water leaves behind. Put hay or wheat in one and a bucket of water in the other and the bank slowly heals the horses it is holding . That is the first thing it does to a horse rather than shelving one. The rule is the one horses already live by out in a field, so there is nothing new to learn: a horse heals if it is hurt, if it eats something a person can hand it, and if there is water — and healing costs it hunger, which is what eating puts back. All the bank does is supply the two things a shelved horse cannot go and find. A bucket is worth about fifty hearts of healing, so one sees off several injured horses; the feed slot takes anything a horse of that diet would eat off the ground, including another mod's horse food, and a hay bale is worth far more than a carrot. It is a slow hospital, and it gets slower the fuller it is The bank mends one horse at a time , in rotation, about a heart and a half every turn. A bank with one hurt horse in it heals that horse at roughly the rate a field would; a bank with fifty gets round to each one far less often. That is the price of the whole feature being cheap — a bank costs the server the same whether it holds one chamber or fifty-four — and a horse in no hurry is exactly what a shelved horse is. A bank with nothing to mend spends nothing. It will not touch the feed slot or open a bucket for a horse that is not hurt, and if every chamber in it is Basic — or empty, or holds healthy horses — it does no work at all. The Supply tab says which of those is the case rather than sitting there looking broken. Those three slots are the one part of the bank a hopper can reach. Feed, water buckets and the empty buckets coming back out can all be piped — by a hopper, a dropper, or another mod's pipework — so keeping a stud farm's bank supplied is not a chore you do by hand. The chambers are not: nothing outside the bank can file or fetch a horse, and nothing is meant to. What the bank still cannot do is collect a horse's drops or breed one in place, which is what the two tiers above Intermediate pay for. Both are on the Roadmap tab. What it refuses, and why it says so A chamber is a crafted item used on purpose, so nothing here fails silently. It will not take a horse that is not yours or not tamed (the same HorseOwnership rule the stall signs, seed jar and transfer papers all use, with no creative exception), it will not take a horse with a rider on its back, and a chamber that already holds a horse says so rather than swallowing a second. Releasing refuses in one more case: if the horse this chamber names is already out in the world , which only happens if the full chamber was duplicated in creative. Two entities sharing one id would corrupt the pedigree, so the copy refuses and says why.
