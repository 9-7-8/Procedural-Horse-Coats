Horse care: gated healing, bond & herds
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Three non-genetic systems that apply to every horse and share one slow tick ( server/HorseCareHandler ): an invisible hunger that pays for healing beside water, a per-horse bond number that changes how a horse behaves toward its owner, and an emergent herd grouping. First slice built 2026-09-02, not play-tested — checklist in the Verification tab . This page is the single source of truth for the machinery; what is still missing is on the roadmap tab .

1. Hunger and healing Every horse has a hunger level you cannot see. It drops slowly on its own — an ordinary-sized horse is hungry after about one Minecraft day and starving after about two — and a hurt horse spends it to heal. A horse that has eaten heals faster than horses used to, and a starving horse cannot heal at all . Hunger never hurts a horse, and nothing else about the horse depends on it. Big horses get hungry sooner. The drain follows the horse’s body, so a shire empties in about two thirds of a day where a falabella takes two and a half, and a yard of draught horses wants feeding noticeably more often than a yard of ponies. It is simply proportional — twice the horse, twice the drain. Where it heals. A hurt horse heals when there is water within 3 blocks ( #horsegenetics:horse_water — water , bubble_column , water_cauldron — or any block holding water) and it is not starving: 1 health a second, 2 in a herd, and every point costs a little hunger. It no longer needs food beside it, because it goes and eats instead. No free healing. Vanilla gives every horse one point about every 45 seconds whatever state it is in. That is switched off for horses, so a starving horse really stays hurt. Donkeys, mules and llamas keep it. When it eats. Once hungry, a horse looks up to 10 blocks round for food and eats until it is nearly full. A horse that is not hungry yet but has eaten into its reserve (below three quarters full) takes a mouthful about once every two minutes: food dropped within a couple of blocks of it if there is any, otherwise the grass it is standing on. What it looks for, best first: its favourite food lying on the ground; any dropped food it will eat; cake; hay bales; crops, pumpkins, melons and sugar cane; grass; moss; mushrooms; flowers. The nearest of the best kind wins. Dropped food is judged by what it grazes, not by what it takes from a hand. An ordinary horse picks up crops, fruit, vegetables, berries, mushrooms, small flowers and cake — and the same things from other mods, so a modded grain or fruit is food without this mod having heard of it. Hand-feeding is unchanged and stays vanilla's shorter list. Eating uses food up. Cake, hay bales, crops, mushrooms and flowers are eaten whole, and grass and moss turn to dirt, as they do under a sheep. With mob griefing off the horse is still fed and the block stays. A hay bale in a pen does not last. Hand-feeding heals exactly as before, needs no water, and feeds the horse too — its favourite food most. A healing horse gives off a couple of heart particles, and heals toward its own max health. Diet changes what counts as food A horse with a narrow diet only goes for what it can eat: a wheat-eater takes hay and wheat, a vegetable-eater carrots, potatoes, beetroot, pumpkins and melons, a cake-eater cake, and none of them graze. A carnivore with nothing to eat hunts cows, pigs, sheep, chickens and rabbits and then eats what they drop, and a fish-eater hunts fish. A blood-drinker never regenerates: its bite is its meal and its heal. A horse that cannot be fed never goes looking. Not built yet Milking — the other half of the original design — is not in this slice. When it lands, its "mare must be at full health" rule is the hook back into this system. 2. Bond Bond Tier Behaviour ( BondFollowGoal ) 0–30 0 Vanilla — ignores the player 31–60 1 Turns its head to face the owner within 10 blocks; no movement 61–80 2 Paths toward the owner only if a route exists ; gives up otherwise 81–100 3 Follows at a walk, re-pathing persistently, stopping ~3 blocks away — fences and walls respected because it is real pathfinding, never a teleport Every tier needs a way to get there — a sight line is not enough. Before it looks at or walks toward its owner, the horse asks the navigator for a real path and drops the whole behaviour if there is none, so a horse shut in a pen whose owner is outside the fence behaves like any other horse: it grazes, spars, grooms and answers a mare in heat instead of standing at the rails. Two goals rather than one, because what a goal claims matters as much as what it does: tier 1 is head-turning, so it claims LOOK alone and sits below band life, while tiers 2–3 claim MOVE and sit above it. Both read the number and return false from canUse() below their tier, rather than being added and removed as the number crosses a line, and both are inert while the horse is leashed or ridden. Gains — capped at +15 per Minecraft day Riding bareback bonds exactly as well as riding saddled. This is worth saying because the obvious implementation gets it wrong: vanilla only regards a rider as the horse's controlling passenger once the horse is saddled, so the natural test ( getControllingPassenger() == owner ) quietly pays nothing for a bareback rider, and the two cases look identical from the saddle. They are not identical in what they mean. Bareback is how you talk a wild horse round in the first place, and it is the least equipment-mediated thing you can do with an animal — a system that rewards you for buying leather first has the relationship backwards. The gate is hasPassenger . Hearts show whenever the bond actually moves , and stop when it cannot. They are emitted from awardBond — the one function that knows whether a point was really granted — so every path that bonds a horse gets the feedback without asking, and a horse that has hit the daily cap goes quiet. That silence is the only signal a player has that the cap exists. A foal born to a tamed dam starts at a quarter of her bond. Starting every foal at zero made a horse born in your own stable, to your own mare, exactly as wary of you as one caught wild that morning — which is backwards. A quarter rather than all of it, because the bond is still the foal’s own to earn: this is the head start of having been born to somebody who trusts you, not an inheritance. It is written straight onto the attachment rather than through awardBond , whose job is metering against a daily cap a well-bonded dam would blow straight through. A "day" is 24 000 game ticks ( DAY_TICKS ); dayStamp = getGameTime() / 24000 , monotonic and reload-safe. bondToday resets when it rolls over. This answers the once-per-Minecraft-day rule for the bond side. Proximity already covers the common "near my owner" case. Taming by hand Crouch, hold out something the horse eats, look at it and keep still. It comes over, eats out of your hand, and each mouthful is one roll at taming — the same roll as being thrown off its back . Deliberately the same odds: this is a second door into taming, not an easier one, and each feed costs an item the way each mount costs a fall. All four conditions are re-tested every tick, so breaking the pose stops the horse mid-walk, which is what teaches the mechanic. Crouching is the gesture and the thing that stops it firing whenever somebody walks past a herd holding wheat; looking picks which horse without needing a click; and stillness is checked against where you stood when it set off, so walking at the animal ends the approach and a little shuffling does not. What counts as food is a genetic question rather than a vanilla one — a carnivore is not coming over for a carrot, and a blood-drinker is not coming over at all. Foals are left out for the same reason vanilla leaves them out of riding. See server/CrouchFeedGoal . Horses come to food — and run for their favourite Any horse drifts toward a player holding something it can eat ( server/FoodTemptGoal ), tamed or wild, foal or adult. No crouch, no gesture: hold food and horses take an interest. It is deliberately a drift — well under walking pace, it stops a couple of blocks short rather than pressing into you, and it ends the instant the food goes away, so a player who keeps walking simply leaves. A horse should be interested in food, not glued to it. Its favourite food is the exception, and it runs. Twice the notice radius and more than twice the speed, so it picks its head up from across the paddock and comes at a gallop. That gap is the point of the feature: it is the only way the preference locus is visible before you have fed it . Until now you had to already know which of the favourites a horse carried in order to try it; now the horse tells you, by reacting to one item in a way it reacts to nothing else. What counts as edible is genetic, the same test the crouch-feed goal uses — a carnivore does not come over for a carrot. A favourite overrides the diet, exactly as it does on the interaction, so a meat-eater with a taste for apples still runs at one. A ridden, leashed or led horse ignores food entirely, because all three mean somebody else is already deciding where it goes. A saddle buys you steering, and steering is what lets you go far enough for the proximity gain and the riding gain to stack for any length of time. So a saddle is worth having; it is simply not what the horse is counting — and at the top bond tier it is not even required (below). Bareback steering — what the top tier is for At behaviour tier 3 (bond 81+), a horse steers without tack. Mount it bareback and it answers the reins as though saddled; drop below the tier and it stops. That is the payoff at the top of the bond ladder, and it is the one bond reward a player can feel rather than read: everything else the tiers do is the horse moving toward you on its own. It works by lending the horse a phantom saddle for as long as the rider qualifies, because vanilla decides who may steer from inside the saddle slot and there is no other hook — server/BarebackSteeringHandler puts it on and takes it straight back off. Three consequences worth knowing: it is a real saddle while it is there, so the only thing between this and an item duplicator is the sweep that removes it ( gap 156 ) — and now also the check in a bolting horse , which takes a saddle off and must not hand that one over; a horse whose bond falls loses the ability mid-ride rather than at a tidy moment; and a horse that bolts stops steering bare too, because the lender stands down for as long as it is running. 3. Wild bands Wild horses live the way free-roaming horses really do (the science tab has the research): not in one herd with a leader, but in small bands that change over time. Nothing here has been played yet — see the verification tab . Who is who Family bands have one band stallion , his mares and their young. The band follows its lead mare — the oldest mare — while the stallion keeps to the edge. Bachelor bands are stallions without mares, following one of their own. Open a horse’s information screen and its Social section shows its role, where it stands among its band-mates, its closest companions and any rival, by name. What you will see Herding. A mare that wanders off gets driven back by her stallion, who comes round behind her with his ears pinned (angry particles over his head). Guarding. An outside stallion who comes near a band finds its stallion standing between him and the mares — and if he keeps coming, a short chase. Sparring. Colts and stallions who know each other square up, rear and shove for a few seconds until one gives way and backs off. It never does damage; it decides who yields to whom. Pushing in at food and water. A horse at a hay bale, a trough or a pond gets shouldered off by a horse that outranks it. No kicks. Mothers and foals. A foal stays near its mother, she goes to it if it strays, and she goes for anything that hurts it. Grooming partners — usually mares — now and then stand head to tail together. How bands change Growing up and leaving. A colt leaves the band he was born in a day or three after he grows up, and joins a bachelor band. A filly leaves later, three to five days on, for another family band — or with a bachelor, who then has a band of his own. Takeovers. Now and then a bachelor challenges a band stallion. It is a real fight, but it stops short: the first to fall below about 40% of his health breaks off and runs, so nobody dies of it. A winning challenger takes the band; the loser becomes a bachelor. A band outlives its stallion. If he dies, the band stays together behind its lead mare until a bachelor claims it. Mares move. Occasionally a mare drifts to a nearby band where she already knows the mares. Tamed horses keep their friendships, sparring, grooming and mothering — but never leave, get taken over or get stolen. Tamed herds Natural wild herds (2026-09-05) Wild horses spawn into a natural herd right away — a traditional or bachelor band built by server/HerdManager , not the together-timer below. Its members share a lead ( HorseCareAttachment.herd = the lead horse's UUID), a breed ( herdBreed ) and a band ( herdBand ). The together-timer herd formation here skips any horse that is inWildHerd() , so it only ever applies to tamed horses now. Each scan, a horse looks for another live horse within 10 blocks . togetherTicks accumulates +30 while company is present (capped at 2 × TICKS_TO_FORM_HERD ) and decays −30 while alone. Crossing 12 000 ticks (ten minutes) with no herd yet: the horse joins a nearby horse's herd if one has an id, otherwise it mints a new UUID and pulls in every herdless neighbour that is itself at least half-way to the threshold. togetherTicks decaying back to 0 while in a herd the horse leaves it (clears its id). Alpha is not stored. The oldest / highest-bond member is computed on demand by whatever needs it, with an explicit tiebreak, so it can't flicker. Herd comfort buff : restated as faster regen (the mod has no stamina resource). A horse in a herd heals +2 per scan instead of +1 when the water/food gate is met. 4. When one of your horses is hurt Your horses tell you when something is happening to them. Whenever a horse you own takes a hit, you get one line in chat — nobody else does — naming the horse, what hurt it, and what to do about that: What the line looks like Ravensong took 1 heart from Zombie. Something is attacking it — light and fence the pen, or move the horse. Ravensong took half a heart from sunlight. Daylight burns this one — shade, a roof or a stable by day. It runs for cover on its own if there is any. Ravensong took 3 hearts from a fall. Fence the drops around the pen. It is down to 2 hearts — a horse heals beside water, once it has eaten. It is an alarm, not a log. One line per horse every five seconds at most, so a pen that catches fire is something you can read rather than a wall of text, and it turns red when the horse is down to a quarter of its hearts. How to heal it is only added when it matters — below that same quarter. Above it, the sentence is about keeping it from happening again. You are not told what you did yourself. Hitting your own horse produces nothing; you are looking at it. You have to be in the same world as the horse. A horse hurt in the horse dimension while you are in the overworld says nothing, and neither does one hurt while you are logged out. That is what the browser's planned log tab is for, not this. A lethal genotype is not an injury. A foal dying of one says so, and says the only thing that would have prevented it: not pairing those two. Server owners who would rather have none of it can turn it off — notices.owned_horse_damage in the server config, on by default. 5. When one of them dies A horse dying is the one thing everybody on the server hears about. One line, once, naming the horse, whose it was and what killed it — grey for everyone, and red in the owner's own chat , so that on a server keeping hundreds of horses you can find your own loss in the scroll: What the line looks like Ravensong, Ixora's horse, was killed by Zombie. Ravensong, Ixora's horse, was killed by lava. Thornfoal, Ixora's horse, did not survive a genetic defect. Only a horse somebody owns. A wild horse, or one a cowboy is still keeping, dies quietly. So does a horse nobody has tamed yet, however good it was. Whoever killed it is named , player or mob. When nothing dealt the blow — lava, a fall, drowning — the line names that instead. Everybody, wherever they are. Unlike the hurt notice, this one does not care which world you are in or whether you were anywhere near it. It is news, not an alarm you can still do something about. You do not have to be online to be named in it. The owner's name comes from the server's own name cache, so a mare that dies while her owner sleeps still says whose she was. It obeys showDeathMessages . A server that turned vanilla's death messages off gets no horse ones either. Turn it off with notices.owned_horse_death in the server config, on by default. 6. When one of them is about to die A horse down to a fifth of its hearts stops taking direction and starts saving itself — with you still on it . Your saddle comes off into your pack, it drops any fight it had picked, and it runs: faster and higher than it can while anybody is steering, at a fence rather than along it, carrying you out of whatever is killing it. What you read when it happens Ravensong has bolted, and will not be steered until it is calm. Your saddle is in your pack. You stay on. It is not throwing you — it is taking over. You are a passenger until it calms down, and then the horse is yours to saddle and ride again. Getting off mid-flight is up to you. Your saddle is not lost. It goes straight into your inventory; if your inventory is full it lands on the ground beside you, and the line in chat says which. Putting it back on a horse that is still bolting does nothing but return the saddle to your pack again — the saddle is how the horse hands the reins back, so it will not wear one until it has calmed. It is faster, and it jumps higher. About a third more speed and two fifths more jump for as long as it is running. The jump is the part that matters: unboosted, the weakest horses cannot clear a fence at all, and with it almost any horse can leave a fenced pen. Its information screen honestly reads the higher figure while it is bolting. A bareback horse loses the reins too. At the top bond tier a horse steers with no tack, and that stops as well while it is bolting — the same behaviour, reached a different way. You are not handed a saddle you never owned. A fifth of its own hearts. A frail horse and a Percheron bolt at very different numbers and at the same fraction of themselves. It keeps going for about ten seconds after the last blow , and stops only once it has healed clear of the threshold — not the moment it crosses back over it, or one point of healing would leave it standing in front of the thing that was killing it. A fighter runs too. A horse with an aggression gene holds its ground against what it has targeted at full health; at two hearts it lets go and leaves. Nothing a horse can be doing outranks not dying — including being read about , which normally holds it still. A tied horse pulls. It is not exempted, so it strains the lead like any other animal that wants to be somewhere else. An empty-backed horse keeps its tack. A saddled horse nobody is riding bolts with the saddle still on it — there are no reins to take away. The fifth is behaviour.escape_health_fraction in the server config. Set it higher for horses that give up sooner, lower for horses that fight on, and 0 to turn the whole thing off. 7. And when the blow lands anyway No horse is killed by a single blow. Damage that would take a horse to zero takes it to half a heart instead, and for six seconds afterwards nothing can touch it at all — not the creeper that nearly killed it, not the lava it is standing in, not the drop it just landed from. Six seconds is what bolting needs to get clear, and a horse on half a heart is bolting already: the save and the run are one behaviour, and the window is the head start. What you read when it happens Ravensong took a killing blow and is still standing, on its last breath. Nothing can touch it for 6 seconds — get it out. It will not be saved again until it is back to full health. It is one save, not a state. When the six seconds are up the horse is as mortal as any other animal, and the next killing blow kills it. The save comes back only once the horse has healed all the way to full — which costs food, water and time, the same as any other healing . That is the whole price, and it is what stops this being immortality. You have six seconds and you should use them. The horse is on half a heart and running; your job is to get it somewhere the next hit does not come. A horse led back into the fight at the end of the window dies there. Every horse, wild or yours. A horse you have never met gets the same save, which is mostly visible as wild horses surviving falls and creepers they used to be flattened by. Donkeys and mules are in, and so are vanilla’s llamas and camels — they are the same family as far as every other part of this mod is concerned, and singling them out here would be the odd decision rather than this one. A genetic defect is still fatal. A lethal foal dies on schedule, and the half-heart a mare loses to an embryonic lethal can still be her last. So can the void, and so can /kill . The save is about surviving the world, not about surviving your own genetics. It is not a totem. Nothing is consumed and nothing has to be equipped. The particles are vanilla's totem burst because that is the effect every player already reads as that should have killed it . Four settings. behaviour.last_stand turns it off entirely (vanilla rules, a horse dies when its health reaches zero); behaviour.last_stand_health is the sliver it is left on; behaviour.last_stand_immunity_ticks is the six seconds, in ticks, and 0 keeps the save and drops the breathing space; and behaviour.last_stand_rearm_fraction is how far back it must heal before it can be saved again. Lower that last one and a horse is rescued again before it has really recovered.
