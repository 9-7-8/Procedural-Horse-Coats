Horse care: gated healing, bond & herds
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Three non-genetic systems that apply to every horse and share one slow tick ( server/HorseCareHandler ): an invisible hunger that pays for healing beside water, a per-horse bond number that changes how a horse behaves toward its owner, and an emergent herd grouping. First slice built 2026-09-02, not play-tested — checklist in the Verification tab . This page is the single source of truth for the machinery; what is still missing is on the roadmap tab .

1. Hunger and healing Every horse has a hunger level you cannot see. It drops slowly on its own — an ordinary-sized horse is hungry after about one Minecraft day and starving after about two — and a hurt horse spends it to heal. A horse that has eaten heals faster than horses used to, and a starving horse cannot heal at all . Hunger never hurts a horse, and nothing else about the horse depends on it. Big horses get hungry sooner. The drain follows the horse’s body, so a shire empties in about two thirds of a day where a falabella takes two and a half, and a yard of draught horses wants feeding noticeably more often than a yard of ponies. It is simply proportional — twice the horse, twice the drain. Where it heals. A hurt horse heals when there is water within 3 blocks ( #horsegenetics:horse_water — water , bubble_column , water_cauldron — or any block holding water) and it is not starving: 1 health a second, 2 in a herd, and every point costs a little hunger. It no longer needs food beside it, because it goes and eats instead. No free healing. Vanilla gives every horse one point about every 45 seconds whatever state it is in. That is switched off for horses, so a starving horse really stays hurt. Donkeys, mules and llamas keep it. When it eats. Once hungry, a horse looks up to 10 blocks round for food and eats until it is nearly full. A horse that is not hungry yet but has eaten into its reserve (below three quarters full) nibbles the grass it is standing on, about once every two minutes. What it looks for, best first: its favourite food lying on the ground; any dropped food it will eat; cake; hay bales; crops, pumpkins, melons and sugar cane; grass; moss; mushrooms; flowers. The nearest of the best kind wins. Eating uses food up. Cake, hay bales, crops, mushrooms and flowers are eaten whole, and grass and moss turn to dirt, as they do under a sheep. With mob griefing off the horse is still fed and the block stays. A hay bale in a pen does not last. Hand-feeding heals exactly as before, needs no water, and feeds the horse too — its favourite food most. A healing horse gives off a couple of heart particles, and heals toward its own max health. Diet changes what counts as food A horse with a narrow diet only goes for what it can eat: a wheat-eater takes hay and wheat, a vegetable-eater carrots, potatoes, beetroot, pumpkins and melons, a cake-eater cake, and none of them graze. A carnivore with nothing to eat hunts cows, pigs, sheep, chickens and rabbits and then eats what they drop, and a fish-eater hunts fish. A blood-drinker never regenerates: its bite is its meal and its heal. A horse that cannot be fed never goes looking. Not built yet Milking — the other half of the original design — is not in this slice. When it lands, its "mare must be at full health" rule is the hook back into this system. 2. Bond Bond Tier Behaviour ( BondFollowGoal ) 0–30 0 Vanilla — ignores the player 31–60 1 Turns its head to face the owner within 10 blocks; no movement 61–80 2 Paths toward the owner only if a route exists ; gives up otherwise 81–100 3 Follows at a walk, re-pathing persistently, stopping ~3 blocks away — fences and walls respected because it is real pathfinding, never a teleport Every tier needs a way to get there — a sight line is not enough. Before it looks at or walks toward its owner, the horse asks the navigator for a real path and drops the whole behaviour if there is none, so a horse shut in a pen whose owner is outside the fence behaves like any other horse: it grazes, spars, grooms and answers a mare in heat instead of standing at the rails. Two goals rather than one, because what a goal claims matters as much as what it does: tier 1 is head-turning, so it claims LOOK alone and sits below band life, while tiers 2–3 claim MOVE and sit above it. Both read the number and return false from canUse() below their tier, rather than being added and removed as the number crosses a line, and both are inert while the horse is leashed or ridden. Gains — capped at +15 per Minecraft day Riding bareback bonds exactly as well as riding saddled. This is worth saying because the obvious implementation gets it wrong: vanilla only regards a rider as the horse's controlling passenger once the horse is saddled, so the natural test ( getControllingPassenger() == owner ) quietly pays nothing for a bareback rider, and the two cases look identical from the saddle. They are not identical in what they mean. Bareback is how you talk a wild horse round in the first place, and it is the least equipment-mediated thing you can do with an animal — a system that rewards you for buying leather first has the relationship backwards. The gate is hasPassenger . Hearts show whenever the bond actually moves , and stop when it cannot. They are emitted from awardBond — the one function that knows whether a point was really granted — so every path that bonds a horse gets the feedback without asking, and a horse that has hit the daily cap goes quiet. That silence is the only signal a player has that the cap exists. A foal born to a tamed dam starts at a quarter of her bond. Starting every foal at zero made a horse born in your own stable, to your own mare, exactly as wary of you as one caught wild that morning — which is backwards. A quarter rather than all of it, because the bond is still the foal’s own to earn: this is the head start of having been born to somebody who trusts you, not an inheritance. It is written straight onto the attachment rather than through awardBond , whose job is metering against a daily cap a well-bonded dam would blow straight through. A "day" is 24 000 game ticks ( DAY_TICKS ); dayStamp = getGameTime() / 24000 , monotonic and reload-safe. bondToday resets when it rolls over. This answers the once-per-Minecraft-day rule for the bond side. Proximity already covers the common "near my owner" case. Taming by hand Crouch, hold out something the horse eats, look at it and keep still. It comes over, eats out of your hand, and each mouthful is one roll at taming — the same roll as being thrown off its back . Deliberately the same odds: this is a second door into taming, not an easier one, and each feed costs an item the way each mount costs a fall. All four conditions are re-tested every tick, so breaking the pose stops the horse mid-walk, which is what teaches the mechanic. Crouching is the gesture and the thing that stops it firing whenever somebody walks past a herd holding wheat; looking picks which horse without needing a click; and stillness is checked against where you stood when it set off, so walking at the animal ends the approach and a little shuffling does not. What counts as food is a genetic question rather than a vanilla one — a carnivore is not coming over for a carrot, and a blood-drinker is not coming over at all. Foals are left out for the same reason vanilla leaves them out of riding. See server/CrouchFeedGoal . Horses come to food — and run for their favourite Any horse drifts toward a player holding something it can eat ( server/FoodTemptGoal ), tamed or wild, foal or adult. No crouch, no gesture: hold food and horses take an interest. It is deliberately a drift — well under walking pace, it stops a couple of blocks short rather than pressing into you, and it ends the instant the food goes away, so a player who keeps walking simply leaves. A horse should be interested in food, not glued to it. Its favourite food is the exception, and it runs. Twice the notice radius and more than twice the speed, so it picks its head up from across the paddock and comes at a gallop. That gap is the point of the feature: it is the only way the preference locus is visible before you have fed it . Until now you had to already know which of the favourites a horse carried in order to try it; now the horse tells you, by reacting to one item in a way it reacts to nothing else. What counts as edible is genetic, the same test the crouch-feed goal uses — a carnivore does not come over for a carrot. A favourite overrides the diet, exactly as it does on the interaction, so a meat-eater with a taste for apples still runs at one. A ridden, leashed or led horse ignores food entirely, because all three mean somebody else is already deciding where it goes. A saddle buys you steering, and steering is what lets you go far enough for the proximity gain and the riding gain to stack for any length of time. So a saddle is worth having; it is simply not what the horse is counting — and at the top bond tier it is not even required (below). Bareback steering — what the top tier is for At behaviour tier 3 (bond 81+), a horse steers without tack. Mount it bareback and it answers the reins as though saddled; drop below the tier and it stops. That is the payoff at the top of the bond ladder, and it is the one bond reward a player can feel rather than read: everything else the tiers do is the horse moving toward you on its own. It works by lending the horse a phantom saddle for as long as the rider qualifies, because vanilla decides who may steer from inside the saddle slot and there is no other hook — server/BarebackSteeringHandler puts it on and takes it straight back off. Two consequences worth knowing: it is a real saddle while it is there, so the only thing between this and an item duplicator is the sweep that removes it ( gap 156 ), and a horse whose bond falls loses the ability mid-ride rather than at a tidy moment. 3. Wild bands Wild horses live the way free-roaming horses really do (the science tab has the research): not in one herd with a leader, but in small bands that change over time. Nothing here has been played yet — see the verification tab . Who is who Family bands have one band stallion , his mares and their young. The band follows its lead mare — the oldest mare — while the stallion keeps to the edge. Bachelor bands are stallions without mares, following one of their own. Open a horse’s information screen and its Social section shows its role, where it stands among its band-mates, its closest companions and any rival, by name. What you will see Herding. A mare that wanders off gets driven back by her stallion, who comes round behind her with his ears pinned (angry particles over his head). Guarding. An outside stallion who comes near a band finds its stallion standing between him and the mares — and if he keeps coming, a short chase. Sparring. Colts and stallions who know each other square up, rear and shove for a few seconds until one gives way and backs off. It never does damage; it decides who yields to whom. Pushing in at food and water. A horse at a hay bale, a trough or a pond gets shouldered off by a horse that outranks it. No kicks. Mothers and foals. A foal stays near its mother, she goes to it if it strays, and she goes for anything that hurts it. Grooming partners — usually mares — now and then stand head to tail together. How bands change Growing up and leaving. A colt leaves the band he was born in a day or three after he grows up, and joins a bachelor band. A filly leaves later, three to five days on, for another family band — or with a bachelor, who then has a band of his own. Takeovers. Now and then a bachelor challenges a band stallion. It is a real fight, but it stops short: the first to fall below about 40% of his health breaks off and runs, so nobody dies of it. A winning challenger takes the band; the loser becomes a bachelor. A band outlives its stallion. If he dies, the band stays together behind its lead mare until a bachelor claims it. Mares move. Occasionally a mare drifts to a nearby band where she already knows the mares. Tamed horses keep their friendships, sparring, grooming and mothering — but never leave, get taken over or get stolen. Tamed herds Natural wild herds (2026-09-05) Wild horses spawn into a natural herd right away — a traditional or bachelor band built by server/HerdManager , not the together-timer below. Its members share a lead ( HorseCareAttachment.herd = the lead horse's UUID), a breed ( herdBreed ) and a band ( herdBand ). The together-timer herd formation here skips any horse that is inWildHerd() , so it only ever applies to tamed horses now. Each scan, a horse looks for another live horse within 10 blocks . togetherTicks accumulates +30 while company is present (capped at 2 × TICKS_TO_FORM_HERD ) and decays −30 while alone. Crossing 12 000 ticks (ten minutes) with no herd yet: the horse joins a nearby horse's herd if one has an id, otherwise it mints a new UUID and pulls in every herdless neighbour that is itself at least half-way to the threshold. togetherTicks decaying back to 0 while in a herd the horse leaves it (clears its id). Alpha is not stored. The oldest / highest-bond member is computed on demand by whatever needs it, with an explicit tiebreak, so it can't flicker. Herd comfort buff : restated as faster regen (the mod has no stamina resource). A horse in a herd heals +2 per scan instead of +1 when the water/food gate is met.
