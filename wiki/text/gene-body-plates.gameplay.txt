Body plates
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One copy and the horse grows overlapping plates of bone over its shoulders and its hips, stepped down the flank like the lames of a suit of armour, the same on both sides. Whether they are smooth, ridged or spiked, and how big, how many, how tightly lapped and how flared they are, is written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, body plate colour .

Crossing two of them One copy, either sex Dominant: Plt/n and Plt/Plt both grow plates, so a plated horse bred to a plain one gives a plated foal at least half the time. It is not sex-limited. Foals grow none; the plates come in when the horse grows up, and stay. Where they sit Two clusters on each side: one over the shoulder, just behind the chest, and one over the hip. The top plate sits just under the line of the back, and each one below it laps over the next. The saddle sits between the two clusters, so a saddle or a rider hides none of them. Finding one Rare in the wild: about three horses in a thousand have plates, and because one copy is enough, every horse that carries the gene shows it. The skeleton horses can grow them in bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts. What varies Form : smooth (plain plates with a rim along the bottom), ridged (a raised rib along the middle of each) or spiked (a spike standing out from each). Size : three classes, small , broad and massive . Small to broad in the wild; a line bred for size reaches massive and stops. Count : how many plates each cluster has, from the top down, three to five in the wild. A line can be bred down to a single plate per cluster. Overlap : how far each plate laps the one below it. Spikiness : how far the plates flare out from the body, and how tall the rib or how long the spike stands. Everything stacks: a horse can wear plates, a row of dorsal spines, a sail and every head part at once. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no armour value, no hitbox. It is a look. Its gene carrot
