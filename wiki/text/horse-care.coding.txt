Horse care: gated healing, bond & herds
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Three non-genetic systems that apply to every horse and share one slow tick ( server/HorseCareHandler ): an invisible hunger that pays for healing beside water, a per-horse bond number that changes how a horse behaves toward its owner, and an emergent herd grouping. First slice built 2026-09-02, not play-tested — checklist in the Verification tab . This page is the single source of truth for the machinery; what is still missing is on the roadmap tab .

Where it lives data/HorseCareAttachment — the per-horse state ( bond , herd id, the bondToday / dayStamp daily-cap pair, a bondTicks fractional accumulator, a togetherTicks herd-formation counter). Registered as horsegenetics:horse_care in ModAttachments , copyOnDeath , serialized by its own MAP_CODEC . server/HorseCareHandler — the shared tick ( EntityTickEvent.Post , every 30 ticks , phase-staggered by tickCount + entityId ), plus the PlayerInteractEvent.EntityInteract hook for feed-bond and the EntityJoinLevelEvent hook that adds the goal. server/BondFollowGoal — the AI goal that reads the bond tier, added at priority 4 to every Horse , with its Watch (tier 1, head-turning, LOOK only) added at priority 8, below all of band life . Its ReachCheck is the shared "can I walk there?" cache — a real createPath every two seconds, not every tick. network/HorseCareSyncPayload client/ClientHorseCareCache — bond + in-herd flag pushed to trackers on change and on start-tracking, shown on the horse information screen 's Overview tab (the i button on the horse inventory). common/care/HurtNotice server/HorseHurtNoticeHandler — the owner-only hurt notice : the words and the quiet period in the game-free module, the damage hook and the owner lookup in the game one. Switched by notices.owned_horse_damage . Block tags data/horsegenetics/tags/block/horse_water.json and horse_food.json . Why the food goals cache the genotype The goal resolves the horse's diet and favourite once and keeps them. Both lookups parse the genetic code out of the record and dietOf parses the epigenome as well, and a goal's canUse runs every tick for every horse - so without the cache a paddock of thirty horses would have been doing sixty full genome parses a tick, doubled for two hands, for as long as anyone stood nearby holding anything. Both facts are fixed at birth, so resolving once is correct as well as cheap. It is lazy rather than done in the constructor because the goal is added on entity join and the record is filled on the founding tick. What counts as dropped food — and why it is not the hand-feeding test There are two questions, and they had one answer until 2026-09-24. DietFoods.accepts is what a hand may offer , and for an ordinary horse it answers false and hands the interaction back to vanilla, which means #minecraft:horse_food : wheat, sugar, a hay block, an apple, a carrot and the three golden foods. DietFoods.acceptsFromGround is what a horse picks up off the floor , and for an ordinary or eat-anything horse it is deliberately wider — the item form of every rung HungerFoodGoal.rungOf already sends it walking to: c:crops , c:foods/fruit , c:foods/vegetable , c:foods/berry , c:mushrooms , #minecraft:small_flowers , and cake; plus #minecraft:horse_food itself, and any mod's bale via HayBales . The common tags are the point of the shape: another mod's grain or fruit is food without this mod naming it, which is the case the owner hit — a heavily modded server drops almost nothing that is in vanilla's eight-item tag. A narrow diet is untouched and still answers accepts , because a wheat-eater walking past the rest is the whole point of it. The defect this closes was visible without mods: the block rungs let an ordinary horse cross a pen for a planted potato, and the item rung, asking GeneAbilityHandler.eats , made it ignore the same potato lying at its feet. That call also re-parsed the genotype per dropped item examined — see the note above — so the search now asks with the diet it resolved once. And because the ask was "food dropped near them", not "when starving", grazeInPlace takes a dropped mouthful within 2 blocks before it takes the grass, on the same one-in-1,200 roll. A horse only walks to food it can reach The owner's pasture backs onto a farm, and on 2026-09-23 every horse in it stood pressed against the dividing fence. The crops were the best rung in sight and well inside the ten-block search; the horse took the nearest one, moveTo handed it the partial path that stops at the rail, and it leaned there for the full pursuit before giving up, ignoring that one block, and starting again on the block beside it. A farm is a hundred such blocks, so the pen never ran out of turns and the shoving never stopped. HungerFoodGoal.search now asks createPath / canReach before it commits to a candidate — the same question BondFollowGoal.ReachCheck asks of an owner behind a door , at vanilla's own moveTo accuracy of 1. That number is the whole trick: a manhattan block still reaches a hay bale the horse stands beside, or the grass block under its feet, and is two short of a crop on the far side of a fence. The check alone would have swapped pushing for starving. CROP outranks GRASS , so a horse in a grass pen beside a fenced farm would reject crops every search and never fall through to what it was standing in. The block scan therefore keeps the nearest candidate of every rung rather than one global best, and walks them best-first until one is reachable; an unreachable farm quietly demotes the horse to its own grass. A rejected candidate is ignored for the usual minute, so the next search does not simply pick its neighbour, and a search spends at most three path tests — fewer than the repaths the pursuit it replaces was already spending every twenty ticks. tick drops a pursuit whose path stops short as well, for the gate that shuts behind a horse that set off while it was open. …and only eats what it can get its mouth to That was the pathing half. The other half of the same report — horses are eating crops through a fence or wall — survived it, and the reason is worth following, because the first fix is exactly what lets the second bug happen. A farm across a fence is usually reachable. There is a gate somewhere, so canReach says yes and the horse commits to the crop. But the eating radius is 2.5 blocks, plain centre-to-centre distance, and a horse that was already stood at the rail is inside it on the first tick. It ate the crop through the fence instead of walking the long way round. tick now asks HungerFoodGoal.exposed of a block, and vanilla's own hasLineOfSight of a dropped item or of hunted prey, before the mouthful. A blocked target is not given up on: the horse simply keeps walking, and the way round is the one search already proved exists, so the natural outcome is a horse that walks to the gate. If it never gets there the ordinary pursuit timeout ends it and the block goes on the ignore list like any other dead end. grazeInPlace 's dropped mouthful asks the same question, since two blocks away through a fence is not “at its feet” either. Where the ray is aimed is the whole of the difficulty. The obvious test — eye to the centre of the block — is wrong for precisely the rung a horse eats most. A grass block sits below the ground the horse is walking on, so a ray to the middle of one five blocks away dives through the turf in between and reports a barrier every single time; grazing would have stopped altogether. exposed therefore aims at the nearest point of the block's box to the horse's eye, clamped axis by axis, which for a grass block is the top edge facing the horse — the part it actually puts its head on — and the ray to it skims the surface instead of burrowing under it. What that predicate stops is set by collision height, not by what the block is called. A fence and a cobble wall both collide to a block and a half, above a grazing horse's eye line, so both stop the ray. A one-block wall deliberately does not : a horse can put its head over that, and can jump it, so the pathfinder was going to walk it there anyway and refusing the mouthful would only look broken. The hurt notice — whose chat, how often, and why sunlight is a special case common/care/HurtNotice is the words and the rules; it is in the game-free module for repro/ReproText 's reason — the wording is the feature, so a test should be able to pin it down ( HurtNoticeTest ). It groups vanilla's damage types into a Cause by the advice each earns rather than by what they are: a campfire, a hot floor and a burning horse are three damage types and one thing to do about it. Each cause carries one prevention sentence. What to do about the injury itself does not vary by cause — it is water and a fed horse every time — so it is appended once, and only below BADLY_HURT (a quarter of the horse's own maximum). A line on every scratch that ended "it heals beside water" would train the owner out of reading the half that changes. server/HorseHurtNoticeHandler is the translation, on LivingDamageEvent.Post — the same hook DebugWorldWatch logs damage from, and the one that reports the damage actually taken after armour rather than the amount aimed at the horse. It answers the entity question before the config one, because it fires for every hit anything in the world takes. The owner, via getOwner() , and only if that returns a ServerPlayer . That method answers only for an owner loaded in the same level, which is exactly this message's audience: a line in the chat log of somebody who logged out an hour ago is not an alarm. The offline and cross-dimension cases are the browser's Log tab , deliberately not this — a death now lands there regardless, and this line stays an alarm for whoever is in earshot. Never when the damage source's entity is the owner. A player who hits their own horse is looking at it — and in the test yard the line would be constant. One line per horse per QUIET_TICKS (five seconds), keyed on the horse rather than on the cause: a burning horse takes damage every two seconds and a horse among zombies several times a second. Five seconds because the message is an alarm the owner is meant to be able to answer, and no shorter because the second line about the same fire says nothing the first did not. The map is a HashMap cleared on LivingDeathEvent and swept of five-minute-old entries whenever it passes 512 horses. Sunlight is told apart from fire by whether the horse is alight. SunSensitivityHandler burns a sun-sensitive horse with vanilla's onFire damage and never ignites it, so isOnFire() is false exactly in the sun case — and HurtNotice.of can never return SUNLIGHT from a damage type alone, which is what the test asserts. Giving the burn its own damage type would have been cleaner and would also have moved it out of the fire-resistance and fire-immunity tags, so it was not done. AbstractHorse , not Horse — a donkey or a mule is somebody's animal too. The name falls back from the tag, to this mod's record, to whatever vanilla calls the species. Damage type ids are matched, not verified (hard rule 8). They are vanilla's own strings and an unrecognised one is simply "something", so a wrong id costs a sentence of advice and never correctness — which is also what makes another mod's damage safe to be hit by. Naming the horse and reading the cause are server/HorseNotices , shared with the death notice below so that the line about a burning horse and the line about the horse that burned to death cannot drift apart. The death notice — why it is not vanilla's sentence Vanilla says nothing at all when a horse dies , not even to its owner. The block that tells an owner their pet died is in TamableAnimal.die , and AbstractHorse extends Animal and merely implements OwnableEntity — so there is no vanilla line here to duplicate, to suppress, or to be beaten to. That answers the question this bullet was filed with: the dedup problem is not against vanilla, it is against the event firing twice. CombatTracker.getDeathMessage() was the obvious thing to broadcast and is not used either, because it names the entity . A horse of this mod carries its name in its record rather than on a name tag, so vanilla's sentence would announce "Horse was slain by Zombie" about a named mare with a pedigree and an owner — and the name, the owner and the cause are the entire content of the message. So the sentence is common/care/DeathNotice , beside HurtNotice and for the same reason, with DeathNoticeTest on it. It reuses HurtNotice.Cause and drops the advice. One table of damage ids for both messages; but a dead horse is not the moment to be told to fence the drops, so only phrase() is read. The one cause that changes the verb is GENETIC_DEFECT — "did not survive a genetic defect", matching the mod's own damage type in lang, because a lethal genotype is not something that killed the foal. The owner's name, online or not , through HorseOwnership.ownerName (widened to AbstractHorse for this) and so through the server's name caches. A name neither cache has seen degrades to "somebody's horse" rather than losing the clause. Everyone gets the line; the owner's copy is red. Which means a loop over getPlayerList().getPlayers() comparing UUIDs rather than broadcastSystemMessage , since the two styles differ. The owner is matched by getOwnerReference() , not getOwner() , so an owner in another dimension or offline is still the owner for naming purposes. Once. LivingDeathEvent fires from LivingEntity.die before vanilla's own already-dead guard, so a second die() in the same tick would say it twice. ANNOUNCED is a HashMap of horse to tick with the hurt notice's sweep. The showDeathMessages game rule is checked , in vanilla's own order of business: a server that turned death messages off has said what it wants of its chat, and a horse is not an exception to that. Bolting at low health — why it is not vanilla's panic with a health check AbstractHorse already registers PanicGoal ( swapped for HorsePanicGoal by HorseAggroHandler ) and it already runs from what hits it, so the first shape this took was a health term inside HorsePanicGoal.holdGround . That is wrong in all three of the things that were actually asked for. Panic never runs while the horse is ridden , because a mounted horse's movement belongs to its passenger and being startled is no reason to take somebody's tack off; it holds ground whenever the horse has a target, which is right for a gladiator at full health and suicidal at two hearts; and it is not gated on health at all, so it is the same behaviour for a scratch as for a mortal wound. The mortal-wound case is its own goal, server/HorseEscapeGoal , sitting above panic. The arithmetic is common/care/Escape with EscapeTest on it, for HurtNotice 's reason: almost every decision in the owner's sentence is a threshold, a duration or a boost, and a threshold with hysteresis is exactly the kind of thing that is wrong in a way nobody notices from inside a running game. RECOVERY_MARGIN is why: without it a horse that heals one point stops dead in front of what was killing it and starts again on the next hit, so what it takes to stop is deliberately more than what it took to start. THREAT_LINGERS_TICKS is the other half — damage is instantaneous and flight is not, and a horse that stops the tick the zombie stops swinging is standing next to a zombie. The line the rider reads is in there too, with the other sentences a horse says about itself: a player whose horse has silently stopped answering the controls reads that as the mod breaking rather than as the horse deciding, and the wording is the feature. Priority 0, added before InspectHoldGoal . Nothing outranks not dying, so it goes above panic and the shade goal at 1 and everything under them. InspectHoldGoal shares priority 0 and Flag.MOVE ; neither can interrupt the other, and the goal selector breaks a priority tie by insertion order, so adding this first is what stops an open information screen pinning a bleeding animal in place. The generated hierarchy reports the pair as a conflict, correctly. It claims MOVE and not JUMP , and it does jump. Vanilla's FloatGoal is also at priority 0 and holds JUMP , so claiming the flag would mean a horse swimming could not start this goal at all — the exact case, being shot at in deep water, where it most needs to move. Vanilla's own PanicGoal claims MOVE alone for the same reason and leaves jumping to the move control. Flagged in the class, because bake-behaviour-hierarchy.mjs reads the setFlags call and the page will therefore say MOVE . Taking the saddle off is the mechanism. The first version of this ejected the rider, which was the worse trade — the owner's amendment is that the horse should carry them out instead. One act does both halves of that, because vanilla keeps both answers in the saddle slot: AbstractHorse.getControllingPassenger() hands control to a player passenger only if isSaddled() , and AbstractHorse.isImmobile() stops the mob's own AI moving a horse only while it is ridden and saddled. So with the saddle gone the rider is a passenger, the navigation moves the animal, and nothing about rider input needs suppressing and no mixin needs writing. Called again from tick , because a rider can re-saddle a horse they are sitting on and a saddle in the slot is the reins back in their hands; only start says anything, or re-saddling would cost a line of chat a tick. Where the saddle goes is ModNetworking.handleTackSlot 's idiom, so a saddle taken off a horse lands in one place whichever way it came off: into rider.getInventory().add , and rider.drop when that fails. An unridden horse keeps its tack — removing it is about who is steering, and a bolting horse shedding gear across a paddock would be item churn for no gameplay. The bareback interaction is the sharp edge, and it cuts twice. Bareback steering is implemented by lending the horse a real saddle carrying ModDataComponents.PHANTOM_SADDLE , re-lent every tick. So (1) a phantom saddle must never reach the rider's inventory, because handing one over mints a free saddle — the same hole reclaim exists to close — so the stack is checked for the component and a phantom is destroyed rather than given; and (2) BarebackSteeringHandler.eligibleRider now returns null while HorseEscapeGoal.bolting holds, or the lender puts the reins straight back on the next tick. That second one would have been a bug of the worst shape available : the escape behaviour working in every test and failing only on the best-bonded horse on the server, which is the only kind anybody rides bare. bolting is a scan of the goal selector rather than a flag on the horse, so there is no state to leak when a bolting horse is unloaded or killed mid-flight, and it is asked last, after the cheap eligibility tests have ruled out almost every animal. The boosts are transient attribute modifiers under escape/ , multiplied against the horse's own total so a boost is a proportion of that animal rather than a flat amount that would be everything to a Falabella and nothing to a Shire. The prefix matters: GeneAbilityHandler.clearAttributes sweeps only ids under gene/ (plus its own lava float), which is the fix for the pregnant mare who lost repro/late_pregnancy to another system's tick — so anything outside gene/ is left alone and must remove itself, which stop does. Escape.JUMP_BOOST is the number that decides whether "jump fences" is true at all: a fence wants about 0.5 of jump strength and the weakest horses sit at 0.4. Moving JUMP_STRENGTH rather than the jump impulse is deliberate — HorseInstantJumpMixin refuses to touch the impulse precisely because the information screen's metres are read off the attribute, so boosting the attribute keeps that figure honest while the horse really is jumping higher. Fences are not pathfound, they are jumped at. A fence is BLOCKED to a ground navigator, so no path over one is ever produced and moveTo simply refuses. SunShadeGoal met this first and this does the same honest thing: on a refusal, drive getMoveControl().setWantedPosition straight at the escape point instead, and press getJumpControl().jump() while horizontalCollision holds. Jump strength is a real stat with a real range, so whether a horse clears the rail is a fact about the horse. An approximation of intent, not a pathfinder. What it runs away from. The attacker's position when getLastHurtByMob() answers, and otherwise the square it was standing on when the bolt began — which is the right answer for lava, a cactus or powder snow, where nothing is chasing it and the place itself is the problem. When even that is where it still stands, it runs along its own view vector, because a horse in a lava pool has no wrong direction. It drops the fight. setTarget(null) on start, so a horse with an aggression locus is not held next to what it was swinging at by a melee goal it has stopped being able to feed. Leashed and tamed are not exemptions. A tied horse strains the lead, which is what "whatever it can" means; a wild one bolts on the same rule as an owned one. The one thing that turns it off is behaviour.escape_health_fraction = 0 . Surviving a killing blow — two events, because they are two different refusals common/care/LastStand holds the arithmetic ( LastStandTest covers it); server/HorseLastStandHandler is the translation. The per-horse state is a single stamp — the game tick of the last save — kept under the key last_stand in the existing HorseCooldownsAttachment , which was already serialized, already copyOnDeath , and already the place this mod records "when did this last happen to this horse". It gained a clear for the purpose, since this is the first gate re-opened by something happening rather than by time passing. The window is a cancel; the save is a rewrite. Immunity is LivingIncomingDamageEvent.setCanceled(true) — the shape a gene's fire immunity already uses, and the only one that means no damage happened rather than zero damage happened . The save itself is LivingDamageEvent.Pre , because that is the first point at which the number is the one that will really come off the health: armour and enchantments are out of it by then, so a horse in barding is not judged on a blow it was never going to take in full. Setting the health inside Pre is safe, and this is why. LivingEntity.actuallyHurt reads the damage container back after the event and only subtracts when it is non-zero, so setNewDamage(0) plus a bare setHealth(…) leaves the horse on exactly that health at the end of the tick. It is also what vanilla's own totem does — checkTotemDeathProtection is a bare setHealth(1.0F) . Everything else about the hit still lands: hurt animation, hurt sound, knockback, and LivingDamageEvent.Post , so nothing that watches a horse being damaged goes quiet. The save and the bolt are one behaviour by arithmetic, not by wiring. resolveMobResponsibleForDamage runs after actuallyHurt , so a saved horse still gets its lastHurtByMob and its hurtTime — which is exactly what HorseEscapeGoal.inDanger reads. A horse on half a heart therefore bolts on the same tick it is saved, and Escape.THREAT_LINGERS_TICKS (200) outlasts LastStand.DEFAULT_IMMUNITY_TICKS (120) on purpose: the running does not stop before the immunity does. Re-arming is read off the health, not ticked. "Healed back to full" is a fact about the horse, so it is evaluated in the incoming-damage handler, which is the one place guaranteed to run whenever the answer could matter. It costs nothing while nothing is attacking, cannot go stale in an unloaded chunk, and cannot miss the heal that finished the job — the same reasoning as creditBondTiers . There is no tick and no countdown anywhere in this feature. Two damage sources are exempt, in both directions. horsegenetics:genetic_defect has to be: LethalFoalHandler is tuned to out-damage the care handler's regeneration, so a foal rescued to half a heart every six seconds would be an immortal lethal foal, and the half-heart a mare loses to an embryonic lethal is deliberately allowed to be her last. Everything in minecraft:bypasses_invulnerability is the other — the void, /kill , and the generic kill LycanthropyHandler falls back to when a were-animal dies of something its horse body shrugs off. That fallback was written for gene immunities and covers this one unchanged. AbstractHorse , not Horse . Matching the hurt and death notices and the care handler’s own herd scan rather than the goal handlers: a rule about not dying should not depend on having been born here. It is a wider family than it sounds — donkeys and mules, which this mod breeds, but also llamas and camels, which it does not. Left that way because AbstractHorse is this mod’s consistent answer to "what is a horse", but it is a call worth an owner’s eye. The chat line is not behind notices.owned_horse_damage . That switch exists to stop a pen on fire becoming a wall of text; this is the opposite kind of line, and it cannot repeat without the player healing a horse to full in between. A horse that walks out of an explosion on half a heart with nothing said reads as the mod failing to kill it — the lethal foal's "the chat line is the feature", pointed the other way. Setting bond for testing — /bond /bond <0-100> sets the bond of the horse you are riding , or failing that the one you are looking at within 12 blocks; /bond on its own reports the number, the behaviour tier and whether the horse is in a herd. Dev runs only — it is registered behind the same FMLEnvironment.isProduction() guard as the test kit , in server/DebugTestWorldHandler . It exists because every real source above is deliberately slow: a daily cap of 15 and a point per real minute of proximity means reaching tier 3 honestly is about an hour of standing about. Before this, exercising a tier meant grinding it or temporarily lowering the thresholds and rebuilding — and the second changes the thing under test. It is not a back door. It writes through the same HorseCareHandler.syncCare every other bond source ends at, so the client is told and the bond progress tasks are credited exactly as if the horse had earned it. A command that set the number without crediting would leave the progress surface lying, and the progress surface is one of the things being tested with it. Performance One handler, 30-tick interval, staggered by entity id. Per scan: one entity lookup (herd radius) always; the ~7×7×7 block scan only when the horse is hurt. The attachment is written back only when a field actually changed, and the sync packet only when bond or in-herd changed. Band life common/herd/ — the model, free of Minecraft and unit-tested ( HerdModelTest ). Relationship is one horse’s view of another: familiarity, rank (“who yields”, −1..1), grooming bond and rivalry. SocialLedger is a capped set of them with growth, decay and contests; HerdRules holds every rate, written per Minecraft day and converted to per-scan odds by perScan , plus contest odds, dispersal timing and the lead-mare choice; BandRole is the label the Social section shows. data/HorseSocialAttachment — birth tick (game time, so sleep does not age a horse), dam, natal band, dispersal day and the ledger. copyOnDeath like the care attachment. server/HerdSocialHandler — the social tick, every SCAN ticks: relationship bookkeeping for every horse, and for a wild band member the lead-mare cache, bachelor-band succession, and adopting the band of the horse it knows best when its own is gone. It also builds the Social summary, sent with the inspect lease ( HorseSocialSyncPayload ). server/BandLife — dispersal, takeovers and mare transfers. A takeover is a registered pair; a LivingIncomingDamageEvent hook at high priority cancels the blow that would take either fighter under HerdRules.FIGHT_YIELD_HEALTH and ends the fight there, and HorseAggroHandler skips a registered fight so the band does not pile in. Fights and bouts are transient: a restart ends them. server/HerdGoals — Spar (3), DamAndFoal (4), StallionGuard (5), Displace (5) and GroomAndRest (7), on every horse; each throttles its own canUse scan. WildHerdGoal now follows the lead mare, and no longer promotes a member when the lead is missing — that promotion is what used to split a band into herds of one. Every number is a first guess The rates in HerdRules are chosen to make things happen on the pace the owner asked for (a Minecraft day), not measured against play. Displacement ignores plain grass — it is in the food tag, and every field would otherwise be a spot worth fighting over.
