Horse care: gated healing, bond & herds
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Three non-genetic systems that apply to every horse and share one slow tick ( server/HorseCareHandler ): an invisible hunger that pays for healing beside water, a per-horse bond number that changes how a horse behaves toward its owner, and an emergent herd grouping. First slice built 2026-09-02, not play-tested — checklist in the Verification tab . This page is the single source of truth for the machinery; what is still missing is on the roadmap tab .

Where it lives data/HorseCareAttachment — the per-horse state ( bond , herd id, the bondToday / dayStamp daily-cap pair, a bondTicks fractional accumulator, a togetherTicks herd-formation counter). Registered as horsegenetics:horse_care in ModAttachments , copyOnDeath , serialized by its own MAP_CODEC . server/HorseCareHandler — the shared tick ( EntityTickEvent.Post , every 30 ticks , phase-staggered by tickCount + entityId ), plus the PlayerInteractEvent.EntityInteract hook for feed-bond and the EntityJoinLevelEvent hook that adds the goal. server/BondFollowGoal — the AI goal that reads the bond tier, added at priority 4 to every Horse , with its Watch (tier 1, head-turning, LOOK only) added at priority 8, below all of band life . Its ReachCheck is the shared "can I walk there?" cache — a real createPath every two seconds, not every tick. network/HorseCareSyncPayload client/ClientHorseCareCache — bond + in-herd flag pushed to trackers on change and on start-tracking, shown on the horse information screen 's Overview tab (the i button on the horse inventory). common/care/HurtNotice server/HorseHurtNoticeHandler — the owner-only hurt notice : the words and the quiet period in the game-free module, the damage hook and the owner lookup in the game one. Switched by notices.owned_horse_damage . Block tags data/horsegenetics/tags/block/horse_water.json and horse_food.json . Why the food goals cache the genotype The goal resolves the horse's diet and favourite once and keeps them. Both lookups parse the genetic code out of the record and dietOf parses the epigenome as well, and a goal's canUse runs every tick for every horse - so without the cache a paddock of thirty horses would have been doing sixty full genome parses a tick, doubled for two hands, for as long as anyone stood nearby holding anything. Both facts are fixed at birth, so resolving once is correct as well as cheap. It is lazy rather than done in the constructor because the goal is added on entity join and the record is filled on the founding tick. What counts as dropped food — and why it is not the hand-feeding test There are two questions, and they had one answer until 2026-09-24. DietFoods.accepts is what a hand may offer , and for an ordinary horse it answers false and hands the interaction back to vanilla, which means #minecraft:horse_food : wheat, sugar, a hay block, an apple, a carrot and the three golden foods. DietFoods.acceptsFromGround is what a horse picks up off the floor , and for an ordinary or eat-anything horse it is deliberately wider — the item form of every rung HungerFoodGoal.rungOf already sends it walking to: c:crops , c:foods/fruit , c:foods/vegetable , c:foods/berry , c:mushrooms , #minecraft:small_flowers , and cake; plus #minecraft:horse_food itself, and any mod's bale via HayBales . The common tags are the point of the shape: another mod's grain or fruit is food without this mod naming it, which is the case the owner hit — a heavily modded server drops almost nothing that is in vanilla's eight-item tag. A narrow diet is untouched and still answers accepts , because a wheat-eater walking past the rest is the whole point of it. The defect this closes was visible without mods: the block rungs let an ordinary horse cross a pen for a planted potato, and the item rung, asking GeneAbilityHandler.eats , made it ignore the same potato lying at its feet. That call also re-parsed the genotype per dropped item examined — see the note above — so the search now asks with the diet it resolved once. And because the ask was "food dropped near them", not "when starving", grazeInPlace takes a dropped mouthful within 2 blocks before it takes the grass, on the same one-in-1,200 roll. A horse only walks to food it can reach The owner's pasture backs onto a farm, and on 2026-09-23 every horse in it stood pressed against the dividing fence. The crops were the best rung in sight and well inside the ten-block search; the horse took the nearest one, moveTo handed it the partial path that stops at the rail, and it leaned there for the full pursuit before giving up, ignoring that one block, and starting again on the block beside it. A farm is a hundred such blocks, so the pen never ran out of turns and the shoving never stopped. HungerFoodGoal.search now asks createPath / canReach before it commits to a candidate — the same question BondFollowGoal.ReachCheck asks of an owner behind a door , at vanilla's own moveTo accuracy of 1. That number is the whole trick: a manhattan block still reaches a hay bale the horse stands beside, or the grass block under its feet, and is two short of a crop on the far side of a fence. The check alone would have swapped pushing for starving. CROP outranks GRASS , so a horse in a grass pen beside a fenced farm would reject crops every search and never fall through to what it was standing in. The block scan therefore keeps the nearest candidate of every rung rather than one global best, and walks them best-first until one is reachable; an unreachable farm quietly demotes the horse to its own grass. A rejected candidate is ignored for the usual minute, so the next search does not simply pick its neighbour, and a search spends at most three path tests — fewer than the repaths the pursuit it replaces was already spending every twenty ticks. tick drops a pursuit whose path stops short as well, for the gate that shuts behind a horse that set off while it was open. This is the pathing half of the pasture-edge report only. A horse that is beside the fence for some other reason can still eat a crop through it : the goal's eating radius is 2.5 blocks and does not ask what is in between, which is a separate bullet. The hurt notice — whose chat, how often, and why sunlight is a special case common/care/HurtNotice is the words and the rules; it is in the game-free module for repro/ReproText 's reason — the wording is the feature, so a test should be able to pin it down ( HurtNoticeTest ). It groups vanilla's damage types into a Cause by the advice each earns rather than by what they are: a campfire, a hot floor and a burning horse are three damage types and one thing to do about it. Each cause carries one prevention sentence. What to do about the injury itself does not vary by cause — it is water and a fed horse every time — so it is appended once, and only below BADLY_HURT (a quarter of the horse's own maximum). A line on every scratch that ended "it heals beside water" would train the owner out of reading the half that changes. server/HorseHurtNoticeHandler is the translation, on LivingDamageEvent.Post — the same hook DebugWorldWatch logs damage from, and the one that reports the damage actually taken after armour rather than the amount aimed at the horse. It answers the entity question before the config one, because it fires for every hit anything in the world takes. The owner, via getOwner() , and only if that returns a ServerPlayer . That method answers only for an owner loaded in the same level, which is exactly this message's audience: a line in the chat log of somebody who logged out an hour ago is not an alarm. The offline and cross-dimension cases are the browser's planned log tab , deliberately not this. Never when the damage source's entity is the owner. A player who hits their own horse is looking at it — and in the test yard the line would be constant. One line per horse per QUIET_TICKS (five seconds), keyed on the horse rather than on the cause: a burning horse takes damage every two seconds and a horse among zombies several times a second. Five seconds because the message is an alarm the owner is meant to be able to answer, and no shorter because the second line about the same fire says nothing the first did not. The map is a HashMap cleared on LivingDeathEvent and swept of five-minute-old entries whenever it passes 512 horses. Sunlight is told apart from fire by whether the horse is alight. SunSensitivityHandler burns a sun-sensitive horse with vanilla's onFire damage and never ignites it, so isOnFire() is false exactly in the sun case — and HurtNotice.of can never return SUNLIGHT from a damage type alone, which is what the test asserts. Giving the burn its own damage type would have been cleaner and would also have moved it out of the fire-resistance and fire-immunity tags, so it was not done. AbstractHorse , not Horse — a donkey or a mule is somebody's animal too. The name falls back from the tag, to this mod's record, to whatever vanilla calls the species. Damage type ids are matched, not verified (hard rule 8). They are vanilla's own strings and an unrecognised one is simply "something", so a wrong id costs a sentence of advice and never correctness — which is also what makes another mod's damage safe to be hit by. Naming the horse and reading the cause are server/HorseNotices , shared with the death notice below so that the line about a burning horse and the line about the horse that burned to death cannot drift apart. The death notice — why it is not vanilla's sentence Vanilla says nothing at all when a horse dies , not even to its owner. The block that tells an owner their pet died is in TamableAnimal.die , and AbstractHorse extends Animal and merely implements OwnableEntity — so there is no vanilla line here to duplicate, to suppress, or to be beaten to. That answers the question this bullet was filed with: the dedup problem is not against vanilla, it is against the event firing twice. CombatTracker.getDeathMessage() was the obvious thing to broadcast and is not used either, because it names the entity . A horse of this mod carries its name in its record rather than on a name tag, so vanilla's sentence would announce "Horse was slain by Zombie" about a named mare with a pedigree and an owner — and the name, the owner and the cause are the entire content of the message. So the sentence is common/care/DeathNotice , beside HurtNotice and for the same reason, with DeathNoticeTest on it. It reuses HurtNotice.Cause and drops the advice. One table of damage ids for both messages; but a dead horse is not the moment to be told to fence the drops, so only phrase() is read. The one cause that changes the verb is GENETIC_DEFECT — "did not survive a genetic defect", matching the mod's own damage type in lang, because a lethal genotype is not something that killed the foal. The owner's name, online or not , through HorseOwnership.ownerName (widened to AbstractHorse for this) and so through the server's name caches. A name neither cache has seen degrades to "somebody's horse" rather than losing the clause. Everyone gets the line; the owner's copy is red. Which means a loop over getPlayerList().getPlayers() comparing UUIDs rather than broadcastSystemMessage , since the two styles differ. The owner is matched by getOwnerReference() , not getOwner() , so an owner in another dimension or offline is still the owner for naming purposes. Once. LivingDeathEvent fires from LivingEntity.die before vanilla's own already-dead guard, so a second die() in the same tick would say it twice. ANNOUNCED is a HashMap of horse to tick with the hurt notice's sweep. The showDeathMessages game rule is checked , in vanilla's own order of business: a server that turned death messages off has said what it wants of its chat, and a horse is not an exception to that. Setting bond for testing — /bond /bond <0-100> sets the bond of the horse you are riding , or failing that the one you are looking at within 12 blocks; /bond on its own reports the number, the behaviour tier and whether the horse is in a herd. Dev runs only — it is registered behind the same FMLEnvironment.isProduction() guard as the test kit , in server/DebugTestWorldHandler . It exists because every real source above is deliberately slow: a daily cap of 15 and a point per real minute of proximity means reaching tier 3 honestly is about an hour of standing about. Before this, exercising a tier meant grinding it or temporarily lowering the thresholds and rebuilding — and the second changes the thing under test. It is not a back door. It writes through the same HorseCareHandler.syncCare every other bond source ends at, so the client is told and the bond progress tasks are credited exactly as if the horse had earned it. A command that set the number without crediting would leave the progress surface lying, and the progress surface is one of the things being tested with it. Performance One handler, 30-tick interval, staggered by entity id. Per scan: one entity lookup (herd radius) always; the ~7×7×7 block scan only when the horse is hurt. The attachment is written back only when a field actually changed, and the sync packet only when bond or in-herd changed. Band life common/herd/ — the model, free of Minecraft and unit-tested ( HerdModelTest ). Relationship is one horse’s view of another: familiarity, rank (“who yields”, −1..1), grooming bond and rivalry. SocialLedger is a capped set of them with growth, decay and contests; HerdRules holds every rate, written per Minecraft day and converted to per-scan odds by perScan , plus contest odds, dispersal timing and the lead-mare choice; BandRole is the label the Social section shows. data/HorseSocialAttachment — birth tick (game time, so sleep does not age a horse), dam, natal band, dispersal day and the ledger. copyOnDeath like the care attachment. server/HerdSocialHandler — the social tick, every SCAN ticks: relationship bookkeeping for every horse, and for a wild band member the lead-mare cache, bachelor-band succession, and adopting the band of the horse it knows best when its own is gone. It also builds the Social summary, sent with the inspect lease ( HorseSocialSyncPayload ). server/BandLife — dispersal, takeovers and mare transfers. A takeover is a registered pair; a LivingIncomingDamageEvent hook at high priority cancels the blow that would take either fighter under HerdRules.FIGHT_YIELD_HEALTH and ends the fight there, and HorseAggroHandler skips a registered fight so the band does not pile in. Fights and bouts are transient: a restart ends them. server/HerdGoals — Spar (3), DamAndFoal (4), StallionGuard (5), Displace (5) and GroomAndRest (7), on every horse; each throttles its own canUse scan. WildHerdGoal now follows the lead mare, and no longer promotes a member when the lead is missing — that promotion is what used to split a band into herds of one. Every number is a first guess The rates in HerdRules are chosen to make things happen on the pace the owner asked for (a Minecraft day), not measured against play. Displacement ignores plain grass — it is in the food tag, and every field would otherwise be a spot worth fighting over. AbstractHorse.aiStep (26.1.2's patched sources) heals every equine one point on a 1-in-900 roll each server tick with no health check, about 1.3 heals a minute per horse. On 2026-09-13 the 12:46 session logged 745 horse healed lines, 725 of them on horses already at full health, and the census counted 364 to 383 such heals each. mixin/HorseRegenMixin switches it off for horses. After the 22:12 build no census logged a single heal at full health. Hunger costs no tick time. The 22:12 build ran at 64 to 71 ms a tick against a steady 50 before, about 14 ticks a second, before any horse was hungry. HungerFoodGoal.canUse asked HorseDietHandler.dietOf first, which parses the whole genotype and epigenome, so about 280 horses parsed a genome tens of thousands of times a second. The same build grazed below 90 hunger, which kept every horse near 96 and turned 1,740 grass blocks to dirt in half an hour, a log line each. Fix: hunger is read first, the diet is resolved once per horse, grazing starts below Hunger.GRAZE_BELOW , and mouthfuls are counted into one line a minute. The 22:45 build's censuses read 54.1 ms (while the yard built), then 50.0 and 50.0. A dispersing filly refuses her own brother (2026-09-18, LEAVING HOME, unattended). The closeKin rule shipped on 2026-09-15 and could not be exercised for three days, because the pen was built in a way that never asked it the question: the colt was staggered three minutes behind his sister, so he had not yet left when she chose and was never a candidate. Dropping the stagger was necessary and not sufficient — the first run with both leaving together had the filly join her brother's band as his lead mare, which looks exactly like the original defect. It was not. closeKin reads motherId and fatherId off the record, the pen's horses are spawned rather than bred so both are empty, and unknown parents never match ; the natal band id that born() carries is not kinship. With the two recorded as full siblings of NATAL STALLION and MOVER MARE 1, the same pen reads: COLT: LEAVES NOW: Bachelor lead of 48d67ec1 FILLY: LEAVES NOW: Band mare of 08758bbf Her natal band was 0c0f5fae and her brother's own band is 48d67ec1 ; she joined neither and he was left a bachelor. The lesson worth keeping is the shape of it — a pen that answers the easy half reads exactly like one that answers the whole thing. Before the fix, a bachelor band's herd id was its lead's UUID, so when he took a family band the bachelors still pointing at that id became his mares' band-mates and followed him; BandLife.takeOver now re-forms them round the best-ranked of them. Dam defence works in rounds 2 to 5: each time the dam went for the zombie within three seconds, killed it within a second, and targeted nothing twenty seconds later. Before the fix she defended once and then kept the corpse as her target, because vanilla's MeleeAttackGoal.stop clears a target only for a creative or spectator player and a tamed horse has no forget-target goal (both checked in the 26.1.2 sources), so HerdGoals.free read her as busy for good; free now counts a dead target as none.
