Aggression
(Magical gene / no coat effect)
What a horse attacks, and when. One locus, one grid: a time of day crossed with what it goes for . Being in the saddle is not protection — a horse that hunts riders throws you off first.

Why a grid, and not a switch Naming the target is what makes this a breeding problem rather than a toggle. A horse that hunts monsters and a horse that hunts you are the same gene one allele apart, and they look identical in daylight. Nothing about the animal tells you which you have until the conditions are right, and by then you are either delighted or on foot. Why the change is total rather than gradual A horse does not become slightly aggressive at dusk. A ramp would read as a mood — something the animal is working through — and the point is the discovery that the animal you ride all week is not the animal in your yard at night. A threshold is a fact about the creature; a gradient is weather. Why aggression and fear are two loci They were one locus once, and it could not say the interesting thing. With hunting and fleeing sharing a set of alleles, a horse could be a monster-hunter or shy of people but never both. Splitting them means “goes for monsters, runs from riders” is a single animal — a herd-guard nobody can catch — and that horse is worth breeding for. See skittishness .
