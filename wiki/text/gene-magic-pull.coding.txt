Magic pull
(Magical gene / no coat effect)
How much the horse can shift. The fifth magical body-stat locus, beside size , speed , health and jump — and the one whose number is a score out of ten rather than a multiplier . A horse carrying nothing here pulls at 5 ; a Shire pulls at 10 and a Falabella at 1. Both copies count and they add, one allele positive and one negative.

Why it is not an AbstractMagicStatGene It is that class’s shape — a codominant locus, one allele up, one down, a per-copy number written on the copy and inherited with it, both copies added — and it differs in the two ways that matter. The sum is added, not multiplied. Speed, health, jump and scale multiply because they have to scale whatever the natural loci settled on, so a magically fast pony stays slower than a magically fast racehorse. Pull has no natural loci to compose with — this gene is the only thing that moves it — so multiplying would buy nothing and cost the one property that makes the number worth having, which is that a copy worth +2.5 is worth two and a half points on every horse that inherits it. There is deliberately no multiplyPullUnclamped . There is no vampiric allele. The other three carry one because the Dhampir’s strength had to travel with the horse. Pull instead gives the Dhampir a per-strain score on its breed sheet — see below . common/genetics/genes/MagicPullGene.java @Override public void contribute(AllelePair pair, Genotype genotype, GeneEpigenetics epigenetics, TraitBuilder out) { double sum = signedDelta(pair.first(), epigenetics.copy(0)) + signedDelta(pair.second(), epigenetics.copy(1)); if (sum != 0.0) { out.addPull(sum); } } The contribution is signed — Strong positive, Weak negative — so the whole combination table falls out of one line and Strong/Weak cancels because a positive and a negative are added, not because anything checks for it. Like every codominant locus it asks its GeneEpigenetics for copy(0) and copy(1) rather than for the expressed copy, which would count one allele twice and the other not at all. A breed’s score reaches its founders "pull": 10 in a breed file’s stats block is the number its horses resolve to. It is the one scored axis with no curve in between : BreedStatCurve.pullBand is the score padded out, where the three additive axes run their score through a multiplier ceiling first. BreedFounder then makes the founder homozygous for the pushing allele and splits the distance from 5.0 across its two copies, unevenly, exactly as it does for the other four. A breed scored within 0.4 of the baseline carries no pull alleles at all rather than being forced homozygous for an allele worth nothing — the same near-baseline rule the other axes follow. So a breed written "pull": 5 and a breed that omits pull entirely produce the same horse; the difference is only whether the sheet says the 5 was a decision. The Dhampir, and why strains can score stats A white Dhampir pulls at 9 and a seal brown at 6. Copy count cannot say that. The other three stat loci give the Dhampir its strength through the Vmp allele, where one copy is worth half of two — but 9 is four times as far above the baseline as 6 is, and two copies of anything are only ever worth twice one. So a Strain carries its own optional stats block, laid over the breed’s axis by axis ( Breed.StatScores.overlaidWith ), and the founder roll picks the strain before it reads any band. The Dhampir is the only breed that uses it today; any strain may. common/resources/horsegenetics/breeds/dhampir.json "strains": [ { "name": "White", "weight": 1, "stats": { "pull": 9 }, "genes": { ... } }, { "name": "Seal brown", "weight": 9, "stats": { "pull": 6 }, "genes": { ... } } ] Where the numbers come from A copy is worth a bounded normal draw of mean 0.5 points, 0.35 , floored at 0.05 so a Strong copy can never come out subtracting. That is the three additive stats’ “a tenth either way” (mean 0.10 against a baseline of 1.0) translated into score points against a baseline of 5. Draw One copy Two copies −6 (the floor) 5.05 5.1 mean 5.5 6.0 +1 5.85 6.7 +6 (the bound) 7.6 10.2 The design range therefore tops out just past a score of ten at two maximal copies — which is exactly where the breed sheets put a Shire. The two agreeing is the calibration, not a coincidence: it means a breed’s stamped founders sit inside what the gene can produce on its own rather than out on a tail it could never reach. Drift is clamped eight design-spans wide rather than to the design range, so a line bred for pull can leave 10 behind — there is no ceiling on Traits.pull() , only the 0.5 floor. Deliberately pull only It moves no other number. A strong puller is not thereby fast, healthy, big or springy — the five axes are bred independently, which is the whole reason there are five loci rather than one.
