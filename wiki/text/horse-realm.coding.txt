The horse realm
(Gameplay / world)
A flat, sunlit, bounded field where nothing can hurt a horse. Step through a hay-bale portal, let your surplus go with a freedom stick , and it stays there — healed, wild, keeping its name and its whole pedigree — until somebody else tames it.

Two dimensions, and why they share nothing horsegenetics:horse_realm is a second dimension beside horsegenetics:debug_pens . Every rule on this page is keyed to the first one and the debug corridor reads none of them — deliberately, and it is the constraint that shaped the implementation. The corridor is an unrestricted test bench . Horses die in its yard on purpose: the blanket “no horse damage” rule was removed from it on 2026-09-13 because it made six genes untestable, and its horses-only entity filter was switched off the day before that because it deleted the zombies and cows two gene tests were about. Both rules are exactly what the public realm needs. So they are written fresh, in server/HorseRealmRules , against HorseRealm.REALM_LEVEL , rather than generalised out of the corridor — which would have quietly undone two deliberate decisions. The entity filter is two rules, not one , because the two questions it is really answering are different. horsesOnly refuses any Mob that is not a horse, whatever made it; horses it waves through, since nearly every horse joining this level is one somebody sent. nothingSpawnsHere covers what that cannot see — EntityJoinLevelEvent carries no spawn reason — and refuses a NATURAL , CHUNK_GENERATION or SPAWNER horse at FinalizeSpawnEvent . Nothing produces one today, because the realm's biome has empty spawner lists in every category; that is a fact about a JSON file, and the promise that the field holds what was put in it and what was born to it should not rest on one. A summon, a breed egg and a foal all carry other reasons and are untouched. The terrain is arithmetic, not a saved layout There is no allocator, no plot registry and no saved map. Everything about a position in the realm is a pure function of its chunk coordinates: Made by What dimension/horse_realm.json The ground. A minecraft:flat generator, fifteen air layers then bedrock then dirt, so that with min_y: -16 the bedrock lands at Y=−1 and the dirt at Y=0. min_y must be a multiple of sixteen , which is why it is not simply −1. server/HorseRealmTerrain The pools, the hundred portals and the perimeter. Queued from ChunkEvent.Load , applied on the next server tick, and idempotent: it reads before it writes, so a second load costs a handful of comparisons and a chunk generated before a change to the class is corrected next time anybody walks into it. Not a custom chunk generator , though that was the obvious answer. The flat generator is a path this mod already runs on and a codec it does not have to register; what it cannot do is a fixed grid (vanilla has no placement modifier meaning “at the origin of every fifth chunk”) or a block with a block entity, which a ProtoChunk makes awkward. Both are trivial against a live ServerLevel , so that is where they happen. Most chunks do no block access at all — the pool grid is one chunk in twenty-five, the portal grid one in ten thousand. Eternal day is an omission, not a setting dimension_type/horse_realm.json carries default_clock and no timelines field . That is the whole of it. 26.1.2 reads sky darkness off the SKY_LIGHT_LEVEL environment attribute, which only varies with the clock when timelines says so, and the field defaults to empty — so a dimension with a clock and no timelines has a sky that is bright forever. This is the same fact the debug dimension was debugged for on 2026-09-12 ( its night ); here it is the feature. One exit, at the origin HorseRealm.arrivalCell returns chunk (0, 0) for every portal, and isPortalChunk is true for that chunk and no other. It used to hash the Overworld portal's own block position onto one of a hundred cells (MurmurHash3's finalizer, twice), so each portal had its own exit and two players could not land on top of each other, and horse_realm_grid_is_stable asserted that over four thousand draws. The hash was correct and the goal was wrong (owner's call). Spreading arrivals does not keep players out of each other's way; it keeps their horses apart, in pockets that never meet, in a dimension whose entire purpose is horses living together. Bands cannot form across that distance — HorseRealmHerds looks thirty-two blocks. arrivalBlock() and arrivalSpot() name that one place once, so the arrival, the reshape's rescue and the wall all agree on where the middle of this dimension is. The field is a circle, and it grows HorseRealmSize is the whole of it: a radius, the rings it has outgrown, and a set of the horses living here. The shape is a circle rather than a square because the field has one entrance now, and a circle is the shape where everywhere is about the same distance from it — the corners of a square are forty per cent further out than its edges, and the corners are exactly where a lost horse ends up. The density number is a decision, not arithmetic. The brief asked for one horse per ten blocks ; at the starting radius that is room for eight hundred thousand horses, so the growth would have been dead code. Ten chunks each puts the first growth at about 3,100 — a number a real stud farm arrives at. The owner picked that after seeing the three thresholds side by side. Growing moves the wall, and the old one has to come down This is the part that is easy to miss. A grown field leaves its previous ring of barriers standing inside itself — an invisible fence across the middle of somebody's paddock. So HorseRealmSize.retiredRings keeps the radii whose walls are still up, and HorseRealmTerrain.clearRing takes them down as those chunks load. Growth is rare, so the list is short. The wall is the eight-neighbour boundary , not a one-block-thick ring at d == r . A four-neighbour ring has corner gaps that an entity moving continuously slips straight through, and the clamp in HorseRealmRules should be the second line of defence rather than the first. The game test walks the whole boundary at four radii and fails on a single hole. Counting horses that are not there HorseRealmCensus . It cannot use HorseWhereabouts , which is the mod's answer to where is every horse , because that only records tamed horses — and the realm's population is precisely the ones that are not. So it is a set of UUIDs kept by the horses themselves: a horse ticking in the realm adds itself; a horse ticking anywhere else removes itself. The same shape as the feral rule , and for the same reason — there is no single event meaning this horse has left , and a condition covers every exit where a hook covers whichever ones somebody remembered. A horse in an unloaded chunk keeps its place in the count , which is the behaviour that matters most: most of the field is unloaded most of the time, and a census that only counted what it could see would shrink every time a player logged off and size the field for nobody. One known drift, in the safe direction: a horse taken out of the realm inside a stasis chamber never ticks anywhere again, so it stays counted. The field ends up slightly larger than it needs to be, and it never shrinks anyway. Carrying the old square field across The realm was a 16,000-block square with its corner at the origin. The circle is centred on the origin. So every horse in an existing realm is now thousands of blocks into the void, and the old field's furniture — a hundred exits, a pool every five chunks, a wall right round it — is scattered across country that is no longer field. The animals need no migration pass. HorseRealmRules.keepInsideTheField already runs on everything that ticks in the realm, and it now separates two cases that must not be answered the same way: just outside the wall is somebody leaning on it, and gets put back against it; far outside is something stranded where the field used to be, and gets carried to the exit. A horse in an unloaded chunk is dealt with the moment anything loads it, which is the first moment it could matter. The one piece of old terrain that is not merely untidy Two of the old square's four walls ran along x = -1 and z = -1 — which are inside the new circle , a few steps from the exit. Left alone they would be a pair of invisible walls straight through the middle of the new field. eraseOldField clears them, along with the ninety-nine abandoned exits and the old perimeter, wherever a chunk that carried one loads. The game test asserts air at (-1, 8) for exactly this reason. Moving the floor, in a world that already has one The surface moved from y 0 to y 64 and from dirt to grass. Every number in Java was already derived from HorseRealm.GROUND_Y , so that half was one constant; the flat layers in dimension/horse_realm.json are not derived and had to move with it, and the game test had four bare literals that are now derived too. This is back-compat code, which the hard rules forbid HorseRealmLift exists because the owner asked for it by name, and because the standing rule's premise has expired rather than the rule being overridden on a whim. “No saves worth keeping” was true when there was one tester and a dev world; there is now a shared server with other people on it and a field of horses living in this dimension, and regenerating it would delete them. It asks the chunk instead of remembering anything. A chunk generated on the old floor has bedrock at y −1; one generated since has air there, because the flat layers put bedrock at 63 now. So the test is one block read, it is exact, and it answers no for every chunk of every world that never saw the old layout — including every world made from here on. It is also idempotent by construction , which a saved flag would not be: a lifted chunk no longer has bedrock down there, so a second pass does nothing, there is no migration marker to drift from the blocks it describes, and a chunk that was somehow missed is caught the next time anybody walks into it. That is HorseRealmTerrain 's own argument against a “decorated” flag, applied again. It is a memmove, and the direction is the whole of its correctness. The source is the entire column below the new surface (−16 to 63) and the destination is that plus sixty-four (48 to 127), so the two overlap on 48 to 63. The walk therefore goes top down . Empty sections are skipped whole, so an old realm chunk is two sections of work rather than twenty thousand block reads. This shipped wrong in 0.5.028, and here is the shape of the mistake The first version walked up , on the reasoning that the offset is larger than anything the realm builds. That is true, and it is about the content ; the ranges are about the column the loop iterates , which is the whole thing. Walking up moves the old bedrock from y −1 to y 63, then reaches y 63 later in the same pass, finds it, and moves it again to y 127 — leaving the new grass with no bedrock under it and a bedrock ceiling over the field. It would also have been unrecoverable : the “does this chunk need lifting” probe reads the bedrock at y −1 that the pass has just cleared, so a second pass would have declined to fix it. The idempotence that makes this design safe is the same property that would have made the bug permanent. Caught by modelling the column walk outside Minecraft and printing both directions, which took less time than the paragraph that got it wrong. Re-derive this if the surface ever moves again , rather than trusting the sentence above. The horses are not moved by it. An entity can be in an entity section that has not loaded when its blocks do, so moving them from there would miss exactly the ones that matter. HorseRealmRules floors anything in the realm below the bedrock instead — which catches them whenever they turn up, and closes an older gap nobody had noticed: the realm is a bedrock sheet with nothing under it and had no floor check, so anything through it fell forever. Quietly, since a horse here cannot die. The frame is cobblestone, because a horse ate a portal A hay bale is the largest single meal in the mod and a horse eats it out of the world ( HayBales , DietFoods ), so a frame built of hay is a frame the animals standing beside it demolish — which collapses the portal through HayPortalBlock.updateShape and strands whoever was on the far side. Neither feature is wrong; they simply meet, and the fix is a frame nothing wants to eat. What a frame is made of was a bare state.is(Blocks.HAY_BLOCK) in five places — the flood fill, the portal block's own “am I still enclosed” ray, the realm's two frame builders and the right-click that lights one. It is now HorsePortalManager.isFrame and frameBlock() , because five copies of one answer is the exact shape of thing that drifts. The golden carrot is unchanged . The id stays hay_portal , and so does the class name Renaming a registered block rewrites every world that has one placed, and there is one of those with people in it. The name is wrong and the id is load-bearing, so the id stays and the comment says why — the same expired premise as the lift above. The breeding rate is a clock, not a scale factor server/HorseRealmRepro . This is the part worth reading before changing anything about fertility. Nothing in common.repro counts down. Every stage is derived from an absolute anchor and the current game tick — where a mare is in her cycle, whether she is due, whether she has spent her one try this heat, how many covers a stallion has had today. There is no timer to divide by four. There is only the reading of “now” that each derivation is handed. So each horse keeps an offset in its persistent data: ticks it has lived through that its reproduction did not. HorseRealmRepro.reproTime(horse) is game time minus that offset, and every repro read and write for that horse goes through it. One number, in one place. A horse that has never been in the realm has an offset of zero and behaves precisely as it did before the class existed. That framing is what buys all four requirements at once: The rate. At 25, a quarter of each elapsed tick is credited and three quarters deferred, so every stage takes four times as long. A rate, not a conception chance. Zero pauses it , existing pregnancies included — which a conception-chance reading of the same config could not have done. A dormant realm advances nothing. If nobody is there the horse is not ticking, so no time is credited and all of it is deferred. The test is the size of the gap since that horse was last scanned: late by more than three of its own periods means it was not being simulated, not that it was being simulated slowly. It survives the trip home. The offset belongs to the horse, not to where it is standing, so it stops growing on the way out and is never taken away. Clearing it on exit would make a mare instantly, retroactively overdue. The one place both clocks appear is NaturalBreedingHandler . Her cycle and her one try use her repro clock; the three-second courtship, the chat throttles and the log stamps use the world’s, because a message is something a player reads in real time whatever the realm’s pacing. What releasing actually changes server/HorseRelease.makeWild , and the reason it is its own class is that there are now two ways it happens with nothing else in common . A freedom stick is a player standing next to a horse deciding, which can hand the tack back and say something. Walking out of the realm is the horse being noticed, later, with nobody there. If each wrote its own version, the second would be the one that quietly forgot the temper, or the persistence, or the wild-spawn mark. Three of them are vanilla's: setTamed(false) , setOwner(null) , and setTemper(0) . The last is the one that is easy to miss and makes the whole feature real — vanilla's RunAroundLikeCrazyGoal rolls against temper, and a horse that was tame is sitting at maximum, so without the reset the first player to mount it tames it instantly. Everything that makes the horse that horse is on the HorseRecord attachment and in HorseAncestryData , and none of it is derived from ownership, so releasing touches none of it. HorseOwnerTrackingHandler mirrors the record's owner to null within two seconds on its own — its javadoc already named “a horse going wild again” as a case it handles, a year before there was one. Re-taming needs no restore code at all: tameWithName sets the owner and the same tracker mirrors it back. The tack comes off because AbstractHorse.canUseSlot(SADDLE) asks isTamed() — leaving a saddle on a horse that has gone wild would quietly destroy it. With a player there it goes to them; with nobody there it is dropped where the horse stands , which is the only honest answer, since the gear is theirs and this dimension does not delete things. Leaving is a condition, not an event HorseRealmFeral . The obvious build is a hook on leaving — find the player's horses, release them — and it is wrong in four ways at once , each of which is a horse that stays tame forever in a field it was turned out into: A player who logs out in the realm fires no dimension change. One who dies there respawns without one either — the debug corridor needed a separate PlayerRespawnEvent subscriber for exactly this. One teleported out by a command or another mod goes through nothing at all. A horse in an unloaded chunk at the moment of any of those cannot be found to release — and in a field sixteen thousand blocks across that is the normal case, not the corner. So the rule is stated as a property of the horse instead: a tamed horse in the realm whose owner is not also in the realm is wild. All four cases satisfy it without being handled, and a horse in unloaded country is released the moment somebody loads it — the first moment it could possibly matter, since until then nobody can see it. The owner check is a walk of the player list rather than resolving the horse's EntityReference : resolving one can load a chunk in another dimension to find a player who is plainly not here, and the question is only ever is that UUID in this level . The scan is every five seconds per horse, staggered by entity id — half HorseRealmHerds ' period , so a horse is turned out before it is asked to join a band rather than a cycle after, since that tick ignores a tamed horse. The delay is also what keeps the rule from being a trap: step back through by mistake and nothing has happened yet. Released horses had to be taught to band server/HorseRealmHerds , and it exists because a released horse falls through every existing gate. HerdManager.assignFounder returns immediately for anything that already has a record, and the whole wild-herd path is gated on the wild-spawn mark that HorseFoundingTickHandler strips from every horse that arrives carrying one. A released horse is the opposite case in every respect: a fully recorded animal, with a name and a pedigree and an owner it no longer has. The band it forms is labelled Feral Mixed , not the lead’s breed. Whoever gathers here is whoever happened to be released nearby, and BreedLineage.FERAL is the mod’s existing word for no herd identity . Each horse keeps its own breed on its own record. Why the field was one ball of horses, and the two things that fixed it Reported by the owner (2026-09-25): “they all stay in one giant ball. Since they’re wild, shouldn’t they form bands and spread out?” Three rules were compounding, and none of them was wrong on its own. Everything arrives on the same block. arrivalCell ignores where a traveller came from and always answers the origin cell — deliberately, see above , and pinned by the horse_realm_grid_is_stable gametest. So every horse released or turned out begins its life in the realm standing exactly where every other one did. A band had no size. HorseRealmHerds put a new arrival into the nearest band within 32 blocks, and there was always one, and it was always the same one. The field therefore did not contain several bands standing close together — it contained one band , of everything. Cohesion had no counter-force. WildHerdGoal closes every member to within five blocks of its lead and StallionGuard drags strays back inside fourteen, against nothing but vanilla’s random stroll at the same goal priority. The fix is (2) and (3), and the order matters: repulsion between bands does nothing while there is only one band , so capping a band's intake is what makes the spacing rule have anything to act on. A band now fills at BreedHerd.MAX_HAREM_MARES + 1 , doubled for foals and hangers-on, after which an arrival founds its own; and server/BandSpacing walks a wild banded horse away from the nearest horse of a different band within forty blocks. The spacing is a per-horse nudge, not a band manoeuvre , and that is what makes it cheap. Cohesion is per-horse too, so the two are the same kind of force and settle against each other; a band whose members each edge away from a neighbour moves as a band anyway, because its own cohesion keeps gathering it up as it goes. Nothing has to compute a band centre, elect who decides, or keep band-level state. It runs on the same hundred-tick scan as the rest of the social layer and actuates through BandLife.retreat , the same short shove a horse gets for losing a fight. Arrival is deliberately left alone. Scattering horses as they land would be the obvious third fix and it is the one not taken: that single arrival point is a property the realm's gametest asserts on four thousand random portals, and it is load bearing for the terrain and the wall. Spreading out is something the horses do afterwards, which is also the version that keeps working when a band wanders. Seen working, 2026-09-26 The owner turned horses out and watched the field: “horses do spread out now in their bands in the horse realm” . So the intake cap is biting — the field holds several bands rather than one — and BandSpacing ’s nudge settles against WildHerdGoal ’s cohesion instead of fighting it. The two failure modes this was watched for, one ball and visible jitter, are both ruled out. F6 is not a dev key any more Moving the hay portal here left the debug corridor and its test yard with exactly one door, so F6 is registered in every build, release jars included. Who walks through it is the server’s business, not the keybind’s: ModNetworking admits a world with debug.tools on, or an operator at LEVEL_GAMEMASTERS — the same bar /testkit already sets, so it adds no reach a command did not already have. Anybody else is told no rather than getting silence, because a key that does nothing without saying why is how a working feature gets reported as broken. The perimeter is built twice on purpose minecraft:barrier blocks sixteen high, one block outside the field, built by the same chunk pass — and a position clamp in HorseRealmRules for anything that gets above them. Both halves are wanted. A clamp on its own reads as teleport-stutter where a wall reads as a wall; a wall on its own has a lid missing. The clamp zeroes horizontal motion as well as the position, or a player holding forward against the boundary is teleported back every tick and calls it lag. What is tested, and what a test cannot reach Three in ModGameTests ( ./gradlew :neoforge-26.1.2:runGameTest ): horse_realm_is_built for the pools, the lit exit and the perimeter, horse_realm_grid_is_stable — which now asserts the opposite of what it was written to assert, that every portal reaches the same middle cell rather than spreading across the hundred — and freedom_stick_crafts . The first of those usually runs in the Overworld, and that is not a mistake. GameTestServer builds its world from a hand-written three-dimension list and never reads the datapack’s dimension/ folder, so the realm cannot exist in that harness however correct its JSON is. The geometry does not care which level it is written into, so it is asserted against whatever level is available, and the two ground assertions — which belong to the flat generator rather than to any Java — are asked only when the real dimension is there. That leaves one thing no test here can see: whether the dimension loads at all . runServer answers it, and its log names ServerLevel[world]/horsegenetics:horse_realm . Repeat that after any edit to the three JSON files, because a typo in them is silent everywhere else.
