The horse realm
(Gameplay / world)
A flat, sunlit, bounded field where nothing can hurt a horse. Step through a hay-bale portal, let your surplus go with a freedom stick , and it stays there — healed, wild, keeping its name and its whole pedigree — until somebody else tames it.

Two dimensions, and why they share nothing horsegenetics:horse_realm is a second dimension beside horsegenetics:debug_pens . Every rule on this page is keyed to the first one and the debug corridor reads none of them — deliberately, and it is the constraint that shaped the implementation. The corridor is an unrestricted test bench . Horses die in its yard on purpose: the blanket “no horse damage” rule was removed from it on 2026-09-13 because it made six genes untestable, and its horses-only entity filter was switched off the day before that because it deleted the zombies and cows two gene tests were about. Both rules are exactly what the public realm needs. So they are written fresh, in server/HorseRealmRules , against HorseRealm.REALM_LEVEL , rather than generalised out of the corridor — which would have quietly undone two deliberate decisions. The terrain is arithmetic, not a saved layout There is no allocator, no plot registry and no saved map. Everything about a position in the realm is a pure function of its chunk coordinates: Made by What dimension/horse_realm.json The ground. A minecraft:flat generator, fifteen air layers then bedrock then dirt, so that with min_y: -16 the bedrock lands at Y=−1 and the dirt at Y=0. min_y must be a multiple of sixteen , which is why it is not simply −1. server/HorseRealmTerrain The pools, the hundred portals and the perimeter. Queued from ChunkEvent.Load , applied on the next server tick, and idempotent: it reads before it writes, so a second load costs a handful of comparisons and a chunk generated before a change to the class is corrected next time anybody walks into it. Not a custom chunk generator , though that was the obvious answer. The flat generator is a path this mod already runs on and a codec it does not have to register; what it cannot do is a fixed grid (vanilla has no placement modifier meaning “at the origin of every fifth chunk”) or a block with a block entity, which a ProtoChunk makes awkward. Both are trivial against a live ServerLevel , so that is where they happen. Most chunks do no block access at all — the pool grid is one chunk in twenty-five, the portal grid one in ten thousand. Eternal day is an omission, not a setting dimension_type/horse_realm.json carries default_clock and no timelines field . That is the whole of it. 26.1.2 reads sky darkness off the SKY_LIGHT_LEVEL environment attribute, which only varies with the clock when timelines says so, and the field defaults to empty — so a dimension with a clock and no timelines has a sky that is bright forever. This is the same fact the debug dimension was debugged for on 2026-09-12 ( its night ); here it is the feature. The arrival hash HorseRealm.arrivalCell mixes the Overworld portal’s block position (MurmurHash3’s finalizer, twice, on X and Z) onto one of the hundred grid cells. A hash rather than a scaled coordinate because the Overworld is millions of blocks wide and the realm is sixteen thousand: any linear mapping puts every portal anyone will ever build into one cell. Both of its properties fail silently if broken. A hash that drifted would strand a player’s released horses out of reach with no error anywhere; one that collapsed would put a whole server in one corner of a 16 000-block field and look, from inside, exactly like a popular meeting spot. So horse_realm_grid_is_stable asserts both over four thousand draws. The breeding rate is a clock, not a scale factor server/HorseRealmRepro . This is the part worth reading before changing anything about fertility. Nothing in common.repro counts down. Every stage is derived from an absolute anchor and the current game tick — where a mare is in her cycle, whether she is due, whether she has spent her one try this heat, how many covers a stallion has had today. There is no timer to divide by four. There is only the reading of “now” that each derivation is handed. So each horse keeps an offset in its persistent data: ticks it has lived through that its reproduction did not. HorseRealmRepro.reproTime(horse) is game time minus that offset, and every repro read and write for that horse goes through it. One number, in one place. A horse that has never been in the realm has an offset of zero and behaves precisely as it did before the class existed. That framing is what buys all four requirements at once: The rate. At 25, a quarter of each elapsed tick is credited and three quarters deferred, so every stage takes four times as long. A rate, not a conception chance. Zero pauses it , existing pregnancies included — which a conception-chance reading of the same config could not have done. A dormant realm advances nothing. If nobody is there the horse is not ticking, so no time is credited and all of it is deferred. The test is the size of the gap since that horse was last scanned: late by more than three of its own periods means it was not being simulated, not that it was being simulated slowly. It survives the trip home. The offset belongs to the horse, not to where it is standing, so it stops growing on the way out and is never taken away. Clearing it on exit would make a mare instantly, retroactively overdue. The one place both clocks appear is NaturalBreedingHandler . Her cycle and her one try use her repro clock; the three-second courtship, the chat throttles and the log stamps use the world’s, because a message is something a player reads in real time whatever the realm’s pacing. What releasing actually changes server/FreedomStickHandler . Three things, and nothing else: setTamed(false) , setOwner(null) , and setTemper(0) . The last is the one that is easy to miss and makes the whole feature real — vanilla’s RunAroundLikeCrazyGoal rolls against temper, and a horse that was tame is sitting at maximum, so without the reset the first player to mount it tames it instantly. Everything that makes the horse that horse is on the HorseRecord attachment and in HorseAncestryData , and none of it is derived from ownership, so releasing touches none of it. HorseOwnerTrackingHandler mirrors the record’s owner to null within two seconds on its own — its javadoc already named “a horse going wild again” as a case it handles, a year before there was one. Re-taming needs no restore code at all: tameWithName sets the owner and the same tracker mirrors it back. The tack comes off and goes to the player because AbstractHorse.canUseSlot(SADDLE) asks isTamed() — leaving a saddle on a horse that has gone wild would quietly destroy it. Released horses had to be taught to band server/HorseRealmHerds , and it exists because a released horse falls through every existing gate. HerdManager.assignFounder returns immediately for anything that already has a record, and the whole wild-herd path is gated on the wild-spawn mark that HorseFoundingTickHandler strips from every horse that arrives carrying one. A released horse is the opposite case in every respect: a fully recorded animal, with a name and a pedigree and an owner it no longer has. The band it forms is labelled Feral Mixed , not the lead’s breed. Whoever gathers here is whoever happened to be released nearby, and BreedLineage.FERAL is the mod’s existing word for no herd identity . Each horse keeps its own breed on its own record. F6 is not a dev key any more Moving the hay portal here left the debug corridor and its test yard with exactly one door, so F6 is registered in every build, release jars included. Who walks through it is the server’s business, not the keybind’s: ModNetworking admits a world with debug.tools on, or an operator at LEVEL_GAMEMASTERS — the same bar /testkit already sets, so it adds no reach a command did not already have. Anybody else is told no rather than getting silence, because a key that does nothing without saying why is how a working feature gets reported as broken. The perimeter is built twice on purpose minecraft:barrier blocks sixteen high, one block outside the field, built by the same chunk pass — and a position clamp in HorseRealmRules for anything that gets above them. Both halves are wanted. A clamp on its own reads as teleport-stutter where a wall reads as a wall; a wall on its own has a lid missing. The clamp zeroes horizontal motion as well as the position, or a player holding forward against the boundary is teleported back every tick and calls it lag. What is tested, and what a test cannot reach Three in ModGameTests ( ./gradlew :neoforge-26.1.2:runGameTest ): horse_realm_is_built for the pools, the lit exit and the perimeter, horse_realm_grid_is_stable for the arrival hash, and freedom_stick_crafts . The first of those usually runs in the Overworld, and that is not a mistake. GameTestServer builds its world from a hand-written three-dimension list and never reads the datapack’s dimension/ folder, so the realm cannot exist in that harness however correct its JSON is. The geometry does not care which level it is written into, so it is asserted against whatever level is available, and the two ground assertions — which belong to the flat generator rather than to any Java — are asked only when the real dimension is there. That leaves one thing no test here can see: whether the dimension loads at all . runServer answers it, and its log names ServerLevel[world]/horsegenetics:horse_realm . Repeat that after any edit to the three JSON files, because a typo in them is silent everywhere else.
