The horse realm
(Gameplay / world)
A flat, sunlit, bounded field where nothing can hurt a horse. Step through a hay-bale portal, let your surplus go with a freedom stick , and it stays there — healed, wild, keeping its name and its whole pedigree — until somebody else tames it.

Two dimensions, and why they share nothing horsegenetics:horse_realm is a second dimension beside horsegenetics:debug_pens . Every rule on this page is keyed to the first one and the debug corridor reads none of them — deliberately, and it is the constraint that shaped the implementation. The corridor is an unrestricted test bench . Horses die in its yard on purpose: the blanket “no horse damage” rule was removed from it on 2026-09-13 because it made six genes untestable, and its horses-only entity filter was switched off the day before that because it deleted the zombies and cows two gene tests were about. Both rules are exactly what the public realm needs. So they are written fresh, in server/HorseRealmRules , against HorseRealm.REALM_LEVEL , rather than generalised out of the corridor — which would have quietly undone two deliberate decisions. The terrain is arithmetic, not a saved layout There is no allocator, no plot registry and no saved map. Everything about a position in the realm is a pure function of its chunk coordinates: Made by What dimension/horse_realm.json The ground. A minecraft:flat generator, fifteen air layers then bedrock then dirt, so that with min_y: -16 the bedrock lands at Y=−1 and the dirt at Y=0. min_y must be a multiple of sixteen , which is why it is not simply −1. server/HorseRealmTerrain The pools, the hundred portals and the perimeter. Queued from ChunkEvent.Load , applied on the next server tick, and idempotent: it reads before it writes, so a second load costs a handful of comparisons and a chunk generated before a change to the class is corrected next time anybody walks into it. Not a custom chunk generator , though that was the obvious answer. The flat generator is a path this mod already runs on and a codec it does not have to register; what it cannot do is a fixed grid (vanilla has no placement modifier meaning “at the origin of every fifth chunk”) or a block with a block entity, which a ProtoChunk makes awkward. Both are trivial against a live ServerLevel , so that is where they happen. Most chunks do no block access at all — the pool grid is one chunk in twenty-five, the portal grid one in ten thousand. Eternal day is an omission, not a setting dimension_type/horse_realm.json carries default_clock and no timelines field . That is the whole of it. 26.1.2 reads sky darkness off the SKY_LIGHT_LEVEL environment attribute, which only varies with the clock when timelines says so, and the field defaults to empty — so a dimension with a clock and no timelines has a sky that is bright forever. This is the same fact the debug dimension was debugged for on 2026-09-12 ( its night ); here it is the feature. Everybody arrives at the middle HorseRealm.arrivalCell returns one cell — the centre of the ten-by-ten grid — whatever portal is asking. It used to hash the Overworld portal's own block position onto one of the hundred (MurmurHash3's finalizer, twice), so that each portal had its own exit and two players never landed on top of each other, and horse_realm_grid_is_stable asserted that over four thousand draws. The hash was correct and the goal was wrong (owner's call). In a field sixteen thousand blocks across, spreading arrivals does not keep players out of each other's way; it keeps their horses apart, in hundred-chunk pockets that never meet, in a dimension whose entire purpose is horses living together. Bands cannot form across that distance — HorseRealmHerds looks thirty-two blocks — and a field nobody can find anybody in is a worse outcome than a crowded gate. The other ninety-nine exits are still built and still work. They are what the grid was genuinely for: a way home for somebody who has walked a long way. The parameter stays in the signature so a change of heart is one method deep rather than a call-site sweep. Moving the floor, in a world that already has one The surface moved from y 0 to y 64 and from dirt to grass. Every number in Java was already derived from HorseRealm.GROUND_Y , so that half was one constant; the flat layers in dimension/horse_realm.json are not derived and had to move with it, and the game test had four bare literals that are now derived too. This is back-compat code, which the hard rules forbid HorseRealmLift exists because the owner asked for it by name, and because the standing rule's premise has expired rather than the rule being overridden on a whim. “No saves worth keeping” was true when there was one tester and a dev world; there is now a shared server with other people on it and a field of horses living in this dimension, and regenerating it would delete them. It asks the chunk instead of remembering anything. A chunk generated on the old floor has bedrock at y −1; one generated since has air there, because the flat layers put bedrock at 63 now. So the test is one block read, it is exact, and it answers no for every chunk of every world that never saw the old layout — including every world made from here on. It is also idempotent by construction , which a saved flag would not be: a lifted chunk no longer has bedrock down there, so a second pass does nothing, there is no migration marker to drift from the blocks it describes, and a chunk that was somehow missed is caught the next time anybody walks into it. That is HorseRealmTerrain 's own argument against a “decorated” flag, applied again. It is a memmove, and the direction is the whole of its correctness. The source is the entire column below the new surface (−16 to 63) and the destination is that plus sixty-four (48 to 127), so the two overlap on 48 to 63. The walk therefore goes top down . Empty sections are skipped whole, so an old realm chunk is two sections of work rather than twenty thousand block reads. This shipped wrong in 0.5.028, and here is the shape of the mistake The first version walked up , on the reasoning that the offset is larger than anything the realm builds. That is true, and it is about the content ; the ranges are about the column the loop iterates , which is the whole thing. Walking up moves the old bedrock from y −1 to y 63, then reaches y 63 later in the same pass, finds it, and moves it again to y 127 — leaving the new grass with no bedrock under it and a bedrock ceiling over the field. It would also have been unrecoverable : the “does this chunk need lifting” probe reads the bedrock at y −1 that the pass has just cleared, so a second pass would have declined to fix it. The idempotence that makes this design safe is the same property that would have made the bug permanent. Caught by modelling the column walk outside Minecraft and printing both directions, which took less time than the paragraph that got it wrong. Re-derive this if the surface ever moves again , rather than trusting the sentence above. The horses are not moved by it. An entity can be in an entity section that has not loaded when its blocks do, so moving them from there would miss exactly the ones that matter. HorseRealmRules floors anything in the realm below the bedrock instead — which catches them whenever they turn up, and closes an older gap nobody had noticed: the realm is a bedrock sheet with nothing under it and had no floor check, so anything through it fell forever. Quietly, since a horse here cannot die. The frame is cobblestone, because a horse ate a portal A hay bale is the largest single meal in the mod and a horse eats it out of the world ( HayBales , DietFoods ), so a frame built of hay is a frame the animals standing beside it demolish — which collapses the portal through HayPortalBlock.updateShape and strands whoever was on the far side. Neither feature is wrong; they simply meet, and the fix is a frame nothing wants to eat. What a frame is made of was a bare state.is(Blocks.HAY_BLOCK) in five places — the flood fill, the portal block's own “am I still enclosed” ray, the realm's two frame builders and the right-click that lights one. It is now HorsePortalManager.isFrame and frameBlock() , because five copies of one answer is the exact shape of thing that drifts. The golden carrot is unchanged . The id stays hay_portal , and so does the class name Renaming a registered block rewrites every world that has one placed, and there is one of those with people in it. The name is wrong and the id is load-bearing, so the id stays and the comment says why — the same expired premise as the lift above. The breeding rate is a clock, not a scale factor server/HorseRealmRepro . This is the part worth reading before changing anything about fertility. Nothing in common.repro counts down. Every stage is derived from an absolute anchor and the current game tick — where a mare is in her cycle, whether she is due, whether she has spent her one try this heat, how many covers a stallion has had today. There is no timer to divide by four. There is only the reading of “now” that each derivation is handed. So each horse keeps an offset in its persistent data: ticks it has lived through that its reproduction did not. HorseRealmRepro.reproTime(horse) is game time minus that offset, and every repro read and write for that horse goes through it. One number, in one place. A horse that has never been in the realm has an offset of zero and behaves precisely as it did before the class existed. That framing is what buys all four requirements at once: The rate. At 25, a quarter of each elapsed tick is credited and three quarters deferred, so every stage takes four times as long. A rate, not a conception chance. Zero pauses it , existing pregnancies included — which a conception-chance reading of the same config could not have done. A dormant realm advances nothing. If nobody is there the horse is not ticking, so no time is credited and all of it is deferred. The test is the size of the gap since that horse was last scanned: late by more than three of its own periods means it was not being simulated, not that it was being simulated slowly. It survives the trip home. The offset belongs to the horse, not to where it is standing, so it stops growing on the way out and is never taken away. Clearing it on exit would make a mare instantly, retroactively overdue. The one place both clocks appear is NaturalBreedingHandler . Her cycle and her one try use her repro clock; the three-second courtship, the chat throttles and the log stamps use the world’s, because a message is something a player reads in real time whatever the realm’s pacing. What releasing actually changes server/HorseRelease.makeWild , and the reason it is its own class is that there are now two ways it happens with nothing else in common . A freedom stick is a player standing next to a horse deciding, which can hand the tack back and say something. Walking out of the realm is the horse being noticed, later, with nobody there. If each wrote its own version, the second would be the one that quietly forgot the temper, or the persistence, or the wild-spawn mark. Three of them are vanilla's: setTamed(false) , setOwner(null) , and setTemper(0) . The last is the one that is easy to miss and makes the whole feature real — vanilla's RunAroundLikeCrazyGoal rolls against temper, and a horse that was tame is sitting at maximum, so without the reset the first player to mount it tames it instantly. Everything that makes the horse that horse is on the HorseRecord attachment and in HorseAncestryData , and none of it is derived from ownership, so releasing touches none of it. HorseOwnerTrackingHandler mirrors the record's owner to null within two seconds on its own — its javadoc already named “a horse going wild again” as a case it handles, a year before there was one. Re-taming needs no restore code at all: tameWithName sets the owner and the same tracker mirrors it back. The tack comes off because AbstractHorse.canUseSlot(SADDLE) asks isTamed() — leaving a saddle on a horse that has gone wild would quietly destroy it. With a player there it goes to them; with nobody there it is dropped where the horse stands , which is the only honest answer, since the gear is theirs and this dimension does not delete things. Leaving is a condition, not an event HorseRealmFeral . The obvious build is a hook on leaving — find the player's horses, release them — and it is wrong in four ways at once , each of which is a horse that stays tame forever in a field it was turned out into: A player who logs out in the realm fires no dimension change. One who dies there respawns without one either — the debug corridor needed a separate PlayerRespawnEvent subscriber for exactly this. One teleported out by a command or another mod goes through nothing at all. A horse in an unloaded chunk at the moment of any of those cannot be found to release — and in a field sixteen thousand blocks across that is the normal case, not the corner. So the rule is stated as a property of the horse instead: a tamed horse in the realm whose owner is not also in the realm is wild. All four cases satisfy it without being handled, and a horse in unloaded country is released the moment somebody loads it — the first moment it could possibly matter, since until then nobody can see it. The owner check is a walk of the player list rather than resolving the horse's EntityReference : resolving one can load a chunk in another dimension to find a player who is plainly not here, and the question is only ever is that UUID in this level . The scan is every five seconds per horse, staggered by entity id — half HorseRealmHerds ' period , so a horse is turned out before it is asked to join a band rather than a cycle after, since that tick ignores a tamed horse. The delay is also what keeps the rule from being a trap: step back through by mistake and nothing has happened yet. Released horses had to be taught to band server/HorseRealmHerds , and it exists because a released horse falls through every existing gate. HerdManager.assignFounder returns immediately for anything that already has a record, and the whole wild-herd path is gated on the wild-spawn mark that HorseFoundingTickHandler strips from every horse that arrives carrying one. A released horse is the opposite case in every respect: a fully recorded animal, with a name and a pedigree and an owner it no longer has. The band it forms is labelled Feral Mixed , not the lead’s breed. Whoever gathers here is whoever happened to be released nearby, and BreedLineage.FERAL is the mod’s existing word for no herd identity . Each horse keeps its own breed on its own record. F6 is not a dev key any more Moving the hay portal here left the debug corridor and its test yard with exactly one door, so F6 is registered in every build, release jars included. Who walks through it is the server’s business, not the keybind’s: ModNetworking admits a world with debug.tools on, or an operator at LEVEL_GAMEMASTERS — the same bar /testkit already sets, so it adds no reach a command did not already have. Anybody else is told no rather than getting silence, because a key that does nothing without saying why is how a working feature gets reported as broken. The perimeter is built twice on purpose minecraft:barrier blocks sixteen high, one block outside the field, built by the same chunk pass — and a position clamp in HorseRealmRules for anything that gets above them. Both halves are wanted. A clamp on its own reads as teleport-stutter where a wall reads as a wall; a wall on its own has a lid missing. The clamp zeroes horizontal motion as well as the position, or a player holding forward against the boundary is teleported back every tick and calls it lag. What is tested, and what a test cannot reach Three in ModGameTests ( ./gradlew :neoforge-26.1.2:runGameTest ): horse_realm_is_built for the pools, the lit exit and the perimeter, horse_realm_grid_is_stable — which now asserts the opposite of what it was written to assert, that every portal reaches the same middle cell rather than spreading across the hundred — and freedom_stick_crafts . The first of those usually runs in the Overworld, and that is not a mistake. GameTestServer builds its world from a hand-written three-dimension list and never reads the datapack’s dimension/ folder, so the realm cannot exist in that harness however correct its JSON is. The geometry does not care which level it is written into, so it is asserted against whatever level is available, and the two ground assertions — which belong to the flat generator rather than to any Java — are asked only when the real dimension is there. That leaves one thing no test here can see: whether the dimension loads at all . runServer answers it, and its log names ServerLevel[world]/horsegenetics:horse_realm . Repeat that after any edit to the three JSON files, because a typo in them is silent everywhere else.
