Double gates
(Block / the yard)
A fence gate two blocks wide . One item places both halves, one click swings both leaves, and there is no post down the middle to catch a horse on. One per wood, and it is a stall ’s boundary in every state exactly as a vanilla gate is.

It extends FenceGateBlock on purpose Not for the inherited behaviour — most of that is overridden — but because StallDetector.isDoorway asks instanceof FenceGateBlock , by block identity, in every state. Extending it makes a stall shut with one of these a closed room for free , and nothing in the stall detector had to learn this class exists. A gate that did not extend it would have read as a hole in the wall, and the floor fill would have walked out through it into the yard — which is the bug that class’s header is mostly about . Which half is which Each block carries a half of left or right , and finds its partner by turning from FACING — counter-clockwise for the left half, clockwise for the right. A half whose partner is gone returns air from updateShape , the way BedBlock does it. Doing it there rather than in a break hook means the pair also survives being taken apart by an explosion, a piston or /setblock . One item out, and the loot table is what enforces it This was written as though returning air from updateShape dropped nothing, and it does not. Block.updateOrDestroy calls destroyBlock(pos, (flags & 32) == 0) , and an ordinary neighbour update carries no UPDATE_SUPPRESS_DROPS — so the struck half paid out, the orphaned partner paid out again, and breaking a double gate gave you two . It shipped that way and the owner found it in play. Vanilla’s doors and beds have the same two-block problem and solve it in the data , not in Java: every gate’s loot table is conditioned on half=left , so whichever half is struck, exactly one of the two removals pays. Compare data/minecraft/loot_table/blocks/oak_door.json , conditioned on half=lower . The condition is written by both generators and a gate that duplicates again means one of them lost it. playerWillDestroy is the other end of the same rule. Because left is the half that pays, a break that should drop nothing — creative, or one day a tool requirement — still leaked one item when the right half was the one struck: creative suppresses your own block, but the partner is removed by updateShape , which knows nothing about who swung. So the paying half is taken out first with flag 35, leaving the shape update nothing to pay for. DoorBlock does exactly this through DoublePlantBlock.preventDropFromBottomPart . The partner takes whichever side is free The first version always put it on the counter-clockwise side and refused outright when that cell was occupied. Because the side is decided by the way the player happens to be facing, that silently did nothing roughly half the time — and the fix a player finds for that is to click the neighbouring block instead, which works, and teaches them the gate takes two clicks. Owner: “clicking and nothing happens, then you have to click again on the other block, is not an intuitive gameplay design” . Placement now tries both sides and only refuses when neither has room. setPlacedBy writes the partner as the opposite of whatever was placed rather than a hardcoded right half, or both blocks would claim the same half and each delete the other. Opening can flip FACING , and that swaps which side the partner is on Vanilla turns a gate to swing away from whoever opened it. Applied here, that same turn reverses the direction each half must look in, so a flip swaps both halves’ half value as well, pointing each back at the block it was already paired with. This is the whole reason the open/close path is written out rather than delegated to super . The two writes must not be observed separately A pair is two blocks and cannot move in one setBlock . Vanilla’s gate flag 10 does not carry UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE , so shape updates run between the two writes — harmless while only OPEN changes, because the halves still differ, and not harmless when the flip has momentarily made both blocks read as the same half. Each then sees no partner opposite it and one deletes itself. Both writes therefore set UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE ; nothing is lost, because neither write changes what the gate is adjacent to and so there is no IN_WALL to recompute. And the first half is re-asserted after the second exists BlockItem.placeBlock writes the first half with flag 11 , which also does not suppress neighbour shape updates — so updateShape ran while the partner cell was still air, saw an orphan and removed it, all before setPlacedBy had a chance to place the partner. The server recovered and the client was never told, so the first half of a brand-new pair rendered as nothing until something forced a chunk update. setPlacedBy now writes the first half again once the partner is down. The assets are generated, and one wood is irregular Twelve blocks, each with a 32-variant blockstate, eight models, an item definition, a recipe and a loot table — over a hundred and fifty files differing only in a wood name and a texture. neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-double-gates.mjs derives all of them from one table; re-run it after touching a wood or any of the geometry. It only ever writes , so removing a wood means deleting that wood’s files by hand as well. The models could not simply parent to vanilla’s. template_fence_gate draws a post at both ends, so two of them side by side show a double post down the middle — exactly what a double gate is meant not to look like. Each half here keeps its outer post, drops the inner one, and runs its rails the full way to the block boundary, so the pair reads as one opening with a post at each end. Bamboo is textured with bamboo_planks , not vanilla’s bespoke block/bamboo_fence_gate sheet. That sheet is drawn for vanilla’s geometry, whose rails sit at x 2–6 and 10–14; these run one rail the full 2–14 and so sample the wrong regions of it, and the gate came out visibly scrambled. A planks texture tiles and has no alignment to lose, which is why the other eleven were fine either way. The modded path makes the same call in the same order — ModdedMaterials.plankTexture takes <name>_planks.png if the mod ships one and only falls back to reading a texture id out of that mod’s own gate model when it does not, because a mod whose gate sheet is as bespoke as bamboo’s would scramble in exactly the same way. The same contract is now written twice, and it can drift Change one, change the other neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-double-gates.mjs writes the nine kinds of file for vanilla’s twelve woods at author time, in JavaScript . compat/GeneratedGates writes the same nine kinds for modded woods at run time, in Java . They are one contract in two languages. Change the shape of the blockstate, a model, the item definition, the loot table or the recipe in one and you must change it in the other. The symptom of missing is a modded gate that is a purple chequerboard while every vanilla one is fine, with nothing logged anywhere. That is this layer’s signature silent failure: an item definition or a blockstate pointing at a model nobody wrote does not warn, it just draws the missing texture. Nothing checks the two agree — there is no parity test for this pair — so the only protection is knowing they are a pair. CLAUDE.md carries the rule as well. The reverse-direction trap is the item icon. There is no bare <id> model to point an item definition at, because a double gate is two blocks and neither is “the” block; both writers point at the left half, which is the one the template gives vanilla’s gate gui transform to. Where it came from, and the style that is not here Written from scratch. The mod that prompted it is Fabric-only, publishes no source, and is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — so there was nothing to port even had that been wanted, and a ShareAlike term would not have sat with this repo’s CC BY-NC 4.0 anyway. Only the idea is borrowed, which is not a thing copyright covers. That listing also advertised “farm-style variant gates” , and a second style shipped on the strength of those four words: the same block with four rails instead of two. With no screenshot and no way to run the original, its appearance was a guess rather than a reproduction, and it was removed the same evening on the owner’s verdict — “what is the double farm gate? It looks weird” . Two gates whose difference nobody can justify is worse than one gate. Both the generator and DoubleGates say so in a comment, so the idea is not rediscovered and re-added on the same evidence. Verified Two vanilla fence gates really do craft one (2026-09-19, by test). Losing the braided rope took this recipe outside the modded-ingredient house rule , and that rule was the only guard against another mod claiming the same inputs — a collision Minecraft does not report, it just makes one of the two items uncraftable. horsegenetics:double_gate_crafts puts two oak fence gates in a CraftingInput and asks the server’s own recipe manager what comes out, so it tests the resolution a player gets rather than whether the JSON parsed. Checked by mutation: adding a stick to the recipe fails it at tick 0 with “two oak fence gates craft nothing”. It covers oak only, and only the twelve hand-baked woods exist at test time — a modded wood’s gate is generated at run time and is still waiting to be drawn . Breaking a double gate gives back one gate, not two (2026-09-19, by test). The owner reported the duplication in play — “breaking a double fence drops two” — and horsegenetics:double_gate_drops_one is the tripwire that followed. It places a pair, breaks it with level.destroyBlock(pos, true) , and asserts exactly one gate item within two blocks; then does it again striking the other half, because left is the half the loot table pays from and breaking only one of the two would pass with the condition on the wrong half. Deliberately not helper.destroyBlock , which passes dropBlock=false and would have passed however wrong the table was. Checked by mutation, not trusted: reverting oak’s loot table to the unconditional one fails it at tick 0 with “Expected 1 items of type Oak Double Fence Gate: found 2” — the reported bug reproduced exactly — while the other six tests go on passing. The creative half of the fix is not covered by it : the test breaks blocks without a player, so playerWillDestroy never runs. Breaking either half in creative and getting nothing is still unseen. Redstone moves the pair, not a half (2026-09-18, by test rather than by hand). horsegenetics:double_gate_redstone places both halves, powers a redstone block beside the left one only, and asserts that the right half reports open and powered . Vanilla's rule asks only about its own block, so a pair inheriting it unchanged would swing the powered half and leave the other shut — which is why the assertion is on the far half. Run it with ./gradlew :neoforge-26.1.2:runGameTest ; it is headless and takes about sixteen seconds. Checked by mutation, not trusted: moving the redstone out of range fails it at tick 102 while the other test goes on passing. Played 2026-09-17, and the pair renders correctly. Confirmed in a world by the owner across several woods: once both halves are down they look, in her words, “GREAT”, and the inventory icons are right. Bamboo confirmed the same day, after the texture change — “bamboo gate now looks correct”. It had been sampling vanilla's bespoke block/bamboo_fence_gate sheet, which is drawn for vanilla's rail positions rather than these, and came out scrambled; it uses bamboo_planks like the other eleven now. The pair survives being worked as fast as a player can click , confirmed the same day: “I can open it as fast as I want with no issues”. That is the check for the UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE change above — before it, opening from the wrong side flipped FACING between the two halves' writes, both blocks briefly read as the same half, and one deleted itself: “half broken, other half invisible but still changing the model of the fence post next to it”. One-click placement confirmed the same day too — “placement works great” — after the partner was changed to take whichever side is free. Still unseen: the item names, their placement in the Building Blocks tab, the in-wall variants, redstone on a pair, and the stall step ticking. See verification . A gate for a modded wood has never been drawn at all — not in a world, not in a slot, and not against a real third-party mod, only a synthetic test one ( §0-FK ).
