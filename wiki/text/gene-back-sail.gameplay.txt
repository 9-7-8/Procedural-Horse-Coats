Back sail
(Magical gene / recessive / grows geometry, paints nothing)
Two copies and the horse grows a sail along its back: a row of spines from the withers to the croup, with a see-through membrane stretched between them. Whether it peaks over the middle of the back, runs low the whole length or sags in scallops, and how tall, arched, long and see-through it is, is written on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own, back sail colour . The one big, rare body part, and the first grown part you can see through.

Crossing two of them Two copies, either sex Recessive: only Sail/Sail grows a sail. Sail/n is a carrier and looks like any other horse. Two carriers bred together give a sailed foal about one time in four, and two sailed horses always do. It is not sex-limited. Foals grow none; the sail comes in when the horse grows up, and stays. Under a saddle The sail runs through where a saddle and a rider sit. A bare horse nobody is riding shows all of it. Put a saddle on, or get on, and the spines under the saddle are hidden, each taking the membrane in front of it, so what stays is a sail over the withers and another over the croup. A horse steered bare at the top bond has no saddle to draw, but its rider still hides the middle. A short sail that covers only the front of the back can vanish under a saddle almost entirely. Finding one Rare in the wild: about one horse in four hundred has a sail, and about one in ten carries a hidden copy. The skeleton horses can grow one as bare bone rays with no skin, as one of their horn-like parts. What varies Form : tall (highest over the middle of the back, like a Spinosaurus ), low (a ridge fin the full length, under half as tall) or scalloped (the tall outline, with the membrane sagging in an arch between each pair of spines). Height : four classes, low to towering , the tallest near a block high. Low to tall in the wild, and a line bred for height reaches towering and stops. Arch : how far the ends fall below the peak, from a gentle dome to a sharp crest. Reach : how far back along the back it runs, from two-thirds of the way to the croup to all of it. Count : how many spines there are, from the withers back. Over half a row to a full one in the wild. Membrane : how see-through the skin is, from faint to nearly solid. It never goes fully solid or fully clear. Everything stacks: a horse can wear a sail, a row of dorsal spines and every head part at once. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no attack, no hitbox. It is a look. Its gene carrot
