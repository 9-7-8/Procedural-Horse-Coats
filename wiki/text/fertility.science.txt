Fertility & gestation
(Start here / breeding)
Mares come into heat, and breeding through this mod’s own tools makes a pregnancy . The foal arrives when it ends, not the moment the pair is bred. Plain golden carrots still give a foal at once, exactly as in vanilla. Fertility is inherited: the fertility gene decides how readily a horse breeds and how often a mare carries twins.

Why golden carrots stay instant The owner’s standing rule is that this mod adds to vanilla and does not take vanilla away, and a pregnancy replaces the instant foal outright. So the pregnancy lives only on paths vanilla never had: the jar, the carrots and the gene. The one thing fertility may do to plain breeding is the subfertile allele, which takes two copies in a parent. An unbred horse is never sf/sf , so unbred stock breeds exactly as vanilla does. The epigenetic number is kept off that path for the same reason. The three options this was chosen from, and every other call, are under settled decisions . What real mares do The two sexes run on different clocks. A mare cycles seasonally and has a short fertile window. A mature stallion can breed all year. And a mare can conceive again while she is still nursing, often within about a week of foaling ( MSD Veterinary Manual ). Breeding’s science tab gives the same biology in brief, for the pedigree side. A seasonal 21-day cycle Mares are long-day, seasonally polyestrous breeders. Most begin cycling as the days lengthen in spring, keep cycling through summer, and stop or nearly stop in winter. That lands an eleven-month pregnancy on the next spring, when a newborn has the best conditions. During the season a cycling mare ovulates about every 21 days. Unreliable: behaviour misleads The practical breeding target is the day or two before ovulation, through ovulation itself. Sperm from a live cover or fresh semen survives long enough to meet the egg. Frozen semen does not last as long, so it is timed much closer to ovulation ( PetMD ). What heat looks like A mare in heat takes an interest in a stallion and stands near him. She raises her tail and repeatedly exposes and “winks” the vulva, urinates in small, frequent amounts, and stands still to be mounted. These signs mean she is receptive . They do not time ovulation, so breeding programmes add teasing, ultrasound and sometimes hormone tests to find the fertile window. Stallions: no heat cycle A stallion does not come into heat. Once he is mature and sound, he can make sperm and breed all year. He is still somewhat seasonal: semen output, libido and even testicular size peak in the long days and drop in winter, varying with the individual, latitude, management, nutrition, age and artificial light. His fertility is also more than willingness. It depends on semen concentration, motility and morphology, on reproductive health, and on how often he is used. So a stallion may show interest at almost any time, while a mare must be in heat and near ovulation for a good chance of conception. Pregnancy and twins Gestation averages about eleven months , usually given as 320–370 days ( Wikipedia, Mare ). Progesterone suppresses cycling, so a pregnant mare does not normally come back into heat. A mare usually carries one foal. Twins are a veterinary emergency in horses and far less likely to end in two normal foals. Foal heat, and why breeders often skip it After foaling the system restarts quickly. Most mares have a first postpartum heat, the foal heat , beginning about 5–12 days after birth, with ovulation often at 7–10 days. A mare can therefore be bred, and become pregnant, while a newborn is still at her side. The natural rhythm of a healthy mare is to foal in spring, come into foal heat a week later, conceive, nurse the foal through much of the next pregnancy, and foal again about a year on. A foal-heat breeding can succeed, but a vet may advise waiting for the next cycle, about 25–35 days after foaling. Common reasons are a retained placenta or abnormal discharge, a difficult delivery, uterine injury or infection, a uterus slow to return to normal (delayed involution), poor body condition or a heavy lactation load, an older or less fertile mare, or a sick foal that needs more from her. The deciding factor is usually uterine recovery and overall condition , not the milk itself. Nursing does not stop conception Unlike many mammals, a nursing mare can cycle, ovulate and conceive. Lactation matters indirectly: a mare carrying a pregnancy and feeding a foal at once needs enough calories, protein, minerals and water, and a poor-condition mare conceives and carries less well. Some mares with a foal at foot also tease as unreceptive because their attention is on the foal, which does not mean they are not cycling ( MSD Veterinary Manual, owners' edition ). Compressing 340 days A normal Minecraft day is 24 000 ticks, about twenty minutes ( Minecraft Wiki ; also xgamingserver ). Fitting a 340-day pregnancy into one day is a 340× compression, so one horse day becomes about 3.5 seconds. Applied evenly to everything, it gives this: Stage Real reference Share of a 340-day gestation At a 20-minute gestation Mare cycle 21 days 6.2% about 74 seconds Estrus 5 days 1.5% 17.6 seconds Peak fertility 2 days 0.6% 7.1 seconds Diestrus 16 days 4.7% 56.5 seconds Foal heat begins 7 days after foaling 2.1% 24.7 seconds after birth Postpartum recovery 10 days 2.9% 35.3 seconds Lactation, and weaning 120–180 days 35–53% 7.1–10.6 minutes Breeding season about 180 days 53% 10.6 minutes A whole year 365 days 107% 21.5 minutes Accurate, and unplayable: a heat would be over before a player noticed it. The general form is stageTicks = gestationTicks × referenceDays / 340 , and everything below is about what to do with the short end. What the mod does: one-day floors, stages that nest Each independent stage is scaled by that formula and then floored at one day: max(dayTicks, gestationTicks × referenceDays / 340) ( ReproTiming , coding tab ). The references are 5 days of estrus, 16 of diestrus and 7 from foaling to foal heat. Nested stages are not floored again, or they would double-count days: The cycle is estrus plus diestrus. It is derived, not floored, so its minimum is two days, not three. Peak fertility is a window inside estrus (its second half, 80% against 50% ), not a stage after it. Foal heat reuses the estrus length. At the default setting everything collapses to “a day in heat, a day out”. Calendars considered and not built The one-day floor was one of several shapes. The others are kept here as options, and none of them is in the mod. An abstract breeding year. An invisible “year” of 24 to 56 Minecraft days, with mares cycling only in its first two thirds and anestrus in the rest, and each mare given a private offset into a 21-day (or 7-day) cycle. Players would only see in season or out of season . The per-mare offset exists: every mare’s phase comes from her UUID, so no two share their timing ( coding tab ). The seasons and anestrus do not. A 3× compression. Gestation of 16–20 Minecraft days (about 5 –6 hours of play), a 7-day cycle with 2 days of heat, foal heat 2–4 days after birth, and lactation of 9–15 days, with small random variation on each. A reproductive year of 24 days, rather than 18–19, keeps every pregnancy from being exactly one year long. The mod gets close to this by setting fertility.gestation_days to 18, but it adds no variation. Minimum floors in minutes instead of days. Keep a twenty-minute gestation but floor the short stages at gameplay lengths: a 10-minute cycle, 3 minutes of heat with a 1-minute peak, 4 minutes of recovery, foal heat 4 minutes after birth, and weaning available at 8 minutes and automatic at 12. Presets would range from instant testing (a 5-minute gestation) to long-form realism (120 minutes), with a strict_biological_scaling switch that removes the floors. The mod floors in days instead, and its testing tools make each day one real minute. Which clock, and what sleep does There are three ways to count reproductive time: World time. Timers follow the calendar, so sleeping advances them. It is intuitive, but on a server where people sleep, animals race ahead. Active ticks. Only ticks that actually run count. Sleeping, /time set and other time-altering mods move nothing. This is what the mod does : every deadline is compared against the level’s game time ( below ). A hybrid. Pregnancy advances with world time, but heat, foal heat and recovery count active ticks, so a player can sleep through a pregnancy without sleeping through a mare’s one-day heat. It is not built. Whichever clock is used, the implementation advice is the same, and the mod follows it. Store absolute tick deadlines (a due tick, a foaling tick), never a counter that pauses when a chunk unloads. Store a pregnancy the moment conception succeeds. Rank the states so a pregnant mare is never also in heat: PREGNANT , then postpartum, then heat, then diestrus. Keep nursing as a flag beside the state rather than a state of its own ( state is derived, never stepped ). Repeated covers should never push the chance past 90–95%: the mod caps every chance at 95% and allows one natural try per heat. A stallion should have a service limit of one to three mares a day: the mod takes the soft half of that and not the hard half — his first three are at full odds and every one after is halved, but there is no limit on how many mares he serves ( why ). The suggested +5–10% breeding-season bonus for stallions is not built, because there are no seasons. What the model leaves out Seasons. There is no anestrus and no spring transition. A mare cycles all year. Stallion seasonality and semen quality. A stallion’s only variables are his fertility alleles and how many covers he has made today. Body condition. A 0–100 condition score was suggested, falling while a mare is pregnant or nursing, gating breeding below about 45, and turning dangerous below 25. The mod’s hunger is the nearest thing, but it pays for healing and has no effect on fertility, pregnancy or nursing. A difficult foaling. There is no retained placenta or slow involution to delay foal heat. Every mare waits the same postpartum time, and recovery and the delay before foal heat are one stage, not two (7 and 10 real days in the reference). Variation in gestation. Every pregnancy lasts exactly the configured length. Real ones range across 320–370 days. Lactation as a timer. Nursing ends when the foal grows up or has been away from its dam for a day ( after the birth ), not on a lactation clock. Geldings. The reference data model lists gelding as a sex. The mod keeps sex genetic and records gelding on the horse’s record ( the hooks ). Why it is built this way Absolute ticks, derived state. A countdown pauses when a chunk unloads, and a stored state has to be stepped through every transition it missed. A state computed from three anchors and the time has nothing to catch up on. Drawn at conception. A carrot’s bias and both live genomes exist then and not at birth. It also means an embryo is a real object, which embryo extraction on the assisted-reproduction roadmap can read. The sire’s whole genome in every embryo. Birth may come after he is sold, unloaded or dead. The parent-stats comparison needs his body, and a stored genome is the only honest source for it. Carrots that wait. A 30-second carrot made sense when a carrot was fed at the moment of breeding. Once a mare has to be in heat, it would mostly have been wasted, so a carrot now waits on the horse until a conception uses it. Every horse breeds, once per heat. The Spontaneous Breeding gene made automatic breeding a rare trait. The owner removed it: a stallion left with mares making foals is simply what horses do. Heat and pregnancy already slow the rate to roughly one foal per mare every few Minecraft days. The one-try-per-heat rule stops a pair rolling until it succeeds, and the local cap bounds what is left. It held a clustered herd at ten for five hours ( verified ); a band spread wider than sixteen blocks is untested. Game time, not the day counter. The mod’s other day-based rules (a horse’s age, the daily bond cap) already count game time, and on a server where people sleep, day time would push pregnancies through in bursts.
