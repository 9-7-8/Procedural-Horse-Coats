Bringing a dead horse back
(Gameplay / operator tooling)
An operator's undo for the one loss nothing else can reverse. Every other way of losing a horse has a tool that finds it: sold, turned out, bottled, stabled, lost across a dimension. A horse that died had nothing, and the thing that dies is not a stack of items — it is an animal with a name somebody chose, a bond they spent weeks on and a pedigree three generations deep. So a dead horse is now kept whole for a while , and /horseresurrect spends that: the horse that stands back up is the same horse , same id, and every foal's pedigree still points at it. It is an operator's command and nothing a player can reach.

What happens when a horse dies If the horse had an owner , the whole animal is kept — not a description of it, the animal: its name, its bond, its hunger, its pedigree, its breed, a pregnancy in progress, the lot. Its owner is told, if they are online, that it can still be brought back and that they should ask an operator soon. Nothing else changes. It still drops what it was going to drop, the death notice still goes out, the pedigree still records it. A wild horse is not kept at all — there is nobody to ask for it, and between the horse realm and ordinary worldgen they die in numbers that would make this the largest thing in the save. This is the last resort, and three things come first A horse only reaches this page when everything else failed. The last stand refuses to let a single blow kill one; a rescuing braid sends a braided horse home; the Emergency Stasis Chamber bottles any horse its owner insured, before the blow lands. All three are better than this one, because the player keeps playing through them. Resurrection is what is left. How long you have ops.resurrect_grace_minutes , sixty by default — but counted in the owner's own time online , not wall clock. Somebody who logged off ten seconds before a creeper found their mare has not spent a minute of their window, because the window is their chance to notice and ask and they were not in the room. An hour of real time while they are asleep would spend a window they never had. Set it to 0 to keep every dead horse for ever . That is a real option and a real cost: a kept horse is a whole entity in the world save, per horse, indefinitely. The elapsed count keeps running while it is 0, so turning a limit back on later starts expiring the backlog rather than sparing it. The command /horseresurrect , permission level 2 (game masters), like /horsebounty . Command What it does /horseresurrect list [player] What is still recoverable, whose it was, how long ago it died and how much of its window is left. With no arguments, /horseresurrect does this. /horseresurrect <horse> It stands up in front of you. /horseresurrect <horse> at <player> In front of somebody else — the usual one, since the owner is generally stood there asking. /horseresurrect <horse> site Where it died. Loads that chunk. This is the one that can put a horse back in the lava that killed it , which is why it is never the default. /horseresurrect <horse> egg <player> Into their inventory as an egg, to place where they want it. <horse> is the horse's name — what the death notice said and what its owner will call it — and tab-completes from what is actually being kept. Two dead horses called Pepper are refused with both ids listed rather than resolved to the more recent one: that is exactly the case where guessing wrong means resurrecting the wrong animal and spending the other one's window while its owner waits. The resurrection egg egg hands the player a preset horse spawn egg with their horse inside it, and the tooltip says “brought back whole — the same horse, not a copy” so nobody mistakes it for the ordinary genotype egg. Right-click the ground and the horse is there. The horse moves out of the server's keeping and into the item at the moment the egg is made. So it stops expiring — it is no longer the save's problem, it is something somebody is holding — and there is only ever one copy. The egg is consumed even in creative, for that reason. What comes back, and what does not The same horse. Not a clone with the same coat: the same id, so its foals' family trees, its stall records and any whistle bound to it all still point at an animal that exists. Its name, its bond, its breed, its pedigree, its epigenome and its age come back exactly as they were. Not its tack. The saddle and the armour dropped on the ground where it fell, and giving them back as well would mint a second set. They are where it died; the horse comes back bare. Nor does whatever killed it come back on it. The fire is out, the effects are gone, its lungs are full and its health is refilled to what its genes say it should be.
