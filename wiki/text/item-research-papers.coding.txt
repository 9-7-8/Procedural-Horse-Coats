Research papers
(Item / knowledge)
A paper naming one gene and one allele pair — Agouti: A/a , not just Agouti . Spend it on a Known Gene Splice carrot and that is the pair the carrot hands on, which is the only way to breed for a specific combination on purpose. It is not something you read.

The component and the routes research_paper carries a research_gene data component holding a common/genetics/ResearchTopic — a gene key and two allele tokens . Right-clicking it does nothing ; the gene database is earned by owning a living horse ( server/GeneDiscoveryHandler ), never by reading. A paper is an ingredient. Why it is strings, and where it normalises ResearchTopic holds tokens rather than Allele objects because a paper outlives the gene it names: a datapack gene removed, a world opened without the mod that added it. Every resolution — gene() , pair() , splice() — answers null instead of throwing, because the alternative is an exception inside somebody’s inventory. The pair is put in canonical order in the record’s constructor, by handing it to AllelePair , which has always sorted its two slots by declaration order. So A/a and a/A are equal, produce the same token() — <geneKey>|<a>|<b> — and stack. That token is the key the research shelf, the menu sync and the block entity’s saved pick all use, which is why it is a flat string and not a record in three more places. The one piece of back-compat in this repo research_gene used to be a bare String gene key, and there are papers in chests, shelves and villager windows holding one. CLAUDE.md hard rule 6 refuses legacy shapes outright; this is the deliberate exception, asked for by name . A stored string still decodes, through ResearchTopic.wholeGene , as the homozygous non-wild pair for that gene — the strongest thing a whole-gene paper could have meant. It falls back to the carrier pair on a locus whose homozygote the gene forbids, because a paper naming an impossible pair would craft an inert carrot. The component keeps its old registry id for the same reason. Renaming it to research_topic would be tidier and would silently drop the component off every existing paper, leaving blanks. The whole of the compatibility is one Codec.either(record, string) in data/ResearchTopicCodecs ; encoding always writes the map form, so a world loaded once never writes a bare string again. It is covered by a gametest , old_papers_still_read , and not by JUnit: a component whose persistent codec refuses its stored value is dropped on load with a warning nobody reads and no crash, and the NeoForge module’s test classpath deliberately carries no Minecraft, so NbtOps and a real ItemStack only exist inside a booted game. Chest loot — injected at 30% into every chest table in every namespace by a global loot modifier, with the gene weighted by its rarity tier ( data/loot/AddResearchPaperModifier ). It was 18% across eight named vanilla tables until 2026-09-24; what replaced the list is a regex condition . The owner calls these “gene books”, after what the shelf copies them onto. Off a horse — server/GeneBookFromHorse , on EntityInteract and EntityInteractSpecific with a book. It collects every locus the horse is not homozygous-baseline at (the same test discovery uses) and picks one uniformly, writing down the pair the horse actually carries . No JSON recipe: the result depends on the animal, and this is the only source of a compound pair. Any horse will do , and it needs no taming: your own, a stranger's, one of the cowboy's branded string, or one released into the horse realm . Ownership gates keeping a horse, not looking at one. Both listeners sit at HIGHEST on purpose — at the default priority the cowboy case worked only because this class sorts earlier in the jar than TransferPaperHandler , which cancels any held item on a branded horse, and NeoForge skips a default listener outright once an event is cancelled. A book on the cowboy's string was one class rename from silently doing nothing. The equestrian supplier — data/loot/SetRandomGeneFunction with target: "paper" , run as a trade’s given_item_modifiers so the offer is rolled once per restock rather than under the player’s cursor. Its homozygous flag narrows the pool to the true-breeding half. Traded from another player — which is the point of the second route: knowledge is shareable rather than hoardable. Which pairs a source may hand out A gene is drawn weighted by rarity, as it always was; the pair inside it then comes from ResearchTopic.lootPool — for each variant allele, the carrier pair variant/wild and the true-breeding pair variant/variant , each filtered through canOccur and sexConsistent . Deliberately not every combination the locus allows (owner’s call). A forty-allele locus has eight hundred pairs, and one of them being in this chest is not a find, it is noise; eighty is a collection. The compound pairs still exist and still make a carrot — a horse hands you one, which is the route that is supposed to be interesting. The recipe reads the pair off the paper server/recipe/KnownGeneSpliceRecipe resolves to a ResearchTopic rather than a Gene , and emits topic.splice() . It refuses a grid whose paper does not isResolved() — a retired gene, or a pair the locus says cannot occur — because refusing to resolve is visible and an inert carrot is not . What the plan expected to die, and did not This page’s Roadmap tab predicted that Gene.geneCarrotHomozygous() and CarrotEffect.defaultSpliceFor would both stop being needed once the player picked both alleles. They survived , because that plan had the shelf binding two single-allele papers into a book, and the shipped shape puts the pair on the paper instead — which leaves three callers that have a gene and no paper in hand: the Horse Browser’s recipe ghost ( menu/SpliceRecipeDisplay ), each gene page’s gene-carrot card through web/DesignerApi.geneCarrotJson , and the scientist’s carrot trades, which draw a gene and never see a paper at all. For those it is now ResearchTopic.defaultFor : an example pair, not a claim about every carrot for the locus. Both the card and the browser say so in those words. The shelf keys on the pair EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity files one paper per pair , not per gene, so Agouti: A/A and Agouti: A/a sit side by side — which is the whole reason to file them, since those two craft different carrots. topicOf , topicsIn and holdsElsewhere all key on ResearchTopic.token() , the Copy tab’s rows read Agouti: A/a , and copyTicks is still the gene’s rarity — what is rare is the locus, and a carrier paper is no easier to transcribe than a true-breeding one. The carrot says which pair it carries A known_gene_splice_carrot has always carried the right data and never shown it, so every one of them looked identical in the inventory. That was tolerable while the pair was a fixed convention per gene and is not now that a player can hold two carrots for one locus that do different things. All eleven breeding carrots therefore share one class, item/BreedingCarrotItem , whose only job is a tooltip built from the carrot_effects tokens — so a combination carrot lists every effect it merged, and a carrot for a gene this build no longer has says so in red rather than being quietly inert.
