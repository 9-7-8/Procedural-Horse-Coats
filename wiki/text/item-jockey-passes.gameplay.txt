Jockey passes
(Item / trading)
Lending somebody your horse for an afternoon. A pass is made out to one animal by its owner, handed to whoever is going to ride it, and fed to that horse to buy a day in the saddle — and then it runs out on its own, which is the whole point of it.

How they work Craft a blank jockey pass : paper, a horse hair and a piece of leather, shapeless. It names nobody and does nothing. Right-click a horse you own with the blank. It becomes a pass made out to that animal by name. Give the pass to whoever is going to ride. It is an ordinary item from here on — hand it over, put it in a chest, sell it at the gate. They right-click that horse with it. The horse takes the pass and they may ride it for one Minecraft day . Why this exists A horse will not carry a stranger . The two standing ways round that — owning the horse, or being on the owner's team — are both permanent relationships, and neither of them can say this person, this horse, this afternoon . Putting a jockey on your team so they can ride one mount in one race hands them every horse you own, indefinitely, and taking them off again afterwards is a chore nobody is going to do. A race meeting needs something that expires by itself. Things worth knowing A pass is made out to the horse, not to the person. That is what makes it worth having as an object: a pass for Kestrel, one day is something you can hand to whoever turns up to ride, sell at the gate, or post to a friend. A pass made out to a person would have to be assigned when it was made, and a jockey who did not show up would take it out of the meeting with them. Only the owner can make one out , and creative is no exception — the same rule every other item you bind to a horse follows. Anybody holding a made-out pass can feed it. Feeding a second pass adds another day ; it does not replace what is left. A three-day meeting is three passes, and a jockey handed two on the first morning is not quietly refunded one. It only works on the horse it names. A pass for Kestrel does nothing at all to anything else, and says so. The owner is told when somebody feeds a pass to their horse, if they are online. Lending a horse is not a secret, and an owner who finds a stranger on their mare should be able to remember why. Selling the horse cancels every pass on it. A pass is permission the previous owner gave; carrying it across a sale would hand the new owner's horse to strangers they never agreed to and could not find out about. You cannot feed one to your own horse. It would buy you nothing, so you are told rather than quietly charged a pass. The command — /horsejockey The same lend, without the crafting, for the case the item is clumsy at: a race steward at the gate with twelve riders to admit and no wish to make twelve passes. It acts on the horse you are riding , or the one you are looking at within eight blocks, and every form needs you to own that horse. Form What it does /horsejockey <player> One pass's worth, the same length an item buys. /horsejockey <player> <days> That many Minecraft days, added to whatever is left. /horsejockey <player> revoke Take it back now. If they are on the horse, they come off. /horsejockey list Who is borrowing this horse, and for how much longer. Revoking has no item , and should not: something you have handed to somebody cannot be un-handed, but the person who lent the horse ought to be able to change their mind. No operator override. An operator settling a dispute can move ownership with /horsegive ; being able to write yourself onto somebody else's horse for a fortnight is a different power and nobody asked for it. commands.horse_jockey turns the command off — not registered, not tab-completed — and leaves the item alone. behaviour.jockey_pass_days is what one pass, or one bare /horsejockey , is worth.
