Rider comfort
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Five small things that make riding less irritating, borrowed from other mods that already do them. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship alone. One of the five has shipped — a lead now comes back to you rather than being left on the ground when this mod teleports the horse wearing it. The other four are still plans, and the last of them — fading the horse out as you look down — touches the coat itself and is the only one with a real unknown in it.

How the lead is returned (2026-09-24) server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player) , and every path that teleports a horse calls it instead of untying the horse itself. The distinction it is built on is one method apart in vanilla. Leashable.dropLeash() unties and spawns an Items.LEAD at the animal’s position; Leashable.removeLeash() is the same untie with the drop suppressed — both are dropLeash(entity, sendPacket, dropLead) with different arguments. So the whole change is removeLeash() plus the same give-or-drop HorseDietHandler uses for a bucket remainder. With the config off, or with no player to give it to, it falls back to dropLeash() and vanilla is back exactly. It is safe on an unleashed horse — it returns immediately — so the call sites dropped their own isLeashed() guards rather than keeping a second copy of the check. TicketHandler.arrive grew a Player parameter for it, which is the only signature this touched. The two portal paths, server/PortalEventHandler and server/HorsePortalManager , are deliberately not converted and still call dropLeash() . They untie a horse that walked into a portal itself, with its holder standing next to it, so the lead is already within reach and vanilla’s behaviour is the one that reads correctly. Guarded by the whistled_lead_comes_back gametest in gametest/ModGameTests — in a gametest rather than JUnit because the NeoForge module’s test classpath carries no Minecraft, and the assertion is about a real Leashable , a real inventory and whether an ItemEntity appeared in a real level. It asserts the two halves separately : no lead on the ground, and a lead in the player’s pack. Passing the first while failing the second would be worse than the behaviour it replaced, so one combined assertion would have been the wrong test. It was confirmed to fail against the old dropLeash() before being kept. It uses helper.makeMockPlayer rather than makeMockServerPlayerInLevel , which is @Deprecated(forRemoval) and really joins the player list. A leash holder only has to be an Entity — Leashable.setLeashedTo never asks whether it is in the level — and a mock player’s inventory is a real one.
