Rider comfort
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Five small things that make riding less irritating, borrowed from other mods that already do them. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship alone. Four of the five have shipped, and one is left — a lead comes back to you now whether this mod teleported the horse out from under it or the leash simply snapped, you can mine from the saddle at the speed you would standing there, ALT + E opens your own pack from the saddle, and a piece of tack goes on with a right-click instead of a trip through the Gear tab. What remains is fading the horse out as you look down, which touches the coat itself and is the only one with a real unknown in it.

How the lead is returned (2026-09-24) server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player) , and every path that teleports a horse calls it instead of untying the horse itself. The distinction it is built on is one method apart in vanilla. Leashable.dropLeash() unties and spawns an Items.LEAD at the animal’s position; Leashable.removeLeash() is the same untie with the drop suppressed — both are dropLeash(entity, sendPacket, dropLead) with different arguments. So the whole change is removeLeash() plus the same give-or-drop HorseDietHandler uses for a bucket remainder. With the config off, or with no player to give it to, it falls back to dropLeash() and vanilla is back exactly. It is safe on an unleashed horse — it returns immediately — so the call sites dropped their own isLeashed() guards rather than keeping a second copy of the check. TicketHandler.arrive grew a Player parameter for it, which is the only signature this touched. The two portal paths, server/PortalEventHandler and server/HorsePortalManager , are deliberately not converted and still call dropLeash() . They untie a horse that walked into a portal itself, with its holder standing next to it, so the lead is already within reach and vanilla’s behaviour is the one that reads correctly. Guarded by the whistled_lead_comes_back gametest in gametest/ModGameTests — in a gametest rather than JUnit because the NeoForge module’s test classpath carries no Minecraft, and the assertion is about a real Leashable , a real inventory and whether an ItemEntity appeared in a real level. It asserts the two halves separately : no lead on the ground, and a lead in the player’s pack. Passing the first while failing the second would be worse than the behaviour it replaced, so one combined assertion would have been the wrong test. It was confirmed to fail against the old dropLeash() before being kept. It uses helper.makeMockPlayer rather than makeMockServerPlayerInLevel , which is @Deprecated(forRemoval) and really joins the player list. A leash holder only has to be an Entity — Leashable.setLeashedTo never asks whether it is in the level — and a mock player’s inventory is a real one. How the mounted mining penalty is removed (2026-09-29) server/MountedMiningHandler , one listener on PlayerEvent.BreakSpeed , and nothing else. The ordering in vanilla is what makes this exact. Player.getDestroySpeed ends with if (!this.onGround()) speed /= 5.0F; and then calls EventHooks.getBreakSpeed with the already-divided figure — read out of the decompiled 26.1.2 method rather than assumed, because if the event fired first the handler would be multiplying by five only for vanilla to divide it straight back. As it stands, one multiply by the same five restores the undivided speed precisely. The guard is on the penalty, not on the posture. The obvious shape — “is the player mounted? then multiply” — becomes a 5× mining boost the moment its premise is wrong, and the premise (that a mounted player’s onGround is false) is a reading of vanilla’s movement code, not something this mod controls. So the condition asks !player.onGround() directly: the multiply happens only where the division provably just happened. If a future version ever leaves a rider standing on their horse, this quietly does nothing instead of handing out five times the mining speed. The vehicle is checked too — getVehicle() instanceof AbstractHorse and that horse onGround() — which is what keeps a jumping or flying horse out. It runs on both logical sides , registered with no Dist . The client predicts break progress from its own call to the same method, so a server-only handler would draw a crack that crept along while the block broke early. That is safe because ServerConfig is ModConfig.Type.SERVER , which FML’s own javadoc on ModConfig.Type describes as “synced to clients during connection” — so both sides read the server’s value rather than two independent defaults. This is the first place in the mod that reads a server config from client-side code; the leadsReturn() -style accessor ( mountedMiningPenaltyRemoved() , catching IllegalStateException ) is what covers the window before the sync lands. Guarded by the mounted_mining_is_exempt gametest in gametest/ModGameTests , which posts three real BreakSpeed events through NeoForge.EVENT_BUS — so a handler written but never registered fails it. It asserts the exemption and both refusals separately , because the failure worth catching is not that the handler does nothing: it is that it fires when vanilla charged nothing. That middle assertion was confirmed to fail, with got 10.0 where 2.0 was expected, against a copy of the handler with the onGround guard deleted. The ground flags in the test are set by hand rather than played out. Whether a real mounted player’s onGround is genuinely false is a claim about vanilla’s movement code, not about this handler — the Verification tab owns that half . What the test owns is that given each posture, the handler does the right thing. How the inventory key is bound (2026-09-29) client/RiderInventoryKeyBindings and client/RiderInventoryKeyHandler , the same two-class split as HorseBrowserKeyBindings / HorseBrowserKeyHandler : the mapping is registered on the mod bus from RegisterKeyMappingsEvent , the clicks are drained on the game bus from ClientTickEvent.Post . No payload, no menu type, no server code at all. Nothing is sent to the server, and nothing needs to be. A player’s own InventoryMenu is not opened on demand — it is live for the whole session — which is why vanilla’s own E branch in Minecraft.handleKeybinds calls setScreen rather than sending anything. HorseBrowserKeyHandler made the same simplification. The one case where vanilla does send a packet is MultiPlayerGameMode.isServerControlledInventory() , which is true precisely when you are riding something with a screen of its own — the case this key exists to step around, so following vanilla there would have defeated it. Creative mode gets no branch , and that is a deliberate departure from the plan this was built from, which said to pick the screen class by game mode. Reading 26.1.2 says not to: InventoryScreen ’s own init and its containerTick both open by checking player.hasInfiniteMaterials() and swapping themselves out for a CreativeModeInventoryScreen . Constructing the survival screen is therefore what vanilla itself does in both modes, and a hand-rolled game-mode test would have been a second copy of that rule — wrong the moment the first one moved. Post rather than the Pre its neighbour uses. HorseInfoKeyHandler has to run in ClientTickEvent.Pre because it is stealing vanilla’s keyInventory click and must get there before handleKeybinds does. This handler steals nothing: it owns its own mapping, so it runs in Post like the browser key. The chord, and why it does not double-fire This is the mod’s first KeyModifier . Every other mapping here is a bare key, and WhistleKeyBindings says in as many words that it avoided a chord to keep the modifier API out of it. The obvious worry is that ALT + E would fire both mappings — this one and vanilla’s bare keyInventory , which HorseInfoKeyHandler is sitting on — leaving you with the horse screen on top and your own inventory swallowed. It does not, and the reason is in neoforge/client/settings/KeyMappingLookup.getAll . With ALT held it collects the bindings registered under ALT first, and only then if (releasing || matchingBindings.isEmpty()) adds the unmodified ones. A live ALT+E match is a non-empty list, so the bare- E fallback never runs and keyInventory.consumeClick() is never satisfied. The suppression is NeoForge’s, not ours, and it is why this needed no coordination with HorseInfoKeyHandler at all. Flagged unverified in the source, per the house rule: that is a reading of the 26.1.2 sources, not something a client has done yet, and it covers the Controls screen too — whether the row draws as “Alt + E” or as a bare E that looks like a conflict with vanilla’s. The Verification tab owns both , and names the fallback if the chord will not hold. The category is KeyMapping.Category.INVENTORY , where the rest of the mod uses MISC . Still a built-in category with nothing to register or localise, so the reason MISC was chosen elsewhere is not violated — but it puts the row directly beneath vanilla’s “Open/Close Inventory”, which is the part of Controls a player is reading when they go looking for what happened to E . No test. There is nothing here a gametest could hold: the whole of it is a keybind registration and a setScreen on the client, and the one claim worth checking — that the chord suppresses the bare key — is a fact about NeoForge’s input layer that only a real client exercises. It is a Verification-tab item rather than a missing test. How the snapped lead is returned (2026-09-29) Two mixins, a stored UUID and thirty lines in server/HorseLeads beside the half that shipped on 2026-09-24. It is much more machinery than that half, and the reason is that neither end of the problem is where you would look for it . The drop cannot be hooked where it happens Leashable.dropLeash() and removeLeash() are both one-line delegates to dropLeash(E, boolean, boolean) , which is private static on the interface — and that is not a place a mixin can inject. NeoForge publishes no leash event either; there is no class in its event package with “leash” in the name, re-checked rather than taken from the note that said so. So the seam is the line it calls, not the method it is. Every lead vanilla loses is spawned by entity.spawnAtLocation(level, Items.LEAD) , and a search of the whole game finds exactly two callers that pass that method a lead — the drop itself, and the fallback in restoreLeashFromSave for a leash whose holder no longer exists on load. Both are in Leashable . So one injection on an ordinary public method of Entity covers every path, including the two that arrive from completely different directions: tickLeash for a snap, and a holder failing canInteractWithLevel for a knot somebody broke. That is mixin/LeashDropMixin , and the general lesson is on api notes . And the owner has to be written down before it breaks By the time the lead is dropped there is no player anywhere in the situation . A knot is the holder, not a person; and dropLeash runs entity.setLeashData(null) before it spawns the item, so even the knot is gone. The only moment the answer exists is when the lead goes on, which is mixin/LeashPlacerMixin on Mob.setLeashData — a concrete override of an abstract interface method, and so reachable where the rest of the leash plumbing is not. The rules it writes are three, and the two that are not “record the player” are the interesting ones. A non-player holder on a fresh leash (a dispenser tying a horse straight to a post) clears whoever was there before, or a lead somebody else tied on would come back to them. A null holder, or null data , leaves the record alone: null data is what vanilla writes the instant before it drops the lead, and clearing there would wipe the answer a moment before it is asked for — while a null holder inside real data is a leash being restored from a save, where clearing would lose the placer of every fence-tied horse on every world load. The fence transition is handled by vanilla’s own control flow rather than by a rule here , which is the part most worth knowing. Leashable.setLeashedTo calls setLeashData only when the animal had no leash data at all; once it has some it takes a leashData.setLeashHolder(holder) branch that never reaches the mixin. That looks like a gap and is the opposite of one: tying a horse to a fence is always a second attachment, because LeadItem.bindPlayerMobs only considers animals already held by you. So the knot inherits the player recorded a moment earlier, for free. The record lives in a leash_placer attachment — serialized, since a horse can stand tied up across a restart; not synced, since nothing on the client reads it. It is deliberately not cleared when the lead comes off, because the drop reads it after vanilla has already untied the horse. A stale value is harmless: nothing consults it unless a lead is being dropped, and the next person to tie one on overwrites it. What the test can and cannot reach vanilla_lead_comes_back in gametest/ModGameTests , and it is shaped by two things that went wrong in its first draft. It drops nothing on the ground. The first version asserted the fallback by really calling dropLeash() and finding the lead on the floor, and it broke whistled_lead_comes_back — whose own assertItemEntityNotPresent reaches sixteen blocks and found this test’s lead in the neighbouring plot. A gametest that litters the shared world fails its neighbours rather than itself, which is a bad way to find out. And it makes no real player. A gametest server has nobody on it, so the only player getPlayerList() could find is one built by makeMockServerPlayerInLevel — @Deprecated(forRemoval) , and it really joins the player list, which fires this mod’s join payloads down an EmbeddedChannel that has negotiated nothing and takes the whole run down with “Payload horsegenetics:gene_database_sync may not be sent to the client” . So the test drives HorseLeads.giveRecordedLeadTo and the one line above it — the player-list lookup — is the only part of this left to the in-game check. Proving the mixins are applied is not the test’s job , and it would be a weaker guarantee than the one already in place: the mixin config’s defaultRequire of 1 means a target that stops matching refuses to boot the game . What the test owns is everything that would still be wrong with both mixins happily applied — that the placer is recorded, that the fence does not erase it, that an unreachable placer is refused so vanilla still drops, that the wrong player is refused, and that the record is cleared afterwards. That last one was confirmed to fail, with “the placer was not cleared… a second drop would mint a second lead out of nothing” , against a copy with the clear deleted. How the tack click works (2026-09-29) server/TackEquipHandler , one listener on PlayerInteractEvent.EntityInteract , plus behaviour.rightclick_equips_tack and the right_click_equips_tack gametest. It is much smaller than it was planned to be, because two thirds of it turned out to be vanilla’s already. What vanilla was already doing The plan this was built from assumed right-click-to-equip did not exist in 26.1.2 and marked it unverified , with an instruction to read AbstractHorse.mobInteract before writing anything. Reading it settles both halves of the piece at once, and against it: The saddle is vanilla. Items.SADDLE carries Equippable.saddle() , which is built with setEquipOnInteract(true) , so ItemStack.interactLivingEntity routes through Equippable.equipOnTarget and the saddle is on before mobInteract reaches doPlayerRide . It even declines to swap already: equipOnTarget requires !target.hasItemInSlot(this.slot) . The barding is vanilla too. mobInteract ends with if (this.isEquippableInSlot(itemStack, EquipmentSlot.BODY) && !this.isWearingBodyArmor()) and equips it. That predicate is character-for-character the one HorseTackSlot.accepts asks for the BARDING slot, so there is provably nothing this mod could add there. So the handler skips every isVanilla() slot deliberately. Taking them over would have put two code paths on the same saddle, and the loser would have been the one with the equip sound and the guaranteed drop. What is left is the seventeen that ride in the HORSE_GEAR attachment , which vanilla cannot see and which had exactly one way in. The treatment’s pre-agreed fallback for this case said to keep the handler for “armour and the mod’s own tack slots”, on the expectation that only the saddle would turn out to be vanilla’s. Armour is vanilla’s as well, so what shipped is the second half of that sentence only. The instruction not to retire the piece was the right call regardless — the seventeen slots are the part nothing covered. Which clicks it claims, and the two it must not LOWEST , the same priority and the same reasoning as HorseInteractionHandler.onFoalInventory : every other horse interaction in this mod cancels the event when it claims a click, and a cancelled event is never delivered to a lower priority. So this only ever sees a click nothing else wanted, and an interaction added later needs no change here. It is above vanilla at any priority, since the event fires from Player.interactOn before mobInteract is called at all. The item is tested before the horse is — isModTack walks the roster asking each non-vanilla slot’s tag — so a click holding anything else is untouched, including by the refusals. Getting that order wrong would have meant a carrot earning a “not your horse” line. Two refusals are claimed and two are not, and the difference is load-bearing. Not-your-horse and a foal cancel the event and send a line, because both are rules this mod invented and an invented rule that fails silently reads as a bug — the same reasoning behind the name tag’s not_yours message. An untamed horse, and a piece whose every slot is already full, are left uncancelled : vanilla then rears the wild horse, and lets you mount your own. A handler that cancelled all four would look right in game until somebody could not get on a horse while holding a spare braid. Ownership is HorseOwnership.isOwner , the same question the Gear tab’s own TackSlotPayload asks before it believes a click, so the two ways in agree. The slot chosen is the first empty one in roster order, which is the paper doll’s reading order, so which slot a piece lands in is predictable for the sets. Creative keeps its stack, which is this mod’s rule everywhere and not vanilla’s — equipOnTarget splits one off whatever the game mode. Guarded by right_click_equips_tack in gametest/ModGameTests , which posts seven real EntityInteract events through NeoForge.EVENT_BUS — so a handler written but never registered fails it. It asserts the claimed and unclaimed cases separately , since a handler that claimed everything is the failure worth catching. Making the full-slot case cancel turned it red with “a third braid with nowhere left to put it: the click was claimed, and it should have been left to vanilla” before the real version was kept. The test opens by asserting the rescuing braid is actually in gear/mane and gear/tail . Fifteen of the seventeen tags are still empty, so if the braid ever left its two the test would go on passing while exercising nothing at all — there would be no item in the game this handler could equip.
