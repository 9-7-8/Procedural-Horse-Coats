Rider comfort
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Five small things that make riding less irritating, borrowed from other mods that already do them. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship alone. Three of the five have shipped — a lead now comes back to you rather than being left on the ground when this mod teleports the horse wearing it, you can mine from the saddle at the speed you would standing there, and ALT + E opens your own pack from the saddle. The other two are still plans, and the last of them — fading the horse out as you look down — touches the coat itself and is the only one with a real unknown in it.

How the lead is returned (2026-09-24) server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player) , and every path that teleports a horse calls it instead of untying the horse itself. The distinction it is built on is one method apart in vanilla. Leashable.dropLeash() unties and spawns an Items.LEAD at the animal’s position; Leashable.removeLeash() is the same untie with the drop suppressed — both are dropLeash(entity, sendPacket, dropLead) with different arguments. So the whole change is removeLeash() plus the same give-or-drop HorseDietHandler uses for a bucket remainder. With the config off, or with no player to give it to, it falls back to dropLeash() and vanilla is back exactly. It is safe on an unleashed horse — it returns immediately — so the call sites dropped their own isLeashed() guards rather than keeping a second copy of the check. TicketHandler.arrive grew a Player parameter for it, which is the only signature this touched. The two portal paths, server/PortalEventHandler and server/HorsePortalManager , are deliberately not converted and still call dropLeash() . They untie a horse that walked into a portal itself, with its holder standing next to it, so the lead is already within reach and vanilla’s behaviour is the one that reads correctly. Guarded by the whistled_lead_comes_back gametest in gametest/ModGameTests — in a gametest rather than JUnit because the NeoForge module’s test classpath carries no Minecraft, and the assertion is about a real Leashable , a real inventory and whether an ItemEntity appeared in a real level. It asserts the two halves separately : no lead on the ground, and a lead in the player’s pack. Passing the first while failing the second would be worse than the behaviour it replaced, so one combined assertion would have been the wrong test. It was confirmed to fail against the old dropLeash() before being kept. It uses helper.makeMockPlayer rather than makeMockServerPlayerInLevel , which is @Deprecated(forRemoval) and really joins the player list. A leash holder only has to be an Entity — Leashable.setLeashedTo never asks whether it is in the level — and a mock player’s inventory is a real one. How the mounted mining penalty is removed (2026-09-29) server/MountedMiningHandler , one listener on PlayerEvent.BreakSpeed , and nothing else. The ordering in vanilla is what makes this exact. Player.getDestroySpeed ends with if (!this.onGround()) speed /= 5.0F; and then calls EventHooks.getBreakSpeed with the already-divided figure — read out of the decompiled 26.1.2 method rather than assumed, because if the event fired first the handler would be multiplying by five only for vanilla to divide it straight back. As it stands, one multiply by the same five restores the undivided speed precisely. The guard is on the penalty, not on the posture. The obvious shape — “is the player mounted? then multiply” — becomes a 5× mining boost the moment its premise is wrong, and the premise (that a mounted player’s onGround is false) is a reading of vanilla’s movement code, not something this mod controls. So the condition asks !player.onGround() directly: the multiply happens only where the division provably just happened. If a future version ever leaves a rider standing on their horse, this quietly does nothing instead of handing out five times the mining speed. The vehicle is checked too — getVehicle() instanceof AbstractHorse and that horse onGround() — which is what keeps a jumping or flying horse out. It runs on both logical sides , registered with no Dist . The client predicts break progress from its own call to the same method, so a server-only handler would draw a crack that crept along while the block broke early. That is safe because ServerConfig is ModConfig.Type.SERVER , which FML’s own javadoc on ModConfig.Type describes as “synced to clients during connection” — so both sides read the server’s value rather than two independent defaults. This is the first place in the mod that reads a server config from client-side code; the leadsReturn() -style accessor ( mountedMiningPenaltyRemoved() , catching IllegalStateException ) is what covers the window before the sync lands. Guarded by the mounted_mining_is_exempt gametest in gametest/ModGameTests , which posts three real BreakSpeed events through NeoForge.EVENT_BUS — so a handler written but never registered fails it. It asserts the exemption and both refusals separately , because the failure worth catching is not that the handler does nothing: it is that it fires when vanilla charged nothing. That middle assertion was confirmed to fail, with got 10.0 where 2.0 was expected, against a copy of the handler with the onGround guard deleted. The ground flags in the test are set by hand rather than played out. Whether a real mounted player’s onGround is genuinely false is a claim about vanilla’s movement code, not about this handler — the Verification tab owns that half . What the test owns is that given each posture, the handler does the right thing. How the inventory key is bound (2026-09-29) client/RiderInventoryKeyBindings and client/RiderInventoryKeyHandler , the same two-class split as HorseBrowserKeyBindings / HorseBrowserKeyHandler : the mapping is registered on the mod bus from RegisterKeyMappingsEvent , the clicks are drained on the game bus from ClientTickEvent.Post . No payload, no menu type, no server code at all. Nothing is sent to the server, and nothing needs to be. A player’s own InventoryMenu is not opened on demand — it is live for the whole session — which is why vanilla’s own E branch in Minecraft.handleKeybinds calls setScreen rather than sending anything. HorseBrowserKeyHandler made the same simplification. The one case where vanilla does send a packet is MultiPlayerGameMode.isServerControlledInventory() , which is true precisely when you are riding something with a screen of its own — the case this key exists to step around, so following vanilla there would have defeated it. Creative mode gets no branch , and that is a deliberate departure from the plan this was built from, which said to pick the screen class by game mode. Reading 26.1.2 says not to: InventoryScreen ’s own init and its containerTick both open by checking player.hasInfiniteMaterials() and swapping themselves out for a CreativeModeInventoryScreen . Constructing the survival screen is therefore what vanilla itself does in both modes, and a hand-rolled game-mode test would have been a second copy of that rule — wrong the moment the first one moved. Post rather than the Pre its neighbour uses. HorseInfoKeyHandler has to run in ClientTickEvent.Pre because it is stealing vanilla’s keyInventory click and must get there before handleKeybinds does. This handler steals nothing: it owns its own mapping, so it runs in Post like the browser key. The chord, and why it does not double-fire This is the mod’s first KeyModifier . Every other mapping here is a bare key, and WhistleKeyBindings says in as many words that it avoided a chord to keep the modifier API out of it. The obvious worry is that ALT + E would fire both mappings — this one and vanilla’s bare keyInventory , which HorseInfoKeyHandler is sitting on — leaving you with the horse screen on top and your own inventory swallowed. It does not, and the reason is in neoforge/client/settings/KeyMappingLookup.getAll . With ALT held it collects the bindings registered under ALT first, and only then if (releasing || matchingBindings.isEmpty()) adds the unmodified ones. A live ALT+E match is a non-empty list, so the bare- E fallback never runs and keyInventory.consumeClick() is never satisfied. The suppression is NeoForge’s, not ours, and it is why this needed no coordination with HorseInfoKeyHandler at all. Flagged unverified in the source, per the house rule: that is a reading of the 26.1.2 sources, not something a client has done yet, and it covers the Controls screen too — whether the row draws as “Alt + E” or as a bare E that looks like a conflict with vanilla’s. The Verification tab owns both , and names the fallback if the chord will not hold. The category is KeyMapping.Category.INVENTORY , where the rest of the mod uses MISC . Still a built-in category with nothing to register or localise, so the reason MISC was chosen elsewhere is not violated — but it puts the row directly beneath vanilla’s “Open/Close Inventory”, which is the part of Controls a player is reading when they go looking for what happened to E . No test. There is nothing here a gametest could hold: the whole of it is a keybind registration and a setScreen on the client, and the one claim worth checking — that the chord suppresses the bare key — is a fact about NeoForge’s input layer that only a real client exercises. It is a Verification-tab item rather than a missing test.
