Rider comfort
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Five small things that make riding less irritating, borrowed from other mods that already do them. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship alone. Two of the five have shipped — a lead now comes back to you rather than being left on the ground when this mod teleports the horse wearing it, and you can mine from the saddle at the speed you would standing there. The other three are still plans, and the last of them — fading the horse out as you look down — touches the coat itself and is the only one with a real unknown in it.

How the lead is returned (2026-09-24) server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player) , and every path that teleports a horse calls it instead of untying the horse itself. The distinction it is built on is one method apart in vanilla. Leashable.dropLeash() unties and spawns an Items.LEAD at the animal’s position; Leashable.removeLeash() is the same untie with the drop suppressed — both are dropLeash(entity, sendPacket, dropLead) with different arguments. So the whole change is removeLeash() plus the same give-or-drop HorseDietHandler uses for a bucket remainder. With the config off, or with no player to give it to, it falls back to dropLeash() and vanilla is back exactly. It is safe on an unleashed horse — it returns immediately — so the call sites dropped their own isLeashed() guards rather than keeping a second copy of the check. TicketHandler.arrive grew a Player parameter for it, which is the only signature this touched. The two portal paths, server/PortalEventHandler and server/HorsePortalManager , are deliberately not converted and still call dropLeash() . They untie a horse that walked into a portal itself, with its holder standing next to it, so the lead is already within reach and vanilla’s behaviour is the one that reads correctly. Guarded by the whistled_lead_comes_back gametest in gametest/ModGameTests — in a gametest rather than JUnit because the NeoForge module’s test classpath carries no Minecraft, and the assertion is about a real Leashable , a real inventory and whether an ItemEntity appeared in a real level. It asserts the two halves separately : no lead on the ground, and a lead in the player’s pack. Passing the first while failing the second would be worse than the behaviour it replaced, so one combined assertion would have been the wrong test. It was confirmed to fail against the old dropLeash() before being kept. It uses helper.makeMockPlayer rather than makeMockServerPlayerInLevel , which is @Deprecated(forRemoval) and really joins the player list. A leash holder only has to be an Entity — Leashable.setLeashedTo never asks whether it is in the level — and a mock player’s inventory is a real one. How the mounted mining penalty is removed (2026-09-29) server/MountedMiningHandler , one listener on PlayerEvent.BreakSpeed , and nothing else. The ordering in vanilla is what makes this exact. Player.getDestroySpeed ends with if (!this.onGround()) speed /= 5.0F; and then calls EventHooks.getBreakSpeed with the already-divided figure — read out of the decompiled 26.1.2 method rather than assumed, because if the event fired first the handler would be multiplying by five only for vanilla to divide it straight back. As it stands, one multiply by the same five restores the undivided speed precisely. The guard is on the penalty, not on the posture. The obvious shape — “is the player mounted? then multiply” — becomes a 5× mining boost the moment its premise is wrong, and the premise (that a mounted player’s onGround is false) is a reading of vanilla’s movement code, not something this mod controls. So the condition asks !player.onGround() directly: the multiply happens only where the division provably just happened. If a future version ever leaves a rider standing on their horse, this quietly does nothing instead of handing out five times the mining speed. The vehicle is checked too — getVehicle() instanceof AbstractHorse and that horse onGround() — which is what keeps a jumping or flying horse out. It runs on both logical sides , registered with no Dist . The client predicts break progress from its own call to the same method, so a server-only handler would draw a crack that crept along while the block broke early. That is safe because ServerConfig is ModConfig.Type.SERVER , which FML’s own javadoc on ModConfig.Type describes as “synced to clients during connection” — so both sides read the server’s value rather than two independent defaults. This is the first place in the mod that reads a server config from client-side code; the leadsReturn() -style accessor ( mountedMiningPenaltyRemoved() , catching IllegalStateException ) is what covers the window before the sync lands. Guarded by the mounted_mining_is_exempt gametest in gametest/ModGameTests , which posts three real BreakSpeed events through NeoForge.EVENT_BUS — so a handler written but never registered fails it. It asserts the exemption and both refusals separately , because the failure worth catching is not that the handler does nothing: it is that it fires when vanilla charged nothing. That middle assertion was confirmed to fail, with got 10.0 where 2.0 was expected, against a copy of the handler with the onGround guard deleted. The ground flags in the test are set by hand rather than played out. Whether a real mounted player’s onGround is genuinely false is a claim about vanilla’s movement code, not about this handler — the Verification tab owns that half . What the test owns is that given each posture, the handler does the right thing.
