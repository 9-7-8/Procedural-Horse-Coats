Back sail colour
(Magical gene / codominant / colours the back sail, paints nothing)
What colour a horse’s back sail is: its membrane and its spines. The same colours and rules as horn colour (white, the rainbow, pink, black, grey or chaos), on a locus of its own. Two different copies split the sail rather than fading it. On a horse without a sail it does nothing.

Crossing two of them The colours, and two-tone sails Exactly the horn colour table . Two copies of one colour give a sail all that colour, its membrane a see-through version of its spines. Two different ones make the membrane one colour and the spines the other. The membrane takes whichever comes earlier in the list, so Red/Blu is a red membrane on blue spines, either way round. Every copy carries a little shade of its own, so no two red sails are quite the same red. Bare bone Two Bon copies are the sail’s bone version: bare ivory rays with no membrane at all , the way a skeleton’s wing would be its bones alone. No wild horse carries Bon . The skeleton horses do, so a skeleton’s sail is a row of bare bones. One Bon copy is only a colour, and keeps its membrane. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing to the horse at all.
