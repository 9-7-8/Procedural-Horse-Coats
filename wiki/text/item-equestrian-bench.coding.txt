Tack Dyeing Bench
(Block / tack)
A saddler’s bench. It dyes a saddle’s seat , its bridle and its metal fittings separately, does the same three-way job on leather horse armour , and one day will cut a banner into a saddle pad. Two horses need not look alike.

Loom-shaped, not furnace-shaped It computes its result the moment a slot changes and keeps nothing between uses, so there is no block entity — no ticker, no save and load, no ContainerData to sync. ContainerLevelAccess is the whole connection to the world, exactly as vanilla’s loom does it. The research shelf needs one only because it ticks ; this does not. The part being dyed travels through clickMenuButton , vanilla’s own plumbing, so unlike the shelf this needs no custom packet — a zone is a small int and vanilla already carries small ints. Three colours on one item Vanilla holds one dye per stack and reads it once , outside its layer loop, so every layer of an equipment asset takes the same value however many there are. That is why this cannot be dyed_color . The three colours live in a tack_tint component and reach the renderer through NeoForge’s getArmorLayerTintColor , which is handed the layer index for exactly this limitation. The component’s indices are the layer order in assets/minecraft/equipment/saddle.json , and the two must not drift. The three textures are disjoint, so a wrong order would not overlap anything — it would quietly paint the bridle in the seat’s colour, which is the kind of bug that looks like a decision. How the three were cut out of vanilla’s sheet, and why an undyed saddle recomposes to it exactly, is on the item roster . Absent means undyed, and that path is load-bearing rather than incidental: with no component each layer resolves its own color_when_undyed , and those constants were chosen so the three recompose to vanilla. It is what lets all of this ride on minecraft:saddle rather than needing an item of our own, so a saddle you already owned can be brought to the bench and dyed. What a slot accepts is now a question, not a table lookup Both slot filters used to be membership tests — METALS.containsKey(item) for the fittings row, stack.has(DataComponents.DYE) for the two leather rows. Both are now a call to the same method the result uses: metalColour(stack) and dyeColour(stack) return a colour or null, and a slot accepts whatever is not null. That matters beyond tidiness. mayPlace , quickMoveStack and the recipe all three used to ask the question in their own words, and anything added to one had to be remembered in the other two. Now the set of things the bench takes is defined in exactly one place per row, which is what made admitting modded materials a change to one method rather than to five conditions. The precedence is inside that method: the hand-picked fifteen answer first and the modded lookup is the fallback, and for dyes it is the other way up — vanilla’s component first, because it is the answer for most modded dyes too, and the art-averaged colour only for the ones that carry no component at all. Verified Played 2026-09-16, and the dyeing works. Confirmed in a world by the owner: a saddle dyes, the three zones take colours independently , recolouring the fittings works, and an undyed saddle is indistinguishable from before — “if there’s a difference, I can’t tell”, which is the visual confirmation of the zero-pixel measurement. Inputs come back when the screen is closed. One thing that play caught and measurement had not: the dyes came out duller than vanilla’s dyed leather. Tinting multiplies, so vividness is decided by how bright the base is, and the base was being divided by vanilla’s leather brown. That constant is free — any value reproduces an undyed saddle, provided no channel clamps — so it is now the per-channel maximum of the leather pixels. Mean luminance went 151.2 to 180.9 on the seat against vanilla leather’s 167.5, and blue, which the old brown had capped at 183, reaches a full 255. The fittings needed no such change because their constant already was their maximum, which is exactly why they looked right when the leather did not. The window, the name field, the tooltip and the icon all confirmed the same day , after the brightening: “the dye is now very bright and looks great … the item caption works perfectly, the item icon recolors, the name box looks great, you can fill all three rows at once”. Shift-clicking routes a saddle, a dye and an ingot each to their own slot. Two defects were found by playing and fixed in the same session — both worth keeping, because neither was visible from the code: The name box trapped Escape. The guard was copied from the anvil without understanding what it tested: EditBox.canConsumeInput() is isActive() && isFocused() && isEditable() — it says nothing about which key, so once focused it swallowed every key including Escape and the window could not be closed. Escape is now handled before the box sees it, and unfocuses the box if it is focused rather than closing straight away. The tooltip never appeared, despite the component implementing TooltipProvider correctly. Implementing it is necessary and not sufficient: ItemStack.addDetailsToTooltipComponents walks a hardcoded list of vanilla components , so a modded one is never asked. It goes through NeoForge’s ItemTooltipEvent instead. Still unseen: the bench that generates at a cowboy’s homestead — the crafted one is confirmed, the placed one has never been laid eyes on — plus the brighter diamond and the quartz fittings, built after that session.
