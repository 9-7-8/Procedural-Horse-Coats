Attached model parts
(Part built / one part ships, the rest planned)
Meshes hung on the horse model, driven by a gene or by a breed — a unicorn horn, a row of dorsal spines, a pair of wings, feathered hooves. They need no animation of their own, because they are parented to a part of the horse that is already animated. It is the mod’s first visual that is not a texture , which is why this is a page rather than a line on a list.

What a player sees First, a unicorn — a horse with two copies of a rare recessive grows a tapering horn on its forehead, and about one of those in eight has a horn that shines in the dark. How long it is, how thick, how tightly it spirals, how far it leans and what colour it is are inherited with the allele and drift over generations, so a line bred for length really does get longer horns. A foal born with the gene wears a small one from the day it is born. And antlers : a rare dominant, so one copy grows a rack — on any horse with Ant , on stallions only with Antm , and never on a foal. How big it is, how many tines, how far it reaches, how thick and what shade are inherited with the allele; four more genes pick its habit (forked, brow-tined, palmate, crowned or spike) and can make its points glow, turn it to crystal, or grow moss on it. A wild rack is the same on both sides; two breeds, the Rimehart and the Greenwood , are antlered on every horse. And ram horns , built on the antler pattern: one copy grows a pair (on any horse with Rh , on stallions only with Rhm ), a shape gene makes them curled, corkscrew, four-horned or scurs, and a tip gene fades the points into a colour of their own. The Mirrorgazer and the Not A Sheep carry them. And dragon horns , the first of the head parts: two copies of a rare recessive grow a pair behind the ears, swept back over the neck — swept, straight or curled, and as long, thick, swept back and splayed as the allele says — on mares and stallions alike, never on a foal. Their colour is a gene of its own with the horn colour’s rules, so a horse’s dragon horns and unicorn horn are bred for colour separately. And dorsal spines , the first of the body parts and the first part off the head: one copy of a rare dominant grows a row of spines along the back, from the withers to the croup. They are uniform, graduated or alternating, and as tall, thick, sharp and many as the allele says. Mares and stallions grow them alike, foals never. Their colour is a gene of its own . A saddle or a rider hides the spines under the saddle and leaves the rest. And a back sail : two copies of a rare recessive grow spines along the back with a see-through membrane between them. A tall sail peaks over the middle of the back, a low one runs the whole length, and a scalloped one sags between its spines. Its height, arch, reach, spine count and how see-through the skin is are on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own : two different copies make the membrane one colour and the spines the other. A saddle hides the part under it, as with the spines. And shoulder and hip plates : one copy of a rare dominant grows overlapping plates of bone over the shoulders and the hips, stepped down the flank like armour, the same on both sides. They are smooth, ridged or spiked, and their size, how many each cluster has, how far they overlap and how far they flare are on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own : two different copies give every plate an edge in the second colour. They sit in front of and behind the saddle, so a saddle hides none of them. And tusks , one very rare dominant gene with three forms: a narwhal horn , one long spiral tusk straight forward out of the front of the muzzle; boar tusks , a pair curling up from the corners of the lower jaw; and sabre fangs , a pair of blades hanging from the upper lip past the chin. One copy grows a form, and a horse with two different forms grows both. Each form’s length, thickness and shape are on its own allele, and their colour is a gene of its own , ivory by default. Every part stacks: one horse can wear a horn, antlers, every body part and two tusk forms at once. What these parts are not is tack. Nothing here is put on a horse by a player, and nothing here can be taken off: a grown part is a piece of the animal, inherited like its coat. Things you buckle on are horse gear , and they will eventually be drawn by this same layer without becoming the same kind of object. They do nothing Deliberately, and this is worth saying plainly because a horn looks like it ought to matter. A grown part moves no stat, grants no ability, changes no hitbox and does not change how the horse is ridden or fed. If it ever did, turning the drawing off would change the world for one player and not another, which is a thing the settings below must never be able to do. If they cost you frames A part is real geometry — a horn is roughly as much again as the horse carrying it — so a large herd of them is the case worth a setting. Three live in this client’s own config file, and every one of them gates a drawing and nothing else: parts.enabled — draw grown parts at all. parts.glow — draw the extra full-bright pass a luminous part needs. Off, it still draws, lit like the rest of the horse. The first thing to try if a shader pack makes parts look wrong. parts.detailDistance — how close a horse must be for its parts to be drawn. The horse itself is unaffected at any distance. A horse with a horn and horns switched off still has the horn: it breeds it, passes it on, and lists it in the horse screen and the browser. Two players on one server may disagree about all three settings and still be looking at the same horses.
