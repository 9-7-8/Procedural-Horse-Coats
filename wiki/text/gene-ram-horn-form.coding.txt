Ram horn form
(Magical gene / dominance series / shapes the ram horns, paints nothing)
The shape of a horned horse’s ram horns : curled, corkscrew, four-horned, horizontal twist or scurs. One shape on both sides. On a polled horse it does nothing.

Gene key horsegenetics.ram_horn_form Class RamHornFormGene Priority 237 (magical band); only a code-order slot Family Grown parts Alleles Crl Crk Fhn Scr Hzt — dominance Fhn > Crl > Crk > Hzt > Scr ; Crl is the default Founders Hardy–Weinberg over Crl 0.66, Crk 0.12, Scr 0.10, Fhn 0.08, Hzt 0.04 Coat effect none Allele order is generator order Crl, Crk, Fhn, Scr, Hzt is the declaration order, the stored order, and RamHornGenerator ’s shape indices, so shapeOf(pair) is the dominant allele’s order() and the first factor of the mesh style. Dominance is its own list. Append, never reorder: Hzt came later and went on the end, so no saved horse changed shape, and RamHornsGeneTest pins the order. The horizontal twist is a flat corkscrew laid level Hzt (2026-10-09, #125) is RamHornGenerator.HORIZONTAL : the corkscrew’s chain — one unbranched run of boxes that roll and never bend — with its root leaned about 83° off upright, a little back, and every box flat ( PartNode.width at 0.6 of its girth), so the roll reads as a turning ribbon. The curl bucket picks the roll per segment (8° to 26°, shallower than a corkscrew’s), and the horn is 0.8 of its class length where a corkscrew is 0.9. A horn is at most twelve boxes against MAX_NODES . It took 0.04 of the founder table from Crl . Neither ram-horned breed’s pool names it, so RamHornBreedsTest checks the four shapes the pools do name. It cost the box budget one raise. One root for four horns A part mesh is one tree with one root ( PartGeneratorTest ), so the four-horned shape grows its curled horn from the base of the upright one ( t = 0 ), not as a second root. The dominance order — four-horned on top, scurs at the bottom — was this build’s call: real polycerate sheep carry a dominant trait, and scurs as the recessive makes a scurred line something you breed for.
