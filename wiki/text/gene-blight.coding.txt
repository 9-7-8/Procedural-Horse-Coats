Blight
(Magical gene / magical coat genes)
The ground dies under it. Grass, moss and mycelium go back to bare dirt where it walks, and whatever was growing there withers to nothing. It paints nothing on the horse itself.

The file common/src/main/resources/horsegenetics/genes/blight.json — a data-driven gene , not a Java class. It is listed in the index.json beside it and registered by Genes ' class initialiser at startup. Key horsegenetics.blight Phase magical — it adds signed colour in phase 3, after the pigment has resolved Priority 185 — where it sits in the one (priority, key) paint order Alleles Bli · n Varies per horse no — every horse carrying it looks the same Built from Why it is built this way THE OPPOSITE OF VERDANT (owner, 2026-09-17). Verdant names a cover and spreads it; this names one that takes cover away. It reuses the same 'spread' verb and the same radius, chance and interval that verdant's three covers use, so the two genes behave identically except in what the translator does with the word - which is the whole point of the verb taking a vocabulary word rather than a block id. WHAT IT WILL NOT EAT. The translator converts grass, moss, mycelium and podzol to dirt, and kills wild growth standing on the ground. It deliberately leaves crops, saplings, potted plants and anything else a player put there alone, for the same reason 'melt' leaves packed and blue ice alone: a gene that eats somebody's wheat field is griefing with extra steps. That judgement lives on the game side with the code that knows the blocks. RECESSIVE, AND SEEDED AS THE EXPRESSING PAIR. The founder table seeds Bli/Bli directly rather than seeding carriers and letting Hardy-Weinberg produce the homozygote, which is what 'flying' does and for the same reason: at any frequency low enough to be rare, p-squared rounds to no wild horse ever having it. About one wild horse in three hundred blights the ground; nothing carries it invisibly. IT PAINTS NOTHING, so it has no layers and no icon of its own, exactly like verdant. A blighting horse looks like any other horse until you follow it.
