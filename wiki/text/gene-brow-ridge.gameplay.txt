Brow ridge
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One copy and the horse grows a low wide ridge of bone across its brow, above and in front of the eyes. Plain, notched into three bumps or set with three short spines, and how tall and wide it is and how tall its bumps stand, is written on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own, brow ridge colour .

Crossing two of them One copy, either sex Dominant: Brw/n and Brw/Brw both grow a ridge, so a ridged horse bred to a plain one gives a ridged foal at least half the time. It is not sex-limited. Foals grow none; the ridge comes in when the horse grows up, and stays. Where it sits Across the top of the face, in front of the eyes. It is lower down the face than a unicorn horn , so a horse with both wears the horn just behind the ridge. Finding one Rare in the wild: about three horses in a thousand have a brow ridge, and because one copy is enough, every horse that carries the gene shows it. The skeleton horses can grow one in bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts. What varies Form : plain (a bar with a narrower step along its top), notched (three bumps) or spined (a row of three short spines). Height : three classes, low , heavy and towering . Low to heavy in the wild; a line bred for height reaches towering and stops. Width : how far across the head it reaches. A wild ridge stays inside the skull; the widest reaches its edges. Bump : how tall a notched ridge’s bumps or a spined one’s spines stand. Everything stacks: a horse can wear a brow ridge with every other part. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no armour value, no hitbox. It is a look. Its gene carrot
