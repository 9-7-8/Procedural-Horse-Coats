Dorsal spines
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One copy and the horse grows a row of spines along the top of its back, from the withers to the croup. Whether they are all one height, fall away toward the tail or alternate tall and short, and how tall, thick, sharp and many they are, is written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, dorsal spine colour . The first of the body parts, and the first grown part that is not on the head.

Crossing two of them One copy, either sex Dominant: Dsp/n and Dsp/Dsp both grow the row, so a horse with spines shows them and a horse without carries none. A spined horse bred to a plain one gives spined foals about half the time. It is not sex-limited. Foals grow none; the row comes in when the horse grows up, and stays. Under a saddle The row runs through where a saddle and a rider sit. A bare horse nobody is riding shows all of it. Put a saddle on, or get on, and the spines under the saddle are hidden; the ones at the withers and over the croup stay. A horse steered bare at the top bond has no saddle to draw, but its rider still hides the middle of the row. Finding them Rare in the wild: about three horses in a thousand have a row. Every one of them shows it, so there are no hidden carriers to find. The skeleton horses can grow a row of bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts. What varies Form : uniform (every spine one height), graduated (tallest at the withers, under half that at the croup) or alternating (tall and short in turn). Height : four classes, low to towering . Low to tall in the wild, and a line bred for height reaches towering and stops. Count : how many spines there are, from the withers back. From about half a row to a full one in the wild; a line bred down can wear a short crest at the withers. Taper : blunt pegs to sharp needles. Girth : thicker or thinner. Everything stacks: a horse can wear spines and every head part at once. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no attack, no hitbox. It is a look.
