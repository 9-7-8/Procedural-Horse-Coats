Gameplay / world Hay portals & the horse dimension Press F6 and you arrive in a private, disposable corridor with a different question down each side : on your right, every marking nobody has confirmed by eye yet, four horses to a pen on four different base coats; on your left, every breed in the game, a mare and a stallion each. It is the mod’s showroom — a place to see what the genes actually do without breeding for a week — and it gets shorter as the mod is tested . The hay portal does not come here any more Since 2026-09-25 a hay-bale portal arrives in the horse realm — the public, protected field where players leave horses for each other. This dimension is the test corridor, and F6 is now its only door . That door is open in every build, release jars included: the server admits an operator ( LEVEL_GAMEMASTERS ) or a world with debug.tools on, and tells anybody else where the hay portal goes instead. Everything on this page still describes the corridor. Read it alongside the realm rather than instead of it: they are two dimensions with deliberately opposite rules, and the realm’s protections are kept out of here on purpose . The names still say “debug” The class and registry names — DebugPenManager , DEBUG_LEVEL , horsegenetics:debug_pens — are legacy from when it was debug-only; a rename is deferred because it would break existing save data. They are now accurate again, which is the one thing that never used to be true about them. Hay-bale portals Lighting one Build a vertical frame of hay bales around an empty rectangle, at nether-portal inner sizes (2–21 wide, 3–21 tall), outside the horse dimension. Right-click any frame block with a golden carrot . server/HorsePortalManager.findFrame tries every in-plane air neighbour of the clicked bale as a flood-fill seed — taking only the first is what used to make a corner click silently do nothing — floods the enclosed air in the frame plane (X first, then Z), requires a filled rectangle fully bounded by hay, and fills it with horsegenetics:hay_portal . The carrot is consumed, and you get a chat line either way; on failure it states the size rule. The portal block hay_portal is a cosmetic, indestructible BaseEntityBlock — no collision, light level 11, no loot, RenderShape.INVISIBLE . Its look is a half-block-thick opaque slab drawn by HayPortalRenderer , inset along the axis the portal plane doesn’t run along, so it faces the player rather than being edge-on. Two draw passes per face: an opaque black pass ( entitySolid , which writes depth and has no alpha discard, so sky, clouds and water can never show through) and then the animated swirl ( entityTranslucentEmissive , full-bright). The texture is a 64-wide vertical strip of 36 frames . The frame rate ramps 12 48 fps as a client-estimated “charge” builds while you stand in it (~10 s ramp, ~2 s decay), so the portal visibly winds up around you. A multi-block portal only renders its outer shell — a face adjoining another hay_portal is skipped. Going through server/PortalEventHandler counts ticks an entity spends standing in a portal block (feet and y+0.9 ): Who Dwell What happens Player, overworld portal 200 ticks (10 s) A fresh plot is allocated and you are dropped at its entrance, facing the corridor, with a paper in the first free hotbar slot. The return is wired back to this portal. Player, dimension portal 200 ticks Back to where you came from — and your tamed horses come with you (below). Horse 60 ticks (3 s) Goes through on its own. While the counter runs, the entity is ringed with gold dust particles (denser as it nears zero — vanilla has no gold portal particle), and a player gets a one-time “grabs hold” line plus a per-second countdown for the last five seconds. A 100-tick cooldown blocks instant re-triggering. The roped-horse shortcut Right-click a hay_portal while leading horses and every leashed horse within 12 blocks is snapped to the portal block, has its lead dropped, and has its dwell counter seeded so it goes through in about three seconds. Owner-verified. Coming back As a player’s exit dwell starts they are warned in chat that anything left behind is lost and that tamed horses come with them. At teleport, evacuateTamedHorses takes your tamed horses — or every tamed horse, if you are the last player out. Each is dropped about two blocks above the return portal on a 3-wide grid with 10 seconds of invulnerability , so the short fall cannot hurt it, and is parked in the portal cooldown so it cannot be sucked straight back through. No terrain is carved. One private plot per visit The dimension generates nothing — the_void biome, a single air layer. Every visit gets its own DebugPenManager.Plot : a corridor built at a unique world X, with plots 8,007 blocks apart (the corridor length plus a thousand) and X slots recycled through a free list, all at a fixed PLOT_BASE_Y of 128. Plots never share chunks, so “two players never land in the same place” holds with no per-player-dimension machinery. PLOTS is strictly one plot per player: entering makes a new one and tears down your previous one, so a revisit always regenerates and nothing you left behind survives. Leaving clears entities, not blocks The geometry is fixed, so an X slot handed back to the free list is rebuilt byte-identically on top of the old one. Teardown is therefore O(entities) , not O(blocks walked): it discards non-player entities in the plot’s AABB and forget s each one from the ancestry database, or every visit would leave hundreds of throwaway pen records in the save forever. The AABB covers the whole X slot, not just what this visit built, or a slot where a previous visitor walked further would accumulate horses indefinitely. A record that references a forgotten horse as a parent is left alone — ancestorsOf already skips ancestors it cannot find, so a foal bred in the dimension and taken home keeps working with a missing parent node. What is in the pens The two sides of the road ask different questions. Walking in from the portal, your right ( +Z ) is the coat check and your left ( -Z ) is the breed book . The corridor used to be one long row of random genotypes on both sides; it is now two lists, and one of them ends. Right: every marking nobody has confirmed One pen per allele combination of every gene whose wiki page carries no Verified block — the record hard rule 9 already requires, read straight off the pages by :common:bakeUnverifiedGenes and planned by common/genetics/CoatCheckPlan . Ask it for its length with CoatCheckPlan.size() ; it is not a number worth writing down here, because it falls every time a gene is confirmed . Confirm a gene, write its Verified block, re-bake, and its pens leave the corridor. This column crashed the dimension on arrival, 2026-09-17 Every pen asks whether its combination belongs to a mare or a stallion, because a sex-linked gene’s hemizygous form is a horse only a stallion can be. That question went to Gene.hemizygousPlaceholder() and the answer was checked for null — but that method throws on an ordinary autosomal gene rather than returning null , so the null check never ran. Every gene in the plan is autosomal, so the corridor’s first pen threw while the player was still being teleported in: the whole dimension was unenterable in 0.5.004, and nothing downstream of it could be tested. CoatCheckPlan.Pen.sex() asks whether the gene is sex-linked first. CoatCheckPlanSanityTest walks every pen in the plan and asks it for exactly what the corridor asks — the four genotype codes, the expression name and the description the signs wrap — so this class of fault costs a test in seconds rather than a world on entry. It failed on all 438 pens before the fix. Four horses to a pen , carrying the same combination on a black , a bay , a chestnut and a white base, named on their nameplates. A marking that only works on one base is then obvious rather than something you have to go looking for. The white horse is the masking check. Its base is KIT W2 / N — one copy of an allWhite allele, which removes every pigment and masks the coat. So anything still drawn on that horse is a gene painting over a mask it should have respected. ( Not W22 , which is sabino-like on its own and all-white only beside a booster.) Worst first. The order is the gene timeline's, newest first, so the near end of the corridor is the genes written most recently — which is where a drawing bug actually is. Genes the timeline cannot date go last. Extension and agouti are excluded , being the base coat rather than a marking on one: every pen shows four of those already. Left: every breed, alphabetically A mare and a stallion of one breed per pen, in alphabetical order by display name, looping for as long as the right-hand column runs. Both are rolled through BreedFounder and stamped with the breed's BreedLineage token, so they read as that breed everywhere downstream rather than being a genotype with a breed's name on the sign. Feral Mixed is skipped — it is the absence of a breed, not one of them. A way out every twenty-five pens Every 25th segment carries no pens. It is a break with a hay-bale portal home. The corridor is thousands of blocks end to end and until now had exactly one way out, the return portal at the entrance — so anybody who walked in a few hundred pens to look at something had to walk all the way back. There is one every 175 blocks ( REST_STOP_EVERY × PERIOD ), so a way home is never more than about ninety blocks away. How many that is depends on how long the corridor is, which is CoatCheckPlan.size() . Counted in segments , so it is twenty-five pens down each side between breaks — which is what “every twenty-five” means to somebody walking past one row of them. The portal is set back off the north side, not across the road. The entrance portal stands in the road because you arrive through it; one of these in the road would teleport out anybody walking the length of the place, which is the opposite of a convenience. You step aside into it. It needs no destination wiring. HorsePortalManager resolves a portal in this dimension through plotContaining , which is an X range — so a portal anywhere in the plot already sends you to that plot's own return position, and takes your tamed horses with you exactly as the entrance one does. Pen numbering stays contiguous. A break builds no pens, so numbering by segment would leave a hole and the signs would read …49, 50, then 53. The breaks before a segment are subtracted, so a pen's number still means “the nth pen you have walked past”. It is still a pure function of the segment index, so a recycled plot rebuilds identically. The breaks lengthen the corridor rather than eating pens. A break is an extra segment, not a segment's pens taken away, so every coat check in the plan still exists. LAST_SEGMENT_INDEX adds them back on top of PEN_SEGMENTS ; skipping that would quietly swallow the last few dozen pens, which is the end of the list nobody would notice was missing. ShowcaseGenotypes — a founder draw with a floor under it No longer what the corridor uses The pens were stocked from this until 2026-09-17 and are not any more — both columns are deterministic lists now, so pen n is the same horse every visit. It is kept because it is still the right answer to “give me a founder worth looking at”, it is still what ShowcaseGenotypesTest pins, and its showcasePairs rule — a combination that can occur, is not wild type, and does not mask — is the same rule CoatCheckPlan applies. An honest wild draw is mostly plain horses: every founder table is weighted hard toward its baseline, which is correct for a wild spawn and useless for a corridor whose job is to show what the genes do. So common/genetics/ShowcaseGenotypes.random rolls an ordinary Genotype.random and then guarantees a floor: At least one natural coat gene beyond extension and agouti is expressing , always. With MAGICAL_CHANCE = 0.5 , a magical gene too. A draw that already clears the floor is left exactly as it fell — only an all-baseline draw gets a gene forced in. Two rules keep it honest. Forcing only ever keeps a combination that actually expresses in this genotype ( Genotype.shows ), so a chestnut is never handed an agouti it will not paint. And a combination that masks — one that hides everything else — neither counts toward the floor nor is ever forced, or a masking gene would quietly exempt part of the corridor from the guarantee. Candidates are derived from the registry, so a drop-in gene joins the pool on its own. The floor guarantees expressed , not perceptible Mushroom on a black horse is a real expression that looks like nothing. Nothing in the model can currently answer “would a player see this”, so this is accepted rather than special-cased. Signs Per pen , on the road one block out from the wall and to the right of the gate as you face the pen (so the two sides of the road mirror): the pen number, then GeneCodeDisplay.shortForm — the same compact genome string the info panel and paper dump use — greedily wrapped over three lines. The wrap deliberately overflows its last line rather than dropping a gene , so a busy horse reads very wide. At the entrance , three blocks in front of the return portal, two signs side by side: Horse Pens / LEFT: breeds / RIGHT: coats / not yet checked , and beside it N coats / 4 horses each: / black bay / chestnut white . At each rest stop , on the road side of the break: Way out / step through / N pens / that way -> , where N is how many pens you have walked past to get there. Past the bedrock core: open void. Pens are 6 × 20 with a two-wide oak-fence-gate opening onto the road — a one-wide gate lets horses escape — and torches on the four corner posts. Amenities sit in the two gate-side interior corners, one block in from the road wall and sunk one block so their tops are flush with the grass: a full water cauldron and a hay block. At full height they were a step the horses hopped the wall from. Pens repeat every 7 blocks; 30 are built ahead of the player and more are laid down as you walk ( DebugPenTickHandler ). The wall behind the return portal ( buildStartCap ) is the same sandwich turned ninety degrees, pushed two blocks back to originX - 3 : bedrock core at originX - 3 , a single oak-plank wall at originX - 2 on a bedrock/dirt base, and a gravel floor strip at originX - 1 with the glowstone line floating above it. The E/W walls are carried out to originX - 1 so the corner has no gap. An earlier version made originX - 1 a full-height gravel column with no base; it fell into the void and left a hole, which is why everything now stands on bedrock and dirt. The return portal itself (hay frame + hay_portal , axis Z) is at originX + 1 , and the entrance sign three blocks in front of it at originX + 4 . House rules inside the dimension Horses take no damage. No block breaking or placing , in survival or creative. Non-horse mobs are refused entry. In a dev build: a stick tames an untamed horse and a clock ages a foal to adult. A pen is built once per plot, so anything already standing in it belongs to a previous occupant of that recycled X slot. penShell discards untamed leftovers before stocking, and leaves tamed ones alone — a player may have ridden one ahead of the build frontier. The test yard Thirty blocks to the right of where you arrive — you land facing +X , so right is +Z — a five-wide gravel path leaves the road through a door cut in the corridor wall and opens into a walled yard. It takes the place of the first pen on that side, which DebugPenManager.buildSegment deliberately does not build; the pen numbers are unchanged, so the yard costs one pen rather than shifting the rest. It exists for the genes that cannot be judged in a pen and should not be judged in a real save — each a claim about something that happens while nobody is watching, or about a cost rather than a picture. Since 2026-09-12 it is built for a night rather than a visit The owner's brief: “I'm going to leave the game and the horse dimension running over night. Add as many time-reliant tests as possible, focusing on those that don't require direct intervention from me.” That rules out more than it lets in. A pen belongs in here now only if its test starts itself , runs on a clock and leaves something readable in the morning — and the third of those is the watch , which is new, because until then nothing in the repo took a reading of anything. Since 2026-09-13 it is most of the mod, and the horses can die in it Two changes on the same day, both on the owner's word. The item, block and villager layer is in the yard — “add the yard able and entire item and gameplay layer as well as villagers. Add literally everything that CAN be tested in the yard.” — and the rule that made horses invulnerable here is gone : “remove the 'no horse damage' exception in the yard.” The second is the fifth time a protection in this dimension turned out to be the reason a gene “did nothing”, after the mob deleter, the spread verb's early return, the missing night and the dhampir's cancelled sunburn. It made six genes untestable here: guardian, gladiator, healer, cleansing light, ender echo and both death loci are entirely about a horse taking or dealing damage. It has a price and the yard pays it in two places. The open yard is lit with invisible full-brightness light blocks, four apart, so nothing spawns in the middle of a pen that is being left to run; and every monster in the yard now comes from a spawner inside a roofed arena , so a fight happens where one is wanted and nowhere else. The yard is a grid , not a packing. Every row owns a band of z ; between any two bands is a four-block aisle; a pen's sign lives in the aisle and its gate opens into it; and nothing crosses the centre walkway, which is what keeps every gate reachable from the door a hundred blocks back up the corridor. That arrangement is a fix, not a design: pens used to be added one at a time and butted against each other, and on 2026-09-13 the owner hit all three consequences in one visit — “some have gates that are inaccessible and are getting their signs overwritten” , “I cannot access the glow room” . The yard's depth is derived from its last row rather than written down, after the literal was found to be seventy-five blocks short of the rows it was meant to contain. Rows O to R are packed, and every pen in them is a world of its own. Every breeding and band check counts horses by distance, straight through walls. Examples: a mare is not covered when fertility.nearby_horse_cap other horses stand within sixteen blocks; a bachelor challenges a stallion whose mare is within 24; a youngster looks 64 blocks for somewhere to go. Until 2026-09-14 these rows were spread over three hundred blocks so that no pen reached another. Now each pen registers with YardPens , and inside the yard those checks see only the horses in their own pen. Pens that should see each other share a group, like LEAVING HOME and HOST BAND. Nothing changes outside the yard. The breeding pens still keep a three-block gap. Golden-carrot breeding is vanilla's own goal, which pairs two horses in love within three blocks through a wall, and YardPens does not reach it. The herd pens share walls. Every herd test starts itself on a clock ( DebugYardHerd ): it sets up the state the event needs, then does whatever a player would have done, and each herd pen logs a social: readout.
