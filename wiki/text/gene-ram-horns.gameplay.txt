Ram horns
(Magical gene / dominant, sex-limited allele / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One copy and the horse grows a pair of ram’s horns — on a mare or a stallion with Rh , on a stallion only with Rhm , the way most horned sheep breeds have horned rams and polled ewes. How big they are, how far they curl, how thick and what shade are written on the allele. The third grown part, after the unicorn horn and antlers , and built on the antler pattern.

Crossing two of them Two ways to have horns Rh — horns on every horse that carries it, mare or stallion. Rhm — horns on a stallion only. A mare with it is polled and passes it on, and her genotype reads exactly like a stallion’s. Both are dominant, and Rh wins over Rhm . Foals grow no horns; they come in when the horse grows up, and stay. Finding them Rare in the wild — a few horses in a thousand carry either allele, mostly the stallion-only one. Two breeds carry them on purpose: the Mirrorgazer , horned on every horse, and the Not A Sheep , horned like a real flock. What varies Size — five classes, bud to massive ; small to standard in the wild, and a line bred for size climbs to massive and stops. Curl — how far round a curled horn goes, from a little over half a turn to more than a full one; on a corkscrew or horizontal-twist horn, how tightly it twists; on a scimitar, how far back it sweeps. Girth and shade — thicker or thinner, pale horn to black. Two more genes finish them: ram horn form picks the shape — curled, scimitar, corkscrew, four-horned, horizontal twist or scurs — and ram horn tip fades the points into a colour of their own. A horse can have a unicorn horn, antlers and ram horns all at once. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no attack, no hitbox. It is a look. Its gene carrot
