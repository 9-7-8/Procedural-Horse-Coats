Research papers
(Item / knowledge)
A paper naming one gene and one allele pair — Agouti: A/a , not just Agouti . Spend it on a Known Gene Splice carrot and that is the pair the carrot hands on, which is the only way to breed for a specific combination on purpose. It is not something you read.

What it does A paper is the write-up of one combination — one gene, and the two alleles you are being told about. Spend it on a Known Gene Splice carrot and the carrot hands that exact pair on to a foal. The tooltip names the pair, says whether it is homozygous or heterozygous, gives the gene’s rarity, and says what that particular combination does to a horse — not what the locus is about in general. A pair, not a gene This is the change worth understanding, because it is what makes papers worth collecting rather than ticking off. Two papers for the same gene are two different items. Cream: Cr/n is a carrier paper. Its carrot hands the foal one copy, so the foal carries cream and may not show it. Cream: Cr/Cr is a true-breeding paper. Its carrot hands on two, so the foal is cream whatever the other parent brought. Particle: Sflm/Gflm is a compound pair — two different variants, neither of them the ordinary one. Only a horse can teach you one of these. A/a and a/A are the same paper. Which parent contributed which copy is not a fact about the horse, so it is not a fact about the paper either: the two normalise to one thing, stack in a slot, and take one place on a shelf. Why this replaced the one-paper-per-gene version A paper used to name a whole locus, and the carrot built from it took whichever pair convention had picked. That was wrong twice over. A breeder standing in a field is asking “what does A/a do here?”, not “what is agouti?” — and on a wide locus the convention was a wall. The particle gene has around forty alleles, and the old carrot could only ever hand over the first one, so no amount of playing would get you a soul flame on purpose. Now every allele has a paper and every paper has a carrot. A paper is not something you read Right-clicking one does nothing. It used to add its gene to your database and unlock that gene's carrot — which made a paper a shortcut past the animals: find one in a chest and you knew a gene you had never met. Your gene database is now earned only by owning a living horse that carries the gene (tamed, bred, bought, given), and that same moment is what unlocks the carrot. So a paper is a component . It goes into an Equine Research Shelf to be copied, and it goes into a gene carrot to be spent. Four ways to get one Find it. Research papers turn up in any chest, vanilla or another mod’s, in about three chests in ten. Which gene a found paper names is weighted by rarity, so the common genes come up far more often. It used to be eight kinds of vanilla chest at not quite one in five, which on a large modded server meant almost none — see every chest, any mod . A chest gives you a carrier or a true-breeding paper, never a compound one. Take it off a horse. Right-click a horse while holding a book and you get a paper for one pair that horse actually carries, at random . You do not choose which — that is the point, and it is why a paper is a discovery rather than paperwork. A completely ordinary horse has nothing to teach and costs you no book. This is the only route to a compound pair , so an odd horse is worth a book even when you already know the gene. Copy one you already have. File a paper into an Equine Research Shelf and that shelf can copy it onto blank books for as long as the original stays in it. One slot per pair, so a locus you have studied properly takes several. Buy or trade for it. The equestrian supplier sells them at its last two tiers, on the same carrier-or-true-breeding terms as a chest. Papers can also be handed to other players — which is the point of being able to write them. Knowledge is shareable rather than hoardable. How many papers is that? Two per variant allele per locus, plus every compound pair a horse can show you. The exact number is not written down here on purpose — it moves with every gene anybody adds. Each gene’s own page carries the count for that locus on its gene-carrot card, asked of the mod rather than typed.
