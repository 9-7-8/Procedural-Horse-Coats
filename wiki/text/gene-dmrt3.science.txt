DMRT3 — gait
(Natural gene / no coat effect)
The “gait keeper”: the gene that lets a horse amble and pace. The locus, its inheritance and every breed’s share are in; the gait itself is not animated yet, so for now it is a fact about a horse’s breeding rather than something you can ride.

A stop codon in the spinal cord DMRT3 is expressed in a set of spinal-cord neurons that coordinate the left and right limbs. The gait variant is a nonsense mutation, Ser301STOP, found by Andersson et al. (2012) in Icelandic horses. It is permissive rather than simply dominant or recessive: it makes lateral gaits possible, and other genes and training decide which one a horse uses. Pace needs two copies. Almost every five-gaited Icelandic is A/A , but only about 70% of A/A Icelandics actually pace. One copy improves the t lt and still scores well at walk, trot, canter and gallop. Two copies give the best t lt and pace and a poorer trot and canter. In Standardbreds , trotters and pacers are both A/A , so other loci decide trot versus pace, and A/A horses race better than C/A ones. The mutation arose within the last ten thousand years. The earliest ancient DNA carrying it is from England around 850–900 AD, and it reached Iceland soon after. Breeds selected for smooth riding gaits have fixed it; breeds selected for the gallop have not. What the mod does not model yet: which lateral gait a gaited horse prefers, which is the modifier loci plus training, and the gait itself. Both wait for movement and animation work.
