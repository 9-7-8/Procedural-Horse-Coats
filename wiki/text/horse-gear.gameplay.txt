Horse gear: the nineteen slots
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Everything a horse wears, in one place. A Gear tab on the horse information screen holds nineteen slots laid out on a picture of the horse itself — head, mane and tail, body, chest, a slot per leg and a slot per hoof, and two for saddlebags. Two of them are real vanilla equipment slots and the other seventeen are a data attachment of our own, which is the constraint the whole system is shaped by. The slots exist; almost nothing to put in them does yet. The roster, the storage and the screen shipped first on purpose — every piece that follows is one row in a tag. What is still planned is on this page’s own Roadmap tab, which is the single home for gear plans.

Where it is Open a horse’s inventory as you always have, and press the small i button beside it. That is this mod’s own horse information screen — the one with Overview, Genes, Health and the rest — and Gear is the second tab along. The H key is a different screen: that is the horse browser , your roster and reference, and it has no slots in it. Overview used to carry the two tack slots in a row under the stats. It now says how many pieces the horse is wearing and sends you here, because a row nineteen slots wide would have pushed the rest of Overview off the page. That line is the first thing on Overview, above the sex and the stats: what a horse has on is the one fact on that page that changes between two looks at it. The nineteen slots They are arranged on a small three-quarter view of the horse, in the place the gear actually goes: the near fore boot is under the near fore leg, the tail slot is behind the tail. You find a slot by looking at the horse rather than by reading a list. Drag the horse to turn it. Press anywhere on the animal itself and move the mouse: side to side spins it all the way round, up and down tips it as far as a steep look from above or below. The three-quarter view it opens on shows one side of the horse properly and the other at an angle, so the off-side boots and the tail are worth turning to — and a horse in full tack is worth walking round anyway. Let go and it stays where you put it; reopen the screen and it is back to the opening pose. The slots take the click first, so a drag that starts on a boot moves nothing and equips as it always did. Start the drag on the horse and you get the horse. A bridle and a halter share one slot on purpose They are genuinely different pieces — a haltered horse can be led and lunged, a bridled one can be steered, and neither substitutes for the other — but a horse cannot wear both at once , so they are two items competing for one slot rather than two slots. This reverses an earlier plan that gave them a slot each; the distinction survives intact, it just lives in the items. What having a halter lets you do is ground work , still on the main roadmap because it is a riding mechanic rather than a piece of gear. Four legs and four hooves, not a set of four A horse can wear unevenly, and a roster that could only say “hoof equipment: yes” could never say which hoof is occupied. Eight leg and hoof slots is the price of being able to. Putting something on Click a slot and it tells you what fits. A short list drops under it: everything in your pack that this slot accepts, each with its picture, and a Take off line at the top for whatever is in there now. Click a line to put it on, click anywhere else to close the list. There is no dragging: this is a plain screen with no container behind it, so there is no carried stack to drag with. An empty list still answers the question. “Nothing you are carrying fits here” and “nothing takes this slot yet” are different sentences, and which one you get is the difference between going to find the item and not bothering. Most slots say the second — see the box below. Nothing is ever deleted. What comes off goes to your inventory, or on the floor at your feet if there is no room. Your horse, and within eight blocks. The screen opens on any horse — a stranger’s, a cowboy’s string — so you can see what one is wearing and no more. A slot you cannot use is drawn dim rather than hidden, because a gap where a slot should be reads as a bug and a greyed slot reads as the rule it is. A foal wears nothing. Every slot is closed until it grows up. Tack sized for an adult is the one thing a growing horse should not be carrying. Fifteen of the nineteen are empty by design The saddle and the barding have had something that fits them all along, and the mane and tail now take the rescuing braid — the first piece of gear of this mod's own to fill a slot of this mod's own. The other fifteen accept nothing: not because they are broken, but because the gear that fills them has not been made. The tab says so on screen, so it reads as unfinished rather than faulty. Each piece joins its slot as it is made, and nothing on this page needs changing when one does. The Roadmap tab is what is coming. Gear does something, or it is not made Cosmetic-only equipment is the thing this mod does not add. Breeding is the one place pure aesthetics are allowed; everything else must provide utility, so tack, barding and hoof equipment carry real effects. That included the mane and tail slots, and the first thing to go in one is the test case: a rescuing braid looks exactly like a decorative plait and is in fact the strongest save in the game, with the colour — the only part of it that is decoration — riding on top of a mechanic that had already paid for the slot. The rule is philosophy § utility, not decoration , and it reversed an earlier “tack gets no stats” call — see design decisions .
