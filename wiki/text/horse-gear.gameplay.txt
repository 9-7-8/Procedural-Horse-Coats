Horse gear: the nineteen slots
(Gameplay / non-genetic)
Everything a horse wears, in one place. A Gear tab on the horse information screen holds nineteen slots laid out on a picture of the horse itself — head, mane and tail, body, chest, a slot per leg and a slot per hoof, and two for the chests it carries. Two of them are real vanilla equipment slots and the other seventeen are a data attachment of our own, which is the constraint the whole system is shaped by. The slots exist; almost nothing to put in them does yet. The roster, the storage and the screen shipped first on purpose — every piece that follows is one row in a tag. What is still planned is on this page’s own Roadmap tab, which is the single home for gear plans.

Where it is Open a horse’s inventory as you always have, and press the small i button beside it. That is this mod’s own horse information screen — the one with Overview, Genes, Health and the rest — and Gear is the second tab along. The H key is a different screen: that is the horse browser , your roster and reference, and it has no slots in it. Overview used to carry the two tack slots in a row under the stats. It now says how many pieces the horse is wearing and sends you here, because a row nineteen slots wide would have pushed the rest of Overview off the page. That line is the first thing on Overview, above the sex and the stats: what a horse has on is the one fact on that page that changes between two looks at it. The nineteen slots They are arranged on a small three-quarter view of the horse, in the place the gear actually goes: the near fore boot is under the near fore leg, the tail slot is behind the tail. You find a slot by looking at the horse rather than by reading a list. Drag the horse to turn it. Press anywhere on the animal itself and move the mouse: side to side spins it all the way round, up and down tips it as far as a steep look from above or below. The three-quarter view it opens on shows one side of the horse properly and the other at an angle, so the off-side boots and the tail are worth turning to — and a horse in full tack is worth walking round anyway. Let go and it stays where you put it; reopen the screen and it is back to the opening pose. The slots take the click first, so a drag that starts on a boot moves nothing and equips as it always did. Start the drag on the horse and you get the horse. A bridle and a halter share one slot on purpose They are genuinely different pieces — a haltered horse can be led and lunged, a bridled one can be steered, and neither substitutes for the other — but a horse cannot wear both at once , so they are two items competing for one slot rather than two slots. This reverses an earlier plan that gave them a slot each; the distinction survives intact, it just lives in the items. What having a halter lets you do is ground work , still on the main roadmap because it is a riding mechanic rather than a piece of gear. Four legs and four hooves, not a set of four A horse can wear unevenly, and a roster that could only say “hoof equipment: yes” could never say which hoof is occupied. Eight leg and hoof slots is the price of being able to. Putting something on Or skip the screen entirely. Hold a piece of gear and right-click your horse, and it goes into the first empty slot that takes it — the same gesture that has always put a saddle or a barding on. The screen is what you want when you are choosing between things or taking something off; the click is what you want when you are holding the one piece and standing next to the horse. It is written up under rider comfort , with the rules it follows and the switch that turns it off. Click a slot and it tells you what fits. A short list drops under it: everything in your pack that this slot accepts, each with its picture, and a Take off line at the top for whatever is in there now. Click a line to put it on, click anywhere else to close the list. Or press Dress and drag. On your own horse, within eight blocks, the Gear tab has a Dress button in its top corner. It opens the same doll in a window of its own with your inventory down the right , and there it works like any chest: pick a piece up and put it on a slot, drag it back off, shift-click it either way. Shift-clicking a stack of something every hoof takes puts one on each hoof. The horse still turns if you drag it. Escape takes you back to the Gear tab; walking away from the horse just closes the window. An empty list still answers the question. “Nothing you are carrying fits here” and “nothing takes this slot yet” are different sentences, and which one you get is the difference between going to find the item and not bothering. Most slots say the second — see the box below. Nothing is ever deleted. What comes off goes to your inventory, or on the floor at your feet if there is no room. Your horse, and within eight blocks. The screen opens on any horse — a stranger’s, a cowboy’s string — so you can see what one is wearing and no more. A slot you cannot use is drawn dim rather than hidden, because a gap where a slot should be reads as a bug and a greyed slot reads as the rule it is. A foal wears nothing. Every slot is closed until it grows up. Tack sized for an adult is the one thing a growing horse should not be carrying. Thirteen of the nineteen are empty by design The saddle and the barding have had something that fits them all along, and the mane and tail now take the rescuing braid — the first piece of gear of this mod's own to fill a slot of this mod's own. The two packs take anything that stores items . The other thirteen accept nothing: not because they are broken, but because the gear that fills them has not been made. The tab says so on screen, so it reads as unfinished rather than faulty. Each piece joins its slot as it is made, and nothing on this page needs changing when one does. The Roadmap tab is what is coming. Gear does something, or it is not made Cosmetic-only equipment is the thing this mod does not add. Breeding is the one place pure aesthetics are allowed; everything else must provide utility, so tack, barding and hoof equipment carry real effects. That included the mane and tail slots, and the first thing to go in one is the test case: a rescuing braid looks exactly like a decorative plait and is in fact the strongest save in the game, with the colour — the only part of it that is decoration — riding on top of a mechanic that had already paid for the slot. The rule is philosophy § utility, not decoration , and it reversed an earlier “tack gets no stats” call — see design decisions . Chests on a horse A horse carries one container on each flank . Not a saddlebag of this mod's making: the chest, barrel or shulker box you already have. Anything you can place in the world and store items in, you can hang on the side of a horse — other mods' chests and crates included, with nothing to configure. You want to Do this Hang one Hold it and right-click your horse. It goes on the flank you are standing at, or the other one if that is taken. Or put it in a Pack slot on the Gear tab or in the Dress window. An adult horse of your own; a foal carries nothing. Open one Right-click the chest itself , where it hangs. A click anywhere else on the horse still does what it always did, which is usually to get on. From the saddle, where there is no clicking it: open the horse's screen, go to Gear, click the Pack slot and choose Open . Take one off Empty it first. A chest with anything in it will not come off, so nothing is ever spilled by a stray click. A shulker box is the exception: it comes off full, with its contents inside it, because that is what a shulker box is — and it goes on full the same way. The chest keeps its own screen and its own size. A chest or a barrel opens as the ordinary twenty-seven slots and a shulker box as a shulker box. Another mod's chest opens the screen it opens when it is placed — its own slots, its own upgrades and buttons. A shelf or a decorated pot, which have no screen even on the ground, open as a small plain grid. Nothing is locked and nothing is shrunk to fit the horse — what the horse limits is below. A modded chest travels as one piece: if the horse dies it drops as a single item with everything still inside, which goes back into it when you place it, rather than spilling. An ender chest opens your ender chest — whoever opens it — exactly as one standing on the ground does. Nothing in it is on the horse. What a load costs The more a horse carries, the slower it goes. Every item weighs the same, whatever it is: a stack of feathers and a stack of anvils are both sixty-four items. How many a horse can carry is its pulling ability — the same score a cart reads — so the horse you bred to haul a wagon is also the one that carries the most. It is a straight line. Half a load is half the slowdown, and an empty chest costs nothing at all. There is a most a horse can carry , and a horse loaded that far stands still until something is taken out. Nothing is refused at the chest — you can always put the item in; the horse answers for it. For an ordinary horse the default is twice what two full vanilla chests hold, so two full chests cost it half its speed and a weak horse cannot move them. An ender chest weighs nothing , however full. What is in it is somewhere else. The Gear tab says how many items the horse is carrying and how much of its speed it keeps. A server can turn the weight off, set the maximum, give every horse a free allowance, bend the curve and let an overloaded horse crawl rather than stop: the packs.* keys on the config page . What happens to it The horse dies: both chests and everything in them drop where it fell, as a donkey's do. A horse brought back afterwards comes back without them. You sell it or turn it loose: the chests and their contents are handed back to you first. You sign it over with a transfer paper : it goes as it stands, luggage and all. A stasis chamber, a ticket, a whistle: the chests travel with the horse. Only the owner can open, hang or take off a chest, and only from within eight blocks. What counts as storage Nobody keeps a list. A thing is storage if placing it makes a block that holds items, which is how another mod's chest works the day it is installed. Machines are left out — hoppers, droppers, dispensers, crafters, furnaces, brewing stands and jukeboxes hold items too, and none of them is somewhere you keep things. Item tags let a datapack adjust it: horsegenetics:horse_storage/denied (never), horsegenetics:gear/saddlebag_left and _right (always), horsegenetics:horse_storage/weightless (weighs nothing, like the ender chest) and horsegenetics:horse_storage/keeps_contents (comes off full, like a shulker box). Only the number of slots comes from the block. A modded drawer that holds thousands of one item in the world is that many ordinary slots on a horse.
