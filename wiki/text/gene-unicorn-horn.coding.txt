Unicorn horn
(Magical gene / recessive / grows geometry, paints nothing)
A horse with two copies grows a single tapering horn on its forehead — and it is the first thing in this mod that is a piece of the horse rather than a picture on it . Nothing about the coat changes. How long the horn is, how thick, how tightly it spirals, how far it leans and what colour it is are all written on the allele copy and inherited with it, so a horn you like breeds true and a line selected for length keeps getting longer.

The combination table Combination Outcome Effect n/n wild An ordinary horse Horn/n unicorn-carrier Nothing — a silent carrier Horn/Horn unicorn A horn on the forehead It paints nothing, and that is the novelty All three outcomes are wild types , so the baked coat texture is untouched and two horses that differ only here share one coat. That much it has in common with cutie mark and particle . What is new is what shows instead: not an overlay on the finished sheet but separate geometry — the first visual in the mod that is not a texture. See attached model parts for the machinery, which this gene is the first and so far only user of. Practically, that means neither coat golden should move for a change to this gene , and when the gene was added neither did: the pipeline golden's keys all grew a horsegenetics.unicorn_horn=n/n segment and not one of the coat hashes changed , and the bake golden gained exactly two rows that hash identically to each other. If a future edit here moves a coat hash, something is wrong that a golden update would hide. The epigenome, sorted by what it costs to change UnicornHornGene.hornFor(genotype, epigenome) reads the expressing copy's values and returns an AttachedPart . The interesting design decision is that the six numbers are grouped by what changing them costs rather than by what they mean: Value Kind Reaches Cost length uniform 0–1 the PartShape size bucket and the axial scale Mesh , plus free twist category, one per HornGenerator style the PartShape style Mesh girth uniform cross-axis scale Free — a pose transform tilt uniform, a little into the negative rotation about x Free tint_r/g/b bright colour channels, pale and high-value the submit's tint Free — a draw argument glow category; outcome zero glows a second full-bright submit One extra draw, only when set Why length is in both columns Length is not only a scale. A three-unit nub and a twenty-four-unit tusk want different segment counts : squashing fifteen boxes into three units leaves boxes a fraction of a texel wide, which shimmer as the camera moves, and stretching three boxes to twenty-four units gives a horn made of three visible blocks. So the segment count steps, and the steps are PartShape.SIZE_BUCKETS geometric buckets. That would leave a seam — a lineage's horns popping as they grew through a boundary — so the same number also drives a scale along the horn's own axis, chosen so the drawn length is exactly what the epigenome asked for. Because the buckets are geometric and there are sixteen of them, that scale is never more than about 7% from 1, so it stretches the mesh without visibly distorting the taper. PartGeneratorTest pins both halves: the drawn length matches the ladder at two hundred points, and the stretch never leaves that band. The bound on cost is structural, not a budget The whole set of meshes this mod can bake is PartKind.styles() × PartShape.SIZE_BUCKETS per kind — a fixed, small number, however many unicorns exist and whatever a drifted epigenome asks for. That is why PartMeshes has no eviction policy and no bake budget, where GeneticCoatTextureFactory needs both: a coat is a per-pixel loop and there are unboundedly many of them, and a part mesh is a dozen boxes of allocation with a hard ceiling on how many can ever exist. How the horn reaches the screen GrownParts.of(genotype, epigenome) — the one door the client uses, so the renderer never learns which loci grant parts and the next one is a line in that file. GeneticHorseRenderer.extractRenderState resolves it once per frame per horse onto GeneticHorseRenderState.parts , along with the three config flags. Deliberately not in the layer: CutieMarkLayer asks the genotype afresh on every submit and folds over the whole registry each time, which is affordable for one emblem and wrong for a pen of twenty horses. AttachedPartLayer walks the list. A horse with no parts returns on the first line, the way BraidLayer does for a horse wearing no braid. PartMeshes.get(shape) bakes the mesh the first time any horse needs it and keeps it. A layer definition can be baked at run time Every other model in the mod is registered during EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterLayerDefinitions , which would have forced attached parts into a fixed set declared at startup — and that would have made a horn's length one of five presets instead of a real number. It is not so constrained: LayerDefinition.bakeRoot() is public and pure ( mesh.getRoot().bake(xTexSize, yTexSize) , touching no registry, no event and no resource manager), so a mesh can be built the first time a horse wants one. Read out of the 26.1.2 sources, not assumed. See API notes . The deferred-submit trap, and why the model is typed on the part submitModel stores a copy of the pose and a reference to the model , and the batch renderer calls setupAnim(state) immediately before drawing each submit. So the obvious way to vary one shared mesh per horse — set visible or xScale on its parts and then submit — is a trap: the second horse in a frame overwrites the first horse's mesh before either is drawn, and the bug is one horse wearing another's horn. PartModel is therefore a Model<AttachedPart> rather than an EntityModel of the horse's state. The type parameter of submitModel is free, and choosing the part means anything that must be applied by mutation can be applied in setupAnim , where it runs per submit with that submit's own numbers. Nothing needs that yet — a horn's length, girth, lean, colour and glow are all a pose transform or a draw argument, and a pose is copied rather than shared — so setupAnim is empty. That emptiness is the interesting thing about the class: it is where an antler's tine count will have to go. The anchor, and the two models that do not agree PartAnchor is an enum and not a bone name, so common/ never learns Minecraft's part names and retargeting an anchor is a client edit rather than a gene edit. AttachedPartLayer is the only place in the mod that knows one. It walks the chain from the root part down — root , head_parts , head — where CutieMarkLayer reaches straight for body and hand-tunes its offsets to compensate. That difference is not tidiness: HdHorseModel.createHdLayer applies MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F) , and that transformer rewrites the root part's own pose (a 1.1 scale and a −2.4016 y offset), so a layer that skips the root works in a space 10% too small and two and a half units too high. Walking from the root means the number never appears in the layer, and means the same code is correct for the foal, whose layer carries no such transform at all. The two models share the bone names and nothing else. The adult head box spans y −11..−6 , z −2..5 ; the foal's spans y −3.95..0.05 , z −6.71..2.30 , on a head part with its own offset. So the layer keeps one offset per model, and a foal's horn additionally carries a scale of its own: vanilla's foal is a big-headed thing whose skull is nearly the adult's, so matching the head would have given a newborn a full-length tusk. Seen, 2026-09-30. Both computed offsets land: the owner watched a horn stay on the head in the dev yard and found it exactly placed on an adult, and in the right spot on a foal. The parenting through root head_parts head therefore does what the sources said it would. The foal's FOAL_SCALE errs the other way from the tusk it was guarding against: the horn comes out comically small. One greyscale sheet for every colour part_sheet.png is 64×64, cut into a 4×4 grid of 16×16 regions; two are painted (horn grain, and a denser darker tip) and the rest are room for antlers, crystals and feathering. It is greyscale on purpose — colour is the per-horse tint, the same method a dyed braid uses — so one file serves every horn a lineage ever drifts to, and no new art is needed when it does. A box reads the corner of its region rather than all of it: a cube's UV rectangle is its own unwrap, and the largest box any part generates is about 10×4 texels inside a 16-texel region. That is what lets a part's proportions change with an epigenetic number without needing a new sheet, and it is why the regions are a uniform tileable grain rather than a picture with a composition. Re-bake with node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-part-sheet.mjs ; the art is procedural and original, so nothing of anybody else's is in it. Why it is not filed with the coat genes GeneFamily.MAGIC_PARTS — “Grown parts” — is a new family, and the one enum constant is what the wiki sidebar, the custom spawn egg's picker and the browser designer's family filter all read. Cutie mark is overridden into the coat family because it is unambiguously a marking; a horn is not. It has a length in model units, it catches the light, it follows the head when the horse grazes, and none of those are things a coat gene can say. Filing the first one under “genes that put colour on the horse” would have been cheaper by one constant and would have given antlers, spines, crystals and wings the wrong home to be born into.
