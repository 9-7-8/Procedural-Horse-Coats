Items & recipes
(Gameplay / items)
Every item the mod adds, and where to read about it. They live in three creative tabs — Horse Genetics , then Horse Breeds for the one spawn egg per breed, then Horse Carts — and nearly all of them are built on one material: horse hair , which you get by shearing a horse.

The items Item What it is for Horse hair The material everything else is made from, taken raw by every recipe that wants it. Shear a horse for it. Breeding carrots — stabilizer, magnifier, epigenetic splice, gene splice Fed to a parent before breeding; changes what it passes on to the foal. Splice carrots — known, unknown, and five themed Add a gene to a foal, either one you chose or one at random. Research papers One gene and one allele pair — Agouti: A/a . Spend it on a splice carrot and that is the pair a foal gets. Equine Research Shelf Files research papers — one per allele pair — and copies them onto blank books, for as long as you keep the original. Tack Dyeing Bench Dyes a saddle’s seat, bridle and metal fittings separately — a dye in each leather row, a material in the fittings row. Any dye, and any ingot or gem, including ones another mod added . Seed jars Store a stallion’s genetics and breed a mare with him later. Vet’s kit Geld your stallion, or examine a mare’s heat and pregnancy. Freedom stick Give a horse back. In the horse realm only: heals it, unclaims it, and leaves its name and pedigree intact for whoever tames it next. Whistles — basic, golden, echo, ender Call every horse you own nearby — or, with an ender whistle bound to one horse, call that horse from anywhere. Double gates — one per wood, including other mods’ woods A fence gate two blocks wide, both leaves on one click. The doorway you can lead a horse through rather than shove it through. Jumps — one item, in any wood including modded ones A showjumping fence in three styles — vertical, oxer, crossrails. Side by side to widen it, stacked to raise it; it stops a horse like a fence does, and every block you add is a block more clearance asked for. The wood is carried on the item rather than being an item of its own, and the rails and the uprights can differ — both are set in the jump’s screen. Modded horse armour — one per metal another mod added A horse armour in that metal, tinted its own colour. You get them by installing a mod that adds an ingot or a gem; nothing here ships one. Stall signs Mark a room as one horse’s stall. Tickets — four tiers Send a horse to its stall. One use; the tier is how far it reaches. Holding pen sign & ticket Your one general pen: send any horse you own there, stall or no stall. Transfer papers How a horse changes hands. A signed one is drawn as the horse itself. The four posts — leatherworker’s, scientist’s, supplier’s, metalsmith’s Makes a villager into that one of the four equestrians . One post, one trade, one villager. Cowboy Hitch The posts’ fifth twin: makes a villager into a cowboy. Written up with the villagers . Spawn eggs — custom, breed, preset Build a horse gene by gene, get a foundation horse of a breed, or keep an exact horse in an item. Stasis chambers — four tiers Put one of your horses inside and it stops being an entity at all — no ticking, no hunger, no growing up, no cost to the server — until you let it out again, unchanged. Reusable. The tiers buy what the bank may do with a stored horse, not the storing itself: a Basic is only kept, an Intermediate is searchable and mended, an Advanced has its drops collected, and a Spacer can be put to stud without leaving the bank. Emergency Horse Stasis Chamber A Basic chamber surrounded by eight ender pearls, and the only item here you never use. Carry it and every horse you own is insured: the instant one falls below a tenth of its health it is in the bottle , wherever you and it happen to be, and nothing can hurt it in there. One chamber is one life — a second horse is refused out loud, and the answer is to carry two. A spare empty one filed in a stasis bank you placed insures your horses too. Horse Stasis Bank A chest that takes stasis chambers and nothing else — any tier, full or empty — so the horses you have shelved live somewhere you can find them. Six rows of nine, and the title bar counts the horses in it. Three tabs: the grid, a searchable list of the horses inside , and a supply room that feeds and mends them, collects what they drop, and breeds a pair you turn out at stud. Rescuing braid Any wool, a horse hair and an ender pearl, worked into a horse's mane or tail. It breaks the instant that horse would die and puts it — and you, if you were riding — back in its own stall, bolting. One use per braid, and a horse has two hair slots. Dyed like leather armour. Two rows of that table are not a fixed length The double gates and the modded horse armours are generated from whatever other mods are installed , so the roster you actually have is longer than the one above in exactly the measure your pack is. Vanilla’s twelve woods ship as files; every other wood, and every ingot or gem any mod added, is found by reading those mods’ jars at startup and written into a pack in .minecraft/phc/generated/ . With no such mods installed — the common case — nothing is generated and no folder is left behind. The whole mechanism, and what about it is still unproven, is on the compatibility page . House rules for every recipe Three things that are true of all of them Shapeless , with exactly one exception: the blank transfer paper is a ring of eight paper around a horse hair, and the ring is the idea. Everywhere else the shapes are not the puzzle. At least one modded ingredient in every recipe, and every output is a modded item, so nothing can collide with a vanilla or third-party recipe. That holds for the generated ones too — a modded metal’s armour takes a horse hair for its padding, and outputs an item in this mod’s own namespace, which is what keeps it off BHAR ’s recipe space and everyone else’s. The one exception is the double fence gates , which are two vanilla gates and nothing else — see below. Horse hair goes in raw. There is no material chain to climb: whatever a recipe wants hair for, it takes horse hair . Nothing is a trap because there is nothing to convert and no conversion to undo. The double gates are outside the modded-ingredient rule Every other recipe in the mod carries something of ours. A double fence gate is two vanilla fence gates of the wood, shapeless, and that is the whole recipe. It took a braided rope for the hinge until the rope was removed; the owner’s call was that a gate should not want horse hair at all, and a fence line is the one thing you build dozens of. Why the risk is judged acceptable: nothing in vanilla consumes a fence gate — every vanilla recipe that mentions one produces it — so there is no collision to have today. What remains is a third-party mod shipping its own two-gate shapeless recipe, which would make one of the two uncraftable with nothing logged. check-recipes.mjs reads only this mod’s folder and cannot see it. If a modded pack ever reports a double gate that will not craft, this is the first place to look. What does not work yet Worth knowing before you spend materials on one: Tickets and stalls shipped together in 0.3.7 and neither has been played . A sheared horse still looks unsheared, so you cannot tell by eye which of your horses you have already done today. Tack: the saddle is dyeable A saddle carries three colours — seat, bridle and metal — and an undyed one looks exactly as it always has. That second half is the point: the change reaches donkeys, mules, skeleton horses and zombie horses too, and none of them should look any different from the day before it landed. Vanilla does not tint saddles — it builds the saddle’s equipment asset with the plain, non-dyeable layer, which is why a dyed-saddle trade was written and withdrawn before this existed. This mod ships its own asset in the vanilla pattern instead: three disjoint layers, each taking its own colour, so the hardware can stay metal whatever the leather does or be dyed with it. Vanilla’s dyed_color could not have done it at any number of layers — it holds one value per stack and is read once, outside the layer loop — so the three live in the mod’s own tack_tint component instead. That is also what brought the trade back: the leatherworker’s dyed tack writes the mod’s own three-zone tack_tint rather than vanilla’s dyed_color , so it reaches the saddle and the fittings both. The textures are derived, not drawn Tinting multiplies , so a brown base has no headroom and most dyes collapse into mud. The base therefore has to be desaturated — and rather than redraw vanilla’s saddle by hand, neoforge-26.1.2/tools/tack/bake-saddle-textures.ps1 derives it. The split needs no artistic judgement, which is why it can be a script: vanilla’s sheet holds exactly nine colours, six saturated browns and three pure greys, so leather is saturated and hardware is R = G = B . That rule is asserted in the bake, so a future vanilla retexture that breaks it fails loudly instead of shipping a muddy saddle. Each leather pixel is vanilla’s colour divided by the tint applied when undyed, so base × undyed reproduces vanilla exactly. Measured worst error is zero — the shipped files were read back and recomposed against vanilla pixel by pixel. The inventory icon carries its own constant, because its palette is genuinely brighter than the sheet’s and the entity’s divisor would clamp it. What it deliberately does not touch Camels, pigs, striders and nautiluses. The asset covers all ten saddle layer types because it replaces vanilla’s whole file, but those five carry vanilla’s entry verbatim. A horse mod has no business repainting camels. The Tack Dyeing Bench is what dyes one. Craft it, or use the one standing at a cowboy’s homestead. It colours the seat, the bridle and the fittings independently, and a saddle you already own can be brought to it — this rides on minecraft:saddle rather than on an item of our own. It conflicts with Saturated Saddles. Both replace the same vanilla file, so whichever loads later wins. They do the same job, so the result is not broken either way — but running both is pointless, and ours needs no resource pack. Tack fits a resized horse now — verified Confirmed in a world on 2026-09-16: “the saddle now fits resized breeds correctly”. This had been open long enough to be written up as a prerequisite for any further tack work, and the recorded diagnosis was wrong : it said the equipment layers did not inherit the horse’s genetic scale. They always did — LivingEntityRenderer scales the pose stack and then iterates the layers, inside the same push and pop. The real cause was a missing mesh transform. Vanilla builds three horse layers with MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F) applied — the body, the armour and the saddle — and applies it in LayerDefinitions , one level above the mesh function. HdHorseModel is a copy of that mesh function, so the transform was never in the part that was copied and its absence is invisible on inspection. The body therefore drew at 1.0 while tack baked from vanilla’s layers drew at 1.1 — and since the transform is scaled(f).translated(0, 24.016 × (1 − f), 0) , the tack sat 2.4 units high as well as 10 % large. So it was never really about resizing. The bug was on every horse including scale 1.0; the size locus multiplied the visible gap rather than causing it, which is exactly why it read as “worse on big horses” and sent the original diagnosis looking in the renderer. One line in createHdLayer() fixed it, and the side effect is that every horse is now about 10 % larger than it used to render, because the HD body had been the small one all along. Somebody else’s tack, in a chest Chests now hold horse tack. About one chest in five — any chest, vanilla or another mod’s — carries one piece: a leather horse armour, a saddle, or an iron, golden or diamond horse armour, plus one of every modded metal’s armour you happen to have. Leather is the commonest and diamond the rarest, so a diamond armour in a chest is still the best thing in it. The leather pieces come already dyed , in the mod’s own three zones, with the two leather zones rolled separately — an unrelated pair, because a piece somebody lost in a dungeon should not look like it came off a shop rack. The fittings are deliberately cheap: iron, copper, bone, coal, basalt or gold, never a gem. The leatherworker’s five tiers are a ladder ending in lapis-dyed tack on diamond, and that is only worth climbing if a chest cannot hand you the top rung. Golden carrot seeds — a crop, and not a gold farm A golden carrot normally costs eight gold nuggets, which puts a hard ceiling on how many horses you can afford to work with. Golden carrot seeds grow them instead: an ordinary crop on ordinary farmland, eight ages, bone-mealable, exactly like vanilla’s carrot in every mechanical respect. No gold comes out of it at any point. The loot table drops golden carrots and its own seeds and nothing else — no nugget, no ingot — and a golden carrot cannot be turned back into gold by any vanilla recipe. It is a way to grow horse food , and a player who plants a field of it ends up with a field of horse food. The gate is getting the first seeds, and there are exactly two ways: The equestrian supplier ’s top rank , at 38 emeralds for one, twice per restock. Chest loot — 20% in any chest, one to three seeds when it hits. Not a list of dungeons any more: it was 7% across eight named vanilla tables, and the owner played a heavily modded server for weeks without ever finding one, because nearly every chest she opened belonged to somebody else’s structure. See every chest, any mod on the Coding tab. They are not craftable from anything , and a golden carrot itself will not plant. That second point is the load-bearing one: if a golden carrot were the seed, every golden carrot ever crafted from eight nuggets would be plantable and the rarity would be worth nothing. A mature plant gives one golden carrot and two seeds, so a field doubles and pays for itself once you have started it. No art ships for it The crop wears vanilla’s own carrot stage textures , tinted gold; the seed icon is vanilla’s wheat seeds, tinted the same gold. Two routes to one colour, because an item tint cannot reach a block in the world and a block tint cannot reach an item in a slot — so the constant lives once, on client/GoldTintSource.GOLD , and both read it. This is also the mod’s first block tint , and 26.1.2 does not have the lambda-per-tint-index block colour handler older versions did: RegisterColorHandlersEvent.BlockTintSources takes a list of one-method BlockTintSource , indexed by the model’s tint index. The four stage models therefore parent a template of ours carrying tintindex: 0 , because minecraft:block/crop has none and a model without one renders plain orange however loud the handler shouts.
