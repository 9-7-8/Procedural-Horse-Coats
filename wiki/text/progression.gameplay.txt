The checklist & the collection
(Gameplay / progression)
Two answers to “what do I do now?” — a checklist that walks you through everything the mod can do, and a collection of every allele in it with the ones you have never met still under fog.

The checklist Not a page of its own any more. Every task sits under the sub-chapter of Getting started that teaches it — the box for hanging a stall sign is directly beneath the paragraphs about hanging one — and each unticked one carries a line saying what to actually do. The count is ProgressTask.values().length , across the chapters below, from watching a wild band to signing a transfer paper. A Checklist section at the end of the contents is the overview: the running total, a bar, and only what is still outstanding , grouped by chapter. A chapter with every box ticked drops out of it entirely, so that section shrinks as you play. A finished sub-chapter goes gold A sub-chapter whose every checklist item is ticked is painted amber in the contents list down the left. It is the one place the page says “you are finished here” without your having to open it and count boxes, and it is the reason every sub-chapter carries at least one task : one with nothing to do could never be finished, so an empty task list is deliberately never coloured. It gates nothing. Nothing in the mod asks whether a task is done. A player who reads this wiki and builds a research shelf on their first day ticks two boxes at once and is not stopped — the moment a checklist gates content it stops being advice and starts being homework. Every task is also an advancement Ticking a box off is how this mod earns you a real Minecraft advancement — the toast in the corner, the line in chat, a card in the advancement screen under a Horse Genetics tab of its own, and a name /advancement and any datapack can use. There is one per task, plus one per chapter and a root that holds the tab, and nothing about them is hand-written: they are baked off the same enum the book’s boxes are drawn from, so the two lists cannot drift. The tree is one tab, eight branches. The root is earned by ticking anything at all; each chapter node by ticking anything in that chapter, so the tree lights up as you wander into a subject rather than only when you have exhausted it; and each task hangs off its chapter. A handful are purple challenge frames — identical twins, a mutation, a dhampir tamed, steering a horse bare — and a handful gold goals , for the moments something opens up. The card’s description is the first sentence of the same hint the book gives you. The mod no longer says anything itself It used to print its own green [✓] line with a running count. That is gone: the advancement announces itself, in vanilla’s words, through vanilla’s settings — so a player who wants silence can turn toasts or the announceAdvancements gamerule off and be obeyed, which a hand-rolled chat line never let them do. Two announcements of one event is one too many. A save from before this pops them all at once The first login on a world whose boxes were ticked before the advancements existed awards every one of them in one go, so expect a run of toasts. It happens once: an advancement already held is never re-awarded. The catch-up is on purpose — a task fires exactly once, ever, so without it those advancements could never be earned at all. The chapters are: Horses in the wild — first, because every horse a player meets is wild until they tame one. Where the breeds are and founding one from a spawn egg; a wild horse drifting over to food and galloping for its favourite; taming from the saddle and from the ground; being kicked by one, and watching one bolt; reading a wild horse's place in its band. Then the band itself — sparring, a dam fetching her foal, a stallion driving a stray mare back in and later losing the band to a challenger, rank settled at the water, grooming partners standing head to tail, grazing, and a meat-eater hunting. Husbandry — what a horse is and naming it, feeding and watering and healing, then the whole injury-to-mended thread — a horse of yours getting hurt, putting food into a hungry one, and seeing it the rest of the way back to full — grooming and what horse hair becomes, bonding to each of its three tiers and the two things riding bare teaches you (that it bonds exactly as well as a saddled ride, and that at the top tier the horse steers without tack), stalls and holding pens and the four tickets — including hanging a stall on a double gate , the doorway wide enough to lead a horse through — the four whistles, dyeing your tack , and milking a mare. Nothing about breeding at all: keeping a horse is a whole game before genetics is. Basic breeding — heat and natural covering, reading a mare with a vet's kit, pregnancy and foaling and twins, filling and using a seed jar, and gelding — having one done, owning one however he got that way, and feeling him settle to you faster than a stallion would. No golden carrots : the first breeding a player does is the one the horses arrange themselves. Genetics — taking a gene off a horse with a book, earning one into your database, the research shelf, the gene carrot, the splice carrots, and the hay portal as a showroom for genes you have not bred yet. Magical horses — its own chapter rather than a corner of genetics, because a magical gene paints after the coat has resolved instead of restricting pigment on the way, and it arrives by its own routes. What one is and the magical splice carrot; finding and taming a dhampir of either strain, the seal brown nine in ten and the white one in ten; a magical herd, where every horse carries and shows the same single magical gene; and the two traits that change how you keep a horse at all — burning in daylight, and drinking blood. Breeding projects — a foal carrying something it does not show, one that breeds true, and line breeding for a recessive. The villagers — trade with an equestrian , meet a cowboy, sign a transfer paper. The trade task takes any of the four, on purpose: which post a player happens to put down first should not be the difference between a ticked box and an unticked one. Some boxes tick because you watched Most of the wild chapter is horses doing things to each other , which a player cannot perform. Those tasks complete through HorseProgress.completeForWatcher — the nearest player within twenty-four blocks, with line of sight, is credited. Their titles say watch for that reason: an unticked box phrased as an instruction, for something nobody can instruct, reads as a bug. The line-of-sight test is deliberate too — a box that ticked for a fight behind a hill would be claiming you had seen something you had not. Every item in the mod has a task Deliberately, and it is worth keeping true as items are added: every registered item a player can obtain in survival is named by at least one checklist task. The only items without one are the custom and preset horse spawn eggs , which have no recipe and no trade and exist for creative and debugging. The collection The Alleles tab is every allele of every gene in the mod. The ones you have met are named and lit; the ones you have not are ??? and a row of question marks the right length. Each gene shows how many of its alleles you have, and the header counts the lot. Meeting an allele is not the same as discovering a gene Every allele on any horse you tame or breed joins the collection — both copies, at every locus, baseline alleles included. That is a record of what you have laid eyes on, and the plain wild-type alleles count exactly as much as the strange ones. Discovering a gene is the other thing and is deliberately harder: it takes owning a living horse that carries something other than the plain baseline there — tamed, bred, bought or given — and that is also what unlocks the gene's carrot recipe. A research paper will not do it; papers are components, not lessons. An ordinary horse fills in a lot of your collection and discovers nothing. So the two tabs answer different questions. The gene database is what can I make? ; the collection is what is left out there?
