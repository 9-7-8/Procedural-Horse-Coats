Antlers
(Magical gene / dominant, sex-limited allele / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One copy and the horse grows a rack of antlers from the top of its skull — on a mare or a stallion with Ant , on a stallion only with Antm . No two racks are alike: how big, how many tines, how far they reach, how thick and what shade of bone are all written on the allele and inherited with it. The second gene in the mod, after the unicorn horn , that grows a piece of the horse rather than colouring it.

Crossing two of them Two ways to have antlers Ant — antlers on every horse that carries it, mare or stallion, the way a reindeer has them. Antm — antlers on a stallion only, the way a red deer has them. A mare with it shows nothing at all and passes it on. The genotype alone will not tell you : a mare's Antm/n reads exactly like a stallion's. Breed her to a stallion and look at the colts. Both are dominant, so one copy is enough, and Ant wins over Antm . Foals of either sex grow no antlers; the rack comes in when the horse grows up, and stays — antlers here are permanent, never cast. Finding one Rare: about four wild horses in a thousand carry an antler allele, and the stallion-only one is the commoner. So most antlered horses you find in the wild are stallions — and somewhere out there are mares carrying Antm that look like nothing at all. The Rimehart is the one breed that is antlered on every horse, mares included. Two racks are not alike Every one of these is on the antler allele, and a foal that inherits the allele inherits the rack: Size — five classes, yearling , small , standard , large and massive . Wild horses are small to standard; breeding for size climbs to massive and stops there. A bigger rack is more beam and more tines, not a small one stretched. Tines — how many points. One to six in the wild; a bred line can reach eight on a massive rack. It grows continuously: a line bred toward more tines grows the next one in gradually rather than snapping it on. The rack itself — where the tines sit and how they turn. It almost never changes between parent and foal. Reach and girth — a little longer or shorter, a little thicker or thinner, on top of the size. Shade — from pale bone to dark umber. Asymmetry — none on any wild horse. Every wild rack is the same on both sides. Only a breed can make a rack lopsided — the Rimehart does — and once a line has it, the foals carry it on. Five genes make a rack This gene decides whether , and how big. Four more finish the rack, and each does nothing to a horse without antlers: Antler form — how it grows: forked, brow-tined, palmate, crowned or a plain spike. Antler glow — the points glow in the dark. Crystal antlers — see-through, in a gem colour. Blooming antlers — leaves, moss or blossom on the tines. A horse can have a unicorn horn and antlers at once. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill. Antlers move no stat, grant no attack, change no hitbox, and do not stop the horse going through a doorway — however big the rack looks. It is worth exactly what you think it looks worth. If it costs you frames A rack is a few dozen small boxes, more than a horn.
