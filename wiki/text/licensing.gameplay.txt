Licensing
(Project / who owns what, and what you may do)
The code is MIT. The art is CC BY‑SA 4.0. A few pieces that ship with the mod are somebody else’s and keep their own terms. This page says that twice — once in plain words, once in the formal language — and then lists the exceptions file by file. The author is not a lawyer and does not sell this software; the licences are here so people know they may share and build on it, not to be defended in court.

In plain words What Licence What you can do The code — every .java , .js , .mjs , .gradle file, the tests and the tools, and the data files that drive the game (genes, breeds, recipes, language files) MIT Anything. Use it, change it, sell it, put it in your own mod, closed or open. Keep the copyright line and the licence text with it. The art — the textures, sprites, icons, models and wiki illustrations we made, and the wiki and README writing CC BY‑SA 4.0 Anything, including selling it. Credit us, link back, say what you changed, and release your changed version under the same licence. Other people’s pieces that ship in this repo or the jar Their own, listed below Read their row. Some you may reuse, some you may not take out of this mod at all. Common questions Can I put it in my modpack? Yes. Credit the mod; nothing else is asked. A paid modpack is fine as far as our own parts go — but see the third-party rows before you repackage the files they own. Can I fork it and sell the fork? Yes for the code (MIT). The art stays CC BY-SA, so a fork that keeps our art must keep it under CC BY-SA and credit it. The three Raven icons may not be redistributed by anyone, forks included. Can I use the code in a closed-source mod? Yes. That is what MIT allows. The only price is carrying the copyright line and licence text along. Can I use the art in a closed-source mod? You can ship it in one, but the art itself stays CC BY-SA: credit it, and if you change a texture, the changed texture is CC BY-SA too. Your code is not infected by that. What about the horses? A coat is drawn by our code from a genome. The picture it makes belongs to whoever made that horse; we claim nothing over it. Screenshots, videos and streams of the game are yours to share. It used to say CC BY-NC. It did. Every old release is now also available under MIT and CC BY-SA, so nobody needs to hold on to the old terms.
