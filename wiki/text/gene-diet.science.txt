Diet — what the horse will eat
(Natural gene / paints nothing)
A wild type that eats what any horse eats, and a set of narrow diets — raw meat, fish, wheat, cake, potions, lava, one metal, one gem, and blood — each needing two identical copies to show. A narrow diet refuses almost everything, and what it does accept feeds it far better: a single gold bar takes a metal-eater to full health however hurt it was.

This is the one gene here with no real locus behind it Every other natural gene on this wiki names a published variant. This one does not: dietary preference is heritable, but there is no equine “lava” allele and no gene that makes a horse eat gold. So what this tab does instead is set out what a real horse actually eats and what the Minecraft food list means to one, because that is the part of the gene a player can be wrong about — and because the Dveg , Dwht and Dhum ends of the allele list are near enough to real feeding to be misread as advice. What a horse is built to eat Horses are non-ruminant hindgut-fermenting herbivores . A relatively small stomach and a very large caecum and colon are built for frequent intake of fibrous plant material; grazing horses eat small amounts for much of the day, and gut microbes fermenting forage fibre supply a large share of their usable energy. Ohio State AS-1022 A sound feeding programme is: Forage as the foundation — pasture, grass hay, sometimes legume hay or forage products. Guidance commonly calls for at least 1.5–2% of body weight per day as forage dry matter; forage should be at least half the ration and is usually much more. Unlimited clean water , and salt or minerals suited to the horse and the local forage. Concentrate only if needed — a growing horse, a lactating mare, a senior with poor teeth, a hard keeper, a horse in real work. Slow changes. Switch hay, pasture or feed over roughly 7–14 days. Abrupt changes or large sugary or starchy meals disrupt the hindgut microbes and raise the risk of colic and laminitis. For scale: a 500 kg adult might need roughly 7.5–10 kg of forage dry matter a day. Treats count toward total calories and should be tiny against that. Kentucky Equine Research Where the mod’s diets sit against that Dveg (raw vegetables) and Dwht (wheat) are the only two narrow diets in the mod a real horse could survive on at all, and even they are wrong in kind — a horse needs forage , and neither carrots nor wheat is hay. Dmeat , Dfish , Dhum (cooked food) and Dcake describe things a real horse should never be fed, and Dlava , Ding , Dgem , Dpot and Dwat are frank fantasy. That is fine — this is a Minecraft gene — and it is worth a player knowing that the one item the mod treats as ordinary horse food, hay , is the only one on the whole list that a real horse actually lives on. The gene’s own source calls this out: it is feral-only and never carried by a named breed precisely because a lava-eating Shire is a curiosity of the unbred population rather than a breed trait. Can a horse eat meat? A horse may mouth or swallow an odd bit out of curiosity. It should not be fed meat deliberately: its feeding ecology and hindgut microbial system are built around continuous fibrous plant matter, not animal protein and fat. A tiny accidental lick of cooked meat is not the same as a poisoning emergency — and it is still not a treat, not a useful protein supplement, and not part of the diet. Raw meat adds foodborne bacteria and parasite risk; fatty, salted, seasoned or spoiled meat adds digestive upset. Fish, and pufferfish above all, are a firmer no. If a real horse has eaten a substantial amount of something inappropriate — especially pufferfish, unidentified mushrooms, green or sprouted potatoes, or a large sugary or starchy quantity — remove access and call a vet or an equine poison-control resource promptly rather than waiting for symptoms. Does breed change the diet? The basic diet does not change by breed. Every horse needs forage-first feeding, water, minerals and gradual transitions. What changes is the amount and the energy density. type or situation feeding implication Ponies, many Morgans and Quarter Horses, drafts, some warmbloods often easy keepers ; fewer calories, careful control of lush pasture, sugary treats and starch to avoid obesity, insulin dysregulation and laminitis Thoroughbreds and similarly lean, high-metabolism horses more often hard keepers ; more calories, ideally from digestible fibre and managed fat rather than large grain meals Draft horses more total feed because they are large — not necessarily more per kilogram ; many gain readily Growing foals and yearlings carefully balanced protein, amino acids and minerals — calcium, phosphorus, copper, zinc — and energy for steady growth Pregnant and lactating mares progressively more energy, protein, lysine and minerals, sharpest in late pregnancy and early lactation Performance horses energy matched to workload, plus water, electrolytes and forage availability Seniors, or poor dentition softer, soaked, high-fibre feeds — soaked hay products, beet pulp — under guidance Breed is only one input. Body weight, condition score, age, individual metabolism, workload, pregnancy, teeth, health conditions, forage analysis, climate and pasture access matter at least as much. Merck names ponies, drafts, warmbloods, Morgans and many Quarter Horses as common easy keepers and Thoroughbreds as tending toward hard keepers — while insisting the starting ration is adjusted to the individual horse’s observed condition. The one thing here the mod could act on Easy keeper versus hard keeper is a real, breed-correlated, quantitative trait, and it is the part of this page with an obvious mod-shaped hole next to it: it is exactly the sort of thing a breed file could carry as a feed-efficiency modifier, sitting alongside this locus rather than inside it. The Diet enum already grades efficiency by breadth — four points an item for Dany , a hay bale’s worth for Dmeat , full health for a single ingot — so the machinery for “this horse gets more out of the same feed” exists and is not currently connected to anything a real horse does. Is dietary preference actually heritable? Partly, and not in the way this gene models. Feed preference and intake behaviour in horses are shaped by early experience with the dam, forage availability, palatability learning, social facilitation in a herd, and individual metabolic state — with breed-level differences in efficiency (the easy-keeper pattern above) better attested than breed-level differences in what a horse will accept. No single equine locus has been validated as controlling what a horse chooses to eat. So the mod’s decision to declare this locus natural is, as its own source says, arguable. It paints in neither phase, so the flag chooses nothing about the coat; it is natural because dietary preference is a real heritable thing, and because a magical gene would be thrown into the breed founder’s magic lottery, which is the opposite of what the locus wants. The lava and ingot ends are frank fantasy. If the natural/magical split ever means more than which coat pass a gene paints in, this is the first gene to revisit.
