Crystal antlers
(Magical gene / recessive / turns the antlers to crystal, paints nothing)
Two copies and a horse's antlers are crystal: the beams and tines see-through in a gem colour, the points solid. Which gem is inherited. On a horse without antlers this gene does nothing at all.

Crossing two of them Finding one Recessive and rare: about one wild horse in twenty has two copies, and almost none of them has antlers. A crystal rack is something you breed — find a two-copy horse, and cross it into an antlered line. What it looks like The rack keeps its shape and takes a gem's colour instead of bone's: clear quartz, amethyst, rose quartz, citrine, emerald, sapphire or ruby. The beams and tines are see-through — you can see the head behind them — and the points are solid, which gives each tine an edge. The gem is written on the allele and almost never changes between parent and foal, so a line of amethyst antlers stays amethyst. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill. Crystal antlers are no harder, sharper or more fragile than bone ones; they are only a look. Its gene carrot
