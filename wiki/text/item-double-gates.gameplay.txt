Double gates
(Block / the yard)
A fence gate two blocks wide . One item places both halves, one click swings both leaves, and there is no post down the middle to catch a horse on. One per wood, and it is a stall ’s boundary in every state exactly as a vanilla gate is.

What it is for A horse is about 1.4 blocks wide and a one-block gateway is a thing you shove a horse through. A double gate is the doorway you actually lead one through — and because both leaves are driven by one click, opening it is one action rather than two. Recipe (shapeless): 2 vanilla fence gates of that wood. That is the whole recipe — no horse hair . It took a braided rope for the hinge until the rope was removed, which priced every gate in a fence line at twelve horse hair . It is the one recipe in the mod carrying no modded ingredient at all. The rope was what kept these off everyone else’s recipe space: every recipe in this mod carries at least one modded ingredient so that none of them can collide with a vanilla or third-party one ( the house rules ). Dropping it gives that up for this one recipe. The trade was judged worth it because a fence line is the one thing you build dozens of, and because nothing in vanilla consumes a fence gate — every vanilla recipe that mentions one produces it — so there is no collision to have unless another mod ships its own two-gate recipe. check-recipes.mjs reads only this mod’s folder and would not see that, so a modded pack reporting a double gate that will not craft should be traced here first. One per wood, filed beside its own gate Every wood vanilla has a fence gate for: oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia, dark oak, pale oak, mangrove, cherry, bamboo, crimson and warped. They live in the vanilla Building Blocks tab, each one directly after the single gate of its own wood, because that is where somebody reaching for a gate looks rather than in a tab about horses. And one per wood any other mod brought Install a mod that adds a wood and you get a double gate in it, with no patch and nothing to switch on. The recipe is the same shape — two of that mod’s own fence gates and nothing else — and the gate is filed in the Building Blocks tab directly after that mod’s single gate, exactly as the twelve are filed after vanilla’s. It wears that mod’s own plank texture . Nothing is drawn for it, because a double gate has never had art of its own — only a pointer at a plank texture — so a modded wood needs that mod’s planks and nothing else. The list is read out of the other mods’ jars before anything registers; how, and what else comes with it, is on the compatibility page . Two things about a modded gate that are not true of the twelve Two mods that both add “maple” give you two gates both called “Maple Double Fence Gate” — distinct items with distinct ids, identical names. The mod’s name is left out on purpose, because “Somebody’s Big Biome Mod Maple Double Fence Gate” is worse to read in the far commoner case where there is only one maple. And a wood whose mod declares a gate it never actually registers gets an uncraftable gate : the block exists and places, but the recipe is skipped rather than written, with a line in the log naming the mod and the id. That is deliberate — a recipe naming an item nothing answers to does not quietly do nothing, it fails to parse in three walls of red. Placing and breaking One item, two blocks. You place the half you are pointing at and the other appears beside it, on whichever side is free. It refuses rather than leaving half a gate. If neither side has room, nothing is placed and the item stays in your hand. There is no such thing as half a double gate standing on its own. Breaking either half takes both , and you get your one item back. The half you did not break is removed rather than dropped, so a gate is never a way to duplicate anything. Opening it Click either half and both swing. Like a vanilla gate it turns to open away from you if you are on the wrong side of it, and both halves turn together. Redstone works on the pair rather than on a block: a signal reaching either half opens both, so a lever at one end of a two-wide gate does what it looks like it should. It closes a stall, like any other gate A stall sign treats a door, a trapdoor and a fence gate as the edge of a room whatever state it is in , open or shut. A double gate is a fence gate for that purpose, so a stall shut with one binds exactly as a stall shut with a vanilla gate does — and the checklist has a husbandry step for building one.
