Back sail
(Magical gene / recessive / grows geometry, paints nothing)
Two copies and the horse grows a sail along its back: a row of spines from the withers to the croup, with a see-through membrane stretched between them. Whether it peaks over the middle of the back, runs low the whole length or sags in scallops, and how tall, arched, long and see-through it is, is written on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own, back sail colour . The one big, rare body part, and the first grown part you can see through.

Gene key horsegenetics.back_sail Class BackSailGene Priority 243 (magical band), with its colour locus at 244; only a code-order slot Family Grown parts Alleles Sail n , recessive Founders Hardy–Weinberg at 0.05 per allele ( WILD_SAIL_FREQUENCY ), the unicorn’s and the dragon horns’ figure Epigenome length (0–0.7, clamp 0–1), count (6 to SailGenerator.MAX_SPINES , never under 1), coverage (0.65–1, clamp 0.35–1), curve (0–1), opacity (0.35–0.70, clamp MIN_OPACITY – MAX_OPACITY ), form (one of three) Part SAIL at the SPINE anchor; spines on BONE and BONE_TIP , membrane on MEMBRANE Coat effect none ; every outcome is a wild type Slice B of the body parts The second body part ( model parts ), and the one that made translucency data on the kind instead of a constant in the renderer ( translucent regions ). Like the spines, it adds no layer, render-state field or config key. It is one kind ( SAIL , centred), SailGenerator and SailSize , and one line in GrownParts.of dressed by its colour locus . The granting locus is in GrownParts.shapes ’s every-part baseline. The mesh: spines with panels between SailGenerator stands its spines in the dorsal row’s places ( DorsalSpineGenerator.rootZ ), so a sail and a spine row number their elements the same way. Each spine is its own root chain in its own group, upright. Unlike a dorsal spine, a fin ray does not rake, because a panel is built in the frame of one spine and has to meet the next. The membrane between spine i - 1 and spine i is COLUMNS flat boxes ( PartNode.width , thin across the horse) hung off spine i ’s root, in group i . Spine 0 has none. That one choice is why the count and the saddle zone need nothing new: hiding a spine hides the panel in front of it, so no panel is ever left with nothing at its back edge, and the spine’s height scale reaches its panel because the panel is its child. A column stands as tall as a straight edge from tip to tip would be over it, less a sine sag on the scalloped form. The epigenome, by cost length : the class is the mesh’s size bucket (four; a taller sail is more boxes per spine), the remainder a per-element height stretch. form and curve : baked, SailGenerator.style(form, curve) , three forms by three curve buckets. The curve is the arch of the outline. count : shown out of the most baked, front to back, the antler’s tine rule. coverage : AttachedPart.span , a pose-stack scale along the row ( row span ). The saddle zone is recomputed for it. opacity : AttachedPart.opacity , the membrane pass’s alpha, times the horse’s own fade. There is no girth . The per-element scale across a spine would scale its panel’s width too, and open or close the gap to the next spine. So backSail passes girth 1, and the spines are as thick as their class bakes them. How its colour lands The colour locus’s base colour is the membrane and its tip colour the spines: the see-through region takes the base, as a crystal antler’s shafts do, and the solid regions the tip. The bone version is Bon/Bon on that locus. GrownParts.of then sets the opacity to 0, and the layer skips the membrane pass, which leaves bare bone rays. One bone copy is only a colour and keeps its membrane ( AbstractPartColourGene.boneOnly ).
