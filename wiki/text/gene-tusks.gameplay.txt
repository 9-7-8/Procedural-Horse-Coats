Tusks
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One gene for the growths of the mouth and muzzle, with a form for each allele. Today it has one form, the narwhal horn : one long spiral tusk growing straight forward out of the front of the muzzle. Its length, thickness, twist and lift are written on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own, tusk colour . Boar tusks and sabre fangs are the next two forms (see the Roadmap tab).

Crossing two of them One copy, either sex Dominant: Nar/n and Nar/Nar both grow the horn, and a second copy does not grow a second horn or a bigger one. It is not sex-limited: unlike the antlers, no allele grows only on stallions. Foals grow none; the horn comes in when the horse grows up, and stays. Where it sits On the front of the nose, in the middle, pointing forward along the line of the face. It moves with the head, so it dips when the horse grazes. It does not sit where a unicorn’s horn does, so one horse can grow both. Finding one Very rare in the wild: about one horse in a thousand has a narwhal horn, and because one copy is enough, every horse that carries the gene shows it. The skeleton horses can grow one in bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts. What varies Length : from a short tusk just past the end of the face, through long , to a great one longer than the horse’s body is deep. Wild horses are short to long; a line bred for length keeps growing it, up to the ceiling. Twist : a spiral or a tight spiral. A narwhal horn is always straight; it never curves. Thickness . Lift : how far the point is raised above the line of the face. Everything stacks: a horse can wear a narwhal horn, a unicorn horn, antlers and every body part at once. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no ability, no hitbox. It is a look. Its gene carrot
