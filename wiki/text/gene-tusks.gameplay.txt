Tusks
(Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing)
One gene for the growths of the mouth and muzzle, with a form for each allele: a narwhal horn , one long spiral tusk straight out of the front of the muzzle; boar tusks , a pair curling up from the corners of the lower jaw; and sabre fangs , a pair of long blades hanging from the upper lip past the chin. A horse with two different forms grows both. Each form’s length, thickness, curve and shape are written on its own allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, tusk colour .

Crossing two of them One copy, either sex Dominant: one copy of a form grows it, and a second copy of the same form does not grow a second set or a bigger one. It is not sex-limited: unlike the antlers, no allele grows only on stallions. Foals grow none; the tusks come in when the horse grows up, and stay. The three forms Narwhal horn ( Nar ): one horn on the front of the nose, in the middle, pointing forward along the line of the face. It does not sit where a unicorn’s horn does, so one horse can grow both. Boar tusks ( Tsk ): a pair, one from each corner of the lower jaw. They leave the jaw sideways and curl up beside the nose. Sabre fangs ( Sab ): a pair of flat blades from the front of the upper lip, hanging down past the chin. All three move with the head, so they dip when the horse grazes. Two forms on one horse The forms combine. A horse whose two copies are different forms grows both : tusks and fangs ( Tsk/Sab ), tusks and a horn ( Nar/Tsk ), or fangs and a horn ( Nar/Sab ). A horse has two copies, so two forms is the most one horse can carry; there is no horse with all three. Each form is grown from its own copy’s numbers, so a line can be bred for long fangs and short tusks at once. One colour gene tints every form on the horse. A horse with one copy of boar tusks reads n/Tsk on its gene list, not Tsk/n . It means the same thing; the order is only how the pair is stored. Finding one Very rare in the wild, and because one copy is enough, every horse that carries a form shows it. About one wild horse in five hundred has boar tusks, about one in seven hundred has sabre fangs, and about one in a thousand has a narwhal horn. No wild horse has two forms: that takes breeding. The skeleton horses can grow any one of the three in bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts. What varies The narwhal horn: Length : from a short tusk just past the end of the face, through long , to a great one longer than the horse’s body is deep. Wild horses are short to long; a line bred for length keeps growing it, up to the ceiling. Twist : a spiral or a tight spiral. A narwhal horn is always straight; it never curves. Thickness . Lift : how far the point is raised above the line of the face. Boar tusks: Shape : a short curl , a longer sweep , or a hooked tusk whose tip turns back on itself, either over the nose or back toward the eye. Length : small , standard , long or great . A great tusk reaches over the top of the nose. Curl : how far the tusk turns upward after it leaves the jaw. Thickness . Sabre fangs: Shape : straight , curved back , or serrated , with small barbs down the back edge. The barbs’ pattern is the horse’s own. Length : short , standard , long or great . Even a short fang reaches below the chin. Lean : how far the points lean forward. Thickness . A pair is always symmetric: both tusks, or both fangs, are the same shape and size. Everything stacks: a horse can wear tusks, a unicorn horn, antlers and every body part at once. Is it good for the horse? It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no ability, no hitbox.
