Horse shoes
(Horse gear / hoof equipment)
A horse shoe is one item to a hoof : four separate stacks in four separate gear slots, each in its own metal, drawn under the foot it is on. Five ship (copper, iron, golden, diamond and netherite) and every metal another mod brings gets one of its own. They are bought from the equestrian metalsmith. A shoe does not change how the horse moves yet: the stat bonus, powder snow and the enchantments are on the Roadmap tab.

How it is built A shoe is an ordinary stack in an ordinary gear slot. Nothing about the slots, the attachment, the sync or the Gear tab is shoe-specific ( Horse gear: the slots ); what this feature added is the items, their place in four tags, and the step that turns four worn stacks into four drawn parts. Built 2026-10-12 (#164). The items item/HorseShoeItem : one class, an item per metal. The metal is the item and not a component, so the colour is a constant of the item ( tint() ). It stacks to 1. common/gear/HorseShoes.Metal lists the shipped metals, their ids and colours. ModItems.HORSE_SHOES registers one per entry. The ids use vanilla's spelling ( golden_horse_shoe ). compat/ModdedShoes registers one per material out of ModdedMaterials.armourMetals() , the list generated horse armour uses, so several mods' steels are one shoe. HorseShoes.ships(material) skips a mod's own copper, iron, gold, diamond or netherite. A shoe whose mod was removed loads as an iron shoe (an alias, by droppedArmours() ). compat/GeneratedShoes writes each modded shoe's item definition, name, its entry in the four tags and its trade. It writes no texture and no model. The slots and the tags The four hoof slots are HorseTackSlot.SHOE_FRONT_LEFT , _FRONT_RIGHT , _HIND_LEFT and _HIND_RIGHT , and each accepts its item tag, horsegenetics:gear/shoe_* . All four tags list every shoe. The generated pack's copies say replace: false and list only the modded shoes, because tags merge across packs. One icon, tinted Every shoe, shipped or generated, uses the one greyscale sprite textures/item/horse_shoe.png (drawn by tools/bake-drawn-items.mjs ; a placeholder for the owner to repaint) through the one model item/horse_shoe . Its item definition names the horsegenetics:metal_tint tint source, and client/MetalTintSource answers with HorseShoeItem.tint() . Drawing them A worn shoe is not an item model. It is the box-built PartKind.SHOE ( the worn side of the attached parts ), one mesh for four feet and every metal. GeneticHorseRenderer reads the four slots once per frame and calls HorseShoes.worn(frontLeft, frontRight, hindLeft, hindRight) with each shoe's tint, 0 for a bare hoof. That returns one WornPart per shod hoof on that hoof's PartAnchor , and AttachedPartLayer draws them. An unshod horse allocates nothing. The icon and the worn shoe take their colour from the same method, so they cannot disagree. The trades The five shipped trades are files under villager_trade/equestrian_metalsmith/level_N/ , listed in that level's trade_set , whose amount went from 3 to 4 so that a shoe never pushes an armour or a buying trade out of a level. A trade set is a registry and does not merge across packs, so the generated pack rewrites a level's file whole: compat/MetalsmithTrades collects what generated armour and generated shoes each add and writes each level once. A modded shoe is sold only where one of its metal's items is registered, the rule its armour follows (#14). Adding shoes changed what the generated pack holds, so the scan's fingerprint rules number went up ( MaterialScan.fingerprint , rules|4 ): a pack generated by an older jar is rebuilt on the first launch. What is tested HorseShoesTest (common): an unshod horse draws nothing; one shoe is drawn on its own hoof; four metals stay apart, in hoof order; a black metal is still a shoe; shipped ids are unique and a mod's copy of a shipped metal gets no second shoe. Reversing the hoof order turns it red. Gametest shoes_fit_their_hooves : every shipped shoe is accepted by all four hoof slots and fits no other slot; four metals on four feet read back as put on; taking one off hands it back and leaves the other three; a foal refuses. Removing one shoe from one tag turns it red.
