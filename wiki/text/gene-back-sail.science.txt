Back sail
(Magical gene / recessive / grows geometry, paints nothing)
Two copies and the horse grows a sail along its back: a row of spines from the withers to the croup, with a see-through membrane stretched between them. Whether it peaks over the middle of the back, runs low the whole length or sags in scallops, and how tall, arched, long and see-through it is, is written on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own, back sail colour . The one big, rare body part, and the first grown part you can see through.

Invented No horse has a sail. The look borrows from the animals that do: the skin-covered neural spines of Dimetrodon , Edaphosaurus and Spinosaurus , whose purpose is still argued over (heat exchange, display, or a hump), and the webbed dorsal fin of a sailfish. This is fantasy and makes no claim about any real gene. It is recessive and rare on purpose (owner, 2026-10-02), so that the sail is the body part you have to breed for. The dorsal spines beside it are dominant and show on every carrier.
