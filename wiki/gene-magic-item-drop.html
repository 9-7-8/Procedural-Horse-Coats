Magical gene / no coat effect

Magic item drop

What the horse leaves behind — diamonds, a spawn egg that puts this horse back, or an enchanted sword. Every variant takes two copies and none of them shows on the living animal, which is the point: it is worth breeding a line for and impossible to spot in a field.

The egg is the interesting one

A self-seeding horse drops a spawn egg carrying its own genotype and its own epigenome, so what comes back out is a clone rather than a horse with the same alleles: the same coat, the same particle colours, the same percentages written on every allele copy.

It is the only thing in the mod that undoes a death, which is why it takes two copies of a recessive that no amount of looking at a horse will reveal.

The sword is a gamble

One enchantment, rolled at the moment of death rather than fixed by the gene, drawn from whatever the world’s registry holds that a sword will accept — so a data pack’s enchantments are in the pool for free. Two horses of identical genotype do not leave identical swords, which makes this the one drop on the locus that is a lottery rather than a certainty.

What you can catch

A fraction of a percent of wild horses are the doubled form of each variant. These are prizes rather than hazards, so the founder table sits on the expressing combinations: a diamond horse can genuinely be caught, and the carriers turn up a generation later.

Health

None whatever. Nothing on this locus affects the living horse.

The yard’s DEATH DROPS II pen (row AS) read a named Egg/Egg horse’s two codes, killed it, and hatched its egg through HorseEggSpawner.spawnPreset, the call the egg item makes: “dead “Wild Snorf” vs hatched “Guarding Dragon” 20 ticks after hatching: genetic code identical (9814 chars), epigenome code identical (87 chars)” (CLOCKWORK PASS, 23:53:48).

Replace, except meat

Every value on the item_drop verb replaces the vanilla drop except meat, which is added beside it. The asymmetry belongs to the genes rather than to the verb: a horse that leaves diamonds instead of its leather is a different animal, and a meaty one is the same animal with more on it. It is why the two are separate loci, and why a diamond horse can also be a meat horse.

The egg is the editor’s egg

It reuses PresetHorseSpawnEggItem rather than introducing an item of its own. The two are the same thing arrived at from different directions — “this exact horse, in an item” — and one of them already has a model, a tooltip and a spawner behind it. The record it carries is StoredGenome, the same payload the stallion seed jar holds.

Horse meat is beef

Not a vanilla item, and the closest honest stand-in needs no new item, no model and no recipe. If the mod ever grows its own, one line in GeneDeathHandler.itemFor is what moves.

A locus that is entirely invisible, on purpose

Nothing about a diamond horse can be seen, felt, ridden or measured. That is unusual even here — most of the hidden loci at least change a number somebody could notice — and it is what makes this one a genuine breeding project rather than a shopping trip.

It also means the locus leans hard on the rest of the mod: the only way to find out what a horse carries is the gene database and the research papers, so this is one of the genes those systems exist for.

Why the three cannot combine

Dia/Egg gives the vanilla drop, not both. A horse leaves behind one thing; two contradictory answers to “what is on the ground” is not a richer outcome, it is an unwritten rule about which one wins. The silent compound heterozygote states the rule instead, and has the pleasant consequence that crossing two prize lines gives an ordinary-looking foal carrying both.

What needs verifying here

The drops replace the leather, the egg carries the dead horse’s exact codes and a two-variant horse is silent, so what is left is whether the clone’s body numbers and looks match and whether the sword’s enchantment really varies.

Still open (NOT played)

The yard’s DEATH ITEMS pen answered what lands on the ground, and DEATH DROPS II showed the egg holds the dead horse’s own genetic and epigenome codes. What is left is downstream of those codes, and a spread across more swords than two.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • The clone’s body numbers match. Read max_health, jump_strength and movement_speed on both. Pass: the same numbers, which is what a matching epigenome must mean downstream.
  • The sword is rolled per death, not fixed by the gene. Kill several Swd/Swd horses of the same genotype. Pass: the enchantments are not reliably the same one. (Exactly one per sword is verified; this needs more than two swords.)

In the game, with your hands

  • The clone looks like the same horse. Stand the re-spawned horse where the dead one stood. Pass: coat, markings, mane and any particle colour are indistinguishable — the codes matching says it should be, and this is the cheap confirmation that the coat pipeline agrees.
What is still planned here

A Chaos allele whose doubled form drops one item chosen by a seed on the allele copy, in place of the leather, out of every item the fixed drops do not cover — the item half of what the three mob loci got on 2026-10-01.

Chaos: an item nobody chose

Asked for out of intake/, “functions similarly to the eye allele”. The owner’s decisions:

  • One more allele, Cha, whose copy carries a chaos seed, inherited exactly — drift only ever re-rolls a seed whole, rarely — so a line breeds true. Same constants and same matched-seed machinery as the mob loci.
  • Recessive like every variant here: it takes two Chaos copies. Two copies with different seeds follow this locus's rule for two different variants — they settle on nothing and the horse drops ordinary leather.
  • A matched pair replaces the leather, as diamonds do, so it stays a choice a breeder makes. One stack of one; count is the ability's min/max, start at 1–1.
  • Unlike the egg layer this locus is not one allele per item, so it needs a new drop kind: "chaos:<seed>" as the drop name, one more case in server/GeneDeathHandler.itemFor’s switch.
  • Founder table: doubled forms only, as the rest of this locus; Chaos gets a small weight of its own, the owner’s to tune once it exists.

Which items Chaos may produce

Shared with the egg layer. Decided: allowed unless blocked. The default blocklist is creative-only and operator items (command, structure and jigsaw blocks, barrier, light, debug stick, knowledge book and the like), every spawn egg, and every container that can hold things (shulker boxes, bundles — a horse that lays a full shulker is a duplication route). This mod’s own items are eligible like anyone else’s, and the Gameplay tab must say so. On top, two config lists by mod id and item id, the same shape as the mob ones (chaos.exclude_mods / chaos.exclude_ids in ServerConfig — extend those or add item twins). Check first: whether the registry can tell a creative-only modded item from a survival one; if it cannot, the default list is the hand-written vanilla set and modded operator items are the pack author’s job, said plainly in the config comment and on the Gameplay tab. The item list is a second list in server/ChaosRoster, built the same lazy, sorted, cached way.
