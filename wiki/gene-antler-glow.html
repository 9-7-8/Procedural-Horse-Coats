Magical gene / recessive / lights the antlers' points, paints nothing

Antler glow

Two copies and the points of a horse's antlers glow in the dark — every tine's tip and the end of each beam, in the antler's own colour. On a horse without antlers this gene does nothing at all.

Crossing two of them

Finding one

Recessive: one copy shows nothing. In the wild a horse carries two copies or none — about one in ten has two — so roughly one antlered wild horse in ten has glowing points. Most horses with two copies have no antlers to light.

What it looks like

Only the points glow, not the whole rack: a dark antler at night with a light at the end of every tine. They glow in the antler's own colour — bone, umber, or a crystal rack's gem. parts.glow in the client config turns the glow off and keeps the antlers.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill, and lights nothing around it — only the points themselves shine.

How it reaches the rack

GrownParts.of sets the antler part's emissive flag. The layer then draws a second, full-bright submit of the same mesh over the regions PartKind.glowRegions() names — for an antler only PartSheet.BONE_TIP, the last box of every chain, where a horn glows all over. One flag, and the region mask is what makes it "the tips". It carries no epigenetic numbers.

There is no real locus here

A magical gene with no real counterpart, and the horn glow locus's twin. Its own locus rather than an antler allele so that any rack, of any habit, size or colour, can also glow. Recessive, with no wild carriers, by the rule every magical trail follows: the allele is in the wild in the form that can show.

What needs verifying here

Glowing antler points have not been seen at night, so whether the tips alone read as a glow is unchecked.

Glowing points at night

An antlered Glw/Glw horse at night, bone and crystal: the tines' tips and the beam ends should glow and nothing else. Then parts.glow off, and the rack should still draw, lit like the rest of the horse. A tine that is only partly grown in should glow at its partial size.
