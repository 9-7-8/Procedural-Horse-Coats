Equestrian Bench

A saddler’s bench. It dyes a saddle’s seat, its bridle and its metal fittings separately, so two saddles need not look alike — and one day it will cut a banner into a saddle pad.

What it does

Recipe (shapeless): 1 crafting table + 1 leather + 2 horse hair.

There is also one already standing at every cowboy’s homestead, at the end of the frontage past the hitch and the horseman’s table — so you are likely to meet one before you know to want it. The recipe means you never have to go back there for it.

Right-click it and you get three slots and three buttons. Put a saddle in the top slot, pick which part of it you are colouring, and put a dye or an ingot in the slot below. The recoloured saddle appears on the right; take it, and the saddle and the material are both spent.

  • Seat — the leather you sit on. Takes any of the sixteen dyes.
  • Bridle — the headstall, noseband, bit and reins. Also a dye, and independent of the seat: a black saddle with a red bridle is two visits, not a compromise.
  • Metal — the buckles and rings. Takes a metal or a gem rather than a dye: iron (plain steel), gold (brass), copper, netherite (dark gunmetal), diamond (pale blue-white), emerald (green) or amethyst (purple).

One part at a time, and that is the price

Each use spends one dye or one ingot and colours one part. A saddle done in three colours therefore costs three materials and three passes, and changing your mind costs another. That is deliberate: the bench is meant to be somewhere you go, not a settings screen you open.

Two things it politely refuses. A dye will not colour metal and an ingot will not colour leather, so the result stays empty until the material suits the part you picked. And if the part is already that colour nothing appears either — the bench will not take a dye for doing nothing. Putting iron in is how you put the fittings back to plain steel, because plain steel is what iron means here.

Nothing is eaten if you change your mind

Close the screen and whatever is still in the slots comes back to you. Only taking the result spends anything.

An undyed saddle is exactly the saddle you know

The bench works by colouring three layers that were cut out of vanilla’s own saddle texture, and a saddle nobody has dyed carries no colour at all — so it renders pixel for pixel as it always has. That matters beyond the horse: donkeys, mules, skeleton horses and zombie horses wear the same saddle, and none of them changed. Camels, pigs, striders and nautiluses were left alone entirely.

Not yet: the saddle pad, and cutting a banner into one. Those are §25 on the roadmap, along with the reasoning behind every decision here.

Loom-shaped, not furnace-shaped

It computes its result the moment a slot changes and keeps nothing between uses, so there is no block entity — no ticker, no save and load, no ContainerData to sync. ContainerLevelAccess is the whole connection to the world, exactly as vanilla’s loom does it. The research shelf needs one only because it ticks; this does not.

The part being dyed travels through clickMenuButton, vanilla’s own plumbing, so unlike the shelf this needs no custom packet — a zone is a small int and vanilla already carries small ints.

Three colours on one item

Vanilla holds one dye per stack and reads it once, outside its layer loop, so every layer of an equipment asset takes the same value however many there are. That is why this cannot be dyed_color. The three colours live in a tack_tint component and reach the renderer through NeoForge’s getArmorLayerTintColor, which is handed the layer index for exactly this limitation.

The component’s indices are the layer order in assets/minecraft/equipment/saddle.json, and the two must not drift. The three textures are disjoint, so a wrong order would not overlap anything — it would quietly paint the bridle in the seat’s colour, which is the kind of bug that looks like a decision. How the three were cut out of vanilla’s sheet, and why an undyed saddle recomposes to it exactly, is on the item roster.

Absent means undyed, and that path is load-bearing rather than incidental: with no component each layer resolves its own color_when_undyed, and those constants were chosen so the three recompose to vanilla. It is what lets all of this ride on minecraft:saddle rather than needing an item of our own, so a saddle you already owned can be brought to the bench and dyed.

Verified

Built 2026-09-16 and not yet played. The three-layer split is verified numerically — the shipped textures were read back and recomposed against vanilla’s with zero differing pixels — and the mod builds and boots a dedicated server clean. What no bench test can tell you is whether the window reads well, which is what verification is for.
