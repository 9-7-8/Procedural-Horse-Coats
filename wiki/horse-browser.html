The horse browser

The horse browser is the mod’s own index of everything you own and everything it knows — an H-key screen carrying a list of your horses, a searchable gene database, the recipes, the alleles, the breeds, a breeding preview and a log of what has been happening to your stable. Part of it ships. Most of the built half is written up on Breeding & pedigree, under client sync, the information screen and the family tree; the Log tab is written up here, and the rest of the plan is on the Roadmap tab.

Where the browser is documented

The browser is half built, and most of the built half is documented on Breeding & pedigree rather than here. This page began as the plan, moved whole from the main roadmap, and has started to grow the other tabs as pieces of that plan ship — the Log tab is the first of them. Breeding keeps the breeding half.

The Log tab — what has been happening to your horses

Press H and pick Log. It is a plain list of things that have happened to horses you own, newest at the top: every cover that went through and every one that did not, every foal, every death, every horse you tamed, bought, were handed or lost. Each row carries the day and the time it happened, what kind of thing it was, which horse it was about, and the result in a sentence.

It is written down whether or not you were there to read it

This is the point of the tab. The mod already says most of this in chat — a mare was not covered, a horse was killed — but chat reaches whoever is standing there, once, and scrolls away. The log is written on the server as things happen, including while the menu is shut, while you are in another dimension, while the chunk is unloaded, and while you are logged out entirely. Come back in the morning and the night is in the list. Turning the chat notices off in the config does not stop the log; that setting is about chat.

The filter chips

Along the top, right of the count: All, then one chip per kind of event, each with how many of that kind you have. Click one to see only those; click it again by picking another. Every kind gets a chip even when the count is zero, because a chip that only appears once you already have one teaches nobody what the log can tell them. Filtering never changes the order — newest is still first.

  • Covers. Both halves: “was covered, and is in foal” and every refusal. A refused cover keeps its reason and its numbers, so “12 other horses within 16 blocks of her, and the limit is 8” reads in the log exactly as it read in chat. Those are the same refusals the chat line can end in, because they are literally the same sentence — see the Coding tab.
  • Births. One row per foal, naming its dam and sire. Both breeding paths are here: a foal fed into being with a golden carrot, and one born overnight from a pregnancy.
  • Deaths. What killed it, by name where something did and by cause where nothing did. Recorded for an owner who was nowhere near, which is the case the hurt and death chat lines deliberately do not cover.
  • Taming. A wild horse you got a rope on. A foal is not a taming, even though a foal born to a tamed mare is tamed on the spot — it gets a birth row.
  • Purchases, sales and transfers. The three ways a horse changes hands on a transfer paper. Buying a dealer’s stock is a purchase; taking on somebody else’s horse is a transfer; and when your horse is the one redeemed, a row appears in your log too — otherwise losing a horse is the one thing that happens to your stable that you are never told about, since the chat line goes to whoever spent the paper.

How much it keeps

The last two hundred events, per player. Past that the oldest fall off the end and the tab says so at the bottom. That is a real loss and it is the right one: a log is what has been happening, and nobody reads to the bottom of one that never ends. The pedigree is the permanent record and is not bounded.

A refusal that is still true two seconds later is not written again — a crowded paddock is re-checked constantly and would otherwise fill the whole log by lunchtime. The same refusal for the same mare is one row for five minutes, which is the same quiet period the chat line uses. A different reason is always news, and two foals five minutes apart are always two rows.

Refresh

There is a Refresh button, top right, the same one My horses and Breeding preview carry. You should not often need it: the server pushes your log to you about once a second whenever something is added to it, so a tab left open fills in as the day goes on.

The roster refreshes on every open (2026-09-24)

HorseBrowserScreen.init() and the tab-switch path both used to ask for the roster only if (!ClientHorseRoster.received()). That reads as “only the first time” and means never again this session: ClientHorseRoster.clear() runs on disconnect and nowhere else, so a player who bred a foal, tamed a horse or sold one saw the stable as it was the first time they pressed H until they found the Refresh button. Both paths now re-ask.

Throttled, though, and that is the part worth knowing. Unguarded, every click on the tab strip would make the server walk the whole ancestry table (HorseRoster.gather, plus the allele and breed sweeps), and flipping through tabs is a casual gesture in a way pressing Refresh is not. requestRosterUnlessJustAsked() skips a sweep within two seconds of the last one — long enough that no realistic clicking costs more than one, far shorter than anything a player could do to change the stable in between. The cooldown is static, so closing and reopening the screen does not reset it, which is exactly the gesture it exists to soak up. The Refresh button is deliberately not throttled: it exists to be believed.

ClientHorseRoster.received() is still read, and now only for what it was always good for — telling an empty stable from the answer has not arrived yet, which is the difference between “no horses” and “asking the server…” in three places including HorseStasisBankScreen. Birth and death pushes are still owed; see the roadmap tab.

The Log tab, as built

Shipped 2026-09-24. The model is game-free and the game module only translates into it, which is the usual split and matters more than usual here: what a row says is the feature, and a sentence is the easiest thing in the mod to unit-test and the hardest to notice going wrong.

Where it lives

  • common/…/log/HorseEvent — one row. Kind, tick, horse UUID, the horse’s name as it read at the time, the other party, and — for a cover only — a CoverNotice.Reason plus the crowd numbers. line() is the sentence; detail() is that sentence with the leading name trimmed off for the table’s own Horse column; when() turns the tick into Day 42, 06:30.
  • common/…/log/HorseEventLog — the store. One log per owner, newest-first reads, bounded at MAX_PER_OWNER, and the deduplication rule. Everything a test can check is in these two classes; HorseEventLogTest checks it.
  • neoforge/…/data/HorseEventData — a server-global SavedData, in the same shape as HorseAncestryData, with HorseEventCodecs for disk and wire.
  • neoforge/…/server/HorseLog — the translation layer and the death hook.
  • HorseLogPayload / HorseLogRequestPayload, client/ClientHorseLog, and the LOG tab in HorseBrowserScreen.

A refusal is a constant, not a string

A row for a failed cover carries the CoverNotice.Reason itself, and renders by calling CoverNotice.line(…) — the same method the chat notice calls. So the log row and the chat line the owner missed are not merely similar, they are the same string built by the same code, and a refusal added to NaturalCover becomes a log row for free. The Horse column would have repeated the name, so detail() trims the leading name off mechanically rather than keeping a second, shorter set of phrasings that could drift from the first.

Why every writer resolves a UUID and never a player

HorseDeathNoticeHandler and HorseHurtNoticeHandler both need a ServerPlayer in the same level, and both are right to: a chat line for somebody who logged out an hour ago is not an alarm. But that owner still lost a horse, and the answer to what happened while I was away has to be written while they are away or it cannot exist. So every writer here goes through HorseOwnership.ownerId, and none of them is gated on the notice config.

For the same reason the death hook is HorseLog’s own LivingDeathEvent subscriber rather than a call from the notice handler, which returns early on three separate conditions — the config, the showDeathMessages game rule, and the horse not being tamed. The duplicate that risks (several handlers, one death) is caught by the same deduplication rule as everything else.

One deduplication rule, in one place

Two different things produce repeats: a refused cover is re-evaluated every two seconds for the whole of a mare’s heat, and a death passes through several handlers. Rather than teach each call site its own throttle — which is how one of them ends up without one — a repeat of the same kind, horse and reason within DEDUPE_TICKS is dropped in HorseEventLog.add. The window is CoverNotice.QUIET_TICKS on purpose, so the log and the chat line agree about what counts as news.

Note what the rule cannot collapse: a birth is keyed by the foal, so two foals five minutes apart are two different horses and two rows. The rule is about one happening reported twice, never two happenings that look alike.

One clock

Every row is stamped with the overworld’s game time whatever dimension the event happened in. Each dimension keeps its own day counter, so stamping locally would interleave the horse dimension’s day 4 with the overworld’s day 900 and newest-first would be nonsense.

The whole log, coalesced

HorseLogPayload carries the player’s whole log rather than a page or a delta, which rests on it already being bounded server-side: two hundred rows of a few dozen bytes is less than the roster this screen already sends. Paging would buy two more states to get wrong — a half-loaded list and a stale page — and would force the filter server-side for a list the client can filter in a frame.

Because the payload is the whole log, writes mark the owner dirty and a server tick flushes once a second instead of sending on every row. A foaling morning can land a dozen rows in one tick, and sending the same two hundred rows a dozen times over is the kind of waste that only shows up on a server with people on it.

A foal is not a taming

HorseOwnerTrackingHandler writes the taming row at the one moment tamedBy goes from empty to filled. That normally happens only to a horse somebody roped — but a foal born to a tamed dam whose owner was offline also arrives with tamedBy empty and gets filled in later by the same poll. So the row is skipped when the record already has a mother: that foal already has its birth row, and without the guard the owner would come back to “You tamed Willow” for a horse they never touched.

It needed no new tab-strip work

The horizontal scrolling tab strip already existed — centred while it fits, scissored and scrolled with a bar once it does not — so adding a seventh tab cost nothing but an enum constant. The rest of that plan is still open: nothing scrolls the selected tab into view, and the tabs are drawn rather than being widgets, so there is no keyboard focus to move through them.

The horse browser

The H-key screen is a real container menu; the horse list, the gene database and the Log tab are built. What is left of the full plan is the vial inventory, the herd view, the bonding view and the generated crafting tree, plus out-of-band actions (teleport a horse to a stall, whistle for bonded horses, recall to a holding pen).

The owned-horse index is still owed, and the horse list is running without it

The expensive part was never the UI. A proper per-player owned-horse registry — updated on tame, breed, death and transfer, holding enough denormalised data (name, sex, age, genotype, bond, herd) that horses in unloaded chunks still appear — does not exist. The My horses tab ships on HorseRoster instead, which walks the whole ancestry table on request, cuts the result at HorseRosterPayload.MAX_ENTRIES and sends it, and the client sorts and filters what it got. What is owed is the registry, with sort and filter running server-side over it, and pagination. HorseQuery is already game-free, so the same predicate can run on the server the day the index exists.

The tab the owner has asked for

The bonding tab. The bond level with each horse, the tier it is at, and concrete guidance on how to raise it — which sources are available, what the daily cap is, how close the next tier is. This is the surface bond is missing. My horses shows the number and sorts by it, which is not the same thing: it says where you are and not how to move.

Tabs that keep working as the menu grows

The horizontal scroll bar is built, and a seventh tab (Log) has since gone in on top of it without touching the strip. What is left of this: the selected tab is not scrolled into view, so picking one at the far end and then resizing the window can leave it off screen, and the tabs are drawn rather than being widgets, so keyboard focus does not move through them at all. The scroll position is client-only and already changes no server state.

The rest

  • A row should open the horse. Clicking a My horses row selects it and fills the footer; it should reach the information screen and the family tree. Both want a HorseRecord and the roster deliberately does not carry one (it carries a HorseListing, a tenth of the bytes), so this needs a fetch-one-record round trip.
  • FamilyTreeScreen should become a tab rather than stay a separate screen. Row portraits exist — client/HorsePortrait, used by the My horses table and both breeding pickers — but they are a second copy of the tree's drawHorseModel rather than a shared one (gap 78), and merging them is part of this. It is also the working reference for a from-scratch retained-mode screen in this codebase, scissor clipping and entity draw included — with the standing warning: build the render state from a throwaway client-only Horse, and never touch ClientCoatCache.
  • The crafting-tree tab goes stale on contact with a modded gene. Generate it from the recipe manager at runtime; do not hard-code the graph.
  • Teleport, recall and whistle need the stall and pen definitions above, plus answers for a destination that no longer exists and for whether any of it crosses dimensions.
  • A natural / magical filter on the Breeds tab. Breed.magical() is already on every breed, so the filter is a toggle beside the list and a clause in HorseBrowserScreen.drawBreedList. Build it with the first magical breed, so it can be seen doing something.

The horse menu — acting on a horse, not only reading about one

The browser's remaining tabs are a separate and larger job; this is the smaller, sharper set of asks about the screen as it stands. The thread running through all of them: the menu is a report today, and every one of these turns it into a console.

Teleport the horse to you, from the Where column

The column exists and is inert. “Where” is the twelfth column on My horses and already prints overworld 118, 71, -204, built server-side by HorseRoster.where. Clicking it should attempt to bring the horse to you.

  • “Use /tp” was the ask, and the mechanism deserves a second look rather than a literal reading. The mod already teleports horses properly — the ender whistle moves a bound horse across dimensions, and tickets send one to a stall. Routing the button through the same code is better than issuing a command: a command needs permissions the player may not have, silently does nothing for them if they do not, and skips every check the whistle makes.
  • It must answer the unloaded case, which is most rows. HorseRoster.entry() only fills Where for a horse in a loaded chunk; everything else reads not loaded, and those are exactly the horses you would want to summon. data/HorseWhereabouts already exists as a SavedData of last-seen dimension, position and death, and the roster does not read it — wiring that in is the prerequisite and improves the column whether or not the button is built.
  • It needs a cost or a gate. A free summon-any-horse-from-anywhere button makes whistles, tickets and holding pens redundant in one stroke. Whatever gates it — a bond tier, a cooldown, an item — should be decided before the button, not retrofitted after players have it.

A waypoint column, when a map mod is running

A checkbox per horse that drops a waypoint on the player's minimap and refreshes it every thirty seconds.

Nothing in the repo touches any map mod today — no Xaero, no JourneyMap, no waypoint code of any kind — so this is a new integration from zero, and it is the one item here with a third-party API at the end of it. Two things to settle first: which map mods (their waypoint APIs are unrelated to each other, so each is its own integration and the column should appear per-mod rather than as one checkbox), and where the thirty-second refresh runs, since it has to keep updating while the screen is closed and therefore cannot live in the screen. The fail-soft rule applies as everywhere else: absent map mod, absent column, no crash and no hard dependency.

Refresh on its own

Asked for on three events — screen opened, a foal born, a horse died. The first of the three shipped on 2026-09-24; the other two are pushes and are the actual work.

  • Birth and death are pushes, not pulls, and that is the actual work. The server has to notice and send, which means a hook in the foaling and death paths and a payload that says what changed. Re-sending the whole roster on every birth in a big stable is the version to avoid; ClientHorseRoster is already a UUID-keyed cache with a version counter, so it is built for a delta rather than a replacement.
  • Transfer and taming belong on the same list, even though they were not asked for — they change the roster for exactly the same reason, and adding them later means touching the same code twice.

RideHUD, and stats while you ride

Wanted integrated rather than assessed. The mod has one GUI overlay today — client/FlightHudLayer, a single line above the hotbar naming the dismount key while flying, and itself unverified at runtime — plus JumpBarCooldownMixin, which tints a vanilla bar. There is no stats readout anywhere outside a screen you have to open.

That makes this the first real HUD in the mod, and the design question is restraint rather than capability: every number already exists on HorseInfoScreen, and the reason to show speed, jump, health and bond while riding is that opening a screen mid-gallop is not a thing anyone does. Keep it to what changes while you ride.

Leave My Bars Alone was read, and the call was to fold it in here rather than rebuild it

Leave My Bars Alone (Fuzs, MPL-2.0) does exactly two things, and it was read from source on 2026-09-21 because it is one of the very few mods on this page with a branch targeting our own Minecraft version. It re-adds the food bar as an extra stacked GUI layer anchored to VEHICLE_HEALTH — it does not move the mount's health bar — and it narrows when vanilla is allowed to swap the XP bar for the jump-charge bar, via one @ModifyVariable into nextContextualInfoState, so XP stays visible unless you are actually mid-charge. Two config booleans, both default on, no keybind, two files in the whole mod.

The owner's call was not to rebuild either half. We do not touch vanilla bars separately; instead this section is designed so that our readout does not add to the clutter in the first place, and the vanilla bar problem stays solved by a maintained mod that already solves it. One thing to check when this is built: JumpBarCooldownMixin already modifies the same vanilla jump bar, so the two want testing together before either is claimed to work.

A different look for the menu — explicitly not committed

The owner likes Biology Dictionary's UI and raised it as “maybe as an option or alternative style”, with “not a requirement, not committed to” said outright. Recorded here so the idea survives, and flagged so nobody builds it as a decision that was made. Worth knowing what it would cost before anyone is tempted: HorseBrowserScreen is a single 3,430-line Screen with its layout inline, so a second skin means extracting a layout seam first — which is real work with no user-visible result, and is the kind of thing that only pays if the restyle is actually wanted.

What needs verifying here

The whole Log tab is unplayed: it boots clean and its rules are unit-tested, but nothing has yet confirmed in game that a cover, a foal, a death, a taming and a transfer each land one correct row — and above all that the rows written while the owner was logged out are there when she comes back, which is the one thing the tab exists for and the one thing no test can reach; and the roster now re-asks on every open, which nothing but a real stable can show is neither stale nor chatty.

The roster re-asks on open (2026-09-24, NOT played)

Client-side timing, so no test reaches it. Two ways it can be wrong and they pull in opposite directions, which is why both are worth a look.

  • It is actually fresh. Open H → My horses and note the count. Close it, breed or tame a horse, reopen. Pass: the new horse is there without touching Refresh. Fail: the old count, which would mean the request is going out but the reply is not being applied.
  • It is not chatty. Click quickly back and forth across the tab strip for a few seconds on a large stable. Pass: no stutter, because the two-second cooldown collapses that into one sweep. Fail: a visible hitch per click, which would mean requestRosterUnlessJustAsked is being bypassed somewhere — the Refresh button is supposed to be the only unthrottled caller.
  • The empty state still reads right. On a fresh character with no horses, the table should say asking the server… for an instant and then settle on the no-horses line — not sit on “asking” forever, which would mean received() is never being set.

The Log tab records what happens (2026-09-24, NOT played)

Shipped today. HorseEventLogTest pins the ordering, the cap, the deduplication and the wording; runServer reaches Done (0.357s) with no ERROR line, so the SavedData and both payloads register. Everything below is what only a session in a world can answer.

What to look at

  • One row each, and the right one. In a pen: let a mare be covered, let a foal be born, kill a spare horse, tame a wild one, and redeem a transfer paper on a cowboy’s horse. Open H → Log. Pass: five rows, newest first, each with a sensible day and time. Fail: a taming row for the foal, which would mean the guard in HorseOwnerTrackingHandler.logTaming is not firing.
  • The offline case — the one that matters. Leave a mare and a stallion penned and log out for a night, or better, have the events happen on a dedicated server while you are off it. Pass: the covers and foals are in the log when you come back. Fail: an empty log, which would mean something in the write path still wants a ServerPlayer.
  • It survives a restart. Stop the server and start it again. Pass: the same rows. Fail: an empty log, meaning HorseEventData is not being marked dirty or not being written.
  • A refused cover reads like the chat line. Crowd a paddock past fertility.nearby_horse_cap. Pass: one Covers row quoting the same count and limit the yellow chat line quotes, and not a new row every two seconds. Fail: a wall of identical rows, which would mean the dedup window is not being reached — check that the tick being stamped is the overworld’s.
  • The chat config does not gate the log. Turn notices.owned_horse_breeding off and crowd the paddock again. Pass: no chat line, but a Covers row.
  • Losing a horse. Two players: give one a transfer paper for the other’s horse and redeem it. Pass: a Transfers row for the taker and a Sales row for the loser. This one needs two accounts and has not been tried at all.
  • The tab fills in while it is open. Leave the Log tab up and let a foal drop. Pass: the row appears within a second or so, with no click.
  • The chips and the layout. Every chip present with a count, zero ones greyed; picking one keeps newest-first; the sentences fit at 1× and 3× GUI scale and at a narrow window rather than running under the scroll bar.
  • The cap. Only reachable after two hundred events; the footer line should appear then. Nothing has been near it.
