Magical gene / dominance series / shapes the antlers, paints nothing

Antler form

How an antlered horse's rack grows: forked like a red deer's, swept back with a brow tine like a reindeer's, broad and palmate like a moose's, crowned with a cup of tines, or a plain spike. One habit, drawn on both sides. On a horse without antlers it does nothing at all.

Crossing two of them

The five habits

AlleleHabitLooks like
PalPalmateA short beam opening into a broad palm, with points round its edge — a moose, a fallow buck
CrnCrownedA tall beam ending in a cup of tines splayed every way — a royal head
BrwBrow-tinedSwept back and over in a long curve, one tine thrown forward over the face — a reindeer
FrkForkedBranched up the beam, the lower tines longest — a red deer. The commonest
nSpikeA plain spike, forking once when it is large — a yearling, a brocket

Each allele hides the ones below it: a horse with Pal and anything else is palmate, one with Brw and Frk is brow-tined. So two forked horses can throw a spike-antlered foal, one time in four, if both carry n underneath.

Finding one

Every horse carries a form, antlered or not — it simply does nothing until the horse has antlers. About half of wild copies are forked, a fifth are spike, and the other three habits are rarer. That makes an ordinary-looking horse a reasonable way to bring a palmate rack into an antlered line.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill — it only changes the shape of antlers it might have.

The allele order is the generator's habit order

Frk, Brw, Pal, Crn, n is the order the alleles are declared and stored in, and AntlerGenerator's habit indices — so habitOf(pair) is the dominant allele's order(), and it becomes the first factor of the antler PartShape's style. Dominance is not the declaration order; it is a separate list. Appending a habit is safe; reordering would rewrite every horse's rack, and removing one makes Genotype.readableStored drop the whole segment.

How it reaches the rack

GrownParts.of passes habitOf to AntlersGene.racksFor, which builds both sides in it. It carries no epigenetic numbers: how many tines and how big are the antler allele's, and this locus only picks the rule that places them.

Analogous, not measured

Invented, like the antler locus it modifies (D8). The five habits are the broad shapes deer antlers take — branched, brow-tined, palmate, crowned and simple — but in real deer they are species characters, not alleles of one gene. The split between this locus and the antler allele's numbers is a gameplay rule, written on the treatment: a kind a breeder wants to cross in on purpose, with a 3:1 ratio, is an allele; a quantity they want to nudge is a number.

Why plain dominance and never codominant

A codominant series would draw one habit on each side. Antlers are symmetric by default (D3), so a palmate-left, forked-right rack would read as a bug; one habit wins and is drawn on both.

What needs verifying here

None of the five habits has been seen on a live horse, so whether each reads as its deer is unchecked.

Each habit, side by side

An antlered horse of each habit at a standard and a massive size. Forked should read as a red deer, brow-tined as a reindeer (the brow tine forward over the face), palmate as a broad plate, crowned as a cup at the top, spike as a spike. The palm is built from square boxes and may read as a clump rather than a plate — that is the habit most likely to need its generator rule redrawn.
