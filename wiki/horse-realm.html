Gameplay / world

The horse realm

A flat, sunlit, bounded field where nothing can hurt a horse. Step through a hay-bale portal, let your surplus go with a freedom stick, and it stays there — healed, wild, keeping its name and its whole pedigree — until somebody else tames it.

What it is for

Breeding horses makes more horses than anyone wants. The honest ways to deal with the rest are all bad: kill them, wall them up in a pen you stop visiting, or turn them loose in the Overworld where a creeper, a drop or a hostile night finishes them. The realm is the fourth answer. It is a commons: everybody’s horses, in one field, safe from everything including each other, and anybody may take one home.

It is not the horse dimension you may already know. That one is a private test corridor full of pens, it is still there, and it is reached by F6 now rather than by a portal. The hay portal goes here instead.

Getting there, and getting back

Exactly as before: build a vertical rectangle of hay bales, right-click a frame block with a golden carrot, and stand in it for ten seconds. Horses go through on their own after three seconds, so the roped-horse shortcut still works — right-click the portal while leading a string of them and they all go.

Your own portal always comes out in the same place. There are a hundred portals in the field, on a grid, and the one you arrive at is decided by where in the Overworld you built yours. That is the whole reason the field is usable: the horses you released last week are where you left them, and two players who built their portals in different places do not land on top of each other.

Any of the hundred takes you home again, to the exact portal you came in by. If somebody mined it while you were away, it is put back. Your own tamed horses standing within about fifty blocks of the exit come with you; everything you released stays.

The field

ThingWhat it is
Size A thousand chunks square — sixteen thousand blocks on a side. Big, and finite: you can walk it, search it, and lose nothing in it.
The ground One flat layer of dirt on bedrock. No hills, no caves, no trees, nothing to fall into or get stuck behind.
Water A small pool every eighty blocks. Not decoration — a horse only heals near water, so a dry field would be one where a hurt horse never recovered.
Exits A hay portal every sixteen hundred blocks, a hundred of them, so you are never more than about a thousand blocks from a way out.
The edge An invisible wall. Nothing walks, gallops or is led out of the field.
The sky Permanent day. It never gets dark, it never rains.
Everything else There isn’t any. No mob but a horse can be in the realm — not spawned, not summoned, not led in.

You cannot build or break there, and neither can anybody else. The field is shared and it stays the way it is.

Nothing happens to a horse here

Every horse in the realm — released, or yours and just visiting — cannot be hurt, cannot die and cannot despawn. Not by you, not by another player, not by fall, fire, drowning, starvation or a lethal foal genotype. All of it comes straight back the moment the horse leaves.

Letting one go

Craft a freedom stick from a feather, a stick and one horse hair, and right-click your own horse with it while you are both in the realm. It is healed to full, its tack comes back to you, and it is wild.

It is still the same horse. Its name, its genes, its breed, its parents and its whole family tree are untouched. Whoever tames it next — the ordinary way, by riding it until it stops throwing them, or by feeding it — gets all of that, and becomes its owner.

You cannot release a horse that is not yours, and the stick does nothing outside the realm. It lasts 64 releases, and a refusal costs nothing.

They breed, slowly

Released horses form bands and breed like wild horses, and their foals are born wild and protected too — so a field that people keep adding to fills up on its own.

It runs at a quarter speed by default. Heat, the wait between covers and the length of a pregnancy are all four times what they are at home. A server owner can change that with realm.breeding_rate_percent, anywhere from 0 to 100; zero stops it entirely, pregnancies included.

Nothing at all happens while nobody is there. A mare left pregnant in an empty realm is exactly as pregnant when somebody next walks in, however long that took. The field does not run in the background, and a server pays nothing for one nobody is visiting.

Two dimensions, and why they share nothing

horsegenetics:horse_realm is a second dimension beside horsegenetics:debug_pens. Every rule on this page is keyed to the first one and the debug corridor reads none of them — deliberately, and it is the constraint that shaped the implementation.

The corridor is an unrestricted test bench. Horses die in its yard on purpose: the blanket “no horse damage” rule was removed from it on 2026-09-13 because it made six genes untestable, and its horses-only entity filter was switched off the day before that because it deleted the zombies and cows two gene tests were about. Both rules are exactly what the public realm needs. So they are written fresh, in server/HorseRealmRules, against HorseRealm.REALM_LEVEL, rather than generalised out of the corridor — which would have quietly undone two deliberate decisions.

The terrain is arithmetic, not a saved layout

There is no allocator, no plot registry and no saved map. Everything about a position in the realm is a pure function of its chunk coordinates:

Made byWhat
dimension/horse_realm.json The ground. A minecraft:flat generator, fifteen air layers then bedrock then dirt, so that with min_y: -16 the bedrock lands at Y=−1 and the dirt at Y=0. min_y must be a multiple of sixteen, which is why it is not simply −1.
server/HorseRealmTerrain The pools, the hundred portals and the perimeter. Queued from ChunkEvent.Load, applied on the next server tick, and idempotent: it reads before it writes, so a second load costs a handful of comparisons and a chunk generated before a change to the class is corrected next time anybody walks into it.

Not a custom chunk generator, though that was the obvious answer. The flat generator is a path this mod already runs on and a codec it does not have to register; what it cannot do is a fixed grid (vanilla has no placement modifier meaning “at the origin of every fifth chunk”) or a block with a block entity, which a ProtoChunk makes awkward. Both are trivial against a live ServerLevel, so that is where they happen. Most chunks do no block access at all — the pool grid is one chunk in twenty-five, the portal grid one in ten thousand.

Eternal day is an omission, not a setting

dimension_type/horse_realm.json carries default_clock and no timelines field. That is the whole of it. 26.1.2 reads sky darkness off the SKY_LIGHT_LEVEL environment attribute, which only varies with the clock when timelines says so, and the field defaults to empty — so a dimension with a clock and no timelines has a sky that is bright forever. This is the same fact the debug dimension was debugged for on 2026-09-12 (its night); here it is the feature.

The arrival hash

HorseRealm.arrivalCell mixes the Overworld portal’s block position (MurmurHash3’s finalizer, twice, on X and Z) onto one of the hundred grid cells. A hash rather than a scaled coordinate because the Overworld is millions of blocks wide and the realm is sixteen thousand: any linear mapping puts every portal anyone will ever build into one cell.

Both of its properties fail silently if broken. A hash that drifted would strand a player’s released horses out of reach with no error anywhere; one that collapsed would put a whole server in one corner of a 16 000-block field and look, from inside, exactly like a popular meeting spot. So horse_realm_grid_is_stable asserts both over four thousand draws.

The breeding rate is a clock, not a scale factor

server/HorseRealmRepro. This is the part worth reading before changing anything about fertility.

Nothing in common.repro counts down. Every stage is derived from an absolute anchor and the current game tick — where a mare is in her cycle, whether she is due, whether she has spent her one try this heat, how many covers a stallion has had today. There is no timer to divide by four. There is only the reading of “now” that each derivation is handed.

So each horse keeps an offset in its persistent data: ticks it has lived through that its reproduction did not. HorseRealmRepro.reproTime(horse) is game time minus that offset, and every repro read and write for that horse goes through it. One number, in one place. A horse that has never been in the realm has an offset of zero and behaves precisely as it did before the class existed.

That framing is what buys all four requirements at once:

  • The rate. At 25, a quarter of each elapsed tick is credited and three quarters deferred, so every stage takes four times as long. A rate, not a conception chance.
  • Zero pauses it, existing pregnancies included — which a conception-chance reading of the same config could not have done.
  • A dormant realm advances nothing. If nobody is there the horse is not ticking, so no time is credited and all of it is deferred. The test is the size of the gap since that horse was last scanned: late by more than three of its own periods means it was not being simulated, not that it was being simulated slowly.
  • It survives the trip home. The offset belongs to the horse, not to where it is standing, so it stops growing on the way out and is never taken away. Clearing it on exit would make a mare instantly, retroactively overdue.

The one place both clocks appear is NaturalBreedingHandler. Her cycle and her one try use her repro clock; the three-second courtship, the chat throttles and the log stamps use the world’s, because a message is something a player reads in real time whatever the realm’s pacing.

What releasing actually changes

server/FreedomStickHandler. Three things, and nothing else: setTamed(false), setOwner(null), and setTemper(0). The last is the one that is easy to miss and makes the whole feature real — vanilla’s RunAroundLikeCrazyGoal rolls against temper, and a horse that was tame is sitting at maximum, so without the reset the first player to mount it tames it instantly.

Everything that makes the horse that horse is on the HorseRecord attachment and in HorseAncestryData, and none of it is derived from ownership, so releasing touches none of it. HorseOwnerTrackingHandler mirrors the record’s owner to null within two seconds on its own — its javadoc already named “a horse going wild again” as a case it handles, a year before there was one. Re-taming needs no restore code at all: tameWithName sets the owner and the same tracker mirrors it back.

The tack comes off and goes to the player because AbstractHorse.canUseSlot(SADDLE) asks isTamed() — leaving a saddle on a horse that has gone wild would quietly destroy it.

Released horses had to be taught to band

server/HorseRealmHerds, and it exists because a released horse falls through every existing gate. HerdManager.assignFounder returns immediately for anything that already has a record, and the whole wild-herd path is gated on the wild-spawn mark that HorseFoundingTickHandler strips from every horse that arrives carrying one. A released horse is the opposite case in every respect: a fully recorded animal, with a name and a pedigree and an owner it no longer has.

The band it forms is labelled Feral Mixed, not the lead’s breed. Whoever gathers here is whoever happened to be released nearby, and BreedLineage.FERAL is the mod’s existing word for no herd identity. Each horse keeps its own breed on its own record.

F6 is not a dev key any more

Moving the hay portal here left the debug corridor and its test yard with exactly one door, so F6 is registered in every build, release jars included. Who walks through it is the server’s business, not the keybind’s: ModNetworking admits a world with debug.tools on, or an operator at LEVEL_GAMEMASTERS — the same bar /testkit already sets, so it adds no reach a command did not already have. Anybody else is told no rather than getting silence, because a key that does nothing without saying why is how a working feature gets reported as broken.

The perimeter is built twice on purpose

minecraft:barrier blocks sixteen high, one block outside the field, built by the same chunk pass — and a position clamp in HorseRealmRules for anything that gets above them. Both halves are wanted. A clamp on its own reads as teleport-stutter where a wall reads as a wall; a wall on its own has a lid missing. The clamp zeroes horizontal motion as well as the position, or a player holding forward against the boundary is teleported back every tick and calls it lag.

What is tested, and what a test cannot reach

Three in ModGameTests (./gradlew :neoforge-26.1.2:runGameTest): horse_realm_is_built for the pools, the lit exit and the perimeter, horse_realm_grid_is_stable for the arrival hash, and freedom_stick_crafts.

The first of those usually runs in the Overworld, and that is not a mistake. GameTestServer builds its world from a hand-written three-dimension list and never reads the datapack’s dimension/ folder, so the realm cannot exist in that harness however correct its JSON is. The geometry does not care which level it is written into, so it is asserted against whatever level is available, and the two ground assertions — which belong to the flat generator rather than to any Java — are asked only when the real dimension is there.

That leaves one thing no test here can see: whether the dimension loads at all. runServer answers it, and its log names ServerLevel[world]/horsegenetics:horse_realm. Repeat that after any edit to the three JSON files, because a typo in them is silent everywhere else.

What needs verifying here

The whole realm is unplayed — it boots and its geometry passes a gametest, but nobody has walked into it, released a horse, come back for one, or watched a foal born in it.

Getting in and out (built 2026-09-25, NOT played)

  • Light a hay portal in the Overworld and stand in it. You should arrive beside a lit hay frame on a flat dirt plain in permanent daylight, not in the pen corridor.
  • Walk a few blocks and step back through. You should come out at the portal you built. Then go back in: the same place as the first time. That is the arrival hash, and its failure mode is a player never finding their horses again.
  • Lead two horses into the Overworld portal, then walk out of a realm portal with them standing beside you. Both should come home; anything you released should not.
  • Mine your Overworld portal while standing in the realm, then leave. It should be put back and you should land at it.

The field itself (built 2026-09-25, NOT played)

  • Walk to a water pool. There should be one within eighty blocks in some direction.
  • Walk (or fly) to an edge. The invisible wall should stop you on the ground and the clamp should stop you above it — try both.
  • Try to break the dirt and to place a block. Both should refuse.
  • Wait out a night’s worth of ticks. It should never get dark.
  • Throw a spawn egg. Nothing but a horse should appear.
  • Hit a horse. Nothing should happen to it.

The freedom stick (built 2026-09-25, NOT played)

  • Craft one: feather, stick, horse hair, shapeless.
  • Use it on your own horse outside the realm — refused, no durability spent. On somebody else’s horse — refused. On an already-wild horse — refused.
  • Use it on your own saddled horse inside the realm. The saddle should come back to you, the horse should read as wild, and mounting it once should throw you rather than instantly re-taming it. That last one is the temper reset, and it is the assertion with teeth.
  • Tame it again and check the info screen: same name, same genome, same parents, and you as the owner.

Breeding and the clock (built 2026-09-25, NOT played)

  • Release a mare and a stallion together and leave them. They should band up within ten seconds or so, and cover in about four times the usual time.
  • Leave a pregnant mare in the realm, go away for a long while in the Overworld, and come back. Her pregnancy should not have moved. This is the one that cannot be checked any other way, and the one most likely to be wrong.
  • Set realm.breeding_rate_percent to 0 with a pregnant mare standing there. Nothing should progress. Set it back to 25 and it should resume.
  • Bring a mare who conceived in the realm back to the Overworld. Her pregnancy should continue from where it was at ordinary speed, not jump.

F6 on a release build (built 2026-09-25, NOT played)

  • On the real server, as an operator, press F6. You should reach the pen corridor.
  • As a non-operator with debug.tools off, press F6. You should get a chat line pointing at the hay portal, not silence and not the corridor.

A world that already exists

The realm was added to a mod that is already on a live server. Datapack dimensions are loaded afresh each start, so it should simply appear in the existing world — but that has only been seen in a fresh dev world, and “the new dimension is missing on the real save” is a failure that would look like the portal being broken.
