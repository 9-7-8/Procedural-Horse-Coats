Magical gene / recessive / lights the horn, paints nothing

Horn glow

Two copies and a unicorn's horn glows in the dark — in whatever colour horn colour gives it, both colours of a two-tone horn included. On a horse without a horn this gene does nothing at all.

Crossing two of them

Finding one

Recessive: one copy shows nothing. In the wild a horse carries either two copies or none — about one in eight has two — so roughly one wild unicorn in eight has a glowing horn. Most horses with two copies have no horn to glow, which makes an ordinary-looking horse a good mate for a unicorn you want a glowing foal from.

What it looks like

The horn is drawn a second time at full brightness, so it stands out at night in its own colour: a red horn glows red, a white one white, and a black horn is a dark shape you can still see in the dark. If a shader pack makes it look wrong, parts.glow in the client config turns the glow off and keeps the horn.

A unicorn that carries light — glowing hooves, mane or eyes — has a glowing horn even without two copies of this gene, the same way its cutie mark glows. A horse that shines everywhere else never wears one dull horn.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill. It gives off no light that lights up the world around it — only the horn itself shines.

How it reaches the horn

GrownParts.of asks HornGlowGene.glows(pair) and sets the part's emissive flag through AttachedPart.dressed. AttachedPartLayer then submits each slice a second time with entityTranslucentEmissive at full brightness and the same tint, so the glow is always the horn's own colour. It carries no epigenetic numbers.

It replaced a one-in-eight glow category on the unicorn locus (2026-09-30), and the founder rate is set to that same one in eight, so the share of glowing wild unicorns did not change — what changed is that it can be bred for.

A light horse's horn glows too (shipped 2026-10-02)

The owner's call (2026-10-01): the horn follows the light locus, the way the cutie mark does. GrownParts.dressHorn sets the horn emissive when HornGlowGene.glows or LightGene.lightsHorse says so. lightsHorse is the one rule both share — any variant copy at the light locus, eyes-only Lteye included — and LightGene.modifyCutieMark now asks it too, so the mark and the horn cannot disagree. Only the glow moves; the horn's colours are still horn colour's. The light locus grows no horn of its own. Pinned by HornGenesTest.aLightHorsesHornGlowsWithoutTheHornGlowLocus, which failed before the change.

There is no real locus here

A magical gene with no real counterpart. It is its own locus rather than an allele of horn colour because glowing is independent of colour — the owner's words were “glow regardless of color” — and a separate locus is the only way any colour can also glow. Recessive, and with no wild carriers, by the same rule as every magical trail: the allele exists in the wild in the form that can show.

What needs verifying here

A glowing horn has not been seen since glow became its own gene, nor a light unicorn's horn lit by the light locus.

A light unicorn's horn, at night

Spawn a unicorn with light Lthf/n and horn_glow n/n beside one with light n/n, and look at both at night: the first horn should glow in its own colour like the hooves, the second stay dull. An Lteye/n unicorn should glow too.

A glowing horn of each kind, at night

Check a white, a red, a black and a two-tone horn with Glw/Glw at night: each should glow in its own colours, the black one as a visible dark shape. Then parts.glow off, and the horn should still draw, lit like the rest of the horse.
