Magical gene / no coat effect

Cleansing light

An aura that burns the undead. Zombies, skeletons, drowned and anything else that counts take steady damage while they stand near the horse, out to a radius written on the allele copy.

Measured in the yard, not yet played

The gene, its inheritance and its founder table are covered by the test suite, and the test yard has measured what it does to undead: who it hurts, how often, how many at once and how far. Those readings are under Verified on the Coding tab. Nobody has yet stood beside one in play.

It costs you the drops

This is the important part and it is deliberate. A mob killed by the horse’s aura was not killed by you, so you get no experience and none of the drops that only fall to a player kill. Ride into a group of zombies with this gene and you will win, and you will come away with nothing.

It is a defensive gene, not a farming one. If you want the loot, hit them yourself first.

The lost loot is a settled decision, not a defect

Kills are credited to the horse, so no player experience and no player-only drops. This was raised in design and accepted deliberately — the gene is protection, and crediting the rider was rejected as making it straightforwardly better than fighting. Recorded here so a later session does not “fix” it.

Keep the interval at twenty ticks or more

Below about a second, the target’s hurt-immunity frames swallow most of the damage — so a faster beat costs the full entity scan and delivers almost nothing extra. The cheap setting and the correct setting are the same setting here.

The group filter must use entity type tags

Five genes share this filter. A hardcoded list makes every modded creature invisible to all five at once, and the failure is silent — the aura simply never fires on anything from another mod. Build it on tags in the change that introduces it.

A healing aura with the sign flipped

Rather than a new verb, this is healing with a negative amount and a group the verb did not previously offer. That keeps one radius-effect implementation rather than two, and it means the cap discipline every radius effect is required to state is inherited rather than re-argued.

Verified in the test yard

The aura kills undead nobody has touched, a carrier does nothing, the filter is the undead tag, and the drops are lost (2026-09-30, the clockwork pens CLEANSING and CLEANSING CARRIER, row AM west, run with nobody there). Each pen is a horse in a glass ring with a zombie, a skeleton, a drowned and a creeper caged three and a half blocks off, inside the smallest radius the allele rolls. Beside the Cln/Cln horse the zombie, the skeleton and the drowned all died nineteen seconds after the build (ten beats of two), of magic; the creeper was untouched at 20/20; and no experience orb appeared in the pen — the §loot cost working as designed, not a defect. Beside the Cln/n horse the zombie was untouched after six minutes, which is the control that separates a working recessive from an aura firing on every horse. The skeleton and drowned dying while the creeper lived is what shows the filter is MobGroups’ EntityTypeTags.UNDEAD lookup and not a list of zombies.

A pen trap: the second run failed the carrier, because its zombie stood six and a half blocks from the neighbouring pen’s Cln/Cln horse, whose radius rolls up to eight. The cage moved to the far side of its pen and the third run passed it. A pen bug, not the gene’s.

The beat, the cap and the radius (2026-10-08, test yard, pens CLEANSING NARROW, WIDE and CROWD, three CLOCKWORK PASS lines at 14:47:52). The pens read every zombie’s health once a tick for fourteen beats, beside horses whose radius was written on both copies.

  • The beat is slow. A zombie in the aura lost exactly CleansingLightGene.DAMAGE (2.0) per hit, and every hit on every zombie came exactly INTERVAL_TICKS (40) after that zombie’s last: nine hits in a row on the nearest zombie of each ladder, “t68,+40,+40,+40,+40,+40,+40,+40,+40”. No hit was lost to the hurt-immunity frames.
  • The cap bounds the scan. Twelve zombies stood within five blocks of a horse of radius 6.5. Each of the first ten beats hurt eight of the twelve (MAX_TARGETS) and no beat hurt more. The other four were first hurt ten beats later, once the first eight were dead, and then four a beat.
  • The radius is a radius. Two horses, written 4.0 and 8.0, each with a zombie at 2.5, 4.5, 6.5, 8.5 and 10.5 blocks. The narrow horse hurt only the zombie at 2.5. The wide one hurt those at 2.5, 4.5 and 6.5 and left 8.5 and 10.5 untouched. So the number on the copy is the reach, and a horse from the top of the range clears four blocks more ground than one from the bottom. Whether that reads as a difference worth breeding for is a matter of taste nobody has yet judged in play.

The pens were deleted on these lines.

A gene that wins fights and loses value

The loot cost is what stops this being simply the best combat gene. It draws a real line between the horse that keeps you alive and the horse that makes you rich, and forces a choice about which one you are riding today — which is more interesting than an aura that does both.
