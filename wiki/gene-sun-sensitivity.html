Magical gene / behaviour

Sun sensitivity

Two copies and daylight burns the horse. Caught under open sky by day it runs for the nearest deep cover or water — jumping a fence if it can — and stays there until dark. One copy shows nothing at all.

Crossing two of them

What happens to the horse

  • It burns. Half a heart every two seconds while it is outdoors, by day, with nothing overhead. Smoke comes off it.
  • Rain, water and a roof all save it. A horse standing in a pond is safe; so is one under trees thick enough to hide the sky, or in a barn.
  • It runs for shelter and prefers somewhere deep — at least two blocks in from the edge of the roof — so it does not end up half in the sun. It will jump a fence on the way if its jump is good enough.
  • It stays put until night. Once sheltered it stands still rather than wandering back out.
  • Ridden or on a lead, it is your problem. It will not bolt out from under a rider or pull a lead; it will just burn.

It is not fire in the ordinary sense, so it does not panic and run in circles the way a horse that walked into lava does — it goes to its shelter. A few wild horses in a hundred carry one silent copy, and none carry two. The white Dhampir has two.

Confirmed on the dhampir; unplayed as its own locus

Burning and running for shade were confirmed in-game on the old dhampir gene on 2026-09-13, after four rounds of fixes that all still live in SunShadeGoal's comments. The split onto this locus is not play-tested — see verification.

The handler and its goal

  • SunSensitivityHandler.onTick — every 40 ticks, staggered by entity id. It checks sunlight first (a few block reads) and only then parses the genome, so the parse only happens for a horse actually standing in the open by day. Damage is onFire at half a heart, not vanilla ignition.
  • SunShadeGoal at priority 1, on every horse. It asks the genome once, lazily, and does nothing for a horse that is not sensitive. It used to be added only to a horse already known to be a dhampir at the moment it joined the level — but a wild founder's record is written a tick after it joins, so a wild horse never got the goal until its chunk reloaded.
  • Panic yields to it. HorsePanicGoal holds its ground when the horse is on fire and sun-sensitive, because vanilla's panic sits at the same priority, holds the same movement flag, and was added first — it starved the shade goal and the horse ran in circles until it died.
  • Blood diet reads it too. A sun-sensitive blood-drinker does not go hunting while it is in the sun.

Not an effects verb, for the reason the dhampir gene gave: a verb with exactly one user is a hand-written handler wearing a disguise.

Verified

Shade-seeking worked on the old dhampir gene (2026-09-13, in play, earlier the same day the gene was split into sun sensitivity, magic white, the blood diet and the vampiric allele): the horse smoked and ran for deep cover or water at sunrise and stayed there until dark.

It survived the split, alone and in the full strain (2026-09-15). The yard's SUN ONLY pen holds a Sun/Sun horse with nothing else of the dhampir, a hay bale, and a roofed corner. DHAMPIR FULL holds the whole white strain with a cow.

  • 08:47 run, 4×4 roof: both burned, reached shade, and held it until dusk (stopped at ... in shade at 08:58:32). SUN ONLY took 11 burns before settling and ate hay once the sun was off it, so it eats normally. DHAMPIR FULL took 17 and bit its cow at dusk, healing 6.
  • 09:09 run, 6×6 roof (a 4×4 roof has no square with two rings of cover): SUN ONLY burned once and DHAMPIR FULL four times.
Each run, the first square each horse chose logged NO PATH, and the goal's retry took it under cover within seconds. Still unchecked: a wild-spawned horse getting the goal without a chunk reload, which the old gene did not (verification §0-R).

Horses really do burn

Sunburn is common on pink-skinned horses — white faces and socks, cremellos, heavily white paints — because unpigmented skin has nothing to absorb ultraviolet light. That is a coat question, not a gene of its own.

Photosensitisation is different and worse: something in the blood reacts to sunlight and damages the skin from within. In horses it is almost always acquired rather than inherited — eating plants such as St John's wort or buckwheat, or liver damage that lets a chlorophyll breakdown product (phylloerythrin) build up. Affected horses seek shade on their own, which is the one piece of real behaviour this gene borrows.

The inherited version belongs to other animals and to people: the porphyrias, a family of mostly recessive enzyme defects in which light-reactive compounds accumulate in the skin. Congenital erythropoietic porphyria turns up in cattle and cats, stains the teeth red, and has been suggested — not convincingly — as a root of the vampire legend. A heritable recessive that makes a horse burn in daylight is fantasy; the mechanism it would need is not.
