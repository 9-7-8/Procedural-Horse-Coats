Gameplay / non-genetic

Horse stasis: chambers and the bank

A cheap way to hold horses without them costing server tick time. A stasis chamber is an item a horse goes into; a Horse Stasis Bank is a block that holds many chambers and lets you read the horses inside them. The whole point is the cheapest tier, so a server with hundreds of horses can put most of them somewhere that costs nothing, instead of every horse being a real, ticking entity forever. The whole feature is built — five chambers, and a bank that keeps them, reads them on its Browse tab, mends them from its feed and water, collects what they drop, and breeds a pair you turn out at stud. The fifth chamber is the Emergency one, which is the only one you never use: it catches a horse of yours that is about to die, by itself, while you are carrying it.

The four stasis-chamber tiers

Craft a Basic Horse Stasis Chamber from a glass bottle, a wheat, a horse hair and a container of water — the water container is handed straight back, so it costs you a bucket of water and not a bucket. Right-click one of your own horses with an empty chamber and the horse goes in. Right-click the ground with a full one and it comes back out, in one piece: the same horse, with its bond, its name, its tack, its pregnancy, its cooldowns and its place in every pedigree exactly as they were.

While it is in there the horse is not an entity at all. It does not tick, does not get hungry, does not grow up, cannot be hurt and costs the server nothing. That is the feature: two hundred horses you are not currently using can sit in a chest instead of standing in a field being simulated.

The chamber is not spent

Letting a horse out hands you the same chamber back, empty and ready for another one (owner's call, 2026-09-24). One chamber holds one horse at a time — that is what “single use” meant. You never have to re-craft a Spacer chamber to get your diamond's worth twice.

A shelved horse stays on your roster, and the browser now says where it is: the whereabouts column reads in a stasis chamber rather than not loaded. That was worth fixing rather than leaving — “not loaded” means somewhere you cannot see right now, which reads as a horse you could go and fetch, and no amount of walking towards a bottle will ever load it. It also means stasis is a word the filter box finds horses by, so which of my two hundred are shelved is one search.

Storage is free at every tier and always will be. What the upper tiers buy is what the bank may do with a horse. Upgrading an occupied chamber carries the horse across — you do not have to let it out first, so a Basic chamber is never a dead end. There is a fifth chamber that is not a rung on this ladder at all — the Emergency one, below, which is a Basic chamber that uses itself.

Why one eye of ender buys two things

Searching a horse and healing it are the same capability with two uses: the bank being able to look inside the chamber. Basic's whole contract is that it cannot, and a bank that cannot look inside cannot know that the horse is hurt, what it eats, or how much of its bar is missing. So Intermediate unlocks both, and nothing above it can search without healing or heal without searching.

The Emergency Horse Stasis Chamber

The fifth chamber, and the only one you never use. Take a Basic chamber, put it in the middle of a crafting grid and surround it with eight ender pearls. Then carry the result. That is the whole of it: from that moment every horse you own is insured, and the instant one of them falls below a tenth of its health the horse is in the bottle — no click, no aim, nothing to remember in the middle of a fight.

It works wherever you are. The chamber does not have to be in your hand, only somewhere on you, and you do not have to be anywhere near the horse: a mare being torn apart by a pack of wolves three thousand blocks away is caught the same way, and the first you hear of it is the line in your chat saying it happened. Because nothing can hurt a horse inside a chamber, being caught is not a reprieve — it is the end of the incident.

One chamber is one life

If it already holds a horse and a second one of yours crosses the line, it refuses and says so — one line naming the horse it could not save. That horse takes its chances. The answer to wanting two saves is to carry two chambers (owner's call, 2026-09-24): the alternatives were a queue that goes stale the moment you log out, or swapping the healthier horse back into the fight that is killing the other one.

It is not spent, and the horse is not stuck. Let the horse out and you have the same emergency chamber back, empty and armed again for the next one. In every other respect it is a Basic chamber: it files in a bank, it is counted and listed by name, and the bank cannot look inside it. An eye of ender will upgrade it with the horse still in — but that trades the emergency away, since what comes out is an ordinary Intermediate chamber. Letting the horse out and filing it properly is the cheaper move.

It sits below the bolt, on purpose

A hurt horse already runs for its life at a fifth of its health — it throws off its rider, drops the fight and bolts. The chamber is set at half that, so the horse always gets its own chance to escape first and is only swallowed when running did not work. Both numbers are settings (behaviour.emergency_stasis_fraction, behaviour.escape_health_fraction); setting the first to 0 turns the self-capture off entirely without stopping the chambers working by hand.

Two things worth knowing before you rely on it. You have to be logged in — an inventory that is not in the world cannot catch anything, and neither can a bank if its owner is offline, so a horse that gets into trouble overnight is on its own. And if you are riding the horse you are set down first: you land on your feet where it was standing, and the horse goes into the bottle from under you a tick later. By then it has usually thrown you off anyway, since it bolts at twice this health.

Spare chambers in the bank count too

You do not have to be carrying one. An empty emergency chamber filed in a stasis bank you placed insures your horses exactly as one in your pocket does — so a rack of spare empties in the cabinet at home is worth having, and the day you ride out having forgotten to take one is not the day your insurance lapses.

The one in your inventory is spent first (owner's call): the bottle on your belt is the one you bought for this moment, and the bank is the backstop. If the carried one is full and a bank has an empty, the bank's is used rather than the horse being refused. The chat line tells you which happened, because the two leave you in different positions — one of them means you are a bottle short in your pocket and the other does not.

It is a bank you placed

A bank is otherwise unowned — anybody who can reach it can open it, file a chamber and take one out — but spending a stranger's emergency chamber would be theft, so the block quietly remembers who put it down. A bank placed before this existed is claimed the first time somebody opens it, which is the whole of what you need to do to an old one. Owning a bank grants nothing else.

It writes it down as well as saying it

A chamber fires with nobody watching, by design — that is most of what you are paying eight ender pearls for. So a rescue is now a row on the browser's Log tab as well as a line in chat, under a Rescues filter of its own. The chat line is the alarm and it scrolls away; the row is the answer to “what happened to my mare” an hour later, when the horse is a bottle in a cabinet with no story attached to it.

The Horse Stasis Bank

Craft one from five planks in a U, an ender pearl in the middle, an iron ingot in each top corner and a horse hair between them — any wood, so a plank you already have on hand will do. Place it, right-click it, and you get two hundred and seven slots that take stasis chambers and nothing else — a double chest's six rows on screen at a time, and twenty-three rows behind them. The grid scrolls: roll the wheel over it and the rows move, with a bar down the right-hand edge saying where in the cabinet you are.

That is the first of its three tabs, and it is worth having on its own: two hundred bottles in an ordinary chest are two hundred identical bottles, and this is where a stud farm's shelved horses live instead. Chambers of any tier go in, full or empty — a rack of spare empties beside the occupied ones is the obvious way to use one. The title bar counts what is in there, so a glance tells you nine horses of twelve rather than nothing at all.

The Browse tab: the bank as a list of horses

The second tab is the same chambers read as animals. Every occupied chamber is a line: the horse's name, what it is — Mare – Bay, tobiano — its breed and generation on the right, and the tier of the chamber it is sitting in. Four fit at a time and the list scrolls.

Above it is a filter box, and it takes the same query language as the Horse Browser's My horses table (the H screen) — see the browser's own page for the full language. mare, gen>2, gene:SB1, -lethal, a bare word for a name or a breed; terms are ANDed and a - negates one. A bank you can ask which of these two hundred are mares carrying sabino is the reason the tab exists — and the deeper the grid got, the more it is the reason, since twenty-three rows of identical bottles is not something anybody is going to read by eye.

The tier gates each line, not the tab

A Basic chamber is listed — with its horse's name, because the chamber's own tooltip already tells you that and a bank that hid it would be arguing with the bottle in your hand — but it says “a basic chamber cannot be looked into” where the details go, and no search will ever return it. When a filter hides rows, the list says how many it could not reach, so a horse never just vanishes from the bank. An eye of ender is what fixes that, and it upgrades the chamber with the horse still inside.

The details come from your stable's own papers rather than from the chamber, which has one visible consequence: a horse the records have nothing on — a chamber somebody else filled and gave you — is listed by name and says it has no papers, rather than being hidden or guessed at. With a filter typed, the count in the corner turns into 3 of 9.

The rows are also buttons. Clicking one turns that horse out at stud, if it is in a Spacer chamber; the line under the list says how many are, or invites you to try it, and a click on a chamber that cannot be marked says which rung can.

Breaking it gives everything back, and it will not burn

Every chamber drops when the bank is broken, the way a chest's contents do. It is also deliberately not flammable, unlike a chest: a chest that burns down loses its diamonds, and a bank that burns down would lose two hundred pedigreed horses to a lightning strike and drop them into the fire that did it.

The Supply tab: feeding and mending what is in there

The third tab is feed, water, the empties the water leaves behind, and a row of nine under them that is the bank's drop buffer. Put hay or wheat in one and a bucket of water in the other and the bank slowly heals the horses it is holding. That is the first thing it does to a horse rather than shelving one.

The rule is the one horses already live by out in a field, so there is nothing new to learn: a horse heals if it is hurt, if it eats something a person can hand it, and if there is water — and healing costs it hunger, which is what eating puts back. All the bank does is supply the two things a shelved horse cannot go and find. A bucket is worth about fifty hearts of healing, so one sees off several injured horses; the feed slot takes anything a horse of that diet would eat off the ground, including another mod's horse food, and a hay bale is worth far more than a carrot.

It is a slow hospital, and it gets slower the fuller it is

The bank mends one horse at a time, in rotation, about a heart and a half every turn. A bank with one hurt horse in it heals that horse at roughly the rate a field would; a bank with fifty gets round to each one far less often, and a full one takes the better part of five minutes to come back round to any particular horse. That is the price of the whole feature being cheap — a bank costs the server the same whether it holds one chamber or every slot it has — and a horse in no hurry is exactly what a shelved horse is.

The deep grid made that trade steeper, and it was taken on purpose. Speeding the rotation up to match would mean a full bank doing four times the work of the old one every tick, which is the one thing the feature is not allowed to do. If you have a horse that actually needs mending, it mends fastest in a bank that is not also storing two hundred others — or in a field.

A bank with nothing to mend spends nothing. It will not touch the feed slot or open a bucket for a horse that is not hurt, and if every chamber in it is Basic — or empty, or holds healthy horses — it does no work at all. The Supply tab says which of those is the case rather than sitting there looking broken.

The whole of that tab is the one part of the bank a hopper can reach. Feed and water going in, the empty buckets coming back out, and the drop buffer being emptied can all be piped — by a hopper, a dropper, or another mod's pipework — so keeping a stud farm's bank supplied, and collecting from it, is not a chore you do by hand. The chambers are not: nothing outside the bank can file or fetch a horse, and nothing is meant to.

The drop buffer: what an Advanced chamber collects

Some horses make things. The egg-layer locus gives a horse two matching copies of a laying allele and it drops that thing on the ground every few hours — an egg, a feather, wool, a slime ball, ink, string, bone or leather, depending which allele it is, and how often is written on the allele copy itself. Put such a horse in an Advanced chamber and the bank catches all of it in the nine slots under the supply row instead.

It is the same clock, at the same rate, on the same genetics: a shelved layer is not a better or a worse layer than one standing in a paddock, and a horse whose egg was nearly due when you shelved it does not lose it. What changes is only that nothing ends up on the floor.

A full buffer stops production rather than wasting it

A horse in a field drops its egg and walks off; a bank has nowhere to drop one. So if the nine slots are full the bank does not produce at all that turn, and the horse's clock is not spent either — empty the buffer and it picks up where it left off. Nothing is ever quietly destroyed, and the tab says full, so nothing more is made rather than leaving you to work that out.

Not milk. Milk cannot work this way

This was planned as a “milk and drops” buffer, and the milk half is not buildable. Every milk locus in the mod — milk, potion milk, the volume gene — works by you holding a bucket out to the horse. There is no passive milking anywhere in the mod for a bank to copy, so a bank that filled itself with milk buckets would be inventing a mechanic rather than shelving one, and a milkable horse would be worth more in a box than in a field. The buffer collects what a horse does on its own; milking is still something you do with your hands.

Putting a horse to stud in the bank

A Spacer chamber can be turned out at stud: open the Browse tab and click the row. The right-hand corner of the row changes from Spacer to at stud, and clicking again brings it back in. Nothing else on the row moves, and a click on any lower tier says so rather than doing nothing.

A bank with a mare and a stallion at stud is a paddock. Both horses stay exactly where they are, and the bank breeds them on the ordinary rules: she is covered when she comes into heat, once per heat and not twice, and a cover that does not take waits for her next one. A stallion who has already covered three times that day is less likely to catch, exactly as he would be in a field. Both of them have to be well, too — which is the other reason the feed and water slots matter.

Her cycle runs while she is shelved. It does not need her to be awake: a mare's heat is worked out from the clock, so a fortnight in a chamber is a fortnight of cycles, and she is in whatever phase she would have been in standing in a field.

She comes out to foal

The pregnancy is carried in the chamber — you can see it on her row in the Horse Browser like any other — and on her due date the bank lets her out, standing beside the block, and she foals within a couple of seconds. So you come back to a mare and a foal next to the bank and an empty chamber where she was. The foal is named, tamed, filed and logged exactly like any other foal, because it is any other foal: the birth is the ordinary one.

It will not foal into a wall. If there is nowhere beside the bank to put her down, she stays in her chamber and the Supply tab says a mare is due — clear a space beside the bank. Nothing is lost while you do.

Two things a bank breeding does not do. Breeding carrots do not reach it — a carrot is armed on a live horse and spent by a breeding you are standing next to, so a bank pairing draws like a wild cover. And there is no crowd limit: the paddock rule that stops breeding in a packed pen counts animals, and a bank holds none. How many chambers you mark is the only cap, which is the point of having to mark them.

What it refuses, and why it says so

A chamber is a crafted item used on purpose, so nothing here fails silently. It will not take a horse that is not yours or not tamed (the same HorseOwnership rule the stall signs, seed jar and transfer papers all use, with no creative exception), it will not take a horse with a rider on its back, and a chamber that already holds a horse says so rather than swallowing a second.

Releasing refuses in one more case: if the horse this chamber names is already out in the world, which only happens if the full chamber was duplicated in creative. Two entities sharing one id would corrupt the pedigree, so the copy refuses and says why.

Capture and release: it is the lycanthropy swap

This page used to say that capture/release of a live, specific entity was “new work with no existing analog in the mod”. That was wrong, and finding out it was wrong is most of why stage one was cheap. LycanthropyHandler has carried a whole horse through a night spent inside a wolf since the LYCAN gene shipped, and it does it with exactly two calls:

  • horse.saveWithoutId(TagValueOutput) → a CompoundTag, then horse.discard().
  • EntityType.HORSE.create(level, EntitySpawnReason.LOAD) → horse.load(TagValueInput.create(...)) → level.addFreshEntity(horse).

On NeoForge saveWithoutId carries the data attachments, and it stores the entity's UUID. That is the whole reason this shape is right and the one this page originally sketched was not: a released horse is the same horse down to the id that the ancestry database, every pedigree, every stall sign and every transfer deed key on. The sketch — a record enumerating horse_record, horse_gear, horse_care and friends by hand — would have lost the id, lost every vanilla field nobody listed (age, custom name, leash, the saddle in the vanilla slot), and rotted the first time somebody added a fifteenth attachment without thinking of that file.

Where the snapshot had to live, against the plan

The plan wanted StasisSnapshot in common/ so the web designer and a future 1.12.2 port could read a chamber. A tag-based capture cannot go there: CompoundTag is a Minecraft type and common/ imports none (hard rule 1). So the split is common/…/horse/StasisTier.java — the four tiers and their capability flags, the one part with no Minecraft in it, with StasisTierTest pinning the ladder — and neoforge/…/data/StasisSnapshot.java for the capture itself. Correct restoration beat portability; the portable half is the half that had something to port.

The bank is a filtered chest with a clock on it

It is modelled on EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity down to the setChanged forwarding and the server-only ticker, because it is the same problem: a SimpleContainer the menu wraps on the server and the ordinary vanilla slot sync fills in on the client, plus one number that advances with nobody looking. Nothing in the chamber grid ever unpacks a snapshot. A horse in stasis is a data component on an item, and the grid moves items; only the upkeep reads inside one.

The shelf's createTickerHelper shape is copied exactly, and two ContainerData values have appeared with it — the water meter and the foaling-blocked flag, both genuinely facts about the block that the screen cannot derive. Everything else on all three tabs is still a loop over slots the client already has: the horse count, every Browse row, what is in the feed slot, what is in the drop buffer, and which chambers are at stud. Every chamber carries its whole horse and its own mark, so both sides can count and read for free.

Two small divergences from the chest it resembles

It is not flammable. Vanilla's chest takes ignitedByLava() and this does not, which is one omitted call: mod blocks are not in FireBlock's flammability table unless they register themselves. A chest that burns down loses its diamonds; a bank that burns down would drop a stud farm's worth of pedigreed horses into the fire that did it.

It is still not a Container. The block entity holds two rather than implementing one, exactly as the research shelf does, so nothing outside can reach the chamber grid. What is reachable is the supply row, and only because it was asked for by name — see below.

The upkeep: four refusals, then one horse

HorseStasisBankBlockEntity.tick is deliberately almost all refusal, in cost order, and nothing past a refusal happens:

  1. Is there anything here worth doing? A cached boolean, recomputed only when a chamber slot moves: the or of the three jobs - a chamber that heals, one that collects, one at stud. A bank of Basic chambers — or of empty ones, or an empty bank — costs one field read a tick and no more. This is the owner's rule, and it is why the ticker may be attached unconditionally: the block has no blockstate properties to pick a ticker by, so the gate has to be the first line of the ticker instead.
  2. Is it this tick? One turn every StasisUpkeep.HEAL_INTERVAL, phase-staggered by block position — the same interval and the same trick HorseCareHandler uses on live horses, keyed by entity id there and by position here.
  3. Whose turn is it? A saved cursor walks the grid one slot a turn, so the work is one chamber's worth however full the bank is. Saved, or a reloaded bank would restart at slot zero every time and favour the top-left horse.
  4. Is that one hurt? Two cheap field reads, before anything decodes a record or resolves a diet.

Only past all four does the bank open a bucket or take a mouthful. That ordering is what lets the feature keep its own promise: a bank must cost less than the horses it replaced, and it has to hold that at HorseStasisBankBlockEntity.SLOTS chambers as well as at one. That promise is why the grid could be made this deep at all: nothing in the turn scales with how many chambers are filed.

Stage five added two more jobs to the same turn rather than a second clock, and each refuses again on its own terms: the buffer on whether anything is owed, the stud on whether she is in heat at all. The whole turn is still one chamber's worth of work, which is the number the promise is about. Reading a stored horse's abilities costs roughly what a live horse's own tick costs — and GeneAbilityHandler pays that every tick, where a shelved horse pays it once every StasisUpkeep.HEAL_INTERVAL at its very busiest, in a bank holding exactly one chamber. A full bank is thirty times cheaper again.

The economy is the field's, which is why it is so small

StasisUpkeep invents nothing. A live horse heals if it is hurt, if its diet is one items can feed, and if there is water nearby, paying Hunger.COST_PER_HEALTH a point and never spending below Hunger.STARVING — and eating is what puts hunger back. The bank applies exactly that and only supplies the food and the pond. The consequences follow for free: a blood-drinker is no more feedable in a chamber than in a field, a starving horse eats first and heals out of the same turn, and food without water is banked rather than wasted. The whole of it is pure Java and unit-tested.

StasisCare is the other half and does the tag surgery, because rebuilding an entity per turn is precisely the work the feature exists to avoid. release builds a real Horse and is right to; doing it once a turn for a chamber nobody is looking at would make a full bank cost more than the two hundred-odd ticking animals it replaced. So the arithmetic runs on four numbers and this class fetches and returns them.

A saved horse's attributes list is very often empty

AttributeMap materialises an instance on demand, and getValue reads the entity type's supplier without creating one — so a horse can be saved with a perfectly good maximum health and an attributes list of []. Reading the bar out of that list alone would have made every bank decide every horse was unhurt and heal nothing, for ever, with nothing logged.

So maximum health comes from the horse's own record, which is where HorseRecords.applyTraitsToEntity gets it from in the first place; the saved list is the fallback, for an unconverted vanilla horse with no papers of ours. This was not reasoned out — the stasis_tag_is_readable gametest caught it on its first run, on a 53-health horse whose saved list was empty, which is the entire argument for having written that test at all.

Why the goods slots are on the wire and the chambers are not

The bank exposes a NeoForge item-handler capability over the goods container only. Feed and water are bulk goods that run out, and keeping a stud farm's bank topped up by hand is exactly the chore automation is for (owner, 2026-09-24) — so a hopper, a dropper or another mod's pipework can push hay and buckets in, pull empties out, and empty the drop buffer. The chamber grid is not part of the handler at all, so the original call stands untouched: nothing outside the bank can file or fetch a live animal.

The slot rules are written once. NeoForge's VanillaContainerWrapper asks Container.canPlaceItem before it inserts, so the per-slot predicates the block entity already writes for the menu are the same rules a pipe obeys — feed in the feed slot, water in the water slot, nothing at all into the empties or the buffer, which are outputs. There is no second copy to drift.

The buffer is in that container on purpose, not for tidiness

The drop buffer could have been a third SimpleContainer beside the other two and it is nine more slots on the existing one instead, with FIRST_DROP_SLOT telling them apart. The capability wraps one container: a second would have wanted a second registration, something to join the two handlers, and a second copy of the slot rules to keep in step. As it is, StasisBankCapability did not change at all when the buffer shipped, and a hopper under the bank pulls eggs out of it for free. The cost is that SUPPLY_SLOTS now means twelve, which is why every reader goes through HorseStasisBankMenu.supplyX / supplyY rather than doing the arithmetic twice.

26.1.2 has no IItemHandler

Not verified in a running game. Written against the 26.1.2 sources: the item capability is Capabilities.Item.BLOCK and its type is ResourceHandler<ItemResource>, NeoForge's newer transfer API, not the IItemHandler every tutorial still shows. The patched HopperBlockEntity falls back to that same capability when the target is not a Container, which is what makes a vanilla hopper work here. See api-notes.html.

Chambers are stacksTo(1) in ModItems already; ChamberSlot.getMaxStackSize() and the container's both say so again, so no shift-click can ever try to merge two horses into one stack. The slot predicate takes any tier, empty or occupied — a bank that refused a Basic chamber would have inverted the whole ladder, since the bottom rung is the one that has to be worth using.

The Browse tab reads the stable's papers, not the chamber

The design call this tab had to make was where a row's details come from, and the Roadmap tab offered two answers: decode a whole entity tag per rendered row, or cache more fields onto the component at capture time. It is neither. A horse in a chamber keeps its UUID, the stable's ancestry records are keyed on exactly that, and ClientHorseRoster already holds every one of those records as a HorseListing — the same rows the Horse Browser's My horses table sorts. So a row is a map lookup on an id the chamber has already told the client, and the tab inherits the browser's coat description, sex label, breed, generation and whole query language for free.

That is also why the filter box takes HorseQuery verbatim rather than a smaller search of its own: a second, weaker query language in the same mod would have been more code and a worse feature. The screen opens by sending HorseRosterRequestPayload, the same request the browser's Refresh button sends, because a player may never have opened the browser on this client.

What that choice costs, and why it is the right side of the trade

The roster is this player's horses, and it is capped at HorseRosterPayload.MAX_ENTRIES. A chamber somebody else filled and handed over, or a stable past that cap, therefore has no row to match — so the tab draws that line by name and says it has no papers, and a search never returns it. The alternative, decoding a bankful of entity tags to draw a list, buys those rows at the cost of the one thing the whole feature exists to avoid: doing work per horse that nobody is looking at. The name is never lost either way, because StasisSnapshot cached it at capture time for exactly this.

StasisBrowseRow is in common/, and holds the gate: a row is searchable only if its own chamber's StasisTier.searchable() says so and there is a listing behind it, and locked() counts the rows that fail that test so the screen can say how many a query could not reach. Both halves are StasisBrowseTest's subject, because a horse silently disappearing from a filtered bank is the one failure this tab must not have: the player's next thought is that the bank lost it.

Two things the screen does that are not about stasis at all:

  • The list is rebuilt on a change, not per frame. A signature over which horse is in which slot, and in what tier, is taken each frame — ids and ordinals, no tag decoded — and the rows are rebuilt only when that, the roster's version, or the query moves. ClientHorseRoster makes the same point about why a table must not resolve a genotype per row per frame.
  • A text box inside a container screen must handle Escape first. EditBox.canConsumeInput() is true for every key once the box has focus, so guarding on it alone traps the player in the window with no way out - the equestrian bench's own override says so, having been reported for it, and this copies that shape rather than rediscovering it. See api-notes.html.

The drop buffer: the same ability verb, a different sink

Passive production in this mod is exactly one thing — GeneAbility.Produce, an item id, an interval and a count — and HorseAbilities.activeFor(genotype, epigenome) is pure common/. So the bank reads a shelved horse's yields off the same call the live translator reads them off, with no second table and no per-gene knowledge anywhere in the stasis code. A gene that gains a produce is collectable in a bank the day it ships.

The epigenome has to be passed. EggLayerGene is an EpigeneticAbilityContribution and its interval is written on the allele copy, so activeFor(genotype) alone would have given every horse in every bank the midpoint interval instead of its own — a difference nothing would ever have reported.

The cooldown had to become durable, and that is an improvement

GeneAbilityHandler keeps its produce cooldowns in a static Map<String, Long> keyed by uuid/geneKey/produce, which is lost on restart and cleared wholesale past eight thousand entries. A shelved horse has no tick to carry one at all, so the bank writes its stamp into the horse's own HORSE_COOLDOWNS attachment under produce:<geneKey> — the same attachment shearing and the yield genes already use. The consequence is the pleasant one: a shelved layer keeps a real clock across restarts, where a live one lays again on every server boot.

Priced, then paid: why due and stamp are two calls

A live horse calls spawnAtLocation and cannot fail. A bank can: nine slots fill up. So StasisDrops.due reads what is owed and changes nothing, the block entity's roomFor runs the insert against a throwaway container, and only if all of it fits does stamp mark the cooldown spent. A full buffer therefore pauses production rather than destroying a yield, and emptying it resumes on the next turn. Half a yield is never taken either, which is why the dry run is over the whole batch rather than per stack.

A condition a chamber cannot answer refuses

GeneAbilityHandler.conditionHolds reads the live entity and the level for almost every flag in the vocabulary — in_water, night, raining, has_rider, near_jukebox. A horse in a chamber is in none of those, and they are not false about it so much as meaningless. StasisProduce.holdsInStasis answers only the flags that are facts about the horse itself — adult, baby, sex_male, sex_female — and refuses everything else, negation included: “not raining” is no more a fact about a bottle than “raining” is. The alternative was a gene quietly behaving differently indoors. Nothing shipped is affected: the egg-layer's condition is always, and no spec gene emits a produce yet.

The Age key is the one bare literal, and it has a tripwire

adult means reading a saved AgeableMob's age, and there is no constant to import for it — vanilla writes the literal "Age". A rename would not throw; every shelved foal would simply start producing as though it were grown. So stasis_tag_is_readable snapshots the same horse twice, once grown and once with setBaby(true), and asserts both.

In-bank breeding: the mark, the cover, and coming out to foal

Three separate problems, and only the third was interesting.

The mark is a Boolean data component, STASIS_AT_STUD, on the chamber item. That is the same call STASIS_SNAPSHOT made and for the same reasons: it syncs to the client with the ordinary slot sync so the Browse tab can draw it with no packet of its own, it travels with the chamber between banks, and there is no copy on a block entity to drift from the chamber it describes. A set of marked UUIDs on the block would have needed syncing, pruning when a chamber left, and a rule for what happens when the same horse is in two banks. The mark alone is not permission: StasisStud.atStud asks the tier as well, because the component is a bare boolean on an arbitrary stack and nothing in the type system ties it to the rung that is allowed to set it. A chamber handed in by a /give with the component spelled out, or by another mod, or by some later recipe, arrives marked without ever having passed the menu's check — so the read asks the tier rather than trusting the flag.

Corrected 2026-09-24: the reason this page gave was not true

It said the tier is re-checked “because StasisUpgradeRecipe copies components and a mark can therefore outlive the rung that bought it”, and StasisStud.atStud's own comment said the same. That cannot happen. The upgrade copies stasis_snapshot and nothing else, so it does not carry the mark at all; and only a Spacer can be marked in the first place (clickMenuButton refuses any rung whose breedsInBank() is false), while a Spacer is the top rung and next() hands back null, so a marked chamber can never be put through an upgrade. Both halves of the story were wrong. The check is right and stays; only the reason for it has been replaced with one that happens. If a rung is ever added above Spacer, the upgrade dropping the mark becomes a real behaviour to decide on rather than a hypothetical — a mare quietly coming back in from stud — and that is the thing to remember from this, not the sentence that was here.

Toggling it is AbstractContainerMenu.clickMenuButton with the chamber slot as the id — JumpMenu's shape, and the rule BenchNamePayload states from the other side: an int fits in a menu button and a string does not. The server re-checks the tier and the occupancy, because a menu button is a packet and a packet is whatever somebody sent.

The cover is StasisMating over Conception.attempt, and it is small because it borrows everything. Reproduction's times are all absolute game ticks and its state is derived from them (ReproRules.stateAt), so a mare's cycle reaches her on a shelf with nothing ticking — the single property that makes this feature possible, and it was there before stasis was. Once-per-heat is ReproRules.mayTryNaturally unchanged, the stallion's three-a-day tiredness is coversOn read off his own stored record, and the health gate is COVER_HEALTH. Nothing here is a stasis number.

The pregnancy is carried in the chamber; the mare comes out to foal

This is the design call of the stage. Gestation is free: a Pregnancy is two ticks and a list of embryos, and it advances on a shelf exactly as it would in a field. Birth is not free, and the reason is worth stating plainly — HorseBreedingHandler.populateFoal names the foal under the dam's owner's NamingPolicy, tames it to her owner, gives it a quarter of her bond, files it in HorseAncestryData, puts it in her wild band, writes its HORSE_SOCIAL birth record and tells everyone nearby. Every one of those is a fact about the dam, and half of them are read off her live entity.

Reimplementing that against a saved tag would have been a second breeding path to keep in step for ever, which is the exact class of drift this repository keeps a table of rules about. So the bank does the cheap half and hands the expensive half back: at her due date it releases her beside the block and ReproHandler's ordinary SCAN tick foals her within two seconds. A bank-bred foal is not like every other foal, it is one.

It also means she is a ticking entity for the minutes around the birth and not for the gestation, which is the trade the whole feature is about. alreadyLoose is checked first, for the creative-copy reason release already had.

It will not foal into a wall

foalingSpot wants a position whose block and the one above it have an empty collision shape — two, because a horse is nearly two tall and setting one down inside a wall is how a released animal suffocates in a cellar. The four horizontal neighbours are tried first, then a small box round the block. With nothing found she stays in her chamber, foalingBlocked goes up, and the Supply tab says so; her turn comes round again. That flag is the second ContainerData value, because it is the one thing the bank can want that a player cannot read off a slot. It is not saved — it is a fact about the room, and a reloaded bank that is quiet for a minute beats one that remembers a wall somebody has knocked down.

Two deliberate omissions. No carrot bias: ARMED_CARROTS lives on a live horse and is spent by a breeding somebody is watching, so a bank pairing folds GameteBias.NONE on both sides, which is what a wild cover draws. And no crowd cap: DEFAULT_NATURAL_CAP counts entities in a chunk radius, a bank holds none, and the number of chambers the player marked is already their own cap.

Why three of these are Java recipes

None is a CustomRecipe for the fun of it, and the reasons are worth keeping:

  • The water container is not consumed. A vanilla recipe eats every input. StasisChamberRecipe overrides getRemainingItems and hands back the input's own declared crafting remainder — which is how a vanilla water bucket returns an empty bucket, the same as in a cake. The ingredient is matched on c:buckets/water (Tags.Items.BUCKETS_WATER), so another mod's container crafts it without anyone naming it here.
  • An upgrade must carry the horse. A shapeless JSON recipe builds its result from scratch and copies no components, so upgrading an occupied chamber would hand back an empty one and delete the animal — silently, with nothing in the log. StasisUpgradeRecipe copies the stasis_snapshot across. The JSON version of this feature is not a cheaper version of it; it is a data-loss bug with a crafting recipe attached.
  • So must the ender pearls. EmergencyChamberRecipe is the same hazard wearing a different shape — the chamber it converts sits in the middle of the grid and may well have a horse in it — so it copies stasis_snapshot too. It is shaped rather than shapeless because the arrangement is the recipe's name: a shapeless nine-item match would also accept the chamber in a corner, which reads like a different recipe that happens to work.
Both serializers are unit codecs, and that is load-bearing

Each recipe has one INSTANCE shared by MapCodec.unit(INSTANCE) (the value overload) and StreamCodec.unit(INSTANCE). A supplier-based map codec decodes a different object, the stream codec's identity check then throws, and because NeoForge sends the whole recipe set to every joining client as one payload, that is a login kick on every dedicated server — which shipped once, in v0.5.014. Singleplayer never encodes a recipe, so nothing short of a real join catches it. See api-notes.html. The every_recipe_encodes gametest is the cheap version of that join, and all three of these pass it.

The component syncs the whole tag, on purpose

It is tempting to give STASIS_SNAPSHOT a lossy stream codec that sends only the name and the id, since that is all a tooltip needs, and keep the heavy tag server-side. That is wrong, and the reason is creative mode: a creative pick-block sends the client's copy of a stack back to the server, so anything the client was never told is anything a creative player can quietly erase. The whole tag goes over the wire. Bandwidth is therefore a real question for the bank, where two hundred of them sit in one container — not for an item in a hand. The bank ships syncing all of it, deliberately: sending less is how a creative player silently erases a horse, and the reverse of that trade is a number to measure rather than a bug to fix. The measurement is on this page's Verification tab.

Small decisions inside release

  • Position comes from the chamber, not the tag. The tag remembers where the horse was captured, which may be a world away; where you open it is where it wakes up, with its velocity zeroed.
  • It starts fed. Hunger is explicitly reset to Hunger.FULL, matching the hunger attachment's own rule that a re-summoned horse is not starving. It is also the only honest answer when the horse has been outside time.
  • A horse that will not load stays in the chamber. If load throws, nothing is consumed and the chamber is unchanged, so a later build may still be able to read it. The alternative is destroying a pedigreed animal to tidy up an exception.

The emergency chamber: a subclass, and one event at LOWEST

Shipped 2026-09-24, and the smallest stage of the six by a distance — three new classes and about a hundred lines, because the only genuinely new thing in it is when to call swallow. Everything after that was already built.

Why it is a subclass and not a fifth tier

EmergencyStasisChamberItem extends StasisChamberItem at StasisTier.BASIC, and that one line is why nothing else in the feature had to change at all. The bank's isChamber is an instanceof on the superclass, so an emergency chamber files, counts, stacks, lists and upgrades exactly as the Basic chamber it was built from; HorseStasisBankBlockEntity, the menu, the Browse tab, the upkeep, the drop buffer and StasisStud are all untouched, and none of them knows the class exists.

A fifth StasisTier was the obvious alternative and is wrong twice over. The ladder is four rungs of what the bank may do with the horse, cumulative and pinned by StasisTierTest; self-capture is not that kind of capability at all, and the chamber is built from the cheapest rung rather than sitting above the dearest one. A rung costing eight ender pearls that bought no bank capability would be exactly the hole in the ladder that test exists to prevent. The visible consequence is the right one: an eye of ender upgrades an emergency chamber into an ordinary Intermediate, horse and all, spending the emergency for the window. That is the escape hatch rather than the intended path — releasing hands the armed chamber straight back — but it means a rescued horse is never stuck somewhere the bank cannot read it.

Where the hook had to go, against the plan

The Roadmap tab expected to hang this off “whatever handler ends up watching for the buck-off-below-20% escape behaviour”. There is no such handler. That behaviour is HorseEscapeGoal, an AI goal that reads the health bar on its own canUse tick, and a goal cannot see a blow coming — by the time it runs the horse is already dead or not. So the model is HorseLastStandHandler instead, which asks the same question at the same moment: is this hit about to take the horse somewhere it should not go?

LivingDamageEvent.Pre, for the last stand's reason: it is the first point at which getNewDamage is the number that will really come off the health, armour and enchantments already taken out of it, so a horse in barding is not judged on a blow it was never going to take in full.

LOWEST priority, and that ordering is the feature

Three handlers are now on this one event, so the priority is not tidiness — it is how they compose. Running last, the rescue sees a horse the other two could not help: a killing blow is caught by the last stand, which zeroes the damage and leaves the horse on a sliver; a rescuing braid at LOW takes that horse home if it is wearing one; and only then does this see a horse still standing on a sliver and take it out of the fight. The player gets the totem particles and the rescue, in that order, which is exactly the story that happened. In the losing order the chamber would swallow a horse the last stand was about to save anyway — spending the insurance on a blow that was never going to land.

It dropped from LOW to LOWEST when the braid shipped, and the reason is the same both times. A braid is the more specific instrument — worn on one horse on purpose, where a chamber covers every horse you own — so it spends itself first. Nothing is lost by that: a horse the braid has sent home is out of the fight and takes no further blows, so the chamber it did not spend is still in your pocket.

The scan the roadmap was worried about does not exist

The open question this stage carried was cost: watching every owned horse's health sounds like precisely the server-wide per-tick sweep the whole stasis feature exists to avoid. It is not one, and the reason is almost too simple to write down — health only moves when something moves it, so the question is asked on a damage event and nowhere else. Past the two field reads that answer “is this a horse, and is it low”, the only cost is one walk of one player's inventory, and only for a horse that is already about to die. A world with no fighting in it pays nothing.

The owner is found by HorseOwnership.ownerId and then getPlayerList().getPlayer(uuid), not horse.getOwner(), which answers only for an owner loaded in the same level — the gap HorseHurtNoticeHandler documents and accepts. A chamber that only worked when you were standing next to the horse would be worth very little, since you are usually not.

Four small decisions

  • A state, not a crossing. StasisRescue.rescues asks where the health is, not whether this blow moved it across the line. A horse already under the threshold - because its owner was offline, or carrying nothing, when it first got hurt - must still be caught by the next chamber that can reach it. Testing for a crossing would make the rescue depend on who happened to be watching the moment it first went low, which is not a thing a player can reason about.
  • The rider is set down, not refused. An ordinary capture refuses a horse with somebody on its back, because passengers would be discarded with it. Refusing here would make the item useless in the exact situation it is bought for, so ejectPassengers runs first and the horse vanishes from under them. It is the uncommon path anyway: HorseEscapeGoal throws the rider off at twice this health.
  • The damage is zeroed after the capture, not before, so a horse that somehow could not be stored takes its blow rather than being handed free immunity.
  • The refusal is throttled per horse, not per player. A full chamber refuses on every blow, and a horse standing in a fire takes one every half second. Ten seconds of quiet per horse, the same shape and the same sweep as HorseHurtNoticeHandler's - two horses in the same fire are two different pieces of news.

The threshold itself lives in common/ as StasisRescue, with the two chat lines, so StasisRescueTest can pin them — including the one assertion that is really about two features at once: the rescue fraction must stay below Escape.DEFAULT_THRESHOLD, or a horse would be swallowed before it was ever given the chance to run.

The bank as a second place to look, without a world search

An empty emergency chamber filed in a bank insures its owner's horses too, and the obvious way to implement that is the one thing this whole feature exists to avoid: finding a block somewhere in some dimension means a search of the world, or a chunk load per bank per blow.

StasisBankIndex is what makes it cheap, and the trick is its second field. A row is a bank's position and its owner — that much is only a phone book. What matters is armed: the block entity publishes, every time its chamber grid changes and again on onLoad, whether it is currently holding an empty emergency chamber. So the usual answer — no, none of your banks can help — is a walk of a short list in memory with no chunk touched at all. A chunk is loaded only for a bank that says it is holding one, which is a horse's life against one chunk load; paying that to find out the answer is no would not have been worth it.

  • The flag is last-known, and the reader copes. It is re-checked against the real container rather than trusted, so a stale row costs one wasted chunk load and finds nothing — which is the right way round for a cache that is deciding whether a horse lives. The block, not the index, is also the authority on who owns a bank.
  • The search shares the refusal's clock. StasisRescue.dueAgain gates the bank walk as well as the sentence, so a horse burning to death with a stale row against it cannot re-open the same chunk every half-second. A failed search starts the quiet period even though it says nothing, which is the one place the two uses of that map differ.
  • Eight banks, at most, per rescue. A player with three never reaches MAX_BANKS_CONSULTED; a player with two hundred does not get to turn a wolf bite into two hundred chunk loads.
  • A bank is claimed by placing it, or by opening an unclaimed one. That second door is the entire migration story for a world that predates this, and it takes nothing from anybody: an owned bank is untouched, and owning one grants no access that opening it did not already give.

The chamber is filled in place inside the bank's grid, exactly as it is in an inventory slot, and chambers().setChanged() is what re-derives the tick gate and republishes the bank as no longer armed. That one call is the only thing the bank half adds to close.

A tick between the eject and the capture

Owner's call: a rider is put down and the horse goes into the bottle on the next server tick, rather than both happening inside one event and the client having to work out where its feet went. The blow is refused up front in that path — unlike the immediate one, where it is refused after the capture — because a horse left standing for a tick still holding the blow that triggered all this would simply die of it.

And it is held harmless for that tick. A CAUGHT set of horse ids is checked on the first line of onBlow and zeroes any damage to a horse mid-grace, so the courtesy cannot be the thing that kills the horse. The deferral only happens when there was a rider; an unridden horse goes in immediately and has no window to protect.

Both failure modes leave the chamber empty and the player insured, which is the only safe way round: a horse gone before the bottle closed, and a chamber somebody filled by hand in the one tick it was reserved for, both abort with a trace line rather than spending anything.

Why a rescue is a log row and not only a chat line

HorseEvent.Kind.RESCUE is the eighth kind, and it is here for the reason the Log tab exists at all: a chamber fires with nobody watching by design, possibly a thousand blocks away, so the alarm that scrolls past was the only record there was. The row's line() defers to StasisRescue.saved / savedInBank rather than writing its own wording, the way a cover defers to CoverNotice — the log is where you look when you missed the chat line, and two different sentences about one event would leave the reader wondering whether they were two events.

HorseLog.rescued takes ids rather than the entity, which is the exception among its writers and is the feature rather than an awkwardness: by the time anything knows a rescue happened, the horse has been discarded and is a data component on an item.

How the browser knows a horse is in a chamber

HorseWhereabouts records stasis the way it already records death, and for the same reason. A horse in a chamber is neither alive in a chunk nor dead: it fires no LivingDeathEvent, it will never be sighted again, and it therefore sat in the roster reading not loaded for ever — the same wrong claim, in the same column, that a dead horse used to make before the roster started dropping them.

The mark is written by swallow before the discard, while the horse still has a position, and cleared by release once the entity is really in the world. Those are the two moments that are certain; nothing is inferred, and an ordinary sighting clears the mark too, since a horse something can see is out of the bottle however it got there.

The label itself is HorseListing.IN_STASIS in common/, and the two places the browser drew this column now both call whereLabel instead of writing loaded ? where : "not loaded" by hand. That was the real defect: with the rule written out twice, the second copy was always going to be the one that got missed.

Every chamber recipe was invisible, and that is not the same as broken

Reported 2026-09-25 from a modded server as “the recipes for the stasis chambers never made it into the game — the bank has a recipe, but not the chambers”, with the decisive detail that clicking a chamber in JEI popped up nothing at all. Every recipe was in fact present, registered and working: the grids below crafted correctly for anyone who already knew them. What no part of the game would do was tell anyone what they were.

The cause is two defaults on CustomRecipe, which all five chamber recipes extend: isSpecial() answers true and placementInfo() answers PlacementInfo.NOT_PLACEABLE. Between them that means no recipe-book entry, no place-into-grid button, and nothing in JEI — which skips special recipes on the entirely reasonable grounds that a recipe declaring no ingredients has nothing to draw. The bank was unaffected because it is an ordinary shaped JSON file.

This is the trap worth remembering, because it is invisible from inside the game code and from every test that was in place. A special recipe still crafts. The gametest suite had EVERY_RECIPE_ENCODES, which proved the recipes reach a joining client, and the class comments explained at length why each had to be Java rather than JSON — all true, all beside the point. Nothing asserted the one thing a player needs, which is that the recipe can be shown to them. STASIS_CHAMBERS_CRAFT now does: for all five grids it asserts the recipe resolves, produces the right item, is not special, and advertises real ingredients.

Why the upgrades became three recipes

Overriding the two flags was enough for the Basic and Emergency chambers. It could not be enough for the upgrades, because a recipe advertises exactly one set of ingredients, and one StasisUpgradeRecipe instance was matching all three rungs by reading tier().next() off whatever chamber was in the grid. There is no placementInfo() that says “a chamber, and whichever item its own tier calls for”, so that instance could never have described itself however the flags were set.

So the tier being upgraded to is now a field on the recipe, read from a "to" key in the JSON, and data/horsegenetics/recipe/ carries stasis_upgrade_intermediate, _advanced and _spacer in place of the single stasis_upgrade. Matching is unchanged in what it accepts: each file takes the rung below its own and nothing else, which is what the one-instance version already did by a different route — including an emergency chamber going up to an Intermediate, since that item is a StasisChamberItem at BASIC and the Intermediate file asks for a tier, not a class.

A side benefit worth naming: the upgrade serializer now has a real codec with a real field instead of MapCodec.unit, which removes it from the shared-instance trap described on the carrot page — there is nothing left for StreamCodec.unit's identity check to fail on.

What is waiting to be checked

The chamber recipes have just been made visible to JEI and the recipe book and nobody has looked; beyond that, the five chambers, the bank and everything it does compile, craft and pass the gametests, but no horse has yet gone into one in a running game — so the whole capture/release round trip, the water-container remainder for a modded bucket, the tier upgrade carrying an occupied chamber, every one of the bank's slots, drops and sync costs, every line the Browse tab draws, the healing upkeep and its hopper plumbing, the drop buffer, every part of in-bank breeding up to a mare letting herself out to foal, the newly deepened grid and the scrolling that reaches it, and the emergency chamber firing at all are unplayed — now including a chamber being spent out of a bank, the tick a rider is set down for, and the whereabouts column saying a horse is in a chamber.

A bank's spare chamber saves a horse (2026-09-25, NOT played)

The newest half of the emergency chamber, and the one with the most moving parts: a saved index, a flag the block publishes about itself, a chunk loaded from inside a damage event, and an owner recorded on a block that never had one. All written against the 26.1.2 sources; none of it watched.

  • The basic case. Place a bank, put one empty emergency chamber in it, carry nothing, and get a horse of yours killed some distance away. Expect the horse gone, a chat line naming your stasis bank rather than a carried chamber, a Rescues row in the H screen's Log tab, and that chamber in the bank now holding the horse.
  • Then walk a long way off and do it again. The whole point is that the bank's chunk need not be loaded, so this is the line that pays for a chunk load — watch for a stutter on the tick it fires as well as for it working at all.
  • An old bank. Find a bank placed before this update, do not open it, and check it insures nothing. Then open it once and check it does. That is the whole migration path and it has exactly one door.
  • Somebody else's bank must never be spent on your horse.
  • Inventory first. Carry an empty chamber and have one filed. The carried one should go.
  • A full carried chamber must not block the bank. Carry an occupied emergency chamber with an empty one filed: the bank's should be used and you should not see the refusal line.
  • The bank should stop advertising itself once its last empty emergency chamber is spent — the next rescue should not load its chunk at all. There is nothing on screen for this; it is a [stasis] trace line or nothing.

The emergency chamber catches a horse (NOT played)

Nothing about this has been watched happen. The threshold and the words are unit-tested and the build is green, which proves neither that the event fires on the right horse nor that a live animal survives being swallowed out of a blow. The first check is the cheap one and settles most of it.

  • It fires. Tame a horse, carry one empty emergency chamber, and hit the horse down past a tenth of its bar. The horse should disappear, the chat should name it, and the chamber in the inventory should now have that name on its tooltip. Shelve the check until there is a second player or a second dimension to hand: leave a horse somewhere it will be attacked, walk (or portal) a long way off, and confirm the line still arrives. This is the part getOwner() would have got wrong and the player-list lookup is meant to fix.
  • The last stand goes first. Hit a healthy horse with something that would kill it outright. The expected order is totem particles, then the rescue: the last stand saves it at half a heart and the chamber takes it in the same tick. If the chamber swallows a horse that was never in danger, or the horse dies with an armed chamber in the inventory, the LOW priority is not doing what this page says it does.

Hoppers and pipes reach the supply slots and nothing else (NOT played)

  • A hopper under the bank pulls empty buckets out — and, worth knowing either way, whether it also pulls the feed back out. That is allowed today; if it turns out to be annoying in practice it is a per-slot extraction rule, not a bug.

The modded water container branch (NOT played, and known imperfect)

The Basic recipe returns the input's own declared crafting remainder, which a vanilla water bucket has (an empty bucket) and a modded fluid container may not. The correct answer is to drain the container through its fluid capability and return whatever that leaves — the same match-by-tag-return-drained-container problem the water troughs on compatibility.html will have to solve — and this waits for that rather than inventing a second remainder rule.

  • Which branch a modded bucket takes. Craft a Basic chamber with one in the heavily-modded server. Pass: either an empty container comes back, or the full one does. Fail: the container is consumed, since “not consumed” is the rule that matters and a full one handed back is the deliberate second-best.

What a full bank costs on the wire (never measured, and now four times the question)

Every chamber's component carries the horse's whole entity tag, on purpose (see the Coding tab), and opening a full bank sends every one of HorseStasisBankBlockEntity.SLOTS of them in a single ClientboundContainerSetContentPacket. The grid went from fifty-four slots to twenty-three rows of nine and this check did not get easier — scrolling changes which rows are drawn, never which are sent, so the packet on open is now roughly four times what it was when nobody had measured it either. This is the first thing to look at in the next play session.

  • Open a full one on a real connection. Fill a bank with occupied chambers, preferably horses with long pedigrees and full gear, and open it over the network rather than in singleplayer — singleplayer never encodes it. Fail: a lag spike, or a disconnect, on open. The answer if it is bad is not a lossy stream codec, which is how a creative player silently erases a horse; with the grid now scrolled rather than shown whole, the honest answer would be sending the rows the player is looking at and no more — which is a real piece of work, not a constant to turn down, because the creative pick-block problem applies to every row the client holds.
  • And there is a packet ceiling somewhere. Nobody has established what size ClientboundContainerSetContentPacket a vanilla client will refuse. At fifty-four this was a performance question; at two hundred it might be a hard failure, and a hard failure on open would be the bank refusing to open at all rather than being slow. Pass: a full bank opens over a network connection without a disconnect.
  • And now it is not only on open. Every heal turn rewrites one chamber's snapshot component, which re-syncs that stack's whole entity tag to anyone with the bank open — one tag every StasisUpkeep.HEAL_INTERVAL ticks per bank, and only while something is actually being mended. That is small next to opening a full bank, but it is continuous where the other is a one-off, and nobody has watched it.
  • Stage five added two more writers to that same path — a collected yield stamps a cooldown into the horse's tag, and a cover writes a whole reproductive record into it. Both re-sync that chamber's entire entity tag, both are far rarer than a heal turn, and neither has been measured either.

That it does not burn (reasoning unverified)

The block deliberately omits ignitedByLava(), on the reasoning that mod blocks are absent from FireBlock's flammability table unless they register themselves. That reasoning has not been checked in a game.
