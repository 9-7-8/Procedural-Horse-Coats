Gameplay / partly built

Horse stasis: chambers and the bank

A cheap way to hold horses without them costing server tick time. A stasis chamber is an item a horse goes into; a Horse Stasis Bank is a block that holds many chambers and lets you read the horses inside them. The whole point is the cheapest tier, so a server with hundreds of horses can put most of them somewhere that costs nothing, instead of every horse being a real, ticking entity forever. The four chambers are built, the bank keeps them, and its Browse tab reads them. What is still planned is everything that makes the bank act on a stored horse: passive healing, the drop buffer, in-bank breeding.

The four stasis-chamber tiers

Craft a Basic Horse Stasis Chamber from a glass bottle, a wheat, a horse hair and a container of water — the water container is handed straight back, so it costs you a bucket of water and not a bucket. Right-click one of your own horses with an empty chamber and the horse goes in. Right-click the ground with a full one and it comes back out, in one piece: the same horse, with its bond, its name, its tack, its pregnancy, its cooldowns and its place in every pedigree exactly as they were.

While it is in there the horse is not an entity at all. It does not tick, does not get hungry, does not grow up, cannot be hurt and costs the server nothing. That is the feature: two hundred horses you are not currently using can sit in a chest instead of standing in a field being simulated.

The chamber is not spent

Letting a horse out hands you the same chamber back, empty and ready for another one (owner's call, 2026-09-24). One chamber holds one horse at a time — that is what “single use” meant. You never have to re-craft a Spacer chamber to get your diamond's worth twice.

ItemRecipeUnlocks, in the bank
Basic Horse Stasis Chamber Glass bottle + wheat + horse hair + a container of water, in any arrangement. Any modded water container matches, by tag. The container is not consumed. Storage only. The horse is listed by name on the Browse tab and nothing else about it can be asked for.
Intermediate Horse Stasis Chamber Any chamber + an eye of ender. All of the horse's information becomes searchable — the Browse tab shows what it is and the filter box can find it. Built.
Advanced Horse Stasis Chamber Intermediate + a gold ingot. The bank passively collects the horse's milk/drops into its 9-slot buffer.
Spacer Horse Stasis Chamber Advanced + a diamond. The horse can be bred while still inside the bank — the other parent must also be in a Spacer chamber — and the foal pops out into the world.

Storage is free at every tier and always will be. What the upper tiers buy is what the bank may do with a horse — today that is the Browse tab's search, which an Intermediate unlocks and a Basic does not, and the two tiers above it are paying for things the bank cannot do yet. Upgrading an occupied chamber carries the horse across — you do not have to let it out first, so a Basic chamber is never a dead end.

The Horse Stasis Bank

Craft one from five planks in a U, an ender pearl in the middle, an iron ingot in each top corner and a horse hair between them — any wood, so a plank you already have on hand will do. Place it, right-click it, and you get a double chest's worth of slots that take stasis chambers and nothing else.

That is the first of its two tabs, and it is worth having on its own: fifty-four bottles in an ordinary chest are fifty-four identical bottles, and this is where a stud farm's shelved horses live instead. Chambers of any tier go in, full or empty — a rack of spare empties beside the occupied ones is the obvious way to use one. The title bar counts what is in there, so a glance tells you nine horses of twelve rather than nothing at all.

The Browse tab: the bank as a list of horses

The second tab is the same chambers read as animals. Every occupied chamber is a line: the horse's name, what it is — Mare – Bay, tobiano — its breed and generation on the right, and the tier of the chamber it is sitting in. Four fit at a time and the list scrolls.

Above it is a filter box, and it takes the same query language as the Horse Browser's My horses table (the H screen) — see the browser's own page for the full language. mare, gen>2, gene:SB1, -lethal, a bare word for a name or a breed; terms are ANDed and a - negates one. A bank you can ask which of these fifty-four are mares carrying sabino is the reason the tab exists.

The tier gates each line, not the tab

A Basic chamber is listed — with its horse's name, because the chamber's own tooltip already tells you that and a bank that hid it would be arguing with the bottle in your hand — but it says “a basic chamber cannot be looked into” where the details go, and no search will ever return it. When a filter hides rows, the list says how many it could not reach, so a horse never just vanishes from the bank. An eye of ender is what fixes that, and it upgrades the chamber with the horse still inside.

The details come from your stable's own papers rather than from the chamber, which has one visible consequence: a horse the records have nothing on — a chamber somebody else filled and gave you — is listed by name and says it has no papers, rather than being hidden or guessed at. With a filter typed, the count in the corner turns into 3 of 9.

Breaking it gives everything back, and it will not burn

Every chamber drops when the bank is broken, the way a chest's contents do. It is also deliberately not flammable, unlike a chest: a chest that burns down loses its diamonds, and a bank that burns down would lose fifty-four pedigreed horses to a lightning strike and drop them into the fire that did it.

It is not a hopper target. Chambers go in and out by hand only — nothing automates filing a horse, and nothing is meant to. What the bank cannot do yet is act on a horse: there is no passive healing, no drop collection and no in-bank breeding, which is what the two tiers above Intermediate pay for. They are on the Roadmap tab, in the order they should be built.

What it refuses, and why it says so

A chamber is a crafted item used on purpose, so nothing here fails silently. It will not take a horse that is not yours or not tamed (the same HorseOwnership rule the stall signs, seed jar and transfer papers all use, with no creative exception), it will not take a horse with a rider on its back, and a chamber that already holds a horse says so rather than swallowing a second.

Releasing refuses in one more case: if the horse this chamber names is already out in the world, which only happens if the full chamber was duplicated in creative. Two entities sharing one id would corrupt the pedigree, so the copy refuses and says why.

Capture and release: it is the lycanthropy swap

This page used to say that capture/release of a live, specific entity was “new work with no existing analog in the mod”. That was wrong, and finding out it was wrong is most of why stage one was cheap. LycanthropyHandler has carried a whole horse through a night spent inside a wolf since the LYCAN gene shipped, and it does it with exactly two calls:

  • horse.saveWithoutId(TagValueOutput) → a CompoundTag, then horse.discard().
  • EntityType.HORSE.create(level, EntitySpawnReason.LOAD) → horse.load(TagValueInput.create(...)) → level.addFreshEntity(horse).

On NeoForge saveWithoutId carries the data attachments, and it stores the entity's UUID. That is the whole reason this shape is right and the one this page originally sketched was not: a released horse is the same horse down to the id that the ancestry database, every pedigree, every stall sign and every transfer deed key on. The sketch — a record enumerating horse_record, horse_gear, horse_care and friends by hand — would have lost the id, lost every vanilla field nobody listed (age, custom name, leash, the saddle in the vanilla slot), and rotted the first time somebody added a fifteenth attachment without thinking of that file.

Where the snapshot had to live, against the plan

The plan wanted StasisSnapshot in common/ so the web designer and a future 1.12.2 port could read a chamber. A tag-based capture cannot go there: CompoundTag is a Minecraft type and common/ imports none (hard rule 1). So the split is common/…/horse/StasisTier.java — the four tiers and their capability flags, the one part with no Minecraft in it, with StasisTierTest pinning the ladder — and neoforge/…/data/StasisSnapshot.java for the capture itself. Correct restoration beat portability; the portable half is the half that had something to port.

The bank is a filtered chest, and that is the whole of stage two

It is modelled on EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity down to the setChanged forwarding, because it is the same problem: a SimpleContainer the menu wraps on the server and the ordinary vanilla slot sync fills in on the client. Nothing here ever unpacks a snapshot. A horse in stasis is a data component on an item, and the bank moves items.

Two things it deliberately does not have yet, each because the thing that needs it is a later stage:

  • No ticker. Nothing advances with nobody looking. Passive healing (stage four) is what will want one, and it will want the research shelf's server-only createTickerHelper shape.
  • No ContainerData, the Browse tab included. There is no progress and no server-side pick to sync. The horse count on the title row, and every row of the Browse list, is a loop over the slots the client already has rather than a synced number — every chamber carries its whole horse, so both sides can count and read for free.
Two small divergences from the chest it resembles

It is not flammable. Vanilla's chest takes ignitedByLava() and this does not, which is one omitted call: mod blocks are not in FireBlock's flammability table unless they register themselves. A chest that burns down loses its diamonds; a bank that burns down would drop fifty-four pedigreed horses into the fire that did it.

It is not a Container. The block entity holds one rather than implementing one, exactly as the research shelf does, so hoppers and pipes cannot reach it. Automating the filing of a live animal is not a thing anybody asked for, and the cheapest way to not have it is to not expose the inventory.

Chambers are stacksTo(1) in ModItems already; ChamberSlot.getMaxStackSize() and the container's both say so again, so no shift-click can ever try to merge two horses into one stack. The slot predicate takes any tier, empty or occupied — a bank that refused a Basic chamber would have inverted the whole ladder, since the bottom rung is the one that has to be worth using.

The Browse tab reads the stable's papers, not the chamber

The design call this tab had to make was where a row's details come from, and the Roadmap tab offered two answers: decode a whole entity tag per rendered row, or cache more fields onto the component at capture time. It is neither. A horse in a chamber keeps its UUID, the stable's ancestry records are keyed on exactly that, and ClientHorseRoster already holds every one of those records as a HorseListing — the same rows the Horse Browser's My horses table sorts. So a row is a map lookup on an id the chamber has already told the client, and the tab inherits the browser's coat description, sex label, breed, generation and whole query language for free.

That is also why the filter box takes HorseQuery verbatim rather than a smaller search of its own: a second, weaker query language in the same mod would have been more code and a worse feature. The screen opens by sending HorseRosterRequestPayload, the same request the browser's Refresh button sends, because a player may never have opened the browser on this client.

What that choice costs, and why it is the right side of the trade

The roster is this player's horses, and it is capped at HorseRosterPayload.MAX_ENTRIES. A chamber somebody else filled and handed over, or a stable past that cap, therefore has no row to match — so the tab draws that line by name and says it has no papers, and a search never returns it. The alternative, decoding fifty-four entity tags to draw a list, buys those rows at the cost of the one thing the whole feature exists to avoid: doing work per horse that nobody is looking at. The name is never lost either way, because StasisSnapshot cached it at capture time for exactly this.

StasisBrowseRow is in common/, and holds the gate: a row is searchable only if its own chamber's StasisTier.searchable() says so and there is a listing behind it, and locked() counts the rows that fail that test so the screen can say how many a query could not reach. Both halves are StasisBrowseTest's subject, because a horse silently disappearing from a filtered bank is the one failure this tab must not have: the player's next thought is that the bank lost it.

Two things the screen does that are not about stasis at all:

  • The list is rebuilt on a change, not per frame. A signature over which horse is in which slot, and in what tier, is taken each frame — ids and ordinals, no tag decoded — and the rows are rebuilt only when that, the roster's version, or the query moves. ClientHorseRoster makes the same point about why a table must not resolve a genotype per row per frame.
  • A text box inside a container screen must handle Escape first. EditBox.canConsumeInput() is true for every key once the box has focus, so guarding on it alone traps the player in the window with no way out - the equestrian bench's own override says so, having been reported for it, and this copies that shape rather than rediscovering it. See api-notes.html.

Why two of these are Java recipes

Neither is a CustomRecipe for the fun of it, and both reasons are worth keeping:

  • The water container is not consumed. A vanilla recipe eats every input. StasisChamberRecipe overrides getRemainingItems and hands back the input's own declared crafting remainder — which is how a vanilla water bucket returns an empty bucket, the same as in a cake. The ingredient is matched on c:buckets/water (Tags.Items.BUCKETS_WATER), so another mod's container crafts it without anyone naming it here.
  • An upgrade must carry the horse. A shapeless JSON recipe builds its result from scratch and copies no components, so upgrading an occupied chamber would hand back an empty one and delete the animal — silently, with nothing in the log. StasisUpgradeRecipe copies the stasis_snapshot across. The JSON version of this feature is not a cheaper version of it; it is a data-loss bug with a crafting recipe attached.
Both serializers are unit codecs, and that is load-bearing

Each recipe has one INSTANCE shared by MapCodec.unit(INSTANCE) (the value overload) and StreamCodec.unit(INSTANCE). A supplier-based map codec decodes a different object, the stream codec's identity check then throws, and because NeoForge sends the whole recipe set to every joining client as one payload, that is a login kick on every dedicated server — which shipped once, in v0.5.014. Singleplayer never encodes a recipe, so nothing short of a real join catches it. See api-notes.html. The every_recipe_encodes gametest is the cheap version of that join, and both of these pass it.

The component syncs the whole tag, on purpose

It is tempting to give STASIS_SNAPSHOT a lossy stream codec that sends only the name and the id, since that is all a tooltip needs, and keep the heavy tag server-side. That is wrong, and the reason is creative mode: a creative pick-block sends the client's copy of a stack back to the server, so anything the client was never told is anything a creative player can quietly erase. The whole tag goes over the wire. Bandwidth is therefore a real question for the bank, where fifty-four of them sit in one container — not for an item in a hand. The bank ships syncing all of it, deliberately: sending less is how a creative player silently erases a horse, and the reverse of that trade is a number to measure rather than a bug to fix. The measurement is on this page's Verification tab.

Small decisions inside release

  • Position comes from the chamber, not the tag. The tag remembers where the horse was captured, which may be a world away; where you open it is where it wakes up, with its velocity zeroed.
  • It starts fed. Hunger is explicitly reset to Hunger.FULL, matching the hunger attachment's own rule that a re-summoned horse is not starving. It is also the only honest answer when the horse has been outside time.
  • A horse that will not load stays in the chamber. If load throws, nothing is consumed and the chamber is unchanged, so a later build may still be able to read it. The alternative is destroying a pedigreed animal to tidy up an exception.
What is still planned here

Passive healing from a food and water supply, the Advanced drop buffer, Spacer in-bank breeding, and the Emergency Horse Stasis Chamber that self-triggers below 10% health — everything, in other words, that needs the bank to act on a horse rather than shelve it and show it to you.

Three stages of the build order below have shipped, all on 2026-09-24: the chambers and the capture/release path everything stands on, the bank itself as a place to keep them, and its Browse tab. What follows is the rest of it, in the order it should be built — and every one of the remaining stages needs the bank to change a horse it is holding, which is the part nothing has done yet.

Passive healing from food and water

If the bank has a supply of food and water — its own small input slots, separate from the chamber grid — it slowly heals every horse stored inside it. This is the one exception to “a horse in stasis does not tick”: healing only, never hunger, growth or ageing. A stored horse is a tag rather than an entity, so this edits health inside the snapshot, and it is the stage that gives the block its first BlockEntityTicker — the research shelf's server-only createTickerHelper shape is the model.

The drop buffer, then in-bank breeding

Advanced's 9-slot milk/drops buffer lives in the bank beside the chamber grid; Spacer's in-bank breeding pairs two Spacer chambers and pops the foal out into the world. Both need the bank to act on a horse that is not a loaded entity, which is the hardest problem in the whole feature, and both are worth doing last. Both also want an affordance on the Browse tab, which exists now and gates per row already — a “collect” control on a row whose chamber answers collectsDrops(), and a pairing control on two that answer breedsInBank().

Bandwidth is the bank's problem, not the chamber's

Every chamber's component carries a whole entity tag over the wire, for the creative pick-block reason on the Coding tab. One in a hand is nothing; fifty-four syncing on container open is a number nobody has measured yet. It ships that way on purpose — sending less is how a creative player silently erases a horse — and the measurement is on the Verification tab, not a plan here.

Emergency Horse Stasis Chamber

A basic stasis chamber surrounded by ender pearls (exact shaped recipe still to decide). Held or carried passively by its owner, it watches every horse that player owns and automatically captures one into it the instant that horse's health drops below 10% — the player does nothing, the capture is the save. A horse cannot take damage while it is inside any stasis chamber, so entering one is a hard stop against dying.

Open question: what happens on a second trigger

If the emergency chamber already holds a horse and a second one crosses the 10% threshold, does the player need a second emergency chamber, does the mechanic refuse and let the second horse take its chances, or does it queue and swap at the next opportunity? This has to be settled before the item is built, alongside how “every horse that player owns” is watched at server scale — see watching health cheaply below.

Watching health cheaply, for the emergency chamber

The emergency chamber needs a per-tick check on “every horse this player owns”, which is exactly the kind of per-tick server-wide scan the whole stasis system exists to avoid doing elsewhere. In practice this only has to watch loaded, ticking horses (an unloaded one cannot be taking damage), so it is a hook on the existing damage/health-change path for owned horses, not a scan of the world. Whatever handler already watches for the intake to-do list's “buck off below 20% health” escape-behaviour entry is the natural place to add a second, lower threshold that captures instead of fleeing.

Anticipated files

What is waiting to be checked

The chambers, the bank and its Browse tab all compile, craft and pass the recipe-encoding gametest, but no horse has yet gone into one in a running game — so the whole capture/release round trip, the water-container remainder for a modded bucket, the tier upgrade carrying an occupied chamber, every one of the bank's slots, drops and sync costs, and every line the Browse tab draws are all unplayed.

The round trip, with a horse worth losing (NOT played)

The one that decides whether the feature works. Everything else on this tab is a detail beside it.

  • It is the same horse, not a lookalike. Take a tamed horse with a bond, a name you gave it, tack on it and a pregnancy in progress, capture it, walk away, relog, and let it out. Pass: all four come back, and the family tree still knows it — the Offspring tab and any stall sign bound to it are the cheap way to see that. Fail: it looks right but the pedigree has lost it, which means the UUID did not survive saveWithoutId and every pedigree, stall sign and transfer deed keyed on that id is now pointing at nothing.
  • The log agrees. Watch latest.log for the [stasis] ActionTrace line on capture. Fail: a [stasis] could not restore error on release — that branch leaves the chamber full on purpose, so the horse is not lost, but it means the tag did not read back.

The modded water container branch (NOT played, and known imperfect)

The Basic recipe returns the input's own declared crafting remainder, which a vanilla water bucket has (an empty bucket) and a modded fluid container may not. The correct answer is to drain the container through its fluid capability and return whatever that leaves — the same match-by-tag-return-drained-container problem the water troughs on compatibility.html will have to solve — and this waits for that rather than inventing a second remainder rule.

  • Which branch a modded bucket takes. Craft a Basic chamber with one in the heavily-modded server. Pass: either an empty container comes back, or the full one does. Fail: the container is consumed, since “not consumed” is the rule that matters and a full one handed back is the deliberate second-best.

Upgrading an occupied chamber (NOT played)

  • The horse crosses the upgrade. Put a horse in a Basic chamber, then craft that chamber with an eye of ender. Pass: the Intermediate still names that horse in its tooltip, and still releases it. Fail: an empty chamber comes out, which has destroyed the animal silently. Guarded in StasisUpgradeRecipe in Java rather than by a test, which is why it is worth doing once by hand.

The refusals (NOT played)

Each should produce a chat line and no mount.

  • An untamed horse, and somebody else's horse — including in creative, where there is deliberately no exception.
  • A horse with a rider on its back, since a passenger would be discarded with it.
  • A chamber that already holds one, rather than swallowing a second.
  • The creative-duplicate guard, which is the fiddly one. Copy a full chamber, release the first, then try the second. Pass: it refuses by name. Fail: a twin spawns, and two entities now share one id.

The bank, opened at all (NOT played)

It has never been placed in a running game. Craft one, place it, right-click it, and take these in one pass.

  • The window comes up, six rows of nine over your inventory, with the title on the left and a count on the right.
  • A chamber goes in and comes back out, by click and by shift-click, empty or occupied, at every tier — Basic included, since a bank that refused the cheap rung would have inverted the whole ladder.
  • A non-chamber item will not go in, by click or shift-click.
  • The count is right. empty with nothing filed, then n chambers with only empties in, then n horses of m. Fail: a wrong number means the snapshot component is not reaching the client, because the count is a client-side loop over the synced stacks rather than anything sent.
  • The one worth being paranoid about: break it with chambers inside. Pass: every chamber drops. Fail: they are gone, and a bankful of pedigreed horses went with them. Guarded by one preRemoveSideEffects override, with nothing testing it and nothing logged if it is wrong.

The Browse tab, in one pass (NOT played)

Worth doing with a bank holding at least one Basic chamber, two Intermediate or better, and one empty, so every branch of the list is on screen at once.

  • The tabs work at all. Two tabs above the window, clicking switches, the chamber grid disappears on Browse and comes back, and the bank re-opens on whichever tab was showing last. Fail: a slot still catching clicks behind the list means activeOnTab is not reaching the slots.
  • Each row says the right thing. An Intermediate+ row reads name / Mare – Bay, tobiano / Arabian, gen 3, and the name and colour agree with the Horse Browser's own row for that horse. A Basic row says a basic chamber cannot be looked into. Fail: every row saying no stable record means the roster never arrived - the screen asks for it on open, so watch for that.
  • The search is the browser's search. mare, gen>2, arabian, gene:SB1, -lethal each cut the list the way the H screen cuts it for the same horses, and the corner count turns into 3 of 9.
  • The Basic chamber never comes back from a search, not even for its own name, and the list says how many rows it could not reach. Fail: a Basic horse appearing in a filtered list is the tier ladder not being enforced; a Basic horse vanishing with nothing said is the failure the count line exists to prevent.
  • Typing does not close the window. Click into the filter box and type e, then mare. Fail: the inventory key closing the bank mid-word. Then press Esc once: it must unfocus, and again must close the window rather than trapping you in it - the bug the equestrian bench had.
  • A horse with no papers. Have a second player capture one and hand you the chamber. Pass: it lists by name, says it has no stable record, and no search returns it. This is the deliberate cost of reading the roster rather than the chamber; if it turns out to be common in play, the answer is a per-bank lookup on the server, not a lossier snapshot.

What fifty-four horses cost on the wire (never measured)

Every chamber's component carries the horse's whole entity tag, on purpose (see the Coding tab), and opening a full bank sends all fifty-four in one ClientboundContainerSetContentPacket.

  • Open a full one on a real connection. Fill a bank with occupied chambers, preferably horses with long pedigrees and full gear, and open it over the network rather than in singleplayer — singleplayer never encodes it. Fail: a lag spike, or a disconnect, on open. The answer if it is bad is not a lossy stream codec, which is how a creative player silently erases a horse; it is a smaller grid, or a paged one.

That it does not burn (reasoning unverified)

The block deliberately omits ignitedByLava(), on the reasoning that mod blocks are absent from FireBlock's flammability table unless they register themselves. That reasoning has not been checked in a game.

  • Set fire to a bank, and drop another in lava, each with a chamber inside. Pass: neither block is destroyed. Fail: it needs an explicit fire-resistance route rather than an omission — and the chambers that dropped into the fire that did it are the reason this is not a cosmetic question.
