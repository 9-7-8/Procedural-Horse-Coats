Magical gene / codominant / colours the cheek spikes, paints nothing

Cheek spike colour

What colour a horse’s cheek spikes are. The same colours and rules as horn colour (white, the rainbow, pink, black, grey or chaos), on a locus of its own. Two different copies make every spike two-tone from root to point. On a horse without cheek spikes it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

The colours, and two-tone spikes

Exactly the horn colour table. Two copies of one colour make every spike that colour, both cheeks alike. Two different ones make each spike one colour at its root and the other at its point. The root takes whichever colour comes earlier in the list, so Red/Yel is red spikes tipped in yellow, either way round. Every copy carries a little shade of its own.

Bare bone

Two Bon copies are the spikes’ bone version: bare ivory bone. No wild horse carries Bon; the skeleton horses do, so a skeleton that grows cheek spikes grows them in bone. A spike is all bone, so nothing is left off.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all. It is only a colour.

One short subclass

A key, a name, a priority and the words its outcomes are written in. Everything else is AbstractPartColourGene’s, and it uses the shared root-to-tip wording with no split. GrownParts.of puts one pair of tints on both cheeks. A spike’s base is a root and its point the last box of its branch, so a two-tone spike is exactly one box of each colour with no blend between.

Invented

Horses have no cheek spikes, so neither is their colour real. It is a separate locus from every other part’s because the owner chose one colour gene per part; a shared one would force every part on a horse to match.

What needs verifying here

Two-tone and bone spikes were seen in still frames (2026-10-09) and are unit-tested; chaos and the rest of the colour table have not been looked at on this part.

The rest of the table

Still frames (2026-10-09) showed Red/Yel spikes with red bases and yellow points, black spikes, and a skeleton’s in bone. Still to see: a chaos copy, and a dark colour on a dark coat, where a spike a few pixels across may disappear.
