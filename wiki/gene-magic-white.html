Magical gene / phase 3

Magic white

Two copies paint the whole horse white, over whatever coat it really has. One copy shows nothing. The white is painted low in the magical order, so any marking the horse also carries still draws on top of it.

Crossing two of them

What it does

  • White from nose to tail. A black horse, a cremello and a leopard all come out the same white.
  • The real coat is still there. It is only painted over, so a white horse bred to a bay can throw a bay foal that never looked white at all.
  • Markings still show. If the horse also carries a magical marking — stripes, a sigil, a pattern — it is drawn on top of the white.
  • Nothing else. No change to the eyes, the body or the appetite. The white half of the Dhampir is this gene; its red eyes, its sunburn and its strength are other loci.

About three wild horses in a hundred carry one silent copy. None carry two, so a magic white horse is always one somebody bred — or a white Dhampir.

Split out of the dhampir

This is the coat half of the old DhampirGene, which was retired on 2026-09-13 in favour of five loci the Dhampir breed puts back together with a strain. The homozygote's pixels are the old gene's white exactly; the pipeline golden moved only because the old gene also painted the eyes.

A backdrop, not a mask

The expression's tint sets every body texel to 255, 255, 255 with ColorField.set, not add — phase 3's accumulator would otherwise let a dark coat show through. But it does not declare itself masking, and it sorts at PRIORITY, below every data-driven magical gene except suit and hood. Wherever a whole-horse paint sits is where every marking above it stops being visible; sitting here means a magic white horse carrying anything else that paints still shows it. That was the owner's call for the dhampir ("so that you aren't stuck with a pure white horse"), and this gene inherited it.

Verified

Magic white on its own is a coat and nothing else (2026-09-15). Since the dhampir split it no longer carries the burning or the blood. The yard's WHITE ONLY pen keeps a hurt, hungry Wm/Wm horse beside hay and a cow for a full game day (the 08:47 run). It ate the hay at once, taking its hunger from 20 to 100, and logged no [trace] sun line, no burn and no bite all day.

Is there a real recessive white?

Not in horses. Every known white in the horse is dominant or incompletely dominant — the KIT dominant-white series, sabino, and the splashed whites — and the classic “two copies make it white” story belongs to lethal white overo, where two copies kill the foal. Recessive whites do exist in other animals: the white Leghorn chicken and some white cats and rabbits carry theirs as a recessive that switches pigment off.

So this gene is honestly magical. It borrows the inheritance pattern of those other species, and it paints rather than removes pigment — which is why it works over any coat, and why the horse underneath is still there for its foals.
