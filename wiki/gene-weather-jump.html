Magical gene / no coat effect

Weather sensitive (jump)

The same idea as the speed locus and a completely separate gene: a positive and a negative allele per weather type, moving how high the horse jumps while that weather holds. Unlinked — inheriting one tells you nothing about the other.

Built, and not yet seen in game

The gene, its inheritance and its founder table exist and are covered by the test suite; the translator that turns it into behaviour was written against the 26.1.2 sources and is flagged unverified in its own comments. Nothing on this page has been watched happening to a horse. What to look at, and what is most likely to be wrong, is on the Verification tab.

What it does

A positive allele and a negative one for each kind of weather. One copy moves jump strength by the percentage written on that copy while its weather holds; two copies add both percentages. One of each very nearly cancels.

What it is like to own

Nothing at all, most of the time. Then it rains, and the horse you have ridden for a week is a different animal. It is the only locus in the mod whose expression you cannot check on demand — you wait for weather.

Health

None directly. A badly weather-sensitive horse is slower or shorter in the jump when it rains, which is a hazard only if you were relying on it.

Correction: this is not a copy of swim speed

An earlier draft of this page said the gene was “nearly a copy” of swim speed on AbstractMagicFactorGene. That base class is strictly three alleles — one that pushes the quantity up, one that pushes it down, and the wild type — so it cannot express a positive and a negative allele per weather type. What it genuinely provides is the inheritance: codominance, a percentage drawn per allele copy, and both copies adding with the down allele counting negative.

So what it actually needs

Two options, and they are a real choice rather than a detail:

  • One locus per weather type — rain speed, storm speed, and so on — each one a genuine AbstractMagicFactorGene subclass costing almost nothing. Cheapest by far, and it multiplies the locus count by the number of weathers, twice over once the jump twin is counted.
  • One locus, several signed pairs — alleles Rain+, Rain−, Thndr+, Thndr− and the wild type, each carrying its own epigenetic delta and its own condition. This needs a generalisation of the factor gene to several conditioned pairs, which is new code — but it keeps the locus count at two and gives a genuinely interesting compound heterozygote: a Rain+/Thndr− horse is better in rain and worse in storms, and both halves apply independently because their conditions never both hold.

The second is what this page describes and what the design settled on. It is not free, and the estimate should say so.

Snow is not weather in Minecraft

There is no snowfall state to test — snow is rain in a cold biome. A snowing flag is therefore a biome lookup, not a weather one, and belongs on an interval sample rather than in a per-tick condition check.

Removal is already handled

The attribute path reconciles every tick and clearAttributes takes off any modifier the horse is no longer asking for — which is the half that would otherwise leave a rain bonus stuck on a horse standing in sunshine. Nothing new to write.

Built as a copy, on purpose

This gene and weather sensitive (speed) are the same class with a different attribute and a different key. Resisting the urge to make one parameterised locus with two outputs is deliberate: one locus would inherit as one unit, and the whole point is that they do not.

Verified

Rain moves the jump, and the speed locus with it. Measured 2026-09-13 by the census, which prints a named attribute's range across the pen: movement_speed 0.188–0.188 dry and 0.194–0.252 in rain; jump_strength 0.500–0.500 dry and 0.584–0.591 in rain. Identical when dry, because there is no modifier at all, and a range under rain, because each magnitude is drawn per allele copy. The owner had reported it as not working, and looking at the horses would have said the same: the test biome carried "has_precipitation": false, copied from the_void, so the level rained and the genes fired while nothing fell. A conditional modifier is a question about numbers, not pictures.

Two loci rather than one gene with two effects

A single weather locus moving both stats would make “good in the rain” a single thing to breed for, and one selection pressure produces one kind of horse. Two independent loci give four corners — fast and springy in a storm, fast and flat, and so on — which is four lines a breeder can keep apart.

What needs verifying here

Rain is measured to move jump_strength on weather sensitive (jump), but only one of its three weathers: the storm and snowfall pairs have never been read, and nothing has yet shown that two copies add rather than one quietly replacing the other.

Still open (NOT played)

Every check here is a number, not a feeling. The locus’s whole effect is a conditional attribute modifier, so the method is the one the Verified note used: point the census’s attribute readout at the pen, read jump_strength, change the weather, read it again. A horse looks exactly the same either way, which is why the owner’s first report of this family was “it doesn’t work” when the real fault was a test biome with no precipitation. Take a dry reading first, every time — a dry reading that is not identical across the pen means a modifier is stuck on and nothing below can be trusted.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • Two copies add. This is the page’s central claim and the thing most likely to be wrong: the gene emits one modifier per allele copy, and the attribute path names its modifier gene/<gene key>/<attribute> — one id per gene per attribute. Spawn an R+/R+ horse and an R+/n horse in one watched pen and read the range in rain. Pass: the homozygote’s value is about twice as far off the dry baseline as the heterozygote’s. Two readings that differ only by the per-copy roll mean one copy is overwriting the other, and “both percentages, added” is fiction.
  • A storm is not just rain again. raining reads isRaining and thundering reads isThundering, and a Minecraft thunderstorm is raining — so the claim that two weathers never both hold needs testing rather than believing. Spawn T+/T+ and R+/Tv horses and read the pen dry, under /weather rain, and under /weather thunder. Pass: the T+/T+ horse is at baseline in plain rain and raised in a storm; the compound horse is raised in rain and lowered in a storm. A compound horse whose storm reading equals its rain reading is both conditions holding at once with one modifier id between them.
  • The snowfall pair works at all. snowing is not a weather flag — it is raining and the biome cold enough to snow at the horse’s height, sampled on an interval and cached, and every world-sampled flag was once silently switched off for a day by a sentinel-arithmetic bug. Nothing has ever read this pair. Put an S+/S+ horse in a snowy biome and another in a warm one, and make it rain over both. Pass: the cold one is raised and the warm one is at baseline. Both at baseline means the flag is off again, and it would log nothing.
  • The modifier comes back off. /weather clear after each reading. Pass: the pen returns to exactly its dry baseline. A rain bonus left standing in sunshine is the failure the “removal is already handled” note calls impossible, and only a second dry reading disproves it.
  • The founder frequency is what the table says. About a fifth of founders carry one weather-jump allele, spread across all six variants, and none carry two. Pass: over a lot of wild horses the genotype codes show roughly that share, all six tokens turn up, and no horse is homozygous. Rv and its siblings are worth looking for specifically: a “suffers in” allele used to write a genotype code its own parser could not read back, and that bug needed a founder to actually roll one before anything noticed.
  • The two loci really are unlinked. Separate genes, separate founder tables. Pass: across wild horses, what a horse carries here predicts nothing about what it carries on weather sensitive (speed).

In the game, with your hands

  • Is the magnitude noticeable at all? The percentages are a first guess, and this is the only part of the locus a number cannot settle. Ride an R+/R+ horse at a fence it can only just clear when dry, then make it rain. Pass: you can tell which weather you are in without reading the attribute. If you cannot, the mean delta is too small, and that is the finding.
