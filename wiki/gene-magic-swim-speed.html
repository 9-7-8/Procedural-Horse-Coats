Magical gene / no coat effect

Magic swim speed

How fast the horse moves in water, and nothing else. It is unrelated to magic speed — not related-but-separate, unrelated — so a horse can be the slowest thing in the paddock and still cross a river faster than a boat.

Crossing two of them

What it does

A horse with the otterlike allele swims faster than it has any business doing. A horse with the stonelike allele crosses water at a crawl. Neither is any different on dry land, which is the entire point of the locus: the thing you bred for is invisible until the horse is in a river.

Both copies count, and they add. One otterlike copy is worth about a quarter more; two are worth about half again, and rather more when both copies happened to roll well. One of each very nearly cancels — the horse swims at about the usual pace while carrying, and passing on, both extremes.

What you can catch

About a quarter of wild horses carry one copy, split evenly between the two alleles. No wild horse carries two of anything, so every genuinely remarkable swimmer in a world is one somebody bred.

Health

None. This locus has no bearing on how long the horse survives underwater — that is magic water breathing, and the two are independent. A horse that crosses a lake quickly and one that can stay at the bottom of it are two different breeding projects.

Verified

It scales how hard a horse pushes through water, loose or ridden - after two rewrites (2026-09-15, formerly gap 249).

  • As built: it multiplied water_movement_efficiency, whose base is 0 on a horse. The yard read base 0.000 value 0.000 on every genotype, so no horse swam any differently.
  • The owner's call: scale in-water movement, on the server and the rider's client alike.
  • First try, a velocity nudge that inferred each tick's push from the velocity: it amplified noise. An Otter horse barely pushing drifted seven times as far as a plain one.
  • What ships: the Java-only ability GeneAbility.Swim(factor) and mixin/HorseSwimMixin, which multiplies the push strength vanilla's LivingEntity.travelInWater hands to moveRelative. A horse that is not pushing is not moved. Vanilla runs that method on whichever side moves the horse, so a ridden horse is scaled on its rider's client, where the factor comes from ClientSwimHandler and the client record cache.
Evidence, the 10:19 run: the SWIM SPEED pen's three horses swim eight five-second laps of walled channels, steered by their move control.
  • Stne/Stne (factor 0.62) covered 1.83 blocks and plain covered 2.90, a ratio of 0.63 - proportional.
  • Otr/Otr (factor 1.56) covered 21.87 blocks, about 7.5 times plain.
The Otter overshoot is most likely because a horse steered by its AI pushes so weakly that a plain one barely gets going (0.37 blocks in five seconds), while the Otter's extra push gets it swimming. That is an inference, not measured. How it feels under a rider, whose push is far larger, is a tester's check (for testers).

Not a body stat, and deliberately so

The four magical body-stat genes push their sum into TraitBuilder, and end up as one of the four numbers on Traits. This gene does not, and neither does water breathing.

Traits is a pure function of the genotype — four numbers and a list of conditions, re-derived wherever they are needed, stored nowhere, and meant to survive a version port unchanged. Swimming speed is not among those four and should not become the fifth: it means nothing without a running game, which is precisely the line GeneAbility exists on. Adding a field to Traits for every magical knob would drag Minecraft-shaped concepts into the one record that is supposed to be portable.

So the genetics are shared and the sink is not. AbstractMagicFactorGene is AbstractMagicStatGene with the last step replaced: the same three alleles, the same per-copy percentage on its own epigenetic seed, the same signed sum — and then a GeneAbility rather than a number.

common/genetics/genes/MagicSwimSpeedGene.java
@Override
protected List<GeneAbility> abilitiesFor(double factor) {
    // A swim nudge, not an attribute (owner, 2026-09-15, gap 249). The attribute this
    // multiplied, water_movement_efficiency, has a base of 0 - the yard's SWIM SPEED pen
    // read 0.000 on every genotype, so no horse ever swam faster or slower.
    return List.of(new GeneAbility.Swim(factor, GeneAbility.Condition.ALWAYS, 1));
}

There is no swim_speed attribute

However much the name suggests itself, vanilla has no such attribute. What it has is water_movement_efficiency — the share of its land speed a mob keeps in water, which is Depth Strider’s attribute and sounds like exactly what “how fast does this horse swim” means. It is not, on a horse: its base is 0, so multiplying it multiplies nothing, which is why this gene no longer uses it (Verified).

The effect format used to offer swim_speed in its attribute list. It was never translated to anything, because there was nothing to translate it to; the list now names the attributes that exist, and the translator’s table is the same set. That table is closed on purpose, so a gene naming something this build has never heard of gets one log line rather than a silent no-op.

Taking the modifier off again

This is how the attribute path works generally — it is no longer this gene’s path, but it is the machinery every attribute gene beside it uses, and the removal half is the part that is easy to get wrong.

The modifier is transient and its id is derived from the gene and the attribute, so re-applying it every tick is idempotent. The removal half is the interesting one: when an ability’s condition goes false it is skipped before it reaches the apply path, so nothing would ever take the modifier off. GeneAbilityHandler.clearAttributes therefore runs unconditionally after the ability loop and strips every modifier in the mod’s namespace that the horse is not currently asking for.

Why land speed and swimming are two loci

Because they compose differently, and folding them together would have made one of the two wrong.

Land speed has three natural loci under it — MSTN, PDK4 and CKM — and the magical version multiplies whatever they settled on, so a magically fast pony stays slower than a magically fast draught horse. That is the right behaviour and it depends entirely on there being something underneath to scale.

Swimming has nothing underneath it. No natural locus in the mod makes one horse a better swimmer than another, so this gene is not scaling anything; it is the whole of the fact. Its percentages are correspondingly larger (mean 25% rather than 10%) because a quarter of nothing-in-particular is a smaller claim than a quarter of a bred racehorse.

What it does to breeding

It adds an axis that nothing else in the mod is correlated with. A breeder who wants a horse that is fast on land and in water has to select for two independent loci at once, and neither one is visible — which is the same shape as the body-stat genes and, like them, is meant to make a finished animal an achievement rather than a catch.

What needs verifying here

Magic swim speed is measured on loose horses steered by their own AI; the ridden path is not, and there the factor comes off the client’s record cache and falls back to 1.0 for any horse whose record has not arrived — which looks exactly like a horse with no gene.

Still open (NOT played)

Vanilla runs travelInWater on whichever side moves the horse, so a ridden horse is scaled on its rider’s client by ClientSwimHandler, which reads ClientHorseRecordCache and returns 1.0 for a horse it has no record for. That is the one path the SWIM SPEED pen cannot exercise, and its failure mode is silence. The pen’s own lap log is the yardstick for everything below: a plain horse covered 2.90 blocks in five seconds, Stne/Stne 1.83 and Otr/Otr 21.87.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • Otr/Stne cancels. Put one in the SWIM SPEED pen’s channel and read the lap log. Pass: its blocks-per-lap sits with the plain horse’s, not with either extreme — the sum is signed, so a horse carrying both should swim ordinarily while throwing both.
  • The pen’s Otter overshoot has an explanation. Otr/Otr covered about 7.5 times the plain distance on a factor of 1.56, which the Verified note ascribes to an AI-steered plain horse barely pushing at all — stated there as an inference, not a measurement. Pass: under a rider, whose push is far larger, the same pair of horses comes out near the factor rather than near 7×. If the ratio stays at 7× when both are pushing hard, the mixin is amplifying something other than the push.

In the game, with your hands

  • A ridden otter horse outswims a ridden plain one. Same start, same stretch of deep water, both ridden. Pass: the otter horse is clearly ahead. If the two are identical, the client had no record for the horse and every ridden horse in the world swims like a plain one.
  • It survives a reload. Leave the world, come back, and ride the same Otr/Otr horse straight into the water. Pass: the same speed as before — the record has to have reached the client again before it matters, and a one-off first-dive failure here is the kind of thing that reads as imagination.
