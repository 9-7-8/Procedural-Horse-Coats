To be verified

The rolling checklist of what is built but not yet confirmed. Defects live on known gaps, not here. Confirmed items are deleted, not ticked, and what was verified, when and on what evidence goes on the coding tab of that thing's own page (hard rule 9).

How this page is ordered: "does it look right" goes LAST

Owner's rule, 2026-09-13. Rank every open test by whether it can be wrong invisibly. A gene that paints wrongly announces itself the moment anybody looks at it. A gene that behaves wrongly, damages a save, costs tick time, or silently does nothing does not — and that is what her time in the game is worth spending on.

The tell is that six visual confirmations were cleared in about twenty minutes on the day this rule was written, while the holy ward needed a warded chamber and an unwarded control thirty blocks from where she stood, the weather loci needed an attribute readout built first, and the lava speed needed a log line before anyone could tell a dead feature from a slow one. Cheap checks should not occupy the top of a list that exists to spend the scarcest thing in the project.

So: behavioural, structural and unattended tests first; "look at it and say" last, and retired outright the moment it is confirmed.

§0-FZ — Jumps, and how a horse jumps (2026-09-20, PARTLY played)

A day's work in one entry, because it all landed together: a per-wood jump block in three styles, and a rewrite of how a ridden horse jumps at all. See jumps.

Confirmed in play, 2026-09-20 — do not re-test

  • The fence collision. A jump stops a horse instead of being stepped over. Owner: it “now actually feels like a challenge”. The first build let collision follow the model and a horse walked over a single jump.
  • The look, and rows. They “look great, expand great” — including the row-of-three case that was broken before release, where the middle block pointed at a model nobody had written.
  • The T-post on verticals, every third block along a run.
  • Instant jump and the draining meter. Press to leap at full height, cooldown drains over about a second. Owner: “the jump meter decays and everything looks perfect”.
  • Gliding still works with the new jump system, and feels good — the one thing the charge removal was most likely to break, since the glide allele is launched by a jump.

Built since that session and NOT played

  • Oxer and crossrails. Two rails with a spread, and two poles crossed in an X. Look at a crossrail facing all four ways: it is the one style that is not UV-locked, because its elements are themselves rotated 45°, and the argument for that is reasoning about the renderer rather than something anybody has seen.
  • Every style in the creative tab. Only the vertical was there at first. Check all three of a wood appear, grouped together.
  • Breaking an oxer gives back an oxer, not a vertical. The loot table is a switch on the style property; before, the style was silently lost.
  • Stacks inherit facing from the jump below, so a two-high fence cannot come out crosswise. Style is deliberately not inherited — an oxer held over a vertical should still place an oxer.
  • The jump’s screen — the whole migration, and none of it is played. Right-click a placed jump. Two plank slots and three style buttons; the current style’s button is greyed out. Drop a plank in the rails slot: that half changes wood and a plank of the old wood arrives in your inventory. Do the same to the standards with a different wood and look at the block — the rails and the uprights should be visibly different woods, which is the whole point of the rework and has never been seen. Then check the T-post takes the standards’ wood, not the rails’.
  • The break-and-replace round trip. Mine a two-wood jump and put it back: it must come back in both its woods and its own style. The item’s name should read “Oak & Birch Oxer”. This is four pieces of machinery in a row (see the page) and any one of them failing looks identical: the jump comes back oak.
  • Stacking still beats the screen. Holding a jump and right-clicking a jump must place, not open the window. This needed an explicit override and is the one regression that would make the block unusable; check it first.
  • The creative tab has three things in it — an oak vertical, oxer and crossrail — where it had thirty-six. Then check the icons track the wood: craft or swap a jump to birch and look at it in a slot. That is a minecraft:select on a component and no vanilla file uses that property, so its JSON shape was read off the codecs rather than copied from a shipped example; a wrong guess shows as every wood drawing as oak.
  • An oxer beside a vertical keeps both sets of standards, and restyling one in the middle of a run grows its own standards back straight away. Only same-style jumps share a rail now.
  • The recipe is 3 fences of one wood + 1 horse hair → 4 of that wood. Twelve recipes now make one item and the wood arrives as a component. Craft spruce and check you get spruce, not oak. Mixed woods still do not craft, which is now deliberate rather than pending.
  • Both families still mine with an axe. The gates and the jumps share mineable/axe and both bakers merge into it. A regression is invisible — the block still breaks, just slowly and with no drop — so check a gate and a jump on purpose.
  • Modded woods need a second mod installed and have never been generated. Wood.fenceId() and the plank id are both composed rather than scanned, so an unusually-named wood gets a jump that cannot be crafted or repainted, and a warning.

The measurement nobody has taken

Which rung of a stack a given horse stops at, and whether it agrees with the metres on the horse screen. This is the whole reason the family was built before the prettier obstacle types: HorseUnits.jumpMetres is an unverified cubic, and a stack of jumps is the first instrument that can test it. It has already paid for itself once — see gap 287, where the Anglo-Arabian turned out to jump high enough to hurt itself landing.

§0-FX — Horses eat other mods' hay bales (2026-09-19, NOT played, needs a second mod installed)

A horse now grazes, heals beside, and can be hand-fed any bale in horsegenetics:hay_bales — vanilla's, plus Bale of Sugar Cane's as an optional entry. None of it has been seen in a game with that mod installed; the ids were read out of its jar, not observed. See compatibility § bale mods.

With no second mod installed — do these first, they need nothing but the dev client

This is the one that matters most and it is the regression check, not the feature: grazing hay stopped being a hardcoded block and became a tag lookup, so if the tag is wrong every horse silently stops eating hay everywhere. With Bale of Sugar Cane installed — the actual feature, none of it ever run

  • Does the mod even load beside this one? Nothing has confirmed the two coexist. Do this before judging anything below.
  • A hungry horse should walk to a sugar cane bale and eat it, at the same priority as hay — above crops, below cake.
  • Hand one to a hurt tamed horse. Expect 20 health, the bale consumed, the eat sound, and bond. A foal should age up 180 seconds with the happy-villager particles.
  • If nothing at all happens, the id is wrong, not the logic — that is the designed-for failure and it is silent by intent. Confirm with /data get or by checking the item's id in F3+H, and compare against data/horsegenetics/tags/item/hay_bales.json.
  • A wheat-diet horse should take a sugar cane bale and an eat-anything horse too. A horse on any other special diet should refuse it with the usual turned-away message — that path is HorseDietHandler's and the risk is the two handlers both firing, which would show as a double heal.
  • Stand a hurt horse beside a sugar cane bale and a water source. Gated healing should tick, the same as beside hay.

§0-FS — Speed and jump in metres on the horse screen (2026-09-18, NOT played)

The information screen printed the raw attributes — 0.188 and 0.50 — which mean nothing to a player. It now prints m/s and the height the horse can get onto, converted by common/trait/HorseUnits. HorseUnitsTest pins the curves against every metre figure already quoted elsewhere in the mod, so what is left to check is whether the numbers describe the animal in front of you.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

§0-FO — Horse-drawn carts (2026-09-18, NOT played, nothing here has been seen)

Six vehicles, 68 ported classes, seven new mixins and a new draught model, and not one of it has been run in a game. The upstream port it came from had never been compiled against the game jars either, so the whole thing is build-green-only. Carts is the page. Start at the top — if the first two fail nothing below them is testable.

  • The game starts. Seven mixins were added to horsegenetics.mixins.json, and it grew a "client" block for the first time. A mixin apply failure or a client class on the server is a hard crash at mod load, so runServer to Done ( before anything else.
  • 85 items in a Horse Carts creative tab. Twelve woods × six vehicles, plus the cart wheel. Any purple icon is a missing model.
  • A placed cart is the right wood. No cart texture ships — each is stitched from that wood’s block sprites at resource-reload time. A purple-black cart means the assembler did not run; a cart that is the wrong wood means the sprite table is wrong. Check a nether one (crimson is a stem) and bamboo (a block), which are the irregular rows.
  • Hitch and unhitch. Place a supply cart, ride a saddled horse near it, press R: attach sound, and the horse pulls it. R again to detach.
  • A strong horse is visibly faster than a weak one on the same cart. This is the whole feature. Two horses with clearly different pull scores, the same wagon, the same stretch of ground. If they look the same, applyDraught is not firing — check the horse’s movement-speed modifiers.
  • A fast weak horse and a slow strong one are close, and a balanced one beats both. The design claim, and the one worth measuring rather than eyeballing. The unit test pins the arithmetic; this checks the arithmetic was wired to the right numbers.
  • Detaching gives the speed back. The modifier is transient and removed on unhitch. A horse that stays slow after unhitching is a leak, and it compounds every re-attach.
  • Two riders on a pull-6 horse, and only one below it. Both the threshold and the seating — the pillion offset is a number picked to look right on vanilla’s model, never measured, so the second rider may well be inside the first.
  • The wagon’s box seat drives the horse. Wired on 2026-09-19 and never run — until then the wagon was the one vehicle that never spawned a postilion, so the seat did nothing at all while the tooltip said it drove. The animal cart and the reaper have always had the same three lines, so if those two steer and the wagon does not, the difference is the wagon’s four seats: it is the first passenger who drives, and a wagon boarded by an animal first may hand the reins to nobody. This is also the path most likely to fight this mod’s own six AbstractHorse mixins. While you are there: does a horse still fly, swim and cross lava? CartHorseMixin overrides travel, and the argument that it does not break HorseFlightMixin is an argument, not an observation.
  • The plow tills, the seed drill plants, the reaper harvests. Tools go inside the plow; the reaper needs a player sitting on it. Craft the reaper first and confirm it is there at all — its recipe had a two-wide row in a three-wide pattern, so the game threw out all twelve woods' files at datapack load and it has been uncraftable since it shipped. Fixed in v0.5.014 and the twelve files now parse on a server boot, but no reaper has been made in a grid.
  • Wagon storage grows with chests, and five carpets make a roof. 9×4 → 9×8 → 12×9. Contents must survive a relog.
  • A loaded cart is slower than an empty one, and it changes while you stand there. New on 2026-09-19 and the whole point of the cargo rule. Hitch an empty supply cart to a horse, note the pace, then fill it from a chest without unhitching: within a second it should be visibly harder work, and emptying it should give the pace back. The same for a wagon as chests go on, and for the animal cart as a second cow climbs in. If nothing changes, refreshDraught is not being reached — it runs from pulledTick, so a cart that is standing still may simply never be asked. A plow must not change when you rack three tools in it.
  • A train of carts costs what its parts cost. Hitch a cart behind a cart behind a horse and load the back one. The horse should slow for cargo it is not touching; if it does not, currentLoad is not walking the drawn chain and a trailer is a free ride.
  • The info screen’s two wagon lines differ. Overview → Draught now shows what the horse keeps empty and loaded to the roof. Two identical percentages mean the screen is reading the bare load twice.
  • The cart tooltip shows two numbers on a carrying vehicle and one on an implement. Shift on a supply cart, a wagon and an animal cart: empty and full. Shift on a plow, reaper or seed drill: one line only, because their load does not move.
  • The supply cart shows its cargo, and a banner flies from a supply cart, animal cart or wagon.
  • “Hauled” climbs on the info screen and is still there after a relog. It is a synced attachment, so a figure that reads zero on the client while the horse has clearly worked is a sync fault rather than a counting one.
  • Recipes appear in the recipe book, and the cart wheel is cart_wheel, not wheel.
  • A dedicated server plus a client. Carts sync, and no client-only class on the server.

Modded woods are a separate session and need another mod installed — see gap 277, which is the one that could crash rather than merely look wrong, and gap 276 for the oak inventory icon that is expected.

§0-FN — Cloudtouched, a flying breed (2026-09-18, NOT played)

A second magical breed, Cloudtouched, is registered and bakes clean, and not one has ever existed in a world. It is the first breed to pin flying, the first to pin bird-boned, and the first to pin the weather loci, so several of these checks are the first time that code has met a breed pool at all.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • It spawns at all. very_rare is 0.75 against a moderate breed's 6.0, and weight is a share within one biome, not an absolute rate. The thinnest biome it lists is cherry_grove (two other breeds), which is where one should turn up soonest; meadow has ninety and is where it should be near-invisible. §0-ES — Roan grows corn spots (2026-09-17, NOT played)

Roan now leaves corn spots — islands of solid base colour inside the roan field, made by suppressing the whitening rather than painting anything over it. A minority trait by design: the propensity is rolled on the copy the way the forehead white is, so most roans have none.

  • Most roans still have none. This is the claim most likely to be wrong and the cheapest to check: spawn a batch of roans and confirm that clean ones clearly outnumber spotted ones. If every roan is spotted the propensity roll is being ignored, and the horse you get is a much rarer animal than the one intended.
  • The spots are islands, not thinning. A corn spot is base coat at full strength with a definite edge, not a patch where the roaning merely fades. A roan with soft pale-grey blotches instead of crisp coloured islands means the suppression is partial where it should be total.
  • They inherit. The lattice seed and the propensity sit on the Rn copy, so a spotted roan's foals that inherit that copy should be spotted too, in the same places. A foal whose spots are unrelated to its parent's means the values are being re-rolled rather than passed on.
  • They do not appear off the roan field. Corn spots only exist where roan was going to put white, so the dark head, the solid mane and tail and the dark lower legs must be unaffected — a "spot" on the head is the mask being applied outside its region.
  • Lowest priority, purely visual: whether the spots read as the real thing at riding distance rather than as damage or as holes in the texture, and whether their size looks right against the horse.

§0-ER — Silver finally draws its dapples (2026-09-17, NOT played)

Checked against an offline render rather than by asking for game time, but a render is a still image of one horse — what it cannot show is the population, which is where the remaining risk is.

  • The dapple strength varies between horses, and reaches zero. Strength is an epigenetic value on the allele running the full range, so some silvers should barely dapple and some should show it clearly. If every silver horse dapples identically the epigenetics are not being read per allele — which is the specific failure this design invites, and the one a single render cannot detect.

  • A chestnut silver still shows almost nothing. The dapple modulates how much black a texel keeps, so on a red horse there is nothing for it to act on. A visibly dappled chestnut would mean the modulation is touching red.
  • Grey and silver together do not fight. A horse carrying both has two dapple lattices at different depths and spacings. They should read as one coherently dappled horse, not as interference or moiré.
  • Lowest priority, purely visual: whether the dappling reads as dappling at riding distance rather than as spots or as noise, and whether the paler mane still reads as flaxen rather than as white.
