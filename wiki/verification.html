To be verified

The rolling checklist of what is built but not yet confirmed. Defects live on known gaps, not here. Confirmed items are deleted, not ticked, and what was verified, when and on what evidence goes on the coding tab of that thing's own page (hard rule 9).

How this page is ordered: "does it look right" goes LAST

Owner's rule, 2026-09-13. Rank every open test by whether it can be wrong invisibly. A gene that paints wrongly announces itself the moment anybody looks at it. A gene that behaves wrongly, damages a save, costs tick time, or silently does nothing does not — and that is what her time in the game is worth spending on.

The tell is that six visual confirmations were cleared in about twenty minutes on the day this rule was written, while the holy ward needed a warded chamber and an unwarded control thirty blocks from where she stood, the weather loci needed an attribute readout built first, and the lava speed needed a log line before anyone could tell a dead feature from a slow one. Cheap checks should not occupy the top of a list that exists to spend the scarcest thing in the project.

So: behavioural, structural and unattended tests first; "look at it and say" last, and retired outright the moment it is confirmed.

§0-EN — Dyeable leather horse armour (2026-09-16, NOT played)

Check the player’s leather armour first, before anything horse-shaped

There is no leather_horse_armor.json. The asset is equipment/leather.json, and that one file carries horse_body, humanoid, humanoid_baby and humanoid_leggings together — leather horse armour and every leather chestplate, helmet, legging and boot in the game read it. Our override rewrites horse_body only and copies the other three verbatim, the way saddle.json leaves camels and striders alone. So put on a leather chestplate, dye one in a crafting grid, and confirm both look exactly as they always have. If the player’s armour changed, the override reached further than intended and that is far more serious than anything below.

The bench now takes leather horse armour as well as a saddle, and the three row labels change with it — Seat / Bridle / Fittings for a saddle, Blanket / Trim / Fittings for armour, and the saddle’s words when the slot is empty. Shift-clicking either from the inventory should land it in the top-left slot. Check the labels actually swap when you drop armour in, since an empty bench shows the saddle’s wording and the swap is the easy thing to get wrong.

Vanilla’s crafting-grid dye must still work, and must still colour only the blanket. This is the one that could quietly get worse than vanilla rather than merely broken: vanilla reads a stack’s dye once and hands it to every dyeable layer, so our two extra layers would take it too and a grid-dyed armour would go one flat colour over trim and buckles alike. Those two layers are pinned to their own undyed constants when no bench tint is present. Dye an armour in a grid and confirm the straps stay brown and the hardware stays grey. Check the icon too — a grid-dyed piece should still show its colour in the hotbar.

Then the two together, in that order, which is the check most likely to catch something. Dye an armour in a crafting grid, take that piece to the bench, and recolour only the trim. The blanket must keep its grid colour. This was a real defect found by reading the committed code rather than by playing: the bench assumed any armour without its own tint was plain brown, so editing one row wrote the blanket back to leather and destroyed the player’s dye silently. It now seeds the blanket from dyed_color. Worth confirming precisely because the failure is invisible — you get a result, it just is not the one you asked for.

An undyed leather horse armour must be indistinguishable from before. Same guarantee as the saddle and the same arithmetic behind it: vanilla’s leather_overlay.png was split three ways — 417 texels of brown trim, 79 of metal, 92 of near-black shadow that is deliberately not dyeable — and the bake asserts zero orphans, no clamping and a worst round-trip error of 1. The shadow stays untinted on purpose: it reads as depth rather than as material, and tinting it is how dyed tack starts looking like a flat sticker.

The split was measured, not reasoned, and two guesses died on the way: that the dark greys were outlines (they are not — the bag outlines are dark brown), and that hardware was grey above 0x3E (that threshold cuts each metal bar in half). The boundary is 0x20, where thresholds plateau and every bar comes out solid.

Two more fittings have never been rendered either: lapis lazuli, which fills the blue the wheel completely lacked, and coal for a true black — netherite is the darkest of the old set but is really a warm plum, so check the two read as different materials rather than as one.

§0-EM — A dyeable saddle: what is still unseen (2026-09-16)

What has been checked already: a dedicated server boots clean on this work — Done (0.289s), no errors or exceptions, 194 genes and 122 breeds registered. That matters because the names drop-in runs from the mod constructor, which is the one place a change fails only at load. It also caught a real bug, a warning about our own README that would have appeared on every boot. What a server boot cannot tell you is anything below, because none of it is rendering.

The saddle is dyeable now, and an undyed one should be indistinguishable from before. That is the part worth checking hardest, because it is what keeps every other mob looking untouched: the textures are derived from vanilla by dividing out the leather tint, and both the worn saddle and the inventory icon were verified to recompose to vanilla with zero differing pixels. So a plain saddle on a donkey, a mule, a skeleton horse or a zombie horse should look exactly as it always has. If any of them looks pale, washed out or grey, the tint is not being applied and the base texture is showing raw — that is the failure mode to watch for.

Camels, pigs, striders and nautiluses were deliberately left alone. Our equipment asset carries vanilla's entry verbatim for those five layer types, so they should be untouched. Worth a glance at a strider or a saddled pig to confirm the override did not reach further than intended.

There is a crafting route now, and it is the whole point: the Equestrian Bench. Craft one, or find the one standing at a cowboy’s homestead past the horseman’s table — the generated one has never been seen, so confirm it is actually there and not buried in a wall or floating. Then dye a saddle’s seat, bridle and fittings in turn and check all three survive on the horse independently.

The bench itself is confirmed and has moved to its own page — colours apply, the three zones are independent, the window, the name field, the tooltip and the icon tint all work, and the brightened dyes were checked against vanilla’s dyed leather. What is left here is only what has never been looked at.

Five fittings have never been rendered: redstone, ender pearl, basalt, bone and prismarine. The three worth actually comparing side by side are the pairs that were deliberately pulled apart rather than set to their material’s own colour — basalt against netherite, bone against quartz, and prismarine against ender pearl. If either half of a pair is not obviously its own colour on the horse, the separation was not enough and the constant should move further, not the material be dropped. Note also that the fittings tooltip now consults the materials before the dyes, so redstone must read “Fittings: redstone” and never “Fittings: red”.

And the horseman’s withdrawn dyed-saddle trade is now unblocked. It was pulled because vanilla did not tint saddles, so it sold a fourteen-emerald item identical to the eight-emerald one. That is no longer true. Reinstating it is a decision rather than a check, so it is not listed as something to look at — but the reason it was withdrawn has expired.

§0-EL — Cowboys with a country, and four tack trades (2026-09-15, NOT played)

Nothing in this section has been seen in a world. It is green on the bench — :neoforge-26.1.2:build, RegionTest, the designer boot check — and every claim below is read off the source.

The cowboy now comes from somewhere. Find a plains village barn and read his name against his string: a man selling Fjords should be a Halvorsen, one selling Andalusians an Olivares. The whole paddock should be from one region once he is founded — the first horse sets where he is from, and every horse after it is drawn from that region. What to watch for is a mismatch: a Scandinavian name over a yard of Quarter Horses means CowboyHandler.found is naming before it picks, which is the exact bug the reordering fixed.

The horseman should share the cowboy's region, not just his surname. Put a Horseman's Table near an existing barn and check his first name suits the cowboy’s region as well as matching his family. Then the harder path: a table in a village with no cowboy at all, where he is the founder and his region is rolled. Both go through HorsemanHandler.regionIdNear.

Three new trades — leather horse armour at tier 1, a dyed leather horse armour at 2, golden horse armour at 4. Each tier still draws three of its pool, so they will not all show at once; level a horseman and watch the pools rather than expecting a fixed shop. The dyed armour is the one worth looking at: sixteen possible colours, rolled once per restock, so it should hold its colour in the window rather than shimmering under the cursor.

A fourth trade, a dyed saddle, was written and withdrawn the same day — vanilla does not tint saddles, so it sold an invisible upgrade. That is settled from the equipment-asset source rather than pending a look; the reasoning is on the villagers page.

The phc/names/ drop-in folder has never been exercised in game. Three things to check, in order of how quietly they fail. First, that the folder is made on a first launch with a README in it, the way phc/breeds/ is — it is created from the mod constructor, so a permissions problem there is logged and otherwise invisible. Second, that a file dropped in actually widens a region: put one name in scandinavia-beta.txt, restart, and found enough cowboys in a Scandinavian biome to see it. Third, the failure path — misspell a region in a file name and confirm the log says so rather than swallowing it. Unit tests cover the parsing and the typo reporting; what they cannot cover is whether the folder is in the place the player is told to look.

§0-EJ — Fourteen more breeds out of intake/, and a changed American Paint (2026-09-15, NOT played)

American Saddlebred, Newfoundland Pony, Nokota, Nonius, Novooleksandrivsky Heavy Draught, Oldenburg, Orlov Trotter, Pindos, Pony of the Americas, Riwoche Horse, Rocky Mountain Horse, Sanhe, Selle Français, Sorraia. Green on the bench: :neoforge-26.1.2:build, BreedFilesTest, BreedSpecParserTest, check-links, build-search-index and the designer boot check. None of these fourteen has been seen in a world, and neither has the changed Paint.

  • Confirm no Oldenburg or Selle Français carries Friesian dwarfism. This is the first check in the batch because it is the one that would be invisible and catastrophic. Both drafts named their WFFS locus PLOD1, which is a real gene symbol and is also the key this mod uses for Friesian dwarfism. A file that had pasted it would parse, load, warn about nothing, and seed the wrong lethal into two sport horses. The files name no PLOD1 pool at all — check a spread of founder genomes and confirm the locus reads clear, then breed a pair repeatedly and confirm no dwarf foal ever appears.
  • The American Paint's HYPP rate moved and it is the one change to a breed that already existed. It went from the generic 1.2% carrier figure to the study's 0.025 allele frequency — roughly 4.9% of founders, about four times as many. HYPP is dominant, so every carrier is affected and visible: a Paint herd should now contain noticeably more unwell horses than before. If it does not, the pool is not reaching the founder; if it contains far more than one in twenty, something is reading the weight as a percentage of the wrong total. This is the single most likely thing in the batch to be wrong in a way nobody would think to look at.
  • Five must never head a wild herd — Nonius, Novooleksandrivsky Heavy Draught, Oldenburg, Orlov Trotter and Selle Français, all managed studbook breeds with a spawn checklist. Three more have wild herds but no cowboy — Newfoundland Pony, Pindos and Sanhe, plus the Sorraia: they should turn up in the world but never in a trader's offers. That is eight source checklists, the most in any batch so far.
  • Every Pony of the Americas must be spotted, with no exceptions at all. It is the first breed in the mod where LP is fixed — no founder is ever lp/lp. Spawn a large number: a single solid, unspotted POA means a fixed pool is not holding, which is the same failure class as a bay Kladruber. Both strains should be visible in bulk, bold blankets and leopards against subtler varnish and snowflake.
  • A pure POA must also never be pinto, and that is a separate failure. The registry bars it, so tobiano, sabino, frame, splash and dominant white are all forced wild. A nearly white few-spot POA is correct and is not a pinto; a POA with smooth rounded patches crossing the topline is a bug. The two are easy to confuse by eye, which is why it is written down.
  • The Nokota is a lethal-white breed and must never throw tobiano. Breeding two frame carriers should be able to go wrong, exactly as in an American Paint herd. Tobiano is the opposite check: the Conservancy's own line is that it only enters by crossbreeding, so a tobiano Nokota out of two pure parents means a forced-wild locus is leaking.
  • The Saddlebred's gait locus is intermediate, not near-fixed, and that is deliberate. Every other gaited breed here sits near 90% A/A; this one should be about a quarter A/A, half A/C and a quarter C/C, because only some Saddlebreds are five-gaited. Check the genotype spread rather than the animation, which does not exist.
  • The Sorraia and the Riwoche Horse must be dun every time, and must never grey. Both fix D in every founder, joining the Henson and the Konik. Both also force grey wild for the Konik's reason — their colour is called "mouse grey" in the old language and STX17 would whiten the one thing either looks like. A greying Sorraia is the specific failure. The Sorraia should be mostly mouse dun and grullo with a bay-dun minority; the Riwoche almost entirely bay dun.
  • The size curve, checked at both ends this time. The Pony of the Americas is the tidiest case in the mod because its registry states the band in inches — 46 to 56, so no founder should fall outside roughly 11.2 to 14 hands. At the other end the Oldenburg and the Selle Français are among the tallest riding horses in the book: stand one of each beside a Hanoverian and a Shire and confirm they land between them and not past the Shire.
  • The Rocky Mountain Horse's chocolate must be silver on black, not flaxen on chestnut. A black-based horse carrying Z should read as dark chocolate with a pale mane and tail; a flaxen chestnut is a different horse and must not be mistaken for it, and a chestnut carrying Z should show almost nothing. Silver is at its highest rate of any breed here, so the batch will produce plenty of both to compare. MCOA is not modelled, so no Z/Z horse should show any eye effect — that is correct today and is on the roadmap.
  • Lowest priority, purely visual: whether the Nokota's blue roan actually reads as smoke with a dark head rather than as a washed-out black; whether the Nonius's dark-skewed shade pool makes a visibly darker herd than an ordinary bay breed, since that pool is standing in for a band the format does not have; whether the Riwoche and the Sorraia are distinguishable from the Konik at a glance, all three being fixed-dun primitives; and whether the Saddlebred's "any colour" pool reads as varied rather than incoherent.

  • The Jutland should read as flaxen chestnut, never palomino. Cream is forced wild, so a pale-maned Jutland is always a red horse with a light mane; anything that looks golden-bodied is cream leaking in from somewhere.
  • The Karakachan is the most visually unified natural breed in the mod — almost every founder bay or brown. A chestnut or black one is roughly a one-in-two-hundred event, so several in a herd means the near-fixed pools are not holding.
  • Lowest priority, purely visual: whether the Mongolian's dun-heavy steppe pool reads as varied rather than muddy; whether the Montenegrin and the Domestic Mountain Pony are actually distinguishable side by side (grey is the only difference, and that may not be enough); and whether the Mangalarga Marchador's heavy grey plus pinto pool is too loud in a herd.

§0-EG — Five more breeds out of intake/ (2026-09-15, NOT played)

Colorado Ranger, Criollo, Croatian Coldblood, Danubian, Domestic Mountain Pony. Green on the bench: :neoforge-26.1.2:build, BreedFilesTest, BreedSpecParserTest, check-parity, check-links and the designer boot check. Every gene pool of all five was confirmed to survive the bake round-trip pair by pair (20, 17, 11, 7 and 6 pools), because a bad allele token is warned and dropped — a breed can load quietly missing a locus with every test still passing. None of these five has been seen in a world.

  • The Colorado Ranger's solid strain must be completely free of leopard — and that path is new. It is the first shipped breed whose strain forces loci wild (lp/lp, n/n, n/n) rather than naming a pool to draw from. Pass: across a large sample, about 11 founders in 20 show no leopard character at all — no spots, no blanket, no varnish — and the other 9 do. Fail, and it is the interesting kind: any spotting on a solid-strain founder means a forced-wild pool inside a strain is not being honoured, which would be quietly wrong for every future breed that tries it.
  • A manchado foal is now reachable from a breed. The Criollo is the first breed ever to name manchado, at carriers only (6%, a design call — there is no test to measure). No founder should ever show it. Pass: no manchado on any wild Criollo, and a ma/Ma × ma/Ma pair eventually throws a foal with the dorsal white sheet, the rounded base-colour islands and a dark head and legs. This is the only route to that pattern besides the wild founder table.
  • Two more breeds that never run wild. The Croatian Coldblood and the Danubian are the second and third to use the spawn checklist after the Budyonny (["cowboy", "spawn_egg", "stable"]). Pass: ride plains, meadow, river, swamp and forest at length and never see a herd of either, while the cowboy still sells one and the eggs still exist.
  • Does a dark-skewed shade pool actually read as dark bay? The Domestic Mountain Pony is the test case for standing shade in for the dropped At allele: its study reports dark bay 44% and black 36%, so a/a carries the black and the shade pool is deliberately skewed to ShD instead of the house-standard spread. Pass: a band that reads as black and very dark brown, with ordinary bright bay a clear minority. If the A_ horses look like ordinary bays, shade is not doing the job the agouti call assumed it would, and that conclusion matters well beyond this breed.
  • The Criollo should be visibly a dun breed. Seven founders in ten are the traditional strain, where dun dominates and a double copy is ordinary. Pass: most of a band is bay dun, red dun or grullo with a dorsal stripe and leg barring; the rest is plainer. Also worth a look: the Domestic Mountain Pony's dun rate is an honest disagreement with its own source — D at 0.09 makes about 17% of founders dun where the study classified only 8% that way by eye. The allele frequency was followed. If duns look too frequent in play, that is the call to revisit, not a bug.
  • Sizes were re-derived, not taken from the drafts — and then corrected once. The drafts' own scale numbers contradicted their own stated heights in all five cases, so each band came from the cited metric or hand heights instead. The curve is not linear: below the 15.75 hh baseline a scale is just hands / 15.75, but above it the part over 1.0 is tripled (BreedStatCurve.BIG_GAMMA), so a draught horse visibly towers. Three of these five were first written with the linear rule and were a third of a hand short at the top. That prompted an audit of every breed, which found the same mistake in four breeds shipped earlier — the Anglo-Arabian, Australian Stock Horse, Azteca and Budyonny were all shorter than their own table row claimed — so seven files were corrected and every height string on both breed pages was regenerated from the files (gap 257). Pass: a Domestic Mountain Pony reads 12.3–14.1 hh, a Croatian Coldblood 14.3–16.1 hh and a Colorado Ranger 14.2–16.0 hh against the table. Nothing asserts this in a test, which is why it is here.
