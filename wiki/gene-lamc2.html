Natural gene / health

LAMC2 (junctional epidermolysis bullosa, draft)

A recessive lethal in the draft breeds. The layer that anchors skin to the body never forms, so the two shear apart wherever the foal takes weight — and the hoof capsules come away with it.

Crossing two of them

One of two JEB loci, and they are deliberately two

LAMA3 is the other. They produce the same clinical picture by breaking the same structure in two different places, and in this mod they are separate genes rather than two alleles of one. That matters at the stud: a horse can carry one and be genuinely clear of the other, so a draft horse’s test result says nothing at all about a Saddlebred, and pairing a LAMC2 carrier with a LAMA3 carrier is completely safe.

Folding them into one locus with two alleles would have made those two mutually exclusive — the ACAN shape, where any two broken copies leave no working one. That is right for ACAN and wrong here, because these are two genes.

No founder is ever affected

The founder table lists only the clear horse and the carrier. A wild-caught horse is an adult that survived, so it can carry this and never have it, and the homozygote’s weight is simply absent from the table. The only way to produce an affected foal is to breed two carriers.

Two loci, one condition name — and two condition ids

Condition equality is by id alone, so two genes can never quietly declare the same disorder. These two deliberately declare different ids (…-lamc2 and …-lama3) even though a player would call both “JEB”. A horse unlucky enough to be affected at both loci reports both, which is correct: they are two separate failures. Contrast splash deafness, which two loci share on purpose and which is reported once.

What breaks

Skin is two layers, and they have to be fastened together. The fastening is a protein assembly at the basement membrane, the thin sheet between the epidermis above and the dermis below, and one of its central components is laminin-332 — a trimer of three chains, of which LAMC2 encodes the gamma 2.

Lose a functional chain and the trimer cannot assemble properly, so the anchoring complex is absent or non-functional. The two layers of skin are then held together by very little. Ordinary mechanical shear — standing, lying down, nursing, being born — separates them, and the epidermis lifts away as blisters and then as open wounds.

The hooves are the same tissue problem in its most load-bearing form. A hoof capsule is epidermal, attached to the underlying structure by the same kind of junction, and in an affected foal it detaches: the capsules loosen and slough. That sign — a newborn foal losing its hooves — is the one that makes the condition unmistakable.

Why it is a lethal rather than a disability

There is no treatment that restores the missing anchor, the lesions are extensive and continuously renewed by ordinary movement, and they are a route for infection and fluid loss over a large surface. Affected foals are euthanised or die within days. The mod uses the ordinary LETHAL_AT_BIRTH path, which for this condition is a fair representation rather than a simplification — unlike FIS, where the real timescale is weeks.

Breeds

This is one of exactly two loci on the American Cream Draft’s real association test panel — the other being PSSM1 (GYS1), which already shipped. That is what made its absence the most conspicuous gap on any shipped breed’s own panel, and it is why this locus exists.

The rate in the breed file is a placeholder

The American Cream Draft carries this at a figure chosen to be plausible, not one measured in the breed. Said plainly here and in the breed’s own notes, because the sibling locus LAMA3 arrives with a genuinely measured number and the two should not be mistaken for equally well evidenced.
