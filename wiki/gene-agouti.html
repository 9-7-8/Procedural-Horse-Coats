Agouti — bay and seal brown

A restricts black to the points: a red-brown body with black mane, tail, ears and hooves, and black that climbs the legs and face by a per-horse random amount. Seal brown is not a separate gene — it is the top of that distribution.

What it does

BodyBlack knocked to BayCoat.BODY_BLACK = 0.32; red kept, so the body reads red-brown.
Mane, tail, earsFull black — setBlack(1), setRed(0).
HoovesAlways black, HOOF_FRACTION = 0.12 of leg height.
LegsBlack climbs a random height, fading out at the top edge.
FaceBlack climbs from the muzzle, but only on horses whose legs already did.

The point-extent roll

One number decides the horse, drawn uniformly off the expressing A copy’s epigenetic seed:

common/coat/pattern/BayCoat.java — 5 nextFloat()s, in this order
double extent = epi.nextFloat();                    // 0 = low socks .. 1 = seal
double leg    = LEG_MIN + extent * LEG_RANGE;       // 0.15 + extent * 0.80
double face   = 0.04 + extent * extent * 0.62;      // squared - see below

for (int i = 0; i < legs.length; i++) {             // one jitter per leg
    legs[i] = leg * (1.0 - LEG_JITTER + epi.nextFloat() * LEG_JITTER * 2.0);  // +/- 14%
}
  • Uniform, not f*f. The old product of two rolls clustered nearly every bay at the bottom of the range. A flat extent is what makes wild bays actually run from low socks to seal.
  • The face is squared, so it only climbs on horses whose legs already did — you get seal brown, not “socks plus a black face”.
  • Per-leg jitter of ±14%, independently, so one horse’s four socks are never exactly level.

The fade

The bottom SOLID_PORTION = 0.3 of each band is solid black; the rest is a smoothstep fade to nothing. Smoothstep has a flat slope at both ends, so the black dissolves into the body colour with no visible cut-off line. (Splash’s socks do have a visible cut-off line, and that is the open issue there.)

Seal brown

There is no seal allele

Seal is a high roll of extent — “the black creeps most of the way up”. A seal-brown mare passes on her point extent with her A copy, so seal runs in families exactly as if it were a gene, without being one. Real horses have A+ / A / At / a; the two-allele form plus an epigenetic extent is the deliberate abstraction.

Bay sets pigment absolutely — and why that matters

BayCoat paints its points absolutely: setBlack(1.0), setRed(0.0), via CoatRegions.blackenPart and blackenLowerLeg. Agouti runs before cream, pearl and champagne in Genes.naturalOrder(), so the dilutions do run after and do scale the points.

What broke, once, was the gradient, not the ordering. With red = 0 the gradient’s zero-red column stays visually jet black all the way down to black ≈ 0.4, so scaling black alone moved the sample without changing the colour: single cream’s black *= 0.7 landed on #111111, double pearl on #272727. Every “diluted” bay still had jet points.

The fix was PigmentField.dilute, which feeds a fraction of the removed eumelanin back in as pheomelanin and walks the sample sideways off that column into the warm browns. The ordering and the absolute bay point both stayed as they were.

Owner-verified in-game, 2026-09-01

A bay carrying champagne, cream or pearl now shows real diluted points — amber champagne chocolate over gold, buckskin dark brown over gold, perlino rusty, pearl bay sepia — instead of the jet black they all rendered before.

Interactions

  • Invisible on a chestnut. No black to restrict — a chestnut can carry A silently for generations.
  • The dilutions decide the colour of the points; agouti still decides where they are.
  • Grey reads bay’s output like any other pigment, and its point-retention knob keeps mane, tail and lower legs darker on the least-greyed horses — which reads as a young grey that used to be a bay.
