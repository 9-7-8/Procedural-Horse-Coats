The answer to "if it does not already" was no — and the reason was worse than missing

    HorseQuery is the filter language, game-free in common/.../horse/, over HorseListing
    rows, and HorseBrowserScreen's My horses box passes the player's text straight into
    it. gene: and carries: walk the genotype's pairs and match one allele token; there
    was no pair form, and splitAt() only ever treated : > < = as separators, so "E/e"
    was not a malformed term — it was a bare word, and fell through to the free-text
    haystack, which holds names and breeds and no genotype text at all. It matched
    nothing, silently, which is the worst of the three possible failures.

    The blocker underneath it is the part worth writing down. terms() lower-cased every
    term on the way in, and carries() compared with equalsIgnoreCase. Extension's two
    alleles are E and e (ExtensionGene:31-32); agouti's are A and a. At those loci case
    IS the allele. So gene:e did not mean "carries a red copy" — it meant "carries E or
    e", which is every horse alive, and gene:E meant the same thing. The single-allele
    filter documented in the tester list has been answering a different question than
    the one asked since it shipped, and no allele combination could be spelled at all
    while the two tokens were the same string.

    Nothing caught it because HorseQueryTest had no case case: its gene tests all use
    lower-case tokens (carries:e, gene:extension, gene:brindle), and every one of them
    passes under either behaviour.

What the owner chose

    Two decisions, put as options rather than guessed:

    Syntax — a separate genotype: key, not an extension of gene:. gene:e keeps meaning
    "carries one copy"; genotype:E/e is the exact pair. The rejected alternative was
    folding the pair into gene:'s value, which reads shorter but makes one key mean two
    different strengths of question depending on whether the value has a slash in it.
    The compact GeneCodeDisplay spelling (gene:Ee) was rejected as unparseable: with
    multi-character tokens like SB1 there is no way to know where the first token ends.

    Case — case-sensitive allele tokens everywhere, not only inside the new pair form.
    The alternative left gene:e loose and made only genotype: strict, so the two keys
    would have disagreed with each other about what a token means. The owner took the
    strict version knowing it changes an already-shipped filter.

The change

    common/.../horse/HorseQuery.java

        terms() no longer lower-cases. The folding moved down to each point of use, so
        the blast radius is exactly the allele tokens and nothing else:
          - term() lower-cases the key before the switch
          - flag() lower-cases before its switch and before the haystack contains
          - contains() lower-cases the needle as well as the haystack (it used to
            assume a pre-lowered needle, which is why this had to move together)
          - equalsIgnoreCase() is now really equalsIgnoreCase, not "b is already lower"
          - carries() compares the token with equals(), and keeps case-insensitive
            comparison for the gene name and key around it
          - expresses() unchanged in behaviour; it takes a lower-cased copy for the
            gene-key suffix test

        genotypeIs() is new. It splits the value on the first /, requires two non-empty
        tokens and no second slash, and walks Genes.codeOrder() for a pair whose two
        tokens are that set — either way round, case-exact. Registered as genotype: with
        pair: as an alias, and added to keys(), which is what the tab's help line prints,
        so the "keys:" row under the table picked it up with no second edit.

        A malformed value matches nothing rather than throwing. The box re-filters every
        keystroke, so genotype:E/ is a term on its way to being whole, not an error.

    neoforge-26.1.2/.../client/HorseBrowserScreen.java

        The empty box's hint, one line: "mare  gen>2  gene:SB1  -lethal" became
        "mare  gen>2  genotype:E/e  -lethal". The Allele… picker still writes
        gene:<token> and needed no change — it takes the token off the Allele object, so
        it was already emitting the real case; that case is now load-bearing rather than
        decorative.

    HorseQueryTest

        Four new tests plus seven malformed values. anAlleleTokenComparesCaseSensitively
        is the regression test for the case fold and fails under the old code.
        namesBreedsAndFlagsStillIgnoreCase is its other half — MARE, ARABIAN,
        BREED:Shir, Name:AMB and gene:EXTENSION all still work, which is the assertion
        that the fix did not over-reach. genotypeMatchesAnExactPair walks the ladder on
        one locus (gene:e, genotype:E/e, genotype:e/e) and covers the pair: alias and
        negation; two more cover order-insensitivity and that the tokens inside a pair
        are still case-exact.

What was not done, and why

    The wasm was not re-baked. The rule in CLAUDE.md is that anything in common/ invalidates
    wiki/horse-designer/wasm/web.wasm, but HorseQuery and HorseListing are not referenced
    anywhere in web/ — grep over web/src returns nothing — so they are not in the
    designer's reachable graph and the bake could only produce a non-reproducible 2MB
    diff. Worth re-checking if the designer ever grows a roster view.

    The breeding overview has no filter or sort controls at all: layoutGeneButtons gates
    horseFilterBox, genePickButton and allelePickButton to the My horses tab, and the two
    pickers read straight off ClientHorseRoster.of(Sex) in insertion order. Its click
    index and its draw index share that list, which is the constraint the next bullet has
    to respect.

Verification
