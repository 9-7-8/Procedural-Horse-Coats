carries tack

    One bullet, the first in the Bugs section of intake/To do.txt, in the owner's words:

        Golden carrot seeds and gene books should be much more common as loot. Add all horse
        armor types, dyed saddles, and dyed leather horse armor to all loot tables.

    Dated first, per START HERE's step 1. The bullet's only appearance in the file's history is
    de4cde82 (2026-09-23); nothing in wiki/session-log/ or on any Verification tab had touched
    chest loot since the golden carrot seeds shipped on 2026-09-18. Open.

Two things had to be decided before any code

    "Gene books." There is no gene-book item. A gene book is what the Equine Research Shelf
    copies a research paper onto, and server/GeneBookFromHorse writes one off a living horse.
    The owner's call: she means the research papers, which are the lootable gene-knowledge
    item. Recorded on item-research-papers.html in her own word so the next session does not
    go looking for an item.

    "All loot tables." Two readings: grow the hand-written id lists out to the full vanilla
    chest set, or reach every chest table in every namespace. Her call was the second, and it
    turned out to be the actual bug rather than a nicety — see below.

Why she had never found a golden carrot seed

    The three existing injections each listed eight to ten vanilla tables by id, because
    neoforge:loot_table_id tests one exact id and "chest loot" therefore has to be written as
    an any_of of whatever somebody thought to name. On the heavily modded server she actually
    plays, nearly every chest a player opens belongs to another mod's structure — and not one
    of those was in any of the three lists. The seeds were documented as 7% across eight
    tables; in practice they were 7% of a slice of chests she rarely opened.

    So the reach was the defect and the rate was the request. Both moved.

What changed

    data/loot/LootTableMatchesCondition — a loot condition of ours that regex-matches the
    queried table id. "[^:]+:chests/.+" is the whole of "every chest table, any mod". It
    matches the path rather than the namespace, because vanilla and nearly every structure mod
    file their chest tables under chests/; it deliberately misses block, entity, fishing and
    archaeology tables, since a horse armour falling out of a sheep is not what was asked for.
    A malformed pattern is a DataResult error, not a thrown exception, so a datapack that
    mistypes one loses that modifier rather than the pack.

    Golden carrot seeds 7% over eight tables -> 20% over every chest. Research papers 18% ->
    30% the same way. The breed spawn egg keeps its named ten: a foundation horse appearing in
    an arbitrary modded chest is a different question from a saddle doing so, and nobody has
    asked it.

    data/loot/AddHorseTackModifier — new, 22% per chest, one piece per hit. Weighted by what
    the piece is worth rather than what it is made of: leather horse armour 10, saddle 8, iron
    6, gold 4, diamond 1.

    Two things about that pool are deliberate and would look arbitrary later.

    Every modded armour shares ONE share of 5, drawn uniformly inside it. compat/ModdedArmour
    registers one armour per metal any installed mod adds, and a 319-jar pack brings dozens; a
    weight each would make vanilla's four the rare ones and turn the draw into a survey of the
    player's modlist. With no such mods installed the bucket is not in the draw at all.

    The fittings are cheap on purpose — iron, copper, bone, coal, basalt, gold, never a gem.
    The leatherworker's five tiers are a ladder whose top rung is lapis-dyed tack on diamond,
    and that ladder is only worth climbing if a dungeon chest cannot hand you its last rung.

    The saddle and the leather armour carry the mod's own three-zone tack_tint, not vanilla's
    dyed_color, which holds one value, reaches only the blanket and does nothing whatsoever to
    a saddle. The two leather zones roll separately and out of the whole wheel: the
    leatherworker draws his pair from one palette because his tiers are a progression, and a
    piece somebody lost in a dungeon has no such excuse. An unrelated pair is what makes it
    read as second-hand.

How far this is actually proven

    A clean runServer boots and logs nothing about loot. That on its own proves nothing — a
    folder nobody scans is also silent. So the same boot was run again with the new modifier's
    regex deliberately set to "[", and it logged:

        Couldn't parse data file 'horsegenetics:add_horse_tack' ... Not a regular expression: [

    from our own codec's error message. That is the positive half: the loot_modifiers folder is
    scanned, horsegenetics:add_horse_tack resolves to the serializer, and
    horsegenetics:loot_table_matches is registered and its codec runs. It also confirms the
    degrade-don't-throw path. Both boots are in the session's scratchpad; the port had to be
    moved off 25565 for the first one, because the owner's own server was on it.

    Nothing has been taken out of a chest. That, and the modded-armour branch — which cannot be
    checked in a vanilla dev world at all — are on items.html's Verification tab.

Two API notes came out of it, now on api-notes.html

    26.1.2 has no LootItemConditionType class: Registries.LOOT_CONDITION_TYPE holds the
    MapCodec itself, the same shape the loot-function registry already used here.

    Global loot modifiers no longer need a data/neoforge/loot_modifiers/global_loot_modifiers.json
    index — LootModifierManager is a SimpleJsonResourceReloadListener over the folder. This mod
    has never shipped one, which was worth confirming rather than assuming, since its absence
    and a silent non-load look identical from outside.
