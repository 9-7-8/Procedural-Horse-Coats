2026-09-17 — The health batch, and the locus that was there the whole time

    “Let’s add all the rest of the genes from the roadmap.”
    Scoped down on the owner’s instruction to all of health, with everything
    else to be checked for existence and put back to her one at a time before building.
    That instruction is the reason this entry exists in the shape it does: auditing before
    writing found that a third of the section was already built.

WFFS never needed a gene, and five breed files were carrying the error

    The roadmap called WFFS “the most-wanted health locus in the mod”, and five
    breed files repeated a warning against confusing it with
    PLOD1: “PLOD1 here is Friesian
    dwarfism and is emphatically not a substitute.” That sentence was itself the
    mistake it warned about. This mod’s PLOD1 is warmblood fragile
    foal syndrome — its condition id is fragile-foal-syndrome and its
    science tab is PLOD1 c.2032G>A throughout — while Friesian dwarfism is
    B4GALT7, a different gene entirely. Somebody
    conflated the two when writing those notes, and four breeds then shipped clear of a
    disorder the mod had had all along.

    The fix was wiring, not building. Gelderland, Oldenburg, Selle Franais and
    Swedish Warmblood now carry PLOD1 at rates measured in those breeds
    (7.63 / 15.53 / 6.0 / 7.4%), three of which came out of the survey table already sitting
    on the PLOD1 page — better evidence than the warmblood-wide ~11% proxy the roadmap
    had been holding. Zangersheide stays clear, because its only figure was a self-declared
    proxy that had already been declined.

Four loci that genuinely did not exist

    SLC5A3 (foal immunodeficiency syndrome),
    LAMC2 and LAMA3 (junctional
    epidermolysis bullosa) and RYR1 (malignant hyperthermia),
    plus a D5 allele on ACAN. Two calls in that set
    are worth keeping:

    JEB is two loci, not one locus with two alleles (owner’s call).
    The draft variant is LAMC2 and the Saddlebred’s is a partial LAMA3
    deletion — genuinely different genes. One locus carrying both as alleles would have
    made them mutually exclusive in the ACAN shape, where any two
    broken copies leave no working one; here a horse can carry one and be clear of the other,
    and pairing a carrier of each is completely safe.

    RYR1 is dominant and costs nothing, which is a first. Every other
    disorder here is a standing condition, but malignant hyperthermia is episodic —
    triggered by anaesthesia or extreme stress, and between episodes the horse is genuinely
    normal. There is no trigger in this game, so pricing it in hearts would invent a symptom
    the real condition does not have. One copy is INFORMATIONAL; the homozygote,
    which has no working channel left, does pay. It is the first locus whose entire value is
    being known rather than felt, which is precisely what the registries screen for.

    That decision broke oneCopyOfADominantAlreadyCostsTheHorse, which asserted
    every dominant takes hearts on one copy. Rather than carve out an exception, the test now
    checks the price against what each condition declares — informational must
    be free, impairing must cost — so a gene disagreeing with its own severity fails.
    Stronger than what it replaced.

Silver stops getting its colour for free

    Silver’s MCOA already shipped as
    homozygous-only. It is now dosed: Z/z carries the cyst phenotype —
    named on the horse and costing nothing — and Z/Z keeps its two hearts.
    The Rocky Mountain Horse is where it lands hardest, at 12%
    Z/Z and 46% Z/z, which is correct: chocolate is the breed’s
    whole identity, so it is the population that should pay for it.

The goldens moved and nothing changed

    Adding four loci rewrote 1056 rows of coat-golden.txt, which looks alarming
    and was not. coat-bake-golden.txt gained nine rows and deleted none,
    and comparing the hash column before and after found zero of the 1056 hashes
    changed. The entire diff was the code-string column growing new locus segments. Worth
    recording as the method as much as the result: a golden that moves wholesale is worth
    decomposing by column before it is copied back, because “every row moved” and
    “every coat changed” look identical in git diff --stat.
