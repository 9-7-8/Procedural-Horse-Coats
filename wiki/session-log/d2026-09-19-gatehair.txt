2026-09-19, later — A gate that duplicated, and a material chain nobody wanted to climb

    Released as 0.5.019. Two separate briefs, both from
    play, and the second one was the more interesting because it was not phrased as a bug at
    all.

    The duplication. Breaking a double fence drops two. The cause was a
    comment that had been wrong since the block was written, and confidently so: the class
    header explained that returning air from updateShape removed the orphaned half
    without dropping it, and that this was why no loot-table condition was needed.
    “One item in, one item out.” It is not true.
    Block.updateOrDestroy calls
    destroyBlock(pos, (updateFlags & 32) == 0), and an ordinary neighbour
    update carries no UPDATE_SUPPRESS_DROPS, so both removals ran the loot table
    and the gate paid twice.

    What made it worth writing down is that vanilla has exactly this problem
    in doors, beds and double plants, and solves it nowhere near the Java: every door’s
    loot table is conditioned on half=lower. The fix is a data change in two
    generators (bake-double-gates.mjs for vanilla’s woods,
    compat/GeneratedGates for everyone else’s) plus a
    playerWillDestroy for the mirror case — in creative, breaking the
    non-paying half still leaked a free gate out of the partner’s removal. The
    general lesson went to api-notes, because any future
    multi-block block walks into it.

    The second brief. Make the recipe to craft double gates not require
    horse hair — and remove the horse rope or bundle or whatever and just make everything
    that needs those just need horse hair. Reading it out, the real complaint was a
    compounding price nobody had ever totalled: 4 hair made a bundle, 3 bundles a braided rope,
    4 bundles a hair cloth, and the things a player actually wanted took the rope or the cloth.
    So a double fence gate cost 12 horse hair, an equestrian post 32, the
    supplier’s post 56 and the cowboy hitch 68 — against a supply
    of one to three hair per horse per Minecraft day. Nowhere in the source or the wiki was
    that number written down; it only existed multiplied out.

    A constraint decided the shape of the answer. These are shapeless recipes
    and the grid holds nine items, so 68 hair cannot be preserved even in principle. Collapsing
    the chain had to make everything dramatically cheaper. That was put to the owner as
    a choice rather than assumed, along with two others: which intermediates to delete (all
    three) and what a hair-free gate should cost (two vanilla gates, nothing else).

    That last one broke a house rule, which is the part worth remembering.
    Every recipe in the mod carries at least one modded ingredient so that none of them can
    collide with a vanilla or third-party one — and a collision is not an error in
    Minecraft, the manager just resolves one recipe and the other item quietly becomes
    uncraftable. Two vanilla gates and nothing else gives that up. It was taken deliberately
    after checking that nothing in vanilla consumes a fence gate (every vanilla recipe
    mentioning one produces it), documented as
    the one exception rather than left to be discovered,
    and — since check-recipes.mjs reads only our own folder and cannot see
    a third-party clash — backed by a test that asks the server’s recipe manager
    what two oak gates actually produce.

    Both fixes are guarded by mutation-checked gametests, not by having been
    looked at. double_gate_drops_one breaks each half in turn (they are not
    symmetric — left is the half that pays, so testing one would pass with
    the condition on the wrong half) and reverting oak’s table reproduces the owner’s
    report exactly: expected 1, found 2. double_gate_crafts fails when a
    stick is added to the recipe. Both on the gate
    page.

    What the removal actually touched, which was far more than the recipe
    folder: three items, six chain recipes, nine lang keys, three item definitions, three
    models and an orphaned texture; GeneratedArmour’s modded-metal padding;
    KnownGeneSpliceRecipe, which had accepted cloth as an alternative to hair;
    three progress tasks that would otherwise have become impossible to complete — caught
    by check-progress-tasks.mjs, which exists for precisely that; the husbandry
    tutorial step; two debug chests; and six villager trades.

    The trades were the one judgement call not put to the owner, and it is
    flagged as such on verification. A literal one-for-one
    substitution would have made a post cost 2 hair and a trade pay an emerald for 2 hair,
    which is a money printer. Working out the ladder turned up something better: in raw-hair
    terms every intermediate trade had already been worse than the supplier’s
    plain level-1 hair trade — 32 to 48 hair per emerald against 16 — so they had
    been close to dead all along. They are now one monotonic ladder, 16/14/12/10/8 per emerald
    by supplier level.
