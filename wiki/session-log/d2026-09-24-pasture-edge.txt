2026-09-24, after the dropped-food fix — a horse only walks to food it can reach

    One bullet, the next in the Bugs section of intake/To do.txt, in the owner's words:

        All the horses are pushing against the pasture edge where the fence separates them
        from the farm/crops.

    Dated first, as START HERE says to. The bullet went into To do.txt on 2026-09-23 and
    nothing in the session log or on any Verification tab had touched pasture-edge pathing,
    so: open, and go and read the code. The symptom map has no row for it; the class was
    obvious from the last two sessions, which had both been in HungerFoodGoal.

Why a pen shoves a fence

    HungerFoodGoal.search took the best rung within ten blocks and the nearest block on it,
    and then simply moved to it. Vanilla's pathfinder does not refuse an unreachable target:
    it returns the *partial* path that gets as close as it can, so moveTo returned true, the
    horse walked up to the rail and stood there pushing. Nothing broke it out until
    MAX_PURSUIT — twenty seconds — after which stop() put that one block on the ignore list
    for a minute and the next search picked the block beside it.

    That is the whole bug. A farm is a few hundred crop blocks; a pen of horses never runs
    out of them, so the shoving has no end. CROP also outranks GRASS, so the crops beat the
    pasture the horses were standing in every single time.

What changed

    search() asks createPath(...).canReach() before committing to a candidate. That question
    was already in the codebase — BondFollowGoal.ReachCheck asks it of an owner behind a
    door, and this session's predecessor shipped it — so the idiom was borrowed rather than
    invented. The accuracy is 1, which is what vanilla's own moveTo(x,y,z,speed) passes, so
    the check and the move that follows it ask one question rather than two. It is a
    manhattan distance: 1 still reaches a hay bale the horse stands beside and the grass
    block under its feet, and is two short of a crop on the far side of a one-block fence.

    The check on its own would have swapped pushing for starving, which is the part worth
    writing down. Rejecting the crop rung does not promote the grass rung: the old scan kept
    one global best, so a horse in a grass pen beside a fenced farm would have rejected
    crops every search and never fallen through to what was under it. The scan now keeps the
    nearest candidate of *each* rung and walks them best-first until one is reachable. An
    unreachable farm demotes the horse to its own grass.

    Around that: a rejected candidate joins the same minute-long ignore list, so the next
    search does not just pick its neighbour; a search spends at most three path tests, which
    is fewer than the repaths the twenty-second pursuit it replaces was already spending
    every twenty ticks, so this is cheaper than what it removes, not an added cost; and
    tick() now drops a pursuit whose path stops short, for the gate that shuts behind a
    horse that set off while it was open. Items and hunted prey go through the same check —
    a carcass over a fence pins a carnivore the same way.

Scope

    This is the pathing half only. A horse that is beside the fence for some other reason
    can still eat a crop through it: the goal's eating radius is 2.5 blocks and does not ask
    what is in between. That is its own bullet further down the Bugs section ("Horses are
    eating crops through a fence or wall") and was deliberately left alone — one bullet, one
    context window.

Not played

    No unit test. The behaviour is NeoForge's navigation end to end, and per
    roadmap-execution's session contract that means leaving the production path narrow and
    writing the runtime reproduction instead of building a harness for it.

    wiki/horse-care.html grew #reachable-food on the Coding tab and #pasture-edge-0924 on
    the Verification tab with four checks. Two of them exist to catch this fix overshooting
    rather than to confirm it: that the pen still eats its grass (the fall-through works) and
    that a hay bale dropped in the open is still walked to (REACH_ACCURACY of 1 is not too
    tight for a block a horse can only stand beside — if it is, cake, pumpkin and melon fail
    the same way). The accuracy figure is a reading of vanilla's pathfinder, not a
    measurement, and the class javadoc says so.
