2026-09-29 — three asks about ownership, and a horse that stopped landing in the wall


    A queue session. Three requests, arriving one at a time while work was
    already running, and all of them turned out to be the same question from
    three directions: who does this horse belong to, and what follows from
    that. Nothing was played. Everything below is build-green and written up
    as unplayed; the one piece with a test has one.

        1. people should not be able to ride a horse they don't own, it
           should buck them off with a warning about "that horse doesn't
           know you", but add in support for teams (like through ftb chunks,
           which creates teams) or allyships, people that are not the owner
           who are allowed to ride the horse
        2. the waystone support should find the nearest open 3x3x3 of pure
           air blocks to put the player on the horse in, even if that's up
           in the air, to prevent the horse suffocating in a wall
        3. create a command line command to transfer a horse to another
           player (works like transfer papers) which can be disabled from
           the config


1. A HORSE THAT DOES NOT KNOW YOU

    Vanilla has no rule here at all. A tamed horse is a saddle anybody can sit
    in; ownership governs whether a lead works and who the horse follows, and
    nothing else. That is invisible in single-player and is the difference
    between a stable and a car park on a server — and this mod's horses are
    worth a great deal more than a vanilla one, which is exactly what makes
    the absence of the rule expensive.

    server/HorseRiding now holds one question, mayRide(AbstractHorse, Player),
    and both answers to it. In order, first yes wins:

        behaviour.owner_only_riding off        -> yes (vanilla, exactly)
        the horse is untamed                   -> yes
        the owner                              -> yes
        the owner's vanilla scoreboard team    -> yes, if teams are allowed
        the owner's FTB team, or an ally of it -> yes, if teams are allowed

    THE UNTAMED CASE IS NOT A LOOPHOLE AND IS THE MOST IMPORTANT LINE IN THE
    CLASS. Climbing on a wild horse until it stops bucking is how a horse is
    tamed. A rule that refused an untamed horse would not be a fussy horse; it
    would be horses having become untameable, and it is the first thing on the
    verification list for that reason.

    Two enforcement points, because they catch two different failures:

        EntityMountEvent, cancelled. Mounting is server-authoritative in this
        version — AbstractHorse.mobInteract calls doPlayerRide behind an
        isClientSide check — so there is no client prediction to undo.

        A sweep on EntityTickEvent.Post, for a rider who is ALREADY up. The
        mount check cannot cover this: a horse changes hands under its rider
        (/horsegive, a redeemed paper), a team is disbanded, an operator edits
        the config with somebody in the saddle. Guarded on isVehicle() as the
        very first thing, so every horse in every paddock with nobody on it
        costs one boolean, and staggered by entity id at 20 ticks the way
        HorseCareHandler's scan is.

    The Ride button on the information screen is the third caller. It reaches
    startRiding by its own path and already had to repeat the cowboy brand
    check for exactly the same reason.

    Both refusals use makeMad() — the rear vanilla already has, which plays the
    angry sound itself — or the sound alone when the horse is already standing,
    since makeMad on a standing horse does nothing and a silent refusal reads
    as a bug rather than as a rule.

    No operator or creative exception, deliberately, and on precedent:
    HorseOwnership's class note already settled this for binding items ("you
    shouldn't be able to make any kind of bound item to a horse you don't own,
    even in creative mode", owner, 2026-09-13). The reasoning carries over
    intact, including the part that matters most — in the test yard the tester
    is always in creative, so an exception would mean the rule never ran at
    all. An operator who genuinely has to move somebody's horse has ask 3
    instead, which leaves a log line.


2. FTB TEAMS, AND WHY NOT FTB CHUNKS

    The ask named FTB Chunks. FTB Chunks is the mod a player sees — the map,
    the claims, the invite button — but a chunk claim is a relationship between
    a player and some GROUND, not between two players. The relationship lives
    one mod down, in FTB Teams, which is what FTB Chunks builds its claims out
    of and what its team screen is really editing. FTB Chunks declares
    ftbteams as a hard dependency, so a server running the one has the other.

    compat/FtbTeamsCompat is the third published API this project builds
    against, after Jade and Waystones, and takes the same deliberate exception
    to HorsePoweredCompat's no-reaching-in rule for the same stated reason: the
    rule is about GUESSING, and dev.ftb.mods.ftbteams.api is a package FTB
    publishes, versions and ships a package-info for. Same guarded Hook split,
    so with FTB absent nothing in dev.ftb is ever loaded or verified.

    Two calls, both taking UUIDs:

        TeamManager.arePlayersInSameTeam(UUID, UUID)
        Team.getRankForPlayer(UUID).isAllyOrBetter(), on the OWNER's team

    Taking UUIDs is the reason this works at all. A horse's owner is usually
    not online when somebody else tries to get on it, and an API that needed
    the owner's entity could only ever have answered for a horse whose owner
    was stood next to it.

    The ally direction is asked of the owner's team and not the rider's: it is
    the owner who decided to trust them, and the two teams need not have allied
    each other back.

    Vanilla scoreboard teams count too, under the same flag, because /team is
    the oldest answer to this question and works with no mods at all. That half
    is NOT symmetrical with the FTB half and cannot be: a scoreboard team keys
    its members on the player's NAME, so an offline owner goes through
    HorseOwnership.ownerName — usercache.json, then NeoForge's UsernameCache,
    never a network call. An owner neither cache has seen answers no. That is a
    deliberate no: a horse whose owner has never been seen on this server is
    precisely the horse that should not be handed to a stranger on a guess.

    BUILD NOTE. FTB is not on Modrinth — they withdrew, and the Modrinth
    coordinate 404s rather than resolving to something wrong, which is the good
    failure mode. The artifact is dev.ftb.mods:ftb-teams-neoforge from
    maven.ftb.dev/releases, pinned to 26.1.2.4, which is what the ATM11
    instance actually ships. ftb-library-neoforge 26.1.2.7 has to come with it:
    Team declares createParty(String, Color4I) and javac completes every member
    of an interface before it can resolve a call on one. Same trap Shogi set
    for the Waystones work, same "cannot access" error.


3. THE 3x3x3

    A waystone's arrival spot is chosen for a person standing up: one block in
    front of the stone, two blocks of air, no more. A horse is wider than that,
    and this mod's horses can be considerably taller — a Shire at the top of
    the size loci is a different animal from a Falabella. So a stone built
    against a hillside, inside a base, or in a one-block doorway puts the rider
    somewhere fine and the horse inside the wall.

    WaystoneTeleportEntityEvent.Pre carries setTargetPosition, which is the
    hook. WaystoneTeleportContext itself has no destination setter — it extends
    Shogi's ShogiContext, which is all readers — and that was worth half an
    hour of looking before the per-entity event turned it up.

    Four things about the implementation are deliberate:

        Only when a horse is in the batch. Ridden, led, or the vehicle the
        existing Prepare hook adds. An ordinary waystone hop with no horse in
        it is left exactly where Waystones put it — relocating every arrival
        on the server is a much larger claim than this integration is making.

        Nothing moves if the spot is already clear. The search tests `from`
        first, so the usual case is 27 block reads and no change. The x/z are
        still re-centred on the block, because Waystones' own spot can be
        off-centre and half a block of a three-wide space is not worth giving
        away.

        Air, not "not solid". The ask said pure air. Water, a carpet, tall
        grass and a snow layer are all things a horse can technically be put
        down in and none of them are what was asked for.

        If nothing within eight blocks is clear, the teleport happens anyway
        at Waystones' own spot. A waystone that cannot be arrived at is a worse
        bug than a horse in a tight spot, and this mod is not entitled to
        decide somebody's trip does not happen.

    No ground requirement, per the ask — "even if that's up in the air". A
    horse that falls two blocks is a horse; a horse in a wall is a corpse.

    TWO THINGS THAT WOULD HAVE BEEN BUGS. First, below the world
    getBlockState answers void_air, and isAir() says yes to void_air — so a
    waystone near bedrock would have found a perfectly "clear" spot eight
    blocks under the floor of the world and dropped the horse out of it.
    isOutsideBuildHeight on both the feet and the top of the cube is not a
    tidiness check. Second, a Chebyshev shell is not a sphere: a corner of
    shell r is r*sqrt(3) away and can be further off than a face of shell r+1,
    so the search finishes one shell past the first hit rather than returning
    on it.

    nearestClearance sits OUTSIDE the guarded Hook class, unlike everything
    else in that file. It names no Waystones type — a BlockGetter and a
    BlockPos — and the rule about Hook is that nothing in it may be loaded
    without Waystones present. Keeping the one piece with real logic in it out
    there is also what makes it testable, and it is the only piece of either
    integration that can be silently wrong: everything else either throws or
    visibly does nothing.

    So it is the only piece with a test. Gametest
    waystone_clearance_is_nearest walls an arrival into a solid 5x5x5, and
    measures what the shell search returns against an exhaustive scan of the
    same box — "found somewhere" and "found the nearest" being the two answers
    it is between. All 17 gametests pass.


4. /horsegive

    The command twin of the transfer paper, and explicitly not its replacement.
    The paper is an OBJECT: a claim the bearer has not collected yet, which can
    be sold, handed on, bought from the cowboy or lost in a creeper hole, and
    which is redeemed by going and finding the animal it names. None of that is
    a command and the paper is the more interesting thing for it. The command
    is for the case the paper is clumsy at — two players in one paddock, one of
    whom wants the other to have the mare they are both looking at.

    /horsegive <player> acts on the horse you are riding, or the one you are
    looking at within eight blocks. Naming a horse by name was considered and
    left out: a stable has four mares called Pepper sooner or later, and the
    failure mode of guessing is giving away the wrong animal — which, unlike
    almost everything else in this mod, the giver cannot undo on their own.

    server/HorseGiveCommand does NOT get its own transfer rules. It repeats
    TransferPaperHandler.redeem's: bredBy untouched, tamedBy filled in only
    when blank, CowboyHandler.clearBrand, both sides written to
    HorseLog.changedHands. Ownership is the one thing that moves.

    Anybody may run it on a horse they own — giving away your own property
    needs no permission, so it is a player's command and not an operator's. A
    gamemaster may give away any horse at all, and that half is a moderation
    tool: somebody has to be able to settle "he tamed my mare while I was
    offline", and §1 deliberately left no operator exception for an op to ride
    it away with instead. ActionTrace records when it was used that way.

    commands.horse_give is read once in RegisterCommandsEvent and the node is
    simply not added when it is off. A command that tab-completes and then says
    no is a worse answer than one that was never offered — the same shape
    debug.tools already uses.

    PERMISSION API NOTE. CommandSourceStack.hasPermission(int) is gone in
    26.1.2. It is Commands.LEVEL_GAMEMASTERS.check(source.permissions()) now —
    LEVEL_GAMEMASTERS is a PermissionCheck, not an int, and Commands
    .hasPermission() returns the predicate form the .requires() calls elsewhere
    in this repo use. Written down because the compile error names neither.


5. WHAT IS LEFT

    Nothing here has been played, and almost none of it CAN be played alone:
    every part of §1 and §4 needs a second player, and §2 and §3 need a client
    with Waystones, Balm and Shogi, which the dev instance is not. Three new
    verification entries say what to look at and, more usefully, what each way
    of failing would mean:

        horse-care.html#riding-0929           — the refusal, and the untamed
                                                check that has to come first
        compatibility.html#verify-ftbteams    — teams and allies, and the
                                                offline-owner case nobody will
                                                think to try
        item-transfer-papers.html
            #verify-horsegive                 — the command, including the one
                                                thing most likely to be wrong,
                                                which is not in the command

    The Waystones entry gained the arrival spot as a second, separate thing to
    watch: the search is tested, but nothing has ever fired
    WaystoneTeleportEntityEvent.Pre, so whether setTargetPosition is honoured
    and whether rider and horse really are given the same position are both
    unobserved.

    Config gained three settings: behaviour.owner_only_riding (true),
    behaviour.riding_allows_teams (true), commands.horse_give (true).

    The one to pick up first is not any of these. It is that the riding rule is
    the largest behaviour change this project has shipped in a while, it is on
    by default, and it changes what happens on an existing server the moment
    the jar lands — so it wants a second player before a release, not after.
