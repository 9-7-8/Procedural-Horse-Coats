2026-09-29, later — lending a horse for an afternoon


    A follow-on from the same day's riding rule, and the ask that the rule
    made necessary the moment it shipped.

        Add a way to add another person to be able to ride a horse, maybe
        temporarily-- thinking temporary jockeys for races-- through both an
        item they can feed them (you create it, then click the horse and it
        binds to the horse, then you give it to the other person, and they
        feed it to that horse and it temporarily allows them to ride, let's
        say 1 minecraft day per item), and then also add this as a command
        which can be disabled from config

    Nothing was played. The arithmetic has a test and everything else needs a
    second player.


1. THE GAP THE MORNING'S WORK OPENED

    HorseRiding answers who may ride from ownership and from teams. Both are
    STANDING relationships, and neither can say "this person, this horse, this
    afternoon". Putting a jockey on your team so they can ride one mount in one
    race hands them every horse you own, indefinitely, and taking them off
    again afterwards is a chore nobody is going to do.

    So the rule needed a third kind of answer: a borrowed one with a deadline
    on it. That is data/RidingPassAttachment — one map, player UUID to the game
    time their pass runs out, and nothing else.

    Game time rather than wall clock or day count, deliberately: it does not
    advance while the server is down, so a pass bought for a Saturday race is
    not spent overnight while nobody is playing.


2. THE PASS IS MADE OUT TO THE HORSE, NOT THE PERSON

    The ask specified this and it is worth writing down why it is right, since
    the other way round is the more obvious design.

    A pass for "Kestrel, one day" is an OBJECT. You can hand it to whoever
    turns up to ride, sell it at the gate, post it to a friend, or lose it. A
    pass for "Rowan may ride something" would have to be assigned to a person
    at the moment it was made, and a jockey who did not show up would take it
    out of the meeting with them.

    It also puts the dangerous half where it belongs. Making a pass out needs
    ownership (HorseOwnership.bindRefusal, creative no exception, the same rule
    every bound item in this mod follows). Feeding one needs only the pass.
    That is exactly the signed transfer paper's shape, and the whole flow was
    built to mirror it on purpose — it is the same kind of object and a player
    who has used one should not have to learn the other.

    Two item ids rather than one component-bearing item, matching the stall
    signs and the papers: a blank and a made-out pass are different things to a
    player and want different words.


3. WHERE IT IS ASKED, WHICH IS NOT WHERE IT LOOKS

    mayRide now asks, in order:

        config flag off        -> yes
        untamed                -> yes
        the owner              -> yes
        A LIVE PASS            -> yes
        the owner's teams      -> yes

    The pass sits ABOVE the teams. That is not arbitrary: a server that has set
    behaviour.riding_allows_teams = false has said "only the owner", and if the
    pass were inside that branch it would have silently switched off the item
    too. They are unrelated answers — one is a standing relationship and one is
    a receipt with a date on it — and one flag gating the other would be a trap
    rather than a setting.


4. FEEDING A SECOND PASS ADDS A DAY

    "1 minecraft day per item", so grant() counts from the existing deadline
    when one is still running and from NOW when it has lapsed. Both halves
    matter and both are invisible when wrong:

        overwrite instead of extend -> a second pass fed on the first morning
            silently costs the jockey a day, and looks identical on the day
        count from a stale deadline -> a pass granted after the old one ran
            out arrives already expired

    Every write also prunes what has lapsed, so a horse does not carry a dead
    jockey's name in the save for ever.

    NONE OF THOSE THREE PRODUCE AN ERROR, A LOG LINE OR A VISIBLE SYMPTOM. So
    they are the one part of this with a test: gametest jockey_passes_add_up.
    And the test was checked against a deliberately broken grant() — `long from
    = now` instead of the max — to confirm it bites rather than merely passing.
    It failed with the sentence it was written to say, and the mutation was
    reverted. All 18 gametests pass.


5. /horsejockey

    Four forms, all on the horse you are riding or looking at, all requiring
    you to own it:

        /horsejockey <player>            one pass's worth
        /horsejockey <player> <days>     that many, ADDED to what is left
        /horsejockey <player> revoke     take it back now
        /horsejockey list                who is borrowing, and for how long

    REVOKE HAS NO ITEM AND SHOULD NOT. Something you have handed to somebody
    cannot be un-handed, but the person who lent the horse ought to be able to
    change their mind. If the borrower is on the horse when it is revoked they
    come off there and then rather than at HorseRiding's next sweep a second
    later — the sweep would do it anyway, but doing it here is what makes the
    cause obvious.

    NO GAMEMASTER OVERRIDE, which is where this and /horsegive part company. An
    operator settling a dispute needs to move ownership, and /horsegive is
    that. Being able to write yourself onto somebody else's horse for a
    fortnight is a different power and nobody asked for it.

    list names an offline borrower through the server's own name caches, the
    same route the scoreboard-team lookup uses, falling back to eight
    characters of the id.

    The two commands share HorseGiveCommand.targetHorse: two commands that
    picked a different horse out of the same crosshair would be a genuinely
    nasty surprise, given that one of them gives the animal away.


6. A SALE CANCELS EVERY PASS

    Ownership moving clears the attachment, in both places ownership can move —
    /horsegive and a redeemed transfer paper. A pass is permission the PREVIOUS
    owner gave; carrying it across would hand the new owner's horse to
    strangers they never agreed to and could not even find out about.


7. THE ART, AND WHY IT IS DERIVED

    Two 32x32 sprites, made once by recolouring horse_ticket.png green with a
    throwaway Node script. Checked in as art, not as a baked artefact and not
    in the regeneration table: nothing regenerates them, so they cannot go
    silently stale. A pass reads as the same family of object as a stall ticket
    and cannot be taken for one at a glance.


8. WHAT IS LEFT

    A new page, wiki/item-jockey-passes.html, with its own verification tab.
    The check that matters most is not the pass WORKING, it is the pass
    EXPIRING: a pass that never runs out is indistinguishable from one that
    works, for the whole of the day somebody tests it on. /time add 24000 and
    watch the horse throw them.

    Config gained three more settings: commands.horse_jockey (true),
    behaviour.jockey_pass_days (1), and the morning's three are unchanged.
    behaviour.jockey_pass_days is read by BOTH the item and the bare command,
    which is its own check — set it to 2 and if only one of them moves, the
    command has a hard-coded 1 in it.
