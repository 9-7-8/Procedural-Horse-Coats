2026-09-24, after the low-health escape — no horse is killed by a single blow

    Not from intake. The owner asked for it directly, between bullets, in her own
    words:

        Horses should never be able to be "one shot" and die — any health which
        would kill them takes them to one health point instead, and they get 120
        ticks of immunity to damage, then the immunity disables until they heal
        back to full health again.

    Then, within the turn, an amendment that changed the shape of the work rather
    than the behaviour:

        This should also be controlled with the config: the duration, the
        threshold, and disabling it entirely. How I described is just the default
        settings.

    "The threshold" is ambiguous in that sentence — her description contains two
    numbers that could be one (the health a saved horse is left on, and the full
    health it has to climb back to before the save returns). Both were made
    settings rather than asking, since each is a real knob and she said "settings"
    plural. Four in the end: behaviour.last_stand, behaviour.last_stand_health,
    behaviour.last_stand_immunity_ticks, behaviour.last_stand_rearm_fraction.

    The owning page is horse-care.html — the same page as the hurt notice, the
    death notice and the escape goal, and this is the fourth thing in a row that
    is about a horse being hurt.

Why it is two events and not one

    The first shape was a single LivingIncomingDamageEvent handler, which is the
    idiom everywhere else in this mod (HorseAggroHandler, FlightFallGuard, BandLife,
    GeneAbilityHandler all use it). It is wrong here, and provably so: that event
    fires BEFORE armour and enchantment reduction, so its amount is not the number
    that will come off the health. A horse in barding would be judged on a blow it
    was never going to take in full, and would be "saved" from damage it would have
    survived — spending the save for nothing.

    So the save is LivingDamageEvent.Pre, whose getNewDamage() is documented in the
    26.1.2 sources as "the current value to be applied to the entity's health after
    this event". The immunity window stays on the incoming event, because a cancel
    there is the only thing that means NO DAMAGE HAPPENED rather than ZERO DAMAGE
    HAPPENED — and the window is supposed to be real immunity, not vanilla's hurt
    cooldown, which lets a bigger hit through the difference.

Reading the source rather than guessing at it

    Two assumptions were load-bearing enough to check against the decompiled
    LivingEntity rather than assert, and both held:

        this.setHealth(this.getHealth() - var10);

    where var10 is the container's newDamage, read back AFTER onLivingDamagePre.
    So setNewDamage(0) plus a bare setHealth(1.0F) inside Pre leaves the horse on
    exactly 1.0 at the end of the tick, because the subtract is skipped entirely
    when the damage is zero. Vanilla's own totem is the same move —
    checkTotemDeathProtection is literally setHealth(1.0F) — which is also why the
    particles are TOTEM_OF_UNDYING: it is the effect every player already reads as
    "that should have killed it".

    The second was the order of what follows. resolveMobResponsibleForDamage and
    the hurtTime assignment both run after actuallyHurt returns, so a saved horse
    still gets its lastHurtByMob and its hurtTime — which is exactly what
    HorseEscapeGoal.inDanger() reads. The save and the bolt are therefore one
    behaviour by arithmetic rather than by wiring, and Escape.THREAT_LINGERS_TICKS
    (200) outlasting LastStand.DEFAULT_IMMUNITY_TICKS (120) is what keeps the horse
    running for the whole window instead of stopping inside it. Neither number was
    changed for this; they already sat the right way round.

    There is also a Preconditions.checkArgument(!this.dead) inside actuallyHurt,
    with a comment saying it is invalid to kill an entity during Pre. This handler
    only ever raises health, so it is not near that line — but it is the reason a
    "set health to zero and let it die" shape would have been wrong to reach for.

The two exemptions, which are the part that could have broken something

    Neither of these is defensive. Both are existing mechanics this would have
    silently destroyed.

    horsegenetics:genetic_defect. LethalFoalHandler is deliberately tuned to
    out-damage the care handler's regeneration — its class comment says so — and a
    foal rescued to half a heart every six seconds would have been an IMMORTAL
    lethal foal, which is the exact inverse of the mechanic. The same damage type
    carries the half-heart a mare loses to an embryonic lethal, which horse-body.html
    explicitly allows to be her last ("breeding an animal that close to death is a
    decision, not an accident"). One exemption covers both.

    minecraft:bypasses_invulnerability. /kill, the void, and — the one that took
    reading to find — LycanthropyHandler.onWereAnimalDeath, which hurts the restored
    horse for Float.MAX_VALUE and then falls back to damageSources().genericKill()
    if it is somehow still alive. That fallback was written for gene immunities
    ("the restored horse may be immune to what killed the animal"), and it covers
    this one unchanged: the first blow is saved, the horse is alive, the fallback
    fires, the horse dies. Nothing had to be added there.

No tick, no countdown

    The state is a single stamp — the game tick of the last save — under a new
    "last_stand" key in the existing HorseCooldownsAttachment. No new attachment:
    that record is already serialized, already copyOnDeath, and already the mod's
    answer to "when did this last happen to this horse". It gained a clear(), since
    this is the first gate in it re-opened by something happening rather than by
    time passing.

    Re-arming is READ OFF THE HEALTH in the incoming-damage handler rather than
    ticked anywhere. "Healed back to full" is a fact about the horse, not an event,
    and the incoming handler is the one place guaranteed to run whenever the answer
    could matter. So it costs nothing while nothing is attacking a horse, it cannot
    go stale in an unloaded chunk, and it cannot miss the heal that finished the
    job. Same reasoning as HorseCareHandler.creditBondTiers. The shared slow tick
    was considered and rejected: it runs every 30 ticks, which would have made the
    re-arm granular for no gain.

What is left open

    AbstractHorse, not Horse. That is the mod's consistent answer to "what is a
    horse" — the notice handlers and the care handler's own herd scan all use it —
    but it is a wider family than it sounds: llamas and camels are AbstractHorse in
    26.1.2, alongside the donkeys and mules this mod actually breeds. Left as-is
    because singling them out here would be the odd decision rather than this one,
    but written down in the class, on the Gameplay and Coding tabs, and as a
    verification check, because it is the kind of call an owner may want narrowed.

    Nothing about the behaviour is tested. LastStandTest covers the arithmetic and
    the wording — that damage exactly equal to remaining health still counts as
    fatal, the window, the Long.MIN_VALUE the cooldown store answers with for a key
    never written, the two ways a setting could hand back immortality, and the
    clamps that stop a configured health of 0 killing what it rescues. Everything
    else is NeoForge.

    The single assumption the whole feature rests on is the setNewDamage/setHealth
    pairing inside LivingDamageEvent.Pre. It was read out of the 26.1.2 sources
    rather than guessed, but it has never been watched working, so the verification
    summary leads with it: a charged creeper against a full-health horse. The second
    check is that a lethal foal still dies in its four seconds, because that is the
    failure that would quietly break something that already worked rather than fail
    to deliver something new.
