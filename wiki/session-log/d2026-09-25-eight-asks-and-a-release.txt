2026-09-25 — eight asks, queued one at a time, and 0.5.030


    It opened as a gameplay question, not a task:

        How do I make a true flight gene splice carrot? I have tf/tf book
        learned, if I make two copies, can I make two tf/tf carrots, and
        guarantee a tf/tf baby?

    The answer was yes — a Known Gene Splice substitutes the pair one parent's
    gamete is drawn from, so a Tf/Tf carrot makes that parent contribute Tf with
    certainty, and one fed to each parent is a guaranteed Tf/Tf foal. Reading the
    code to answer it turned up two sentences that were no longer true, and the
    first ask followed from the answer's own cost: a mythic locus wanted TWO
    NETHER STARS for one deliberate foal.

    Seven more arrived while that was being done. What follows is in the order
    they were asked. Three of the eight were not really the thing they looked
    like, and those are marked.


1. RETIRE THE GENE CARROT'S RARITY CHARGE.

    A Known Gene Splice carrot is now golden carrot + research paper + horse
    hair. Three slots, at every tier.

    The fourth slot took the ingot for the gene's GeneRarity — iron, gold,
    diamond, emerald, netherite, nether star. It was prohibitive at exactly the
    tiers people want the carrot for, and the arithmetic above is why: the
    natural use is two carrots, because two is what guarantees a homozygous
    foal.

    server/recipe/RarityItems is DELETED rather than left unused, and the
    javadoc in four other files that used it as the worked example of "a table
    that lives on the game side" now points at DietFoods and HorsePrices.

    The tier still prices the PAPER — GeneRarity.lootWeight() for chest loot,
    and the research shelf's copy time. So rarity now says how hard a gene is to
    LEARN and never how hard it is to spend once learnt. That distinction is
    written into GeneRarity's own javadoc, because the enum previously opened by
    calling itself "the gene-carrot recipe cost".

    NOT THE ASK, found on the way: the gene-page carrot card still said "two
    carrot-fed parents give the usual 25/50/25", which stopped being true when a
    paper started naming a PAIR — two true-breeding carrots are 100%, which is
    the whole of the question that opened the session. Its hair slot also still
    offered "or hair cloth", an item that no longer exists.


2. MAKE IT EASIER TO SPAWN SPLICE CARROTS, AND WRITE DOWN HOW.

    /testkit splice <gene> [<a> <b>] and /testkit paper <gene> [<a> <b>].

    These two items are the only things in the mod /give cannot spawn by name:
    each is ONE item parameterised by a data component, so the gene is not in
    the id. By hand it means typing

        known:horsegenetics.flying:Tf:Tf

    exactly right, with no completion — and a mistyped token is not an error. It
    is an item that looks perfectly normal, parses to nothing and does nothing,
    which you find out one breeding later. Both subcommands complete the gene
    (short name or full key) and then both alleles off the live registry, and
    refuse a pair the locus says cannot occur.

    The write-up went on BreedingCarrotItem and ResearchPaperItem — the raw
    /give forms, the shape of both tokens (colons for the carrot, BARS for the
    paper, which normalises so a|A and A|a are one paper), and a note to prefer
    the command and why.

    The owner was asked where "comments in the jar" should live, with options.
    Command-plus-javadoc was chosen, and the scope widened to the paper as well.


3. THE GOLDEN CARROT SHOULD ALWAYS WORK.

    Asked as: "the golden carrot should ALWAYS make two horses have a baby — the
    only limit being age (both adults) and being opposite sex."

    A full gate map was taken first, and it found EIGHT reasons a golden-carrot
    pairing could fail. The owner was given them as options and chose the
    literal reading, then refined it twice unprompted:

        the golden carrot should still trigger pregnancy messages to the log and
        chat

        the golden carrot actually shouldn't result in a pregnancy at all, it
        should instantly create a baby, even if the mom is currently pregnant

    That second line settled a hazard the options had flagged: a pregnant mare
    conceiving again would have OVERWRITTEN the embryo she was carrying. Since
    the golden carrot never conceives, there is nothing to overwrite — she gets
    the instant foal and the one she was already carrying, on its own clock.

    What went: the vanilla 6000-tick cooldown (BreedingCooldownHandler zeroes
    the age on the feed), vanilla's isTamed() and getHealth() >= getMaxHealth()
    tests (mixin/HorseAlwaysParentMixin, guarded to Horse so donkeys and llamas
    are untouched), the gelding check, the sf/sf subfertility roll, the estrus
    cycle, and the pregnant-mare refusal. An untamed adult is put in love by the
    feed handler itself, because vanilla's handleEating will not.

    Three of those were INVISIBLE rather than unfair, which is the argument
    worth keeping. A horse one heart down from a fall reads as perfectly
    ordinary, eats the carrot, shows hearts and does not breed — and vanilla has
    no message for it, so the mod cannot add one to a condition it never
    evaluates. The subfertile roll looked exactly like bad luck.

    THE BIG ONE, and it was not in the ask: a carrot-armed parent used to divert
    the whole pairing to a PREGNANCY. Conception is gated on the cycle, so an
    armed pair out of heat — sixteen days in twenty-one — stood there eating
    golden carrots with no foal, no pregnancy, and the carrots still armed. A
    splice carrot could sit on a horse for most of a week doing nothing visible.
    The bias now folds into the instant foal, splice lineage mark and all.

    Conception.vanillaFoalChance is deleted with the rule it served, and its
    test is rewritten to pin that FertilityGene reaches breeding only through
    ReproRules now.

    Kept: same-sex, either parent a baby, and a draw that comes out an embryonic
    lethal. The last is genetics rather than a gate — it is what met/met and the
    KIT lethals are FOR — and the commit says so explicitly so the owner can
    overrule it.

    About twenty now-false sentences were corrected across fertility.html,
    breeding.html, carrots.html, gene-fertility, gene-census, horse-dimension,
    item-vet-kit and the tester scripts. Two of those were VERIFIED records
    describing measurements that were correct and are now history: the
    subfertile pen's one-in-four, and the gold-timer pen's cooldown clearing.
    They say so rather than being deleted.


4. A TICKET THAT SENDS A HORSE TO THE REALM AND UNCLAIMS IT.

    The turnout ticket: blank ticket, horse hair, feather.

    It is two existing halves bolted together and DELIBERATELY not a third
    implementation of either — TicketHandler.arrive for the travel, and
    HorseRelease.makeWild for the release, which is the single definition of
    what "wild" means and the only thing that remembers the temper reset and the
    wild-spawn mark (without which a released horse never finds a band).

    Order is load-bearing: MOVE FIRST, RELEASE SECOND, so makeWild hands the
    tack to a player still standing in the world the horse left rather than
    dropping a saddle on the realm floor where they cannot reach it.

    It is also the first release that clears the horse's StallRecord. The other
    two paths can never meet a horse that still has a stall — the freedom stick
    only works inside the realm, and HorseRealmFeral only fires on a horse
    already there — so a stall sign bound to a horse nobody owns was not
    previously reachable.


5. E WHILE LOOKING AT A HORSE OPENS ITS MENU.

    NOT WHAT IT FIRST LOOKED LIKE. The obvious reading is vanilla's horse
    inventory, and the first attempt was a payload asking the server to open
    one. Both halves of that were wrong: this mod's horse menu is
    HorseInfoScreen, whose Gear tab holds the saddle and barding that screen
    used to, and it opens CLIENT-SIDE off ClientHorseRecordCache with no packet
    at all. The payload was deleted rather than left in.

    HorseInventoryKeyHandler is HorseInfoKeyHandler with one line changed, and
    that is the point: every hard-won detail in that class applies unchanged —
    take the key in ClientTickEvent.Pre rather than swap the screen afterwards
    (vanilla's is a CONTAINER screen the server opened, so swapping leaves a
    menu bound to the player with nothing to close it), drain the clicks in a
    loop, and bail WITHOUT consuming the press when there is no record so
    vanilla still opens for a donkey.

    The rider case wins by construction rather than by priority: this one bails
    on isPassenger() before touching the key, so looking at a second horse from
    the saddle cannot open the wrong animal's window. Two subscribers draining
    one key in one tick is a thing to make impossible, not to order carefully.


6. HEAT STATUS ON JADE, INCLUDING NOT IN HEAT AND HOW LONG TO GO.

    A DELIBERATE REVERSAL, and worth recording as one. Jade showed a state word
    alone — "Pregnant", "In heat", or nothing — from a separate, shorter
    ReproText.glanceLine whose javadoc argued the case at length: no countdown
    to tick in the corner of a player's eye, and nothing at all for a mare
    merely between heats, because "a glance asked no question".

    The owner asked for the countdown and for "not in heat". That is the better
    call: a mare between heats is precisely the case a breeder scans a paddock
    to find out about, and a blank tooltip is indistinguishable from the mod not
    reporting at all.

    glanceLine had no other caller, so it is deleted along with the argument it
    served, and its tests are rewritten to pin that every state answers and that
    twins still stay the vet kit's alone. The reversal is written down in both
    JadeHorsePlugin and compatibility.html, because the old reasoning was good
    enough that a future session would otherwise reinstate it.


7. WILD HORSES CLUMP INTO ONE GIANT BALL.

    Asked as: "shouldn't they form bands and spread out? Can you add some
    motivator that drives wild horses to not want to be near other bands?"

    A map of the herd system found THREE rules compounding, none wrong alone:

      - Every portal lands on the same block. Deliberate, documented, and pinned
        by the horse_realm_grid_is_stable gametest over four thousand portals.
      - HorseRealmHerds put every arrival into the nearest band within 32 blocks
        — and there always was one, and it was always the same one.
      - WildHerdGoal closes each member to within five blocks of its lead and
        StallionGuard drags strays back inside fourteen, against nothing but
        vanilla's random stroll at the same goal priority.

    THE LOAD-BEARING INSIGHT: the field did not hold several bands standing
    close together. It held ONE band, of everything. So the motivator asked for
    would have done nothing whatsoever on its own.

    So a band now FILLS UP — BreedHerd.MAX_HAREM_MARES + 1, doubled for foals
    and hangers-on — after which an arrival founds its own. That is what makes
    the second band exist. Then server/BandSpacing is the motivator: a wild
    banded horse walks away from the nearest horse of a DIFFERENT band within
    forty blocks.

    It is a per-horse nudge rather than a band manoeuvre, and that is what makes
    it cheap. Cohesion is per-horse too, so the two are the same kind of force
    and settle against each other instead of fighting for the navigation; a band
    whose members each edge away from a neighbour moves AS A BAND, because its
    own cohesion keeps gathering it up as it goes. Nothing computes a band
    centre, elects who decides, or keeps band-level state. It rides the same
    hundred-tick social scan and actuates through BandLife.retreat — the same
    short shove a horse already gets for losing a fight.

    Arrival is deliberately LEFT ALONE. Scattering horses as they land is the
    obvious third fix and it is the one not taken: that property is what the
    gametest asserts, and it is load bearing for the terrain and the wall.
    Spreading out is something the horses do afterwards, which is also the
    version that keeps working when a band wanders.


8. H IN THE REALM LISTS THE WHOLE FIELD.

    A Horse realm tab: every horse standing in the realm, in the same sortable,
    filterable table as My horses, with a Bring home button that spends one
    holding pen ticket to tame the horse and put it in the player's pen. In the
    realm, H opens straight onto it.

    It is ONE RENDERER over two rosters rather than a second table —
    showingRealm() is asked once and the source, the version, the received flag,
    the empty-state note, the column heading, the button label, the ticket test
    and the click target all read through it. A column added to the stable is in
    both by construction. RealmRosterPayload reuses the stable's hand-written
    entry codec for the same reason, and that codec went from private to
    package-private with a comment saying why.

    They are separate payloads and separate client caches only so that arriving
    in the realm cannot overwrite what the client knows about the player's own
    stable.

    THE DESIGN CALL WORTH RECORDING: taming is BOUGHT here rather than earned.
    Everywhere else, taming a horse is being thrown off it until it gives in,
    and that is the right price for an animal standing in front of you. It
    cannot be the price here, because the point of the field is that it holds
    MORE HORSES THAN ANYONE WILL EVER RIDE — a table that could only list them
    would be a catalogue you cannot order from. The ticket is the price, which
    makes this the exact inverse of ask 4: one ticket out, one ticket back.

    RealmClaim tames first and travels second, and spends the ticket only after
    the horse has actually moved, so no failure can leave a wild horse in
    somebody's pen or a ticket spent on nothing.


WHAT WENT WRONG IN THE SESSION ITSELF

    THE FIRST runServer NEVER EXITED. Its Gradle wrapper reported exit 0 and the
    log ended with "All chunks are saved", which reads exactly like a clean
    shutdown — it was an autosave, and the JVM sat at the console for half an
    hour holding the world's DirectoryLock. The next boot failed with an
    IOException that looks identical to the owner's own client being open, which
    is the documented cause and was the wrong diagnosis here. The tell is
    -Dfml.modFolders in the command line of a java process that should be gone.

    The owner's LIVE SERVER was running throughout (PID 8448, three hours of
    CPU, real players on it) and was not touched. The dev run directory is on
    port 25599, so there was never a collision to manage.
