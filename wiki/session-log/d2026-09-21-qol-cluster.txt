2026-09-21, later still — the quality-of-life cluster, and a reversal: the rider is half of what a horse can do

    No code. The second survey session in a day, and the one that worked off
    the backlog's largest row — the six-mod
    quality-of-life cluster, which the row itself insisted be read “as one batch, not
    six”. The owner asked for every call to be put to her as a choice rather than decided on
    her behalf, the same brief as the Simply Equine entry below, and the most important answer of
    the session was one she wrote herself instead of picking an option.

The row was wrong about two of its own six names

    The cluster is five mods, and one of the links pointed at something that does not exist

        The Horse Buff at 9minecraft is a fabricated repost. It
        advertises builds for Minecraft 26.2 and 26.3 dated three weeks ago; the real mod is
        archived on GitHub (April 2025) and on Modrinth, topping out at 1.21.4, and the one fork
        still moving stalls at an unreleased 26.1 branch. The page's feature list paraphrases the
        real mod but drops three features it has and adds one it never had
        (mount armour rendered see-through for the rider). It was dropped rather than read. The
        genuine mod's own README names Horseman as its
        successor — which we had already read and which sits on the compatibility page
        already.

        Horse Upgrades is two unrelated mods sharing a name. The
        CurseForge link in the row is Lothrazar's 2016 Minecraft 1.8 mod, whose whole design is
        carrots granting permanent stats. The Modrinth mod of the same name is horseshoes forged
        on an anvil — which the horse-shoe roadmap already owns
        as its own section. Both were read; they went to opposite places.

        This is the second session running where a store page and the artefact described different
        mods, and the second where reading the thing itself was the whole value of the row. The
        Simply Equine entry below has the same finding in stronger form.

The reversal: a horsemanship skill is in, and the objection is answered rather than dropped

    Two mods in the cluster let a horse exceed its own stats — Superb Steeds by training it
    with riding, Horse Upgrades by feeding it carrots — and both ran straight into a
    position this project had taken twice: a player-progression ladder cuts against breeding being
    the progression. I offered four ways to refuse them.
    The owner took none of the four and reframed the question instead: gate the
    gain behind both bond and a rider skill, because
    “bonding with a horse makes it listen better, and rpg skill makes you a better horse
    rider”.

    That is a better answer than any of mine, because it relocates the gain. Nothing makes the
    horse a better horse; what improves is the rider, and how well a particular horse listens to
    them. The consequence, settled as
    its own call, is that
    the bonus belongs to the pairing and is never written to the animal — an
    AttributeModifier applied on mount and removed on dismount. The horse's stored
    stats stay exactly its genome, the info screen keeps telling the truth, and a sold horse
    carries none of the seller's skill.

    That distinction is also what lets the same session accept one and refuse the
    other. The carrots are refused —
    written up beside the rule they break —
    because a carrot changes the horse permanently, and riding well changes only the ride. Speed
    and jump only; never health or size, which would stop a frail horse being legible at a glance.
    The magnitude is the owner's call and the one number carrying real design risk:
    uncapped, with sharply diminishing returns, which means the curve is the only
    thing keeping a well-ridden ordinary horse from overtaking a bred one. The roadmap says to
    pick it against measured genome spread rather than in the abstract.

The hook was chosen by reading three jars, not three store pages

    The owner supplied her own RPG stack and asked which to integrate with. All three were
    unpacked from her server and read as bytecode.
    Pufferfish's Skills is the answer, and it
    is not close.

    - The tree is pure data and needs no code at all. Pufferfish scans every
        namespace for data/<ns>/puffish_skills/config.json, so a Horsemanship
        category shipped inside our own jar is read when the mod is installed and inert when it is
        not — no registration, no isLoaded probe, no presence check. Skill Tree
        ships its own categories exactly this way, which makes its jar the schema
        documentation.

    - The effect needs no branch either. The built-in attribute reward applies
        a modifier to any registered attribute, including one we register unconditionally —
        so our horse code reads a value that simply stays at baseline when nothing ever grants it.
        A genuine zero-branch soft dependency, and the shape to copy for future optional
        integrations.

    - The other two cannot host it. RPG Attribute System's
        isAvailable() is compiled as a literal return true and is
        useless as a presence check, it exposes no events at all, and its eight attributes are
        fixed. Skill Tree has no api package and hard-requires two spell mods.

    With no skills mod installed, bond alone carries the feature at a lower ceiling.
    That was the deliberate choice over letting it not exist: most players run no skills mod, and
    it also means the whole system can be built and tuned against bond before any hook is written.

Superb Steeds looked like a head-on collision and turned out not to be

    It is the rare overlapping mod that can be installed beside this one — a
    maintained NeoForge branch on our exact version — and it mixins away vanilla's per-horse
    stat variance including offspring attributes, which reads as a direct attack on the genetics.
    Checking the code rather than assuming changed the verdict.
    HorseRecords.applyTraitsToEntity writes base values from the genotype on every
    join, so its flattening is overwritten by ours the next time a horse loads, and its offspring
    mixin is moot because our foals never inherit an attribute. What actually survives is its
    star-training, which is applied as modifiers and composes on top of us.

    So the real finding is subtler and more useful: it is a second implementation of the
    feature this session just designed, differing in where the gain lives. Two training
    systems on one horse is a bug generator, the same argument that already sits on Icy's bond.
    Its page plans runtime detection rather than a
    patch, because the bad outcome is silent: stars climbing on a horse whose genome says
    otherwise, with no way to tell which mod is talking. Three of its smaller tweaks were
    harvested; its “no saddle needed to steer” was not, because
    bareback steering at bond tier 3 already does
    it better.

What else was taken, and what was refused

    - Rider comfort — the
        mining-speed debuff while mounted, a keybind for your own inventory while riding, fading
        the horse as you look down, leads returning to your inventory, and right-click-to-equip.
        Two carry warnings written into the section: the fade is the first thing to ask a
        procedural coat to render half-transparent, and right-click-to-equip lands on the
        same interaction as our own i button.

    - Two bond behaviours — a
        trusted horse stops bucking, and a bonded horse waits where you left it. Both were keyed
        to a saddle in their original mods; keying them to bond instead is the
        adaptation, and it keeps a low-bond horse behaving exactly as vanilla does.

    - Horse Tweaks' seven saddle upgrades become
        enchantments, which is also
        what that section's own rule already implied. Two of the seven we ship by other means
        already, and feather fall is flagged as the one that is a larger change than it
        looks.

    - Leave My Bars Alone is folded into
        the RideHUD section rather than rebuilt. It
        is maintained, MPL-2.0, and two files long, on a branch matching our Minecraft version. We
        design our readout not to add clutter; the vanilla bar problem stays solved by the mod
        that solves it.

    - Refused: the stat and recolour carrots (above); Horse Buff's step-height
        change, because it moves a number every other mod's pathing assumes; and creative
        instant-tame, which was offered and declined because it would reopen the
        no-creative-exception ownership rule set on 2026-09-13.

    Licences: ideas only, always. The five mods are MIT, GPL-3.0, MPL-2.0,
    CC BY-NC-SA and All Rights Reserved, and the owner's call was one rule rather than five
    — nothing is ported whatever the terms allow. The MIT one is the tempting exception,
    since Horse Tweaks is also Minecraft 1.12.2 and would double as a
    backport reference; it was declined anyway.

    Two survey rows closed — the quality-of-life cluster, and the
    RPG-skills row, the latter by building the thing it was hedging about. The count of what is
    left is on the table itself and is deliberately not
    repeated here — the entry below quoted a count that was already wrong when written, and
    it has been taken out rather than corrected, which is the whole argument against writing a
    derived number into prose.

    One defect fell out of measuring ourselves against other mods, which is the real
    argument for doing it. Several of the five protect an owned horse from wandering or
    vanishing, and checking what we do revealed that
    setPersistenceRequired is called on cowboy stock, egg-spawned horses, stable
    stock and the debug pens — and never on taming or ownership. Whether vanilla already
    protects a tamed horse was looked for and not established, so it is recorded as a question
    with the test attached rather than as a bug: gap 300.
