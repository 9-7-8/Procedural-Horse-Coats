2026-09-25 — two Nether breeds arrived from somewhere else, and were checked rather than trusted

    The ask was not "build something". It was:

        I had copilot implement two breeds. Can you pull those, look at its
        justification in intake, and make sure it did everything correctly?

    So this is a review session, and the interesting thing about it is the shape of
    what was wrong. The genetics — the part that looks hardest, and the part a
    reviewer is most tempted to skim because it is 260 lines of JSON — were right.
    Everything around the genetics was where the misses were.

    Commit ac342df5 was the work under review. b886e6ed is the repair; this file and
    the commit after it are the documentation that should have come with it.


WHAT ARRIVED

    Two magical breeds, deliberately built as a pair.

    The Netherhorse is a liver-bay tank for the Nether floor: nearly twice the size
    and health of an ordinary horse, slow, no jumper, fireproof, millable for lava,
    and hunting everything that comes near it — its own owner included, even once
    tamed. Feed a grown one nether wart and it stops seeing you as prey. A foal
    cannot be talked down at all.

    The Nightmare is the same liver-bay base and nothing else the same: hand-sized,
    true-flying, fleeing everything alive on sight, unable to fight at all. Cake
    draws it in and buys it permanently, at any age. Milk it and the bottle comes
    back carrying Weakness and Darkness at once.

    They share the molten-hooves locus at the same pair, banded red on one and gold
    on the other, so the two breeds' glowing hoofprints read as a matched set from a
    distance. That is a nice piece of design and it survived review untouched.

    Alongside them: two new passification offerings (nether wart, cake), two new
    potion-milk brews (Weakness, Darkness), and a fix to the passification veto so a
    calmed player stops being a reason to run.


WHAT WAS RIGHT, AND HOW THAT WAS ESTABLISHED

    Every gene key, allele token and band key in both files resolves. That was
    checked by hand against the Java and the spec genes rather than assumed —
    `Aaa` and `Faa` are assembled from a prefix and a suffix rather than written
    anywhere as literals, and `MltC`, `Tf`, `Rflm`, `Cha`, `Glo`, `Mnsld` and
    `Tlsld` all had to be found individually. Every banded range sits inside its
    knob's declared range. The offering indices 12 and 13 really are nether wart and
    cake, and appending rather than inserting was the right call.

    The strongest single piece of evidence was one the review nearly talked itself
    out of needing: BreedFilesTest's first case is
    `everyShippedFileLoadsWithNoErrorsAndNoWarnings`, and the breed parser *warns*
    rather than throws on an unknown gene key or an unknown epi name. So the test
    being green is not merely "it parsed" — it is "nothing in either file was
    quietly skipped". Worth remembering: the parser's leniency is not a hole,
    because the test closes it.

    The one piece of integration that could have been badly wrong and was not: the
    Nightmare has to be lured with cake while fleeing everything on sight, and those
    are two systems commanding the same navigation. FoodTemptGoal notices a
    passification offering at radius 20 and re-issues its path every tick;
    GeneAbilityHandler's flee works at radius 16 and re-picks every twenty ticks.
    The lure wins on both counts. That is not luck — it is the numbers happening to
    be right — but it was not reasoned about anywhere in the handover, so it was
    reasoned about here instead, and it is now written on the passification page's
    Verification tab as the first thing to watch.


THE FIVE THINGS THAT WERE WRONG

    Two derived artefacts left stale, which is the failure this repo is most prone to
    and the reason the Build & test table in CLAUDE.md is as long as it is.

      - coat-bake-golden.txt never gained the two new potion-milk alleles, so
        CoatBakeGoldenTest was red on main. Two rows, both hashing identically to
        every other allele of a gene that paints nothing. Trivial — and red is red.

      - web.wasm was never rebuilt after common/ changed, so the horse designer was
        serving genetics from before Weakness and Darkness existed. A real 212-byte
        change, not build noise. The tell is exactly the one CLAUDE.md names: a git
        status showing common/ changed and wiki/horse-designer/wasm/ unchanged.

    A half-implemented fix. The intake file asked for two things and got one.
    `GeneAbilityHandler.flee` now asks `Passification.suppresses`, which stops the
    horse *choosing* you again — but a flee is not a target. A target is re-read
    every tick, so dropping it is enough; a flee is a `moveTo` twelve blocks away,
    already issued, and it runs itself out regardless of whether the reason still
    stands. So the Nightmare you had just paid in cake walked twelve blocks off
    before turning round, which is not what "it stops the moment you pay" means to
    anybody watching. `Passification.forget` now calls a new
    `GeneAbilityHandler.stopFleeingFrom`, keyed to that player only.

    That method is more careful than it looks, in two ways, and both were nearly got
    wrong. It drops only the entry keyed to the player who paid — a horse running
    from two people is still running from the other one, and picks it up on the next
    beat rather than being told here where to go. And flee's own no-candidate path
    stops the navigation *only when it was the thing that started it*: stopping
    unconditionally every beat would cut the legs off whatever goal holds the
    navigation instead, which on this breed is FoodTemptGoal walking a calmed horse
    back over — the exact loop the fix exists to make work.

    A false statement in shipped prose. netherhorse.json's own note said adding
    nether wart "does not change what any existing horse wants", and that wild
    horses "still only ever roll the first twelve entries". Neither is true. The
    schema is `EpiValue.category(ITEM, OFFERINGS.size())` and EpiRoll takes
    `rng.nextInt((int) v.max())`, so the length of the list *is* the width of the
    wild roll. Roughly one passifiable wild horse in seven now asks for nether wart
    or cake rather than a food you can grow, and a horse that wants nether wart is
    one you cannot buy off until you have been to the Nether.

    PotionMilkGene has the same shape by a different route. Its founder table
    spreads a fixed WILD_CARRIER_PERCENT across however many brews exist, so
    appending two dropped every existing brew's wild rate and put the locus's first
    two *negative* effects into the wild population. Ordinary wild mares can now
    hand back Weakness and Darkness milk.

    Both were put to the owner with options. Both stand: "fine — just document it".
    So they are documented, on gene-passification.html and gene-potion-milk.html,
    and the breed file's note now says what the code does instead of the opposite.

    This is the general lesson and it is not about this breed. A breed file looks
    like a thing that only configures itself. Two of its lines reached out and
    changed the wild population, and the author noticed neither — and then wrote a
    confident sentence saying they had not. A claim about blast radius in a handover
    note is a claim to check, not a claim to read.

    A band that could not draw. nightmare.json banded emberveins' `hue` to 0–10,
    with a note explaining that this reads redder and less orange than the gene's
    full range. The `hue` knob is read only by the rust expression, `Emr/Emr`; the
    glowing expression the breed actually carries, `Emb/Emb`, paints fixed hex and
    never looks at it. So the band stored and inherited a number nothing on that
    horse could draw, and the note described a difference that would not appear.
    Removed, and the note now says why the two breeds' veins look the same.

    Nothing about this was catchable by any tool in the repo: the gene key is real,
    the knob name is real, the range is legal. It needed reading the gene's spec to
    the end. Worth a thought if a "band is inert for this expression" check is ever
    cheap to write.


PROCESS, WHICH WAS THE OTHER HALF OF IT

    The commit shipped no hand-written wiki prose at all — the two breeds appeared
    in exactly two files under wiki/, and both are baked. Meanwhile 66 lines of
    design rationale were appended to intake/Implementation advice.txt, which is
    precisely backwards: intake is the inbox, it is meant to empty, and the wiki is
    the source of truth. That content is now on the pages it belongs to, and the
    appended block is gone.

    intake/Passification fleeing.txt is deleted rather than marked done, per START
    HERE step 3, now that both its required behaviours exist.

    Pages that were actively *wrong* rather than merely silent, which is the worse
    half of no-documentation:

      - gene-passification.html promised the veto was asked at "four chokepoints".
        Flee is the fifth. The box making that promise was right about the principle
        and wrong about the count — which is the failure mode it exists to warn
        about, sitting there for eight days.

      - the same page's "What it does not do" said "Passification is about
        targeting, and nothing else". The Java class doc was rewritten in the very
        same commit to say the opposite.

      - horse-care.html's tempt section said what counts as edible is genetic and
        "a carnivore does not come over for a carrot". A passification offering is
        now a favourite-tier lure whatever the diet says.

      - breeds.html's "carries neither tolerance locus" sentence was stale again —
        the identical defect the Enderpony session had to fix for Cloudtouched, on
        the identical sentence, five days later. Twice is a pattern. If a third
        breed lands without it, that sentence should be generated.

THE TWO TESTS, AND THE CHECKER

    NetherBreedsTest is the Dhampir's kind of test: five hundred founders of each
    breed, and every claim the files make about the pool pinned — the pairs, that
    the passification route really resolves to nether wart at adults-only and cake
    at any age, that the colour bands land where they say, and that the
    Nightmare's tail is *not* gold, which is the assertion a later tidy-up is most
    likely to break by copying the mane's band across.

    All seven cases passed first run, which is the moment to be suspicious rather
    than pleased. Two deliberate mutations went in — the emberveins pair changed
    to n/n, and a sclera band tightened past what any founder could satisfy — and
    both failed, in the two separate test methods, so the assertions are live
    rather than quietly reading empty values. Worth the sixty seconds every time.

    BreedBandsReachTest is the general form of the inert-band defect, and it is
    the more valuable of the two. For every breed in the catalogue it resolves the
    expression each pinned pair actually produces and fails any band on a knob
    none of them reads. This is only possible because "$hue" survives parsing as a
    Value.FromKnob *index* rather than being resolved away into an opaque
    supplier — had the spec engine been one step more eager, the check could not
    have been written at all.

    It was proved by putting the Nightmare's old hue band back and watching it go
    red, and its message names the knobs those pairs *do* read, so whoever trips
    it has somewhere to go rather than just a refusal. It sees data-driven genes
    only — a hand-written gene reads its knobs in Java, where there is nothing to
    walk — and it counts and reports what it skipped for that reason instead of
    implying a coverage it does not have.

    Then check-breed-heights.mjs, off the other loose end. Both wiki pages print a
    height range per breed, both hand-typed, both derived from the same JSON
    through BreedStatCurve.handsFor — and the two pages use *different*
    conventions on purpose, stable notation on breeds.html and decimal hands in
    the book, so one horse legitimately reads 16.1 on one page and 16.3 on the
    other. The script checks each page under its own convention and does not
    unify them.

    It reads BASELINE_HH and BIG_GAMMA out of the Java at run time rather than
    keeping a copy, and hard-fails if it cannot find them: a checker carrying its
    own stale constant is precisely the defect it exists to catch, and it would
    have gone green for exactly the wrong reason. It also fails on a breed with no
    row at all, which is the other half of how the two new breeds went unnoticed.

    One judgement call in it, made by the agent that wrote it and worth knowing:
    the stored size is itself a rounding of an exact hand figure, so a strict port
    demanded 13.7 where the Tarpan correctly prints 13.8. It therefore accepts any
    figure some scale inside the stored value's own rounding interval formats to.
    The cost is that it cannot see an error of a single step. The three it found
    were all larger than that.

    Which were: the Dhampir a whole hand too tall in the book (the one already
    known), and then the Oldenburg and the Rocky Mountain Horse both a step short
    in the table on breeds.html — neither suspected, both cross-confirmed by the
    book already agreeing with the JSON. Three hand-typed derived numbers wrong on
    two pages, found the first time anything recomputed them. That is the whole
    argument for the rule about derived numbers, arriving unprompted.
