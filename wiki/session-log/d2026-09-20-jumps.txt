2026-09-20 — Jumps, how a horse jumps at all, and a model architecture left half-migrated

    Started as “can we take the models from this mod” and ended with the mod’s
    first custom block model. Three existing Minecraft jump projects were checked and
    all three are All Rights Reserved for their assets — the one that
    splits its licences reserves precisely the half worth having — so the geometry here is
    drawn from the real-world object. That finding is on
    the jumps page so nobody repeats the search.

What shipped and was played

    A per-wood jump block: a rail spanning the block, standards
    drawn only at the ends of a run, a T-post every third block, and no height
    property — you stack them. It shipped wrong first: collision followed
    the model, so the rail sat under a horse’s 1.0 step height and got walked over. The fix
    is vanilla’s own fence trick — collide half a block taller than you draw —
    and the owner’s verdict was that it “now actually feels like a challenge”.
    The ground-pole framing that had justified the original was a rationalisation of a
    limitation, and was deleted rather than kept.

    Then how a ridden horse jumps, which turned out to matter more than the
    block. Vanilla charges: hold the key, a meter fills, the horse leaps on release at
    whatever it reached — so jump height is partly a fact about the rider’s thumb, and
    a well-bred horse ridden badly clears less than a mediocre one ridden well. In a mod whose
    argument is that genetics decide what a horse can do, that is the player’s hand on the
    scale. Pressing now launches at full height with a cooldown that drains the meter.
    Mojang had already shipped this on the camel: getJumpCooldown() is
    already in the interface, LocalPlayer.aiStep already skips its charge block when it
    is nonzero, and the HUD already shows the bar for it. Confirmed in play, gliding included.

The finding that paid for the whole thing

    A stack of jumps is the first instrument in the mod that makes jump visible —
    everything else prints the attribute. On its first outing it found
    gap 287: the Anglo-Arabian, a natural breed, jumps high
    enough to hurt itself landing. The instant-jump change did not cause that, it stopped
    hiding it — charging meant a horse only reached its ceiling if you held the
    key, and the reflex on a horse that leaps too far is to tap, which silently capped it.

The half-migrated part — read this before touching jumps

    The owner asked that the standards and the rails be independently coloured. As blockstate
    properties that is 12 x 12 x everything else = 13,824 block states, every one
    allocated at registry bootstrap on the server as well as the client, so the woods have to be
    block-entity data and the model has to read them per position.

    That architecture is built and proven; nothing is migrated onto it yet. The
    twelve per-wood blocks are still registered and still work. The new single
    horsegenetics:jump block exists beside them with a block entity, a working custom
    model and a blockstate — but no recipe, no loot table, no creative-tab entry and no
    screen. A client boots clean with no bake failures, which is what proves the codec parses and
    the model bakes. See the API notes for the shape,
    because everything written before 1.21.4 is wrong about it.

    What is left, in order: a two-slot screen on the block (rails wood, standards
    wood, the displaced plank pops back out — owner’s design, and it replaces both the
    stick-restyle and the plank-repaint); loot and a recipe on the new block; deleting the twelve
    old blocks and their thirty-six items; then three things that were all waiting on materials
    being data — a wall style (“wall” means cobblestone and
    brick, a material axis that is not the twelve woods), crossrails that span up to three
    blocks as one crossed pair rather than a row of separate X’s, and
    half and third height jumps as walkable ground poles. The last needs its own
    warning: stacked clearances do not add on a block grid, so a third-height jump on top
    of a full one clears exactly what the full one did.

    Also settled along the way: style is cosmetic — all three collide
    identically, so a stack of three means the same thing whatever it is built from, which is the
    only reason the height ladder stays an honest reading of genetics. And jumps live in their own
    creative tab for now, to be folded back into the horse tab once the rework drops the item
    count from thirty-six to about nine.
