2026-09-29 - the lead that snapped, and a seam one line away from the method you wanted


    Rider comfort piece 4, the one the treatment had deferred as "a decision
    first, not a build". The decision turned out to be answerable by reading,
    which is the theme of the whole piece.

    What the decision actually was. The Roadmap tab said: "the only seams are a
    mixin or an EntityJoinLevelEvent filter that would have to tell a snapped
    lead from one a player threw on the floor. Decide which before building
    it." The treatment had already decided - a mixin - and left the building
    session to find WHERE. That is the part that was genuinely open.

    Re-proved the premise first, per the note that said it was not re-proved.
    NeoForge 26.1.2 publishes no leash event: there is no class in its event
    package with "leash" in the name at all. Confirmed by unpacking the sources
    jar and grepping, not taken from the older note.

    The seam, and why the obvious one does not exist. Leashable.dropLeash() and
    removeLeash() are both one-line delegates to

        private static <E extends Entity & Leashable> void dropLeash(E, boolean, boolean)

    on the INTERFACE. Mixin cannot inject into an interface's private static
    method. 26.1.2 has moved a lot of behaviour into interfaces this way, so
    this is going to keep happening; it is written up on api-notes.html as its
    own entry.

    The answer is to stop looking for the method and look at the line it calls.
    Everything dropLeash does to the world it does through ordinary public
    methods: it spawns the item with Entity.spawnAtLocation(ServerLevel,
    ItemLike). Grepping the whole game for Items.LEAD reaching that method finds
    EXACTLY TWO callers, both inside Leashable - the drop itself, and the
    fallback in restoreLeashFromSave for a leash whose holder is gone on load.
    So one injection on one ordinary method covers every path, including the two
    that arrive from completely different directions: tickLeash for a snap, and
    a holder failing canInteractWithLevel for a broken knot. That is
    mixin/LeashDropMixin.

    The other end was harder than the drop. By the time the lead is dropped
    there is no player in the situation at all: a knot is the holder rather than
    a person, and dropLeash runs setLeashData(null) BEFORE spawnAtLocation, so
    even the knot is gone. The placer has to be written down when the lead goes
    on. mixin/LeashPlacerMixin does that on Mob.setLeashData - a concrete
    override of an abstract interface method, and therefore reachable where the
    rest of the leash plumbing is not - into a serialized leash_placer
    attachment.

    The nicest thing found all session. The fence case needs no rule of ours at
    all. Leashable.setLeashedTo calls setLeashData only when the animal had NO
    leash data; once it has some it takes a leashData.setLeashHolder(holder)
    branch that never reaches the mixin. That reads like a gap and is the
    opposite of one: tying a horse to a fence is always a SECOND attachment,
    because LeadItem.bindPlayerMobs only considers animals already held by you.
    So a knot inherits the player recorded a moment earlier, for free, out of
    vanilla's own control flow. The gametest pins it, because it is a claim
    about vanilla rather than about this mod.

    Two rules that are deliberately "do nothing". The attachment is NOT cleared
    when the lead comes off - the drop reads it after vanilla has untied the
    horse, so clearing on setLeashData(null) would wipe the answer an instant
    before it is asked for. And a null holder inside real leash data is left
    alone too, because that is a leash being restored from a save; clearing
    there would lose the placer of every fence-tied horse on every world load.
    Both were found by reading the call order rather than by testing.

    Two false starts in the gametest, both worth recording because both are
    traps for the next test in this file.

    First, it dropped a real lead on the ground to assert the fallback - and
    broke whistled_lead_comes_back, whose own assertItemEntityNotPresent reaches
    sixteen blocks and found this test's lead in the neighbouring plot. A
    gametest that litters the shared world fails its NEIGHBOURS, not itself,
    which is an expensive way to find out.

    Second, it needed a player the server's player list could find, and the only
    way to make one is makeMockServerPlayerInLevel - @Deprecated(forRemoval),
    and it really joins the player list, which fires this mod's join payloads
    down an EmbeddedChannel that has negotiated nothing: "Payload
    horsegenetics:gene_database_sync may not be sent to the client", and the run
    dies. The previous session's whistled_lead_comes_back had avoided that
    helper for a different reason and was right for a second one.

    So the test drives HorseLeads.giveRecordedLeadTo, split out from the lookup
    for exactly this reason, and the single line above it - the player-list
    lookup - is the only part of the feature left to the in-game check. That is
    an honest gap and it is first on the Verification tab.

    What is NOT the test's job: proving the mixins are applied. defaultRequire
    is 1, so a target that stops matching refuses to boot the game - a stronger
    guarantee than any assertion. What the test owns is everything that would
    still be wrong with both mixins happily applied, and the clear-the-record
    assertion was confirmed to fail ("a second drop would mint a second lead out
    of nothing") against a copy with the clear deleted. All 22 gametests green
    afterwards, no ERROR anywhere in the run.

    The risk that is not in any test. LeashDropMixin sits on
    Entity.spawnAtLocation, which every item any entity ever drops passes
    through. The guard is one identity comparison against Items.LEAD and an
    instanceof, but if it is ever wrong, items go missing all over the game. The
    Verification tab's highest-value check is therefore not about leads at all -
    it is "kill a horse wearing tack, shear a braid, break a cart, mine a block,
    and confirm everything still drops what it always did".

    behaviour.leads_return now governs both halves and its comment says so; it
    used to end by stating that the vanilla cases were excluded.

    Open for the next session: rider comfort is down to one piece, and it is the
    fade. It starts with a rendering spike, not a feature - force a fixed alpha
    on the ridden horse in GeneticHorseRenderer and look at what the generated
    coat, the cutie-mark layer, the emissive layer and the worn gear do under
    it. Translucency also changes sort order and can show a horse's insides
    through itself. If the coat cannot be drawn cleanly half-transparent the
    standing instruction is to stop and report rather than ship a guess.
