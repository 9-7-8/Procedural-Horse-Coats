2026-09-24 — stage five: the bank collects what a horse drops, breeds a pair you turn out at stud, and lets the mare out to foal

    The last two jobs on the six-stage build order on wiki/horse-stasis.html, both hung off the
    rotation stage four built: Advanced's drop buffer and Spacer's in-bank breeding. Everything
    the four chamber tiers advertise is now built; only the Emergency chamber is left, and it
    needs a health-threshold hook rather than more of the bank. One bullet, per
    intake/START HERE.txt — the owner chose "stage 5 only, then stop" when asked.

Milk was never buildable, and finding that out is most of why the buffer is small.

    The roadmap called this "Advanced's 9-slot milk/drops buffer" from the day it was sketched.
    It cannot be. Every milk locus in the mod — MilkGene, PotionMilkGene, MagicMilkVolumeGene —
    emits GeneAbility.Yield or YieldCharges, whose trigger is OnInteract, and
    GeneAbilityHandler.onHorseTick explicitly no-ops on both of them: milk happens because a
    player holds a bucket out to a horse, and there is no passive milking path anywhere in the
    mod for a bank to copy. A bank that filled itself with milk buckets would have been
    inventing a mechanic rather than shelving one, and would have made a milkable horse worth
    more in a box than in a field.

    What is left is one verb. GeneAbility.Produce — an item id, an interval, a min and a max —
    and today exactly one gene emits it (EggLayerGene, eight laying alleles, the interval
    written on the allele copy). So the buffer collects what a horse does on its own, and the
    Gameplay tab now says that in as many words, with a warn box explaining why milk is not
    among it. The page had been promising something for days that the code it describes cannot
    do.

The buffer reads the same call the live translator reads.

    HorseAbilities.activeFor(genotype, epigenome) is pure common/ and needs no entity, so
    common/…/horse/StasisProduce.java walks it, filters to Produce, and answers with a list of
    (geneKey, item, count). No second table, no per-gene knowledge in the stasis code: a gene
    that gains a produce ability is collectable in a bank the day it ships.

    The epigenome has to be passed. EggLayerGene is an EpigeneticAbilityContribution and its
    interval lives on the allele copy; activeFor(genotype) alone hands back the midpoint, so
    every horse in every bank would have laid at exactly the same rate and nothing would ever
    have reported it. There is now a verification bullet aimed straight at that: two horses with
    different intervals must visibly fill the buffer at different speeds.

The cooldown had to become durable, and that is an improvement on the live path.

    GeneAbilityHandler keeps its produce cooldowns in a static Map<String, Long> keyed by
    uuid/geneKey/produce — lost on restart, and cleared wholesale past 8192 entries. A shelved
    horse has no tick to carry one at all, so the bank stamps the horse's own HORSE_COOLDOWNS
    attachment under "produce:<geneKey>", the same attachment shearing and the yield genes
    already use. The consequence is the pleasant one: a shelved layer keeps a real clock across
    a restart where a live one lays again on every boot. An absent stamp reads as ready, which
    is HorseCooldownsAttachment's own documented rule, so a horse shelved with an egg nearly due
    does not lose it.

Priced, then paid — because a bank has no floor to drop an egg on.

    A live horse calls spawnAtLocation and cannot fail. A bank can: nine slots fill up. So
    StasisDrops.due reads what is owed and changes nothing, the block entity's roomFor runs the
    insert against a throwaway SimpleContainer, and only if *all* of it fits does
    StasisDrops.stamp mark the cooldown spent. A full buffer therefore pauses production rather
    than destroying a yield, and emptying it resumes on the next turn. The dry run is over the
    whole batch rather than per stack, so half a yield is never taken either.

A condition a chamber cannot answer refuses, negation included.

    GeneAbilityHandler.conditionHolds reads the live entity and the level for nearly every flag
    in the vocabulary — in_water, night, raining, has_rider, near_jukebox. A horse in a chamber
    is in none of those, and they are not false about it so much as meaningless.
    StasisProduce.holdsInStasis answers only adult, baby, sex_male and sex_female and refuses
    everything else — and refuses Not(unanswerable) too, because "not raining" is no more a fact
    about a bottle than "raining" is. Guessing either way would have been a gene quietly
    behaving differently indoors. Nothing shipped is affected: the egg-layer's condition is
    ALWAYS and no spec gene emits a produce yet.

    "adult" means reading a saved AgeableMob's age, and there is no constant to import for it:
    vanilla writes the literal "Age". A rename would not throw — every shelved foal would simply
    start producing as though it were grown — so the gametest now snapshots the same horse twice,
    once grown and once with setBaby(true), and asserts both directions.

The buffer is in the supply container, and that is the capability's doing.

    Nine more slots on the existing goods container rather than a third SimpleContainer beside
    it, with FIRST_DROP_SLOT telling them apart. The item-handler capability wraps *one*
    container: a second would have needed a second registration, something to join the two
    handlers, and a second copy of the per-slot rules to keep in step. As it is,
    StasisBankCapability did not change at all when the buffer shipped, and a hopper under the
    bank pulls eggs out of it for free. The cost is that SUPPLY_SLOTS now means twelve, which is
    why every reader goes through HorseStasisBankMenu.supplyX/supplyY instead of doing the
    arithmetic twice.

In-bank breeding: a mark on the item, not state on the block.

    A Spacer chamber is turned out "at stud" by clicking its row on the Browse tab. The mark is
    a new Boolean data component, stasis_at_stud, which is the same call stasis_snapshot made
    and for the same three reasons: it syncs with the ordinary slot sync so the tab can draw it
    with no packet of its own, it travels with the chamber between banks, and there is no copy on
    a block entity to drift from the chamber it describes. A set of marked UUIDs on the block
    would have needed syncing, pruning when a chamber left, and a rule for the same horse being
    in two banks.

    The mark alone is not permission. StasisStud.atStud asks the tier as well, because
    StasisUpgradeRecipe copies components across and a mark can therefore outlive the rung that
    bought it. StasisBrowseRow.atStud() counts only rows whose own tier breedsInBank(), and
    StasisBrowseTest pins that.

    Toggling it is AbstractContainerMenu.clickMenuButton with the chamber slot as the id —
    JumpMenu's shape, and the rule BenchNamePayload's own comment states from the other side: an
    int fits in a menu button and a string does not. The server re-checks the tier and the
    occupancy, because a menu button is a packet and a packet is whatever somebody sent.

The bank invents no breeding rule. That is the whole of why StasisMating is short.

    Owner, asked directly, on where the foal comes from and how often: "only creates a new foal
    once per the mare's heat, same cycle as on land". That is not a cap this had to invent, it is
    the rule it had to reuse — ReproRules.mayTryNaturally, unchanged, the same once-per-heat stop
    that keeps a paddock from breeding flat out. The stallion's three-covers-a-day tiredness is
    coversOn read off his own stored record; the health gate is COVER_HEALTH. Nothing in
    common/…/horse/StasisMating.java is a stasis number.

    Her cycle reaches her on a shelf for free, and that is the single property that makes the
    feature possible at all: every time in a Reproduction is an absolute game tick and the state
    is *derived* from those anchors (ReproRules.stateAt), so a mare who has spent three cycles in
    a chamber is in exactly the phase she would have been in standing in a field. It was true
    before stasis existed. StasisMatingTest walks a whole cycle past a shelved mare and asserts
    she comes into heat on her own.

The design call of the stage: the pregnancy is carried in the chamber, the mare comes out to foal.

    The roadmap tab had been treating the foal as the hard part — "a foal is a new horse, and
    nothing here has yet had to make one out of two stored tags". It turned out not to need
    making from tags at all.

    Gestation is free: a Pregnancy is two ticks and a list of embryos and it advances on a shelf
    exactly as it would in a field. Birth is not free, and the reason is worth stating plainly.
    HorseBreedingHandler.populateFoal names the foal under the dam's owner's NamingPolicy, tames
    it to her owner, gives it a quarter of her bond, files it in HorseAncestryData, puts it in
    her wild band, writes its HORSE_SOCIAL birth record and tells everyone nearby. Every one of
    those is a fact about the dam, and half of them are read off her live entity. Reimplementing
    that against a saved tag would have been a second breeding path to keep in step for ever —
    the exact class of drift CLAUDE.md keeps a table of rules about.

    So the bank does the cheap half and hands the expensive half back: at her due date it
    releases her beside the block and ReproHandler's ordinary SCAN tick foals her within two
    seconds. A bank-bred foal is not *like* every other foal, it is one. She is a ticking entity
    for the minutes around the birth rather than for the gestation, which is the trade the whole
    feature is about.

    It will not foal into a wall. foalingSpot wants a position whose block and the one above it
    both have an empty collision shape — two, because a horse is nearly two tall and setting one
    down inside a wall is how a released animal suffocates in a cellar. The four horizontal
    neighbours first, then a small box round the block. With nothing found she stays in her
    chamber, a second ContainerData value goes up, and the Supply tab says "a mare is due — clear
    a space beside the bank". That flag is not saved: it is a fact about the room, and a reloaded
    bank that is quiet for a minute beats one that remembers a wall somebody has knocked down.

    Two deliberate omissions. No carrot bias — ARMED_CARROTS lives on a live horse and is spent
    by a breeding somebody is watching, so a bank pairing folds GameteBias.NONE both sides, which
    is what a wild cover draws. And no crowd cap: DEFAULT_NATURAL_CAP counts entities in a chunk
    radius, a bank holds none, and the number of chambers the player marked is already their own
    cap.

StasisCare grew rather than splitting, on purpose.

    StasisDrops and StasisStud both reach into a stored horse, and both do it through StasisCare
    rather than naming a key of their own: health(), isBaby(), cooldowns(), repro(),
    putCooldowns(), putRepro(), and one generic putAttachment that creates the attachments
    compound if the tag has none. So the class's claim changed from "the only place that reaches
    into a snapshot's contents" to "every string that has to match something Minecraft or
    NeoForge wrote is in this one file, where one gametest can walk the lot of them", which is
    the claim that was load-bearing anyway.

    putAttachment is deliberately unlike putHunger, which refuses to invent one. Hunger's
    absence means the horse was never charged for its healing, so writing one would be healing it
    for free; a missing cooldown or reproductive record means only that nothing has happened to
    this horse yet, and the bank is about to be the thing that happens.

The gametest now ends by really letting a horse out, and it also had a flake.

    stasis_tag_is_readable gained the Age key, a cooldown round trip, a reproductive-record round
    trip, and then the half no amount of tag-reading proves: it releases a real Horse from the
    tag StasisCare wrote to and asserts ReproHandler.of(released).lastNaturalTry() is the number
    the bank put there. That is exactly the call the bank makes when a mare is due, and every step
    of it fails silently — a wrong attachment id writes into nowhere, a broken codec parses to a
    default, and either way she comes out of the chamber empty and nobody is told she lost
    anything. The release has to happen after horse.discard(), because a tag remembers its UUID
    and addFreshEntity refuses a second entity on one id; that refusal is alreadyLoose's whole
    reason for existing.

    Fixing a flake in the same test on the way. The test spawns a horse, waits ten ticks for it
    to be founded, and snapshots it — but a NeoForge attachment is materialised on first access,
    and the thing that usually asks for hunger is HorseCareHandler on a slow tick phased by
    entity id, which by tick ten may or may not have come round. Without a hunger attachment
    StasisCare.putHunger correctly refuses to invent one, the upkeep reports healing nothing, and
    the healing assertion fails on perhaps one run in three with nothing at all wrong with the
    bank. It now asks for the hunger itself first, with a comment saying why. Confirmed by
    stashing the session's work and running the test on a clean HEAD, where it passed — the
    first run against the new code had failed on that assertion and it would have been very easy
    to go hunting for a regression that was not there.
