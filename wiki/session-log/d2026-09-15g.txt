2026-09-15, later still — fourteen breeds, four drafts that were two horses, and a stub declined

    The fourth intake batch, and the first run at more than five at a time: the queue from the Posavac
    to the Hucul Pony, alphabetically. 79 breeds ship now. Sixteen intake files went in
    and fourteen breeds came out, because two pairs of files were each one horse twice.

The batch

    Posavac, Debao Pony, Dlahest, Fell Pony, Finnhorse, Florida Cracker Horse, Gelderlander,
    Gotland Pony, Baise Horse, Guoxia, Henson Horse, Hequ, Highland Pony, Hucul Pony.

    - Two pairs of drafts were the same breed. Croatian Posavina Horse.md
        and Posavac.md are one horse; Hucul Horse.md and
        Hucul Pony.md are another. Both pairs were merged rather than shipped twice, and
        both pairs disagreed with themselves in ways worth recording.

    - The two Posavac drafts gave opposite coat identities. One led bay and seal
        brown with flaxen explicitly unsupported; the other led chestnut with flaxen as a defining
        trait. Neither cited a coat survey. The better-sourced draft won — it is also the one
        carrying the peer-reviewed heights (Ivankovi etal.) — so bay leads
        with chestnut a real ninth and a mild flaxen on it. The disagreement is written into
        the breed file rather than averaged silently, because the loser is not obviously wrong.

    - The Hucul's reputation and its genotype disagree, and the genotype won. One
        draft was evidence-driven (a published Polish allele-frequency study), the other
        studbook-driven (what the registry admits). The study makes dun about a quarter of the herd
        where the prose everywhere calls it the signature. This is the
        Domestic Mountain Pony situation again and was resolved the same
        way; a 2019 study finding recorded Hucul colours disagreeing with genotype is the
        justification. Tobiano is the one value that splits the two drafts and is a judgement call.

    - Every draft's size scale was wrong. All fourteen. This is now four batches
        running and should be assumed rather than checked for. The Dlahest draft wanted
        ×1.00–1.11 for a 148–156cm horse and the Guoxia ×0.42 for an
        86cm one. All were re-derived from cited heights through
        BreedStatCurve. Two landed exactly on shipped neighbours as a check: the Florida
        Cracker on the Mustang's [0.857, 0.984], and the Gelderlander's
        1.238 on the Irish Draught's 17.0hh.

    - The Guangxi is properly the Baise Horse and ships under that name (owner's
        call). Its own draft opened by arguing the point, and it is right: Guangxi is the autonomous
        region, and the Debao Pony and the Guoxia in this same batch come from the same ground.

    - The Hucul is Romanian here, on intake/breedbycountry.md, over
        both drafts leaning toward Poland as holder of the Studbook of Origin. The owner's table wins;
        the homeland genuinely spans four countries and a breed is pinned to a nation regardless.

    - The Gotland ships single cream and cannot ship double. Its conservation
        profile excludes the homozygote, not the allele, so Cr/N is in the pool and
        Cr/Cr is simply absent from it — a founder is never cremello, though
        breeding two carriers still gets there. Grey and tobiano are excluded outright by the same
        profile. It also has the rare measured negative: a published sample of 28 found
        DMRT3 at zero frequency, so the no-gait call is evidence rather than omission.

    - The Guoxia forces dun wild on purpose, which is the most interesting
        restraint in the batch. Its primitive markings are described in every source and tied to the
        tested D allele in none, so naming a dun pool would assert a molecular result
        nobody has. Neither it nor the Debao borrows a dwarfism allele for being tiny; the size band
        carries both, as it does for the Domestic Mountain Pony.

    - Hequ is the first breed to use strains for a
        regional colour cline. Three named plateau varieties — Jiaode, Suoke, Kesheng
        — differing in grey alone, Jiaode much the greyest. They differ in size in life
        and cannot here, since a strain may override gene pools and nothing else; the file says so.

    - The Finnhorse's interesting half is its minorities. Chestnut is past 90% by
        studbook policy rather than ancient type — the 1907 closure refused grey, palomino,
        piebald and white as foreign — so the pool's real content is bay at ~6%, black at ~1.2%
        and percent-level traces of grey, cream, silver, dun, roan, rabicano and SW1.
        Sabino is deliberately absent despite the breed having a common sabino-like pattern,
        because that pattern is documented as non-SB1. Its four official sections are performance
        categories over one gene pool and are pointedly not strains.

Two disorder genes went to the roadmap, not into a guess

    FIS (foal immunodeficiency syndrome) is the sharpest health gap the mod has: a
    recessive lethal that about 38% of real Fell Ponies carry — 82 of 214 in the
    foundational screen — shared with the Dales Pony, and tested for by the societies since 2010.
    A Fell that carries nothing is the one thing the real breed is not, and both breeds ship clear and
    say so in their files. WFFS is the real modern concern across the Dutch warmblood
    population and the Gelderlander names no rate for it; PLOD1 is Friesian dwarfism and is
    not a substitute, which is the trap worth flagging. Both on
    roadmap §12 (retired 2026-09-21; PLOD1 shipped).

One draft was declined

    The Dutch Warmblood is not in this batch and its file stays in intake/.
    The draft is a 28-line stub — no breed JSON, no loci, no frequencies, no disorders, no
    population, no size — and ends by offering to write a real one later. Shipping it would have
    meant inventing a warmblood off the Hanoverian and calling it a translation. Owner's call was to
    skip it and ship fourteen rather than backfill, so the batch is 14 and not 15.
