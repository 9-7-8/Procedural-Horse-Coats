2026-09-24 — stage four: the bank feeds and mends the horses in it, and a gametest caught the bug that would have made it do nothing

    Stage four of the six-stage build order on wiki/horse-stasis.html: three supply slots on
    the Horse Stasis Bank — feed, water, and the empties the water leaves — and a server-only
    ticker that slowly heals the horses it is holding. This is the first thing the bank does
    *to* a horse rather than shelving one and showing it to you, which is what the whole rest
    of that roadmap is made of. The drop buffer, in-bank breeding and the emergency chamber
    are still planned and still on that page's Roadmap tab. One bullet, per intake/START
    HERE.txt.

The economy is the field's, and that is nearly the whole design.

    HorseCareHandler already heals a live horse under three conditions — it is hurt, its diet
    is one that items can feed at all, and there is water within a few blocks — charging
    Hunger.COST_PER_HEALTH a point and never spending it below Hunger.STARVING, with eating
    putting hunger back. The bank changes none of that. It supplies the two things a shelved
    horse cannot go and find: a mouthful out of the feed slot, and the pond, as a meter topped
    up from a water bucket.

    Everything else falls out for free, which is the argument for doing it this way rather
    than inventing a bank-specific number. A blood-drinker is no more feedable in a chamber
    than it is in a field. A starving horse eats first and gets health out of the same turn.
    Food without water is banked rather than wasted. A horse that is not hurt costs nothing at
    all — the bank will not open a bucket or take a mouthful for it — so a bank of fifty well
    horses is not a standing bill, which was the failure mode worth designing against.

    The arithmetic is common/horse/StasisUpkeep, pure Java, with StasisUpkeepTest pinning
    every one of those claims. neoforge/server/StasisCare does the tag surgery. The split is
    the same one StasisTier/StasisSnapshot made in stage one and for the same reason.

Healing is a tier capability, and it is the tick gate. (Owner, mid-session.)

    The first cut healed every chamber alike. The owner's correction was that the bank should
    only tick when it holds a chamber of sufficiently high type, and otherwise not tick at
    all. So StasisTier gained heals(), true from INTERMEDIATE up.

    Which rung was the only real design call, and it is not arbitrary: searching and healing
    are one capability with two uses, namely the bank being able to look inside the chamber.
    Basic's whole contract is that it cannot, and a bank that cannot look inside cannot know
    that the horse is hurt, what it eats, or how much of its bar is missing. So one eye of
    ender buys both, and StasisTierTest now asserts heals() == searchable() for every tier so
    the two can never drift apart. That test's old "each rung adds exactly one capability"
    rule had to go with it; the rule now is that each rung adds something.

    The gate itself is a cached boolean on the block entity, recomputed only when a chamber
    slot moves. A block entity's ticker is chosen per blockstate and this block has none, so
    the ticker is attached unconditionally and the gate is its first line: a bank of Basic
    chambers, or of empties, or an empty bank, costs one field read a tick and never scans,
    reads a tag or touches a supply. Past that gate the tick is three more refusals — is it
    this tick (one turn every 30, phase-staggered by block position, the same interval
    HorseCareHandler gives a live horse), whose turn is it (a saved cursor walks the grid one
    slot a turn), and is that one hurt (two cheap field reads) — before anything decodes a
    record or spends anything.

    One chamber per turn is also the answer to "fifty-four horses is not free": a bank costs
    the same whether it holds one chamber or every slot, and what a fuller bank costs is
    speed, not server time. A bank is a slow hospital and it gets slower the more patients it
    has, which is a legible rule and the honest one.

The supply slots are pipeable; the chamber grid deliberately is not. (Owner's ask.)

    Stage two chose to have the block *hold* a container rather than *be* one, so nothing
    outside could file or fetch a horse. That call stands. But feed and water are bulk goods
    that run out, and keeping a stud farm's bank topped up by hand is exactly the chore
    automation exists for — so the bank now registers an item-handler capability over the
    supply container and nothing else. Hoppers, droppers and other mods' pipework can push hay
    and buckets in and pull empties out; none of it can see a chamber.

    The per-slot rules are written once: NeoForge's VanillaContainerWrapper asks
    Container.canPlaceItem before inserting, so the predicates already written for the menu are
    the rules a pipe obeys. Feed in the feed slot, water in the water slot, nothing at all into
    the empties, which is an output.

    26.1.2 has no IItemHandler. The capability is Capabilities.Item.BLOCK, typed
    ResourceHandler<ItemResource> — NeoForge's newer transfer API, not the name every tutorial
    still uses. The patched HopperBlockEntity falls back to that same capability when its
    target is not a Container, which is what makes a vanilla hopper work. Written up on
    wiki/api-notes.html#item-capability; unverified against a real hopper.

The gametest earned its place on its first run.

    A stored horse is a saveWithoutId tag on an item, and the upkeep reaches into it by name —
    Health, the max_health attribute, the hunger and horse_record attachments. Every one of
    those names fails silently: rename one and the reads return defaults, nothing throws,
    nothing is logged, and the bank quietly never heals anything again. So stage four shipped
    with stasis_tag_is_readable, a gametest that takes a real horse, hurts it, snapshots it
    exactly as a capture would, and asserts both that the numbers come back out and that one
    turn of upkeep actually moves the health in the tag.

    It failed immediately, on real code that had been reasoned about carefully:

        the entity says 53.0 and its saved tag reads 0.0

    A saved entity's `attributes` list holds only the attribute instances something has
    actually materialised. AttributeMap.getInstance is a computeIfAbsent, but getValue — which
    getMaxHealth() goes through — reads the entity type's AttributeSupplier without creating
    anything. So a horse can be saved with a perfectly good maximum health and an attributes
    list of []. Reading the bar out of that list alone, which is what the roadmap's sketch
    implied and what the first implementation did, would have made every bank decide every
    horse was unhurt and heal nothing, for ever, with nothing in the log and no crash.

    Maximum health now comes from the horse's own record, which is where
    HorseRecords.applyTraitsToEntity gets it from in the first place; the saved list is the
    fallback for an unconverted vanilla horse with no papers of ours. The general lesson is on
    wiki/api-notes.html#saved-attributes: read a value from wherever it was written from, not
    from the tag.

    Worth saying plainly, because it is the reusable part: this was not found by reasoning, by
    a code review, or by a green build. Three separate reads of the NeoForge and Minecraft
    sources went into writing that code and none of them caught it. It was found by a test
    that put a real horse through the real path, in the first thirty seconds it ran.

    The test also needed a second correction of its own: a horse is founded on a later tick
    than the one it spawns on (HorseFoundingTickHandler), so snapshotting it immediately was
    testing a horse with no papers — a case the bank never meets. It now waits ten ticks and
    fails loudly if the record still is not there, so a broken premise reads as a broken
    premise rather than as a broken bank.

What is now waiting to be looked at

    All of it. The gametest proves the tag can be read and written and that one turn moves the
    health inside a chamber; nothing else about stage four has been seen in a running game.
    The whole healing loop, the Supply tab's four status lines, the water meter counting down,
    the empty bucket coming back, hunger actually being charged, and every part of the hopper
    plumbing are on wiki/horse-stasis.html's Verification tab. The one that matters most is
    that no hopper can reach a chamber in either direction: a hopper that can drain a bank of
    horses is a disaster rather than a nuisance.

    Two numbers were picked rather than derived and may want tuning once they have been
    played: one bucket is worth 100 health points, and a hay bale feeds a stored horse the
    same Hunger.Food.HAY rung it feeds a live one.
