The tack-fit bug closed, and the recorded diagnosis was wrong

    “The saddle now fits resized breeds correctly.” That closed
    the last unverified thing hanging over the tack work, and it is worth keeping why
    the roadmap had it wrong for so long. The entry said equipment layers did not inherit the
    horse’s genetic scale. They always did — LivingEntityRenderer
    scales the pose stack and then iterates the layers, inside one push and pop. The
    real cause was that vanilla applies MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F) in
    LayerDefinitions, one level above the mesh function, and
    HdHorseModel is a copy of the mesh function — so the transform was
    never in the part that was copied and its absence is invisible on inspection.

    Which means it was never about resizing at all. The transform is
    scaled(f).translated(0, 24.016 × (1 − f), 0), so tack sat
    10% large and 2.4 units high on every horse including scale 1.0.
    The size locus multiplied a gap that was always there, which is exactly why it read as
    “worse on big horses” and sent the original diagnosis hunting in the renderer.
    Two roadmap sections were deleted rather than marked done: the scale defect, and one that
    still claimed “nothing in the game dyes a saddle”.

Eight fittings became fifteen

    Redstone, ender pearl, basalt, bone and prismarine, then lapis lazuli and coal. Three of
    those sit close to an existing one, and each was pulled away from its
    material’s own average colour rather than set to it — basalt cooler and
    lighter than netherite, bone creamier and darker than quartz, ender pearl deeper than
    prismarine. Two fittings a player cannot tell apart are worth one option, not two.

    Coal is deliberately not 0x000000, and that is a trap rather than a
    taste call. These colours feed getArmorLayerTintColor, where
    returning zero means skip this layer entirely — so pure black
    would have rendered no hardware at all rather than black hardware. The tooltip also
    learned to consult materials before dyes, or a redstone fitting would have reported
    itself as “red”, naming a material the bench will not accept in that slot.

Leather horse armour, and two guesses that had to die first

    It needed no new artwork. Vanilla’s leather.png is
    already a pure greyscale tint mask — 624 texels, four flat tones, zero coloured
    — which is why vanilla’s dyed armour looks as good as it does. All the work
    was splitting leather_overlay.png, the part vanilla never tints: 417 texels
    of brown trim, 79 of metal, 92 of near-black shadow left deliberately undyeable because
    shadow reads as depth rather than as material.

    That split was measured, not argued, and the arguing lost twice. The
    saddle’s rule — hardware is “R equals G equals B” — does
    not transfer, because the overlay holds twenty-odd greys rather than three. First guess:
    the dark greys are outlines. Wrong; a false-colour map came back with the bag outlines
    green, meaning they are dark brown and never were grey. Second guess: hardware
    is grey above 0x3E. Wrong; at that threshold every metal bar renders as a
    dark body speckled with bright dots, because a bar is shadow plus highlight and
    the cut ran through the middle of it. The boundary is 0x20, where thresholds
    plateau and each bar comes out solid. Three attempts at a reasoned answer, one
    picture to settle it.

    Two hazards worth carrying forward. There is no
    leather_horse_armor.json: the asset is equipment/leather.json,
    and that one file also serves every leather chestplate, helmet, legging and boot in the
    game, so the override rewrites horse_body only and copies the three humanoid
    entries verbatim. And vanilla’s crafting-grid dye had to keep working without
    getting worse than vanilla — it reads a stack’s dye once and hands it
    to every dyeable layer, so the new layers would have turned a grid-dyed armour one flat
    colour over straps and buckles alike. They are pinned to their undyed constants whenever
    no bench tint is present.

A bug found by reading the commit, not by playing it

    The bench would have destroyed a grid-dyed armour’s colour, silently.
    Reviewing what had just been pushed: undyedFor treated any armour without a
    bench tint as plain brown, but a player can arrive with an armour dyed blue in a crafting
    grid years earlier. Recolouring only its trim would have written the blanket back to
    leather and thrown the dye away without a word. It now seeds the blanket from
    dyed_color. The failure mode is the nasty kind — you get a result, it
    simply is not the one you asked for — so it is a named check on
    the verification list rather than
    something to stumble into.

    Three PowerShell attempts were also spent on one line that converts RGB to signed ARGB,
    which exists precisely so nobody hand-computes it. 0xFF000000 is neither a
    long nor a uint in PowerShell — it is Int32 -16777216 — so the
    arithmetic overflowed one way, then underflowed the other. It builds the value from four
    bytes now and has no literal in it at all.
