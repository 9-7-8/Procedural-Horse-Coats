2026-09-15, later again — fifteen breeds, and three drafts that disowned their own numbers

    The fifth intake batch, and the first one to ship the full count asked for: the queue from the
    Jutland to the Murgese, alphabetically. 94 breeds ship now. Fifteen files went in
    and fifteen breeds came out — no duplicates, no stubs, and nothing in the batch needed a gene
    the mod has not got, which is the first time that has been true.

The batch

    Jutland, Kaimanawa, Karabair, Karakachan Horse, Kentucky Mountain Saddle Horse, Kiso Horse,
    Kladruber, Konik, Mangalarga Marchador, Medimurje Horse, Misaki Horse, Missouri Fox Trotter,
    Mongolian Horse, Montenegrin Domestic Hilly Horse, Murgese.

    - Three drafts contradicted their own cited sources, and the sources won every time.
        The Misaki is the clearest: its own prose says its JSON block is not consistent with the
        99-horse study it cites and should not be used, and gives corrected figures that reconstruct
        79.2% bay, 12.9% black and 8.0% chestnut. Those shipped. The Kiso's allele figures would have
        produced chestnut at a tenth of the rate its own 125-horse census recorded, and the
        Kaimanawa's at a twentieth of the field tally. The lesson is now four batches old and
        worth stating plainly: read the draft's evidence, not its JSON.

    - The Kiso's dun pool is the most deliberate thing in the batch. The census
        recorded a dorsal stripe on 66.4% of the breed and a dun dilution on only 4%. Those two
        numbers cannot both come from D. So d1 — non-dun with primitive
        marking — was solved for directly (it lands near 0.40) and D sits near
        absent at 0.02. Raising D to hit the stripe count would have painted two thirds of
        the breed with a body dilution nobody has ever observed in it. The same reasoning the
        Domestic Mountain Pony used, arrived at from the opposite end.

    - Every draft's size scale was wrong. All fifteen. Five batches running now.
        All were re-derived from the cited heights through BreedStatCurve. Two land exactly
        on shipped neighbours as the check: the Kladruber's 1.238 on the Irish Draught's
        17.0hh, and its 1.048 on the Suffolk Punch's 16.0.

    - The Montenegrin Domestic Hilly Horse was the owner's call, and it ships. Its
        draft never mentions the Bosnian horse, but claims the same native name
        (Domaći brdski konj) and the same English alias as the
        Domestic Mountain Pony already shipped under Bosnia and
        Herzegovina. They are sister national populations of one transboundary Balkan type, and FAO
        DAD-IS does hold a separate Montenegrin record. Owner's decision was to ship it as Montenegro's
        own record rather than treat it as a re-supply — with the relationship written
        into the breed file, including the fact that nobody has shown the two populations to
        be genetically distinct. Grey is the only real difference in the pools: heavy here, excluded by
        the programme there.

    - The Medimurje's draft argued against itself, and the culprit was a three-letter
        abbreviation. Its lede demands a black-only breed with agouti fixed recessive; its own
        body then explains that the “only black and grey” rule comes from a genomic study of
        the Italian Murgese, coded MUR, and is an unrelated breed. The bay-dominant pool
        shipped. The actual Murgese is in this same batch and is black-only for its own, real reasons
        — the only natural breed here with both base loci fixed.

    - Three gaited breeds, and two of their drafts assumed the mod had no gait locus.
        It does. The Kentucky Mountain Saddle Horse, the Missouri Fox Trotter and the Mangalarga
        Marchador all ship DMRT3 near-fixed, alongside the existing gaited breeds. The gait
        itself remains a roadmap item; the genotype is real regardless.

    - One of the three is a lethal-white breed and one deliberately is not. The
        Missouri Fox Trotter's registry documents frame, so it carries O at a real rate and
        two founders can throw a lethal white. The Mangalarga Marchador's does not, so frame is forced
        wild — seeding it would have manufactured a breeding hazard the real population has not
        got.

    - Two breeds lock three loci at once — the Konik (E,
        a, D, so every founder is blue dun) and the Kladruber (E,
        a, plus strains that own grey). The Konik's grey is forced wild on purpose:
        “mouse grey” is a description of a dun, and seeding STX17 would whiten
        the one thing the breed looks like. The Murgese carries the same trap under the name
        “iron grey”, which is blue roan.

    - The Kladruber and the Kaimanawa are the second and third breeds to use strains,
        after Hequ. The Kladruber's are the historically separate grey and black court herds, differing
        in grey alone over a fixed black base. The Kaimanawa's are the measured north–south split:
        every grey horse ever sampled was southern and of one maternal haplogroup, so the Northern
        strain forces grey wild and the Southern is near-fixed for it.

    - Eight files' tolerance pools were snapped onto the three tiers
        breeds.html documents (near-fixed 60/35/5, partial 5/30/65, light 15/85).
        They had been written with bespoke shares that the page's own vocabulary could not describe, and
        the page is the thing that has to stay true. The Kladruber and the Missouri Fox Trotter carry no
        tolerance locus at all and are named in that page's neutral list.
