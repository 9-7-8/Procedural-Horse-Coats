2026-09-24 — four asks about the horse menu, and a chart of every horse in the world

    A session of the owner's own queue, in the order it arrived, all of it on the
    screens rather than the simulation.

    Tack goes to the top of Overview. The wearing-count line sat below Body and
    Draught; it now opens the page, above Sex. The reasoning is worth keeping
    because it generalises: what a horse has on is the one fact on that page
    likely to have changed since you last opened it, and everything under it is a
    fact about the animal. Bond gained the rule it used to inherit from the block
    that moved.

    E from the saddle opens this mod's menu rather than vanilla's horse
    inventory. The interesting half is where the key is taken:
    HorseInfoKeyHandler consumes it on ClientTickEvent.Pre, which Minecraft.tick
    fires before handleKeybinds — the loop whose keyInventory.consumeClick()
    reaches isServerControlledInventory() and sends OPEN_INVENTORY. The obvious
    alternative, letting vanilla open and swapping the screen on
    ScreenEvent.Opening, is wrong twice over: that screen is a container screen
    the server opened, so a plain screen in its place leaves a menu bound to the
    player with nothing to close it. Take the key and the server is never asked.
    A donkey, a mule or a horse with no record does not consume the press. The
    screen also closes on E now — it is a plain Screen and got none of
    AbstractContainerScreen's key handling for free, and a focused barn-name box
    swallows the key first so a name with an "e" in it still types. See
    breeding.html#H.

    Every other horse named on the screen became a link. Companions and the rival
    on Overview, descendants on Offspring; a click opens that horse's own copy of
    the screen with the one you came from as its parent, so Escape walks back.
    That needed the id beside the name — HorseSocialSyncPayload.Companion
    replaced a bare string — and the Offspring answer is now merged into
    ClientHorseRecordCache so a foal opens with no round trip. A horse we hold no
    record for is fetched with the family-tree request, which already means "the
    record for this id, please", and dropped after three seconds of silence.

    The family overview — the big one, and the owner's own design: every horse in
    the world on one chart, a row per generation, lines from each horse down to
    every foal it got, drag and zoom to browse, and a click that hides everything
    a horse has nothing to do with. Two decisions are worth having written down.
    First, the rows are the horse's stored generation, which is one more than the
    higher of its parents — so a founder mare's foal by a fourth-generation
    stallion sits five rows down with a long line reaching up past four rows to
    its dam. The owner called this out in advance and called the result messy; it
    is, and the mess is the true shape of a line-bred family. Second,
    PopulationDataPayload sends light entries rather than records: the family tree
    sends whole HorseRecords because it draws real coats, and a record carries an
    epigenome of thousands of characters, which is affordable for a dozen horses
    and absurd for every horse a world has ever bred. See
    breeding.html#family-overview.

    And a layout bug with a lesson in it. The Breeding preview's mare list "had
    weird negative space at the bottom of the box". The box was painted down to a
    fixed bottom edge while its rows were counted by flooring that height into
    26-pixel steps, so (bottom - top) % 26 pixels of empty panel always hung below
    the last row — with the scrollbar's track drawn full height to outline it. The
    mares had it worse by construction: the six-pixel separator between the two
    lists is charged to their box alone, so their remainder is always six less
    (mod 26) than the stallions', which for many window heights is most of a blank
    row on one side and almost nothing on the other. The box now ends on a row
    boundary. A fixed edge and a floored row count in the same box is a bug
    wherever it appears, and it appears a lot.

    Nothing here has been played. The new checks are on breeding's Verification
    tab; the one that matters most is whether the generation rows are right, since
    it is the only claim the overview makes about the data rather than about
    pixels.
