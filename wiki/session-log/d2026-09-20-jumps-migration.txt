2026-09-20, later — The jumps become one item, and get a screen, paint and a height

    The migration the morning left half-done, and then most of a day of the owner playing it and
    sending it straight back. Twelve blocks and thirty-six items are now one block and one
    item, and everything that used to be an item is a control in the block’s own
    screen. Shipped as v0.5.020.

What a jump is now

    One horsegenetics:jump block, one Horse Jump item. Right-click a
    placed one and you get two slots and a row of controls: the wood of the rails,
    the wood of the standards, a dye for either half, the
    style, and the height. Style and height apply to the whole
    run; woods and paint to the block. The whole page is
    item-jumps.html.

    Three things about that are worth keeping. The plank swap is reversible and the dye is
    not — a plank hands the displaced one back, a dye is spent for good, and the only
    way to strip paint is a fresh plank in that half. The wood rides on the item
    rather than behind the screen, because a jump is crafted out of a wood and three birch
    fences have to give you something already birch. And every future style is a button,
    not a thirty-seventh item, which is the point of there being one.

The three wrong turns on paint, which are the useful part

    Paint went through three designs and the first two failed for reasons that generalise.

    - Tinting the wood. A block tint is a MULTIPLY, so it can only darken and can
        only deepen a hue the texture already has. Oak’s blue channel sits around a third, so
        blue-on-oak came out a dark navy-brown and blue on a dark modded wood came out
        “nearly black purple”. Lightening the dye does not split the difference
        — multiplying oak by a pale blue gives slightly darker oak, because the ratio between
        the channels barely moves.

    - Painting a pale neutral pole. Fixed the colour and threw the wood out with
        it: “we’re losing too much of the original texture/shading”. Correct
        — there was no wood left to see.

    - An overlay. A third part laid over the wood, flat white at a fixed alpha,
        tinted — so the blend is alpha rather than multiply and is not bounded by what is
        behind it. The grain survives at full contrast and the colour reads as itself.

    The art is generated rather than derived: the obvious implementation was a desaturated copy of
    vanilla’s plank sheet, and that is Mojang’s art. tools/bake-paint-overlay.mjs
    writes it from nothing, and carries the only PNG encoder in the repo’s tools.

Crossrails, which took four goes

    The owner said “looks bad” three times before the framing landed:
    a crossrail is not an X per block, it is two poles crossing once. So the poles
    are defined across the whole run and each block draws the slice through it — one X up to
    three blocks wide, at the run’s own angle.

    Two findings underneath that. Element rotation is no longer limited to 22.5 and
    45 — verified against the artefacts, the angle is an unvalidated float in
    26.1.2 and there is a second multi-axis form. The comment in crossArm had been
    asserting the old limit as intended design, which is why this was stuck at one block. And
    a bar must be sized by its projection, not its length: a bar 16 long turned 45
    degrees only spans 11.3, which is why the old X sat marooned in the middle of its block touching
    nothing.

Three bugs worth naming

    - Handedness. “Left” is the counter-clockwise side and lies at
        high x, but runs are walked from the low-x end — so posting a group start with
        its left standard put the upright one block in from the end. One cause, three symptoms: a
        pole in the middle of a two-wide crossrail, “one off” on a three-wide.

    - A doubled model suffix. The blockstate’s standards field
        became a base that the model appends a connection suffix to, and only the crossrails branch
        was updated — so verticals and oxers asked for _standards_lr_l and every
        jump with a neighbour was a missing-texture cube. A lone one was fine, which is why it read
        as “broken above 2 or 3”.

    - A centred T in a three-wide run. The post was placed every third block by
        world position rather than every third block of the run. Both it and the
        post blockstate property are gone; every style now posts with an upright at
        each group boundary, which halved the blockstate count.

    Two process notes, both mine

        check-block-models.mjs passed through the purple-cube bug, twice.
        It walks the blockstate and asserts every model id named there exists — and every id
        named there did. The doubling happened in Java, one layer past anything that checker can
        see. A green run from it does not cover that class of bug.

        “Zero errors” was the wrong grep. The client log had said so on
        the first boot — 104 Missing block model lines — at WARN, while I
        was grepping ERROR and FATAL and reporting clean boots. Grep for missing models explicitly.

Also this session

    - The clearance margin dropped from half a block to an eighth. Eight pixels is
        vanilla’s fence number and vanilla needs it because its fence is exactly one block; a
        jump has a height, so the margin only has to make a full-height one unsteppable. It also
        moved the walkable line to where you can see it: everything under a block is walked over,
        everything from a block up is jumped.

    - Breed eggs have their own creative tab. One egg per breed is a list that
        grows every time somebody writes a breed file, and it was burying the carrots and papers.
        The three now read Horse Genetics, Horse Breeds, Horse Carts.

    - A placement hint on the action bar, which stops for good once the player has
        opened a screen — one item with everything behind a right-click has nothing about it
        to say so.

    - Gap 288: a painted run has a visible seam at every
        block join. Accepted for this release; the suspect is the overlay inflating along the rail
        axis and doubling its own alpha at each seam.

What is not built

    Standards of any solid block — cobblestone, brick, glass — is
    scoped and parked on the roadmap at the owner’s call: not
    needed for the first release. The scoping is written down there rather than re-derived: no art
    is generated, the per-material model files are avoidable entirely, the roster can be a runtime
    test, and the real cost is that Unbaked.bake loops every material once per
    blockstate variant.

    Nothing about the jumps has been measured against a horse yet. The height ladder is now a far
    better instrument than a stack of whole blocks was — that was the argument for adding it
    — and verification is where the unplayed list lives.
