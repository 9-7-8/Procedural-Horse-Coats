2026-09-24, after the stasis bank — a research paper names an allele pair, not a gene, and every
paper already written still works

    The owner's brief, in her own words:

        Can you please implement the swap for gene books to allele pair books, following all the
        advice in the intake folder? This DOES need to be backwards compatible, so convert all
        existing allele books into homozygous non-wild allele pair books for that gene

    "Gene books" is her word for research papers — settled on 2026-09-24 in the chest-loot
    session and recorded on item-research-papers.html so nobody goes looking for an item that
    does not exist. The advice is the "research papers as allele-pair books" proposal at the
    end of intake/Implementation advice.txt.

The plan on the wiki and the plan in intake/ were not the same plan

    item-research-papers.html already carried a Roadmap tab for this, and it is worth writing
    down that the two differed, because the shipped shape is the intake one and the wiki tab's
    predictions therefore did not all come true.

        Roadmap tab: a paper carries ONE allele. The research shelf's Copy tab lets you pick
        TWO of them and binds them into one book. That book is what the carrot recipe takes.
        Consequence it predicted: Gene.geneCarrotHomozygous() and CarrotEffect.defaultSpliceFor
        both stop being needed and should go.

        intake/: a paper IS a pair. One item, one normalized pair key, keyed by geneId +
        alleleA + alleleB.

    The owner's prompt says "allele pair books" and asks for existing papers to become
    "homozygous non-wild allele pair books", which is the intake shape — one item holding a
    pair, not two items bound together. Built that way. The Roadmap tab was deleted rather
    than marked shipped, per the hard rule, and the divergence written onto the Coding tab,
    because the two names it said would die did not (see below).

Two decisions put to the owner before any code, both of which change the game

    Neither had a defensible default, and both are gameplay-facing rather than structural.

        Which pairs may a chest or a villager hand out? Her call: carrier and true-breeding
        only — X/n and X/X for each variant allele, so a locus has roughly twice as many papers
        as it has alleles. The alternative the intake note implies literally is every viable
        pair, which on the forty-allele particle locus is about eight hundred papers, and one
        of them turning up in a chest is noise rather than a find. Compound pairs still exist
        and still craft; a HORSE is the only thing that hands you one, which is the route that
        was supposed to be interesting anyway.

        Does the shelf's Copy list stay a flat list of pairs? Her call: yes. Rows read
        "Agouti: A/a", sorted by gene name then pair, in the same five-row scrolling list. The
        alternative was grouping pairs under a gene heading, which reads better on a full shelf
        and needs new row-type handling in both the screen and the menu sync. Set aside, not
        rejected — it is on the shelf page's Verification tab as what to do if the flat list
        turns out unusable in play.

What was already built, which is most of it

    The load-bearing half had shipped earlier: CarrotEffect.KnownGeneSplice has named both
    alleles for a while (token known:<gene>:<a>:<b>), and its javadoc already complained that a
    whole-gene paper could only ever reach alleles().get(0) — "no carrot could ever hand over a
    soul flame, and KIT could only ever hand over one of its thirteen". So this session is the
    other half: getting a real pair into the recipe.

common/genetics/ResearchTopic — the new type

    A record of three STRINGS: geneKey, alleleA, alleleB. Strings rather than Allele objects
    because a paper outlives the gene it names (a datapack gene removed, a world opened without
    the mod that added it), and a paper for a retired gene must be inert, not an exception
    thrown inside somebody's inventory. gene(), pair() and splice() all answer null.

    Normalization was free. AllelePair has always sorted its two slots by declaration order so
    that a pair equals its reverse and a genotype code round-trips; the record's constructor
    just hands the two tokens to it when the gene is registered and knows both. So A/a and a/A
    are equal, produce the same token(), and stack. A pair this build cannot resolve keeps the
    order it arrived in, so its token still round-trips unchanged rather than being silently
    rewritten.

    token() is "<geneKey>|<a>|<b>" — flat, because it has to be a component value, a menu sync
    string and a block entity's saved pick, and a record in three more places is three more
    codecs. A bar is safe: ResearchTopicTest asserts no registered gene key or allele token
    contains one, the same guarantee KnownGeneSpliceTest already made about the colon the
    carrot token splits on.

    lootPool(gene) is the owner's carrier/true-breeding rule, filtered through canOccur AND
    sexConsistent. breedsTruePool(gene) is its homozygous half, which is what the supplier's
    top tier and the loot function's `homozygous` flag narrow to.

The back-compat, which is the one deliberate exception in the repo

    CLAUDE.md hard rule 6 is "no legacy or back-compat code". The owner overrode it by name for
    this, so it is written down as an exception rather than smuggled in:

        The component keeps its old registry id, research_gene. Renaming it to research_topic
        would be tidier and would drop the component off every existing paper, leaving blanks.

        Its persistent codec is Codec.either(record, string). A stored bare string decodes
        through ResearchTopic.wholeGene, which reads it as the HOMOZYGOUS NON-WILD pair — the
        owner's instruction, and the strongest thing a whole-gene paper could have meant.

        wholeGene falls back to the carrier pair on a locus whose homozygote the gene forbids
        (a KIT lethal, met/met). A paper naming an impossible pair would craft an inert carrot,
        which is worse than a slightly weaker one. ResearchTopicTest asserts that EVERY gene
        with a carrot migrates to a pair that still crafts.

        Encoding always writes the map form, so a world loaded once never writes a bare string
        again.

    This could not be unit-tested, and that is not a cop-out — it is the reason the failure
    mattered. A component whose persistent codec refuses its stored value is DROPPED on load,
    with a warning nobody reads and no crash: the paper becomes Blank, files into no shelf and
    crafts no carrot. And the NeoForge module's test classpath deliberately carries no
    Minecraft (build.gradle says so, for MaterialScan's sake), so NbtOps and a real ItemStack
    only exist inside a booted game. So it is a GAMETEST: old_papers_still_read, which decodes
    the legacy StringTag through the real component codec, asserts it equals wholeGene's answer,
    then does the whole thing again through ItemStack.CODEC with a hand-built components tag and
    checks the result is accepted by the research shelf. runGameTest: "All 10 required tests
    passed".

What each source now writes

    GeneBookFromHorse — the pair the horse ACTUALLY carries at the chosen locus. This is the
    only route to a compound pair, and the reason an odd animal stays worth a book after you
    already know its genes.

    AddResearchPaperModifier — gene weighted by rarity as before, then a flat draw over
    lootPool.

    SetRandomGeneFunction (target: "paper", the supplier's two trades) — the same pool;
    `homozygous` narrows it to breedsTruePool. Its javadoc used to say the flag was
    "carrots only"; it now means something on both.

    SpliceRecipeDisplay.researchPaper(gene) — an EXAMPLE paper, ResearchTopic.defaultFor, since
    that display has a gene and no paper.

    EquineResearchShelfBlockEntity.copyOf — the filed paper's own topic.

KnownGeneSpliceRecipe reads the pair, and refuses rather than making a dud

    resolve() returns a ResearchTopic instead of a Gene, and assemble() emits topic.splice().
    The new condition is isResolved(): a paper naming a gene this build retired, or a pair the
    locus says cannot occur, does not resolve at all. That is deliberate — refusing to resolve
    is visible in the grid, and a carrot that does nothing when fed is not.

What the Roadmap tab said would die, and why it did not

    geneCarrotHomozygous() and defaultSpliceFor survive. The plan that predicted their death
    had the shelf binding two single-allele papers, so by the time a carrot existed the player
    had named both alleles. With the pair on the paper there are still three callers that have
    a gene and no paper in hand:

        menu/SpliceRecipeDisplay — the Horse Browser's recipe ghost;
        web/DesignerApi.geneCarrotJson — the gene-carrot card on every gene page;
        the scientist's CARROT trades, which draw a gene and never see a paper at all.

    So defaultSpliceFor now delegates to ResearchTopic.defaultFor and both are documented as
    an EXAMPLE pair rather than a claim about every carrot for the locus. The browser's row, the
    wiki card and the carrots page all say "example" in those words.

    While doing that, defaultFor gained the viability ladder wholeGene already had. It did not
    have one before (nor did the old defaultSpliceFor), so it could name a pair the locus
    forbids — harmless while the result was only a ghost item, and a visible lie now that the
    gene-carrot card prints the pair in prose. Changes nothing today; stops the card lying the
    first time a gene forbids its own homozygote.

The shelf files by pair

    Every static on the block entity went from a gene key to a token: topicOf, topicsIn,
    holdsElsewhere, stores, displayName, copyTicks, the saved "selected" string, and
    ShelfActionPayload (whose stringUtf8(256) budget a token comfortably fits). copyTicks is
    still the GENE's rarity, not the pair's — what is rare is the locus, and a carrier paper is
    no easier to transcribe than a true-breeding one.

    canCopy gained a guard it did not need before: copyOf can now return EMPTY, because a token
    that no longer parses has no paper to make. Without the guard, out.isEmpty() would be true,
    canCopy would say yes, and tick would spend a book per cycle forever putting nothing in the
    result slot.

    A PICK saved before today is a bare gene key, which matches no filed paper, so the shelf
    simply has nothing selected and the player picks again. The filed PAPERS migrate properly.

The carrot tooltip, which the Roadmap tab said to do regardless

    "A Known Gene Splice carrot already carries the right data and has never shown it — every
    one of them looks identical in the inventory. That is worth doing whether or not the rest
    lands." It is now the thing that makes the whole change visible: two carrots for one locus
    that do different things and look the same is a guessing game, not a mechanic.

    All eleven breeding carrots share one new item/BreedingCarrotItem whose only job is a
    tooltip built from the carrot_effects tokens — so a combination carrot lists every effect it
    merged, the four base carrots name themselves, and a carrot for a gene this build no longer
    has says so in red rather than being quietly inert. DebugYardGameplay's Splice record needed
    `Supplier<? extends Item>` to keep accepting them.

    The paper's own tooltip changed too, and for the same reason the pair is the better unit: it
    shows Expression.name() for THAT COMBINATION where it used to show Gene.description() for
    the whole locus. "what A/a does", not "what agouti is".

Tests

    ResearchTopicTest, 15 new tests in common/. The three contracts the item layer leans on and
    cannot check itself: that A/a and a/A are one paper; that a pre-change paper still resolves
    AND still crafts, for every gene with a carrot; and that every pair any source can hand out
    is one the gene allows and the recipe accepts, including the sex-linked loci where a pair no
    horse of either sex could carry is the easy mistake. Plus: the loot pool never offers the
    plain baseline, has no duplicates, breedsTruePool is its homozygous subset, a wide locus is
    reachable at more than ten of its alleles, a retired gene and a retired allele are both
    inert rather than fatal, and defaultSpliceFor agrees with defaultFor.
