The bullet
==========

    Next bullet off intake/START HERE.txt, which puts the Bugs section of
    "To do.txt" ahead of everything else. The bullet:

        Horse names are too duplicated. Default naming should give daughters
        their mother's last name and sons their father's last name, with
        random first names. Make the first/last-name choice and parent source
        configurable per player for their own horses, even on a server.

    Step 1 of START HERE is "check whether it is already fixed", and the
    answer here was the interesting kind of no: the naming code was not
    absent, it was deliberate, unit-tested, and had been watched working in
    the yard nine days earlier. What it had never been checked against was a
    herd.

Why the old schedule produced duplicates
========================================

    HorseNames.breedNth varied a foal's name by how many foals its exact
    pairing had already produced - foal 1 took dam.first + sire.last, foal 2
    the other combination, foals 3-6 one parent half plus a rolled word, and
    foal 7 onward a wholly rolled name. The intent was right and the
    verification was real: the 08:47 yard run on 2026-09-15 followed two pairs
    through fifteen foals, every one landed in its documented slot, and no two
    names repeated.

    The defect is not visible from inside one pairing, which is the only place
    it was ever looked at. Across pairings, every pair's first two foals are
    assembled *entirely* out of words their parents already carry, and foals
    3-6 keep one of those halves as well. A herd bred from a few founders
    therefore recirculates a small pool of words, and after a few generations
    the same full name comes round again. The owner's heavily modded server is
    a herd; the debug yard was two pairs in two pens.

    Worth writing down as a shape rather than an incident: a check that
    exercises one instance of a thing cannot see a defect that only exists
    between instances. The pen proved the schedule and the schedule was still
    wrong.

What it is now
==============

    HorseNames.foal(dam, sire, foalSex, policy, generator, rng). One half is
    inherited from a parent, the other is always rolled. By default the
    inherited half is the last name and it comes down the same-sex line - a
    filly carries her dam's, a colt his sire's. The surname then reads as a
    family, and the rolled first name is the thing separating two full
    siblings, which it can do because it has a whole word table to draw on
    instead of the four words the parents happen to hold.

    The foal's sex is already the product of the same Mendelian draw as
    everything else (HorseRecord derives sex() off the code), so the surname
    line is genetic and needed nothing new.

    breed and breedNth are gone, and with them HorseRecords.offspringCount,
    which existed only to feed the schedule. HorseDatabase.offspringCount
    stays: "how many foals has this pair had" is a fair question for the
    browser and the papers, it just no longer has a caller.

Where a per-player setting can live
===================================

    This was the half that needed a decision, and it is more constrained than
    it looks. Two facts rule out both obvious homes:

      - It cannot be a server config. On a shared server each player names
        their own horses their own way; a server config holds one answer.
      - It cannot be applied on the client. A foal is named on the server, at
        a due date that routinely arrives while its owner is offline and their
        client is not running at all.

    So the client suggests and the server decides, which is also what
    intake/Implementation advice.txt already said about naming ("clients may
    suggest preferences, but they are not the authority"). The player edits
    naming.inheritedHalf and naming.parentSource in phc/client.toml;
    ClientLifecycleHandler sends both on every login as NamingPolicyPayload;
    HorseNamingData - a server-global SavedData keyed by player UUID, built on
    HorseProgressData's shape - files it, and nothing else ever names a foal.
    Two players on one server keep different rules and neither reaches the
    other's herd. Owner chose this surface over a command or an H-menu row.

    Enum names on the wire and on disk, not ordinals, for the reason
    ProgressSyncPayload already gives: reordering an enum must not silently
    relabel somebody's setting. Every read is total - policyFor(null), an
    unknown player and an unreadable name all answer NamingPolicy.DEFAULT,
    because naming a foal must not be a path that can fail.

    Whose policy applies is the dam owner's, not the breeder's. A foal is born
    where its dam is, to whoever owns her, and that is the herd the name has
    to sit in. The breeder is the fallback for a tamed stallion put to an
    unowned mare; a wild birth has neither. HorseOwnership gained ownerId(),
    which answers by UUID and so works for an owner who is offline - isOwner()
    now goes through it.
