2026-09-24 — stage six, the last one: the Emergency Horse Stasis Chamber, which uses itself

    The sixth and final item on the build order on wiki/horse-stasis.html. A Basic chamber
    surrounded by eight ender pearls; carry it and every horse you own is insured, because the
    instant one falls below a tenth of its health it is in the bottle — wherever you are, and
    whether or not the blow would have killed it. A horse inside a chamber cannot be hurt at
    all, so the capture is not a reprieve, it is the end of the incident.

    One bullet, per intake/START HERE.txt, which names this stage by name as the worked example
    of how routing is meant to go. The horse-stasis Roadmap tab is now gone entirely and the
    page has left wiki/roadmap.html by itself; roadmap-execution.html's coverage row went with
    it, since that check hard-fails on a covered page that is no longer a roadmap page.

The handler the roadmap expected to hang this off does not exist.

    The Roadmap tab said to add a second, lower threshold to "whatever handler ends up watching
    for the intake to-do list's buck-off-below-20% escape behaviour". There is no such handler.
    That behaviour is HorseEscapeGoal — an AI goal, reading the health bar on its own canUse
    tick — and a goal cannot see a blow coming; by the time it next runs the horse is dead or it
    is not. So the model is HorseLastStandHandler instead, which asks the same question at the
    same moment and gets it from LivingDamageEvent.Pre.

    That is a case of the thing verify-a-prescribed-fix-first is about: the plan named a hook
    site confidently and the hook site was a different shape from the one described. Ten minutes
    of grep, not a wasted session, but only because it was checked before it was built on.

LOW priority is not tidiness, it is how the two features compose.

    HorseLastStandHandler is already on LivingDamageEvent.Pre at default priority. The emergency
    handler is on the same event at EventPriority.LOW, deliberately, so it runs second. A killing
    blow is caught by the last stand, which zeroes the damage and leaves the horse on half a
    heart; the rescue then sees a horse on half a heart and takes it out of the fight. The player
    gets the totem particles and then the rescue, in that order, which is the story that actually
    happened.

    In the losing order the chamber would swallow a horse the last stand was about to save
    anyway — spending a one-shot insurance on a blow that was never going to land. Nothing in the
    event bus would have reported that; it would just have felt wasteful, months later.

A subclass, not a fifth tier, and that is why nothing else changed.

    EmergencyStasisChamberItem extends StasisChamberItem at StasisTier.BASIC. The bank's
    isChamber is an instanceof on the superclass, so the emergency chamber files, counts, stacks,
    lists and upgrades exactly like the Basic chamber it was built from —
    HorseStasisBankBlockEntity, the menu, the Browse tab, the upkeep, StasisDrops and StasisStud
    are all untouched, and none of them knows the class exists.

    A fifth StasisTier was the obvious alternative and is wrong twice. The ladder is four rungs
    of what the bank may do with a horse, cumulative and pinned by StasisTierTest; self-capture
    is not that kind of capability, and this chamber is built from the cheapest rung rather than
    sitting above the dearest. A rung costing eight ender pearls that bought no bank capability
    is exactly the hole in the ladder that test exists to catch.

    The visible consequence is the right one: an eye of ender upgrades an emergency chamber into
    an ordinary Intermediate, horse and all, spending the emergency for the window. That is the
    escape hatch rather than the intended path — releasing hands the armed chamber straight back
    — but it means a rescued horse is never stuck somewhere the bank cannot read it.

The per-tick scan the roadmap was worried about does not exist either.

    The tab's one open question was cost: watching every owned horse's health sounds like the
    server-wide sweep the whole stasis feature exists to avoid. It is not one, for a reason
    almost too simple to write down — health only moves when something moves it. The question is
    asked on a damage event and nowhere else, and past the two field reads that answer "is this
    a horse, and is it low", the only cost is one walk of one player's inventory, for a horse
    that is already about to die. A world with no fighting in it pays nothing at all.

    The owner is found with HorseOwnership.ownerId and getPlayerList().getPlayer(uuid), not
    horse.getOwner(), which answers only for an owner loaded in the same level —
    HorseHurtNoticeHandler documents and accepts that gap, and a chamber that only worked while
    you stood next to the horse would be worth very little.

Four smaller calls, each written up on the page.

    A state, not a crossing. StasisRescue.rescues asks where the health is, not whether this blow
    moved it across the line, so a horse already under the threshold when its owner logs back in
    is still caught by the next blow. Testing for a crossing would make the rescue depend on who
    was watching the moment it first went low.

    The rider is set down, not refused. An ordinary capture refuses a horse with somebody on its
    back because passengers would be discarded with it; refusing here would make the item useless
    in the exact situation it is bought for, so ejectPassengers runs first. Uncommon anyway —
    HorseEscapeGoal throws the rider off at twice this health.

    The damage is zeroed after the capture, not before, so a horse that somehow could not be
    stored takes its blow instead of being handed free immunity.

    The refusal is throttled per horse, not per player, ten seconds, the same shape and sweep as
    HorseHurtNoticeHandler's. A full chamber refuses on every blow and a horse in a fire takes
    one every half-second; two horses in the same fire are two different pieces of news.

The recipe is the third Java recipe in this feature, for the third time for the same reason.

    EmergencyChamberRecipe is a CustomRecipe because the chamber in the middle of the grid may
    have a horse in it, and a JSON recipe copies no components — so surrounding an occupied
    chamber with pearls would hand back an empty one and delete a pedigreed animal, silently,
    with nothing in the log. It copies stasis_snapshot across, exactly as StasisUpgradeRecipe
    does. Shaped rather than shapeless because the arrangement is the recipe's name: a shapeless
    nine-item match would also accept the chamber in a corner.

    Both codecs are MapCodec.unit(INSTANCE) / StreamCodec.unit(INSTANCE) on one shared INSTANCE,
    the shape whose absence was a login kick on every dedicated server in v0.5.014.

Not played, and the verification tab says so in seven bullets.

    Nothing about this has been watched happen. The cheapest check settles most of it — carry one
    empty chamber, hit a tamed horse past a tenth, and see whether it vanishes and comes back
    whole. The two that are worth doing deliberately are the last-stand ordering (totem
    particles, then the rescue, in that order) and converting an occupied Basic chamber with the
    pearls, which is the one failure on the list that would be silent and permanent.

    Two known non-defects, both written on the page: an offline owner's horse is not insured,
    because the inventory is not in the world; and a donkey or mule cannot be caught, because a
    chamber holds a Horse and both capture and release are written against EntityType.HORSE.

One thing noticed and not chased, so it is not lost

    The Coding tab says StasisStud.atStud re-checks the tier "because StasisUpgradeRecipe copies
    components and a mark can therefore outlive the rung that bought it". The code does not: the
    upgrade copies stasis_snapshot only, and drops STASIS_AT_STUD. The defensive check is still
    right; the stated reason for it is wrong. Left alone as another bullet's work rather than
    widened into this one.
