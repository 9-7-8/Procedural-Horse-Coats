2026-09-17, to finish — Dun’s d1 gets its bars and shoulder bar

    The last row of the roadmap’s “shipped genes whose vocabulary is too narrow”
    table, so the table went with it. d1 — non-dun,
    but marked — drew the dorsal stripe alone, where a real nd1 horse
    can also carry faint leg barring and shoulder shadowing.

The recorded blocker was half true, and that is the whole fix

    The roadmap row named its own obstacle: “d1 does not dilute, so there
    is nothing on a leg to countershade a bar against.” That is true of a black
    leg and false of everything else, and separating the two cases is all the change
    amounts to. d1 takes 5% off the visible red, so a bar it draws
    countershades against whatever red that texel had spare:

PigmentField.diluteNeutral, for d1 (keepBlack = 1)

chestnut / bay barrel  (1, 0)   room = 1    red × 0.95, the bar reads
black coat             (1, 1)   room = 0    red never written
a bay's absolute point (0, 1)   room = 0    red never written

    So the bars and the shoulder bar now run on both marked outcomes with
    no allele check in the painter. They appear on the bases that can show them
    and resolve to nothing on the ones that cannot, by arithmetic rather than by a branch —
    which keeps the gene’s “one dilution, one mask, no special case anywhere”
    property intact rather than bolting an exception onto it. A real non-dun black showing no
    primitive markings is the correct output, not a gap, and it now falls out for free.

    The face mask and cobwebbing stay D-only. A cobwebbed forehead is a strong-dun
    feature, and both the roadmap row and known-gaps asked for bars and shoulder
    shadowing only.

Depth was deliberately not reduced

    The obvious reading of “faint” is a scaling constant, and it is the wrong one.
    A bar can never out-contrast the dilution it countershades, so d1’s 5%
    ceiling already makes it an order of magnitude fainter than a dun’s, which
    countershades a 58% black take. Halving it on top of that would have bought a
    0.025 red delta — almost certainly below what a 128px sheet can show,
    which is to say a layer that technically paints and practically never appears.
    Full depth, no new constant (owner’s call, offered against two
    alternatives). The faintness is a property of the allele, not of a number.

The goldens carried a checkable invariant

    Reordering the Math.max chain to apply the face last is commutative, so
    every real dun had to come out bit-identical — which makes the golden
    diff a test of the change rather than a chore after it. It held exactly:
    12 of 1092 whole-horse rows moved, every one d1/d1 or d1/d2,
    none carrying a D. Two existing tests also stayed green untouched,
    which is the sharper evidence: both are built on a solid-black field, precisely where
    d1’s new markings are correctly a no-op.

    What is not settled is whether 5% is visible at all in game. The tests prove
    the pigment moves, not that an eye can see it.
    §0-EU leads with that question and says
    plainly that if nothing reads on a d1/d1 chestnut’s foreleg, this goes back
    to known gaps rather than being quietly called shipped.
