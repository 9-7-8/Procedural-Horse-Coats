2026-09-30 - a breed in every modded biome, and floors kept off-world


    The ask: a pack of 352 jars carries 226 modded biomes, and the owner wanted
    at least one of our breeds in all of them. The scan that listed them came
    from another session; this one re-read the biome JSONs out of the ATM11
    instance's jars (temperature, rainfall, what each already spawns) and the
    mods' tags to sort overworld from Nether, End, cave and other dimensions.

    The mechanism is a table, not 127 edited breed files. The owner chose it
    over the literal "patch every breed" option. common/breed/BiomeAnalogues
    maps each modded biome to the vanilla biome it stands in for -
    terralith:steppe is plains and windswept hills - and Breeds.forBiome, the
    feral setting and the herd modifier's "on the list" test all read it. Three
    reasons it won: a modded biome gets its analogue's whole pool rather than a
    hand-picked few (in a Terralith world most of the plains ARE steppe and
    highlands); drop-in breeds and breed-spawning.toml overrides carry over with
    nothing restated; and an install whose toml was written before this - the
    owner's own pack - still gets it, where per-file patches would have been
    shadowed by the toml's copy of the old lists until its sections were deleted.

    Scope took two rounds. First answer: overworld only. Then: the modded Nether
    and End biomes too, but no horses in any dimension a mod added (Aether II,
    Undergarden, AllTheModium's, Mahou Tsukai's). Caves are out because a horse
    needs lit grass; oceans map to beach, for island ponies on a grassy islet.

    The finding worth keeping. The obvious End design - give the Enderpony
    Nullscape's floors - was caught by SpawnGroundTest, which exists to catch
    exactly it: a breed's spawn_ground belongs to the breed, not the biome, and
    the Enderpony lives in forty overworld biomes. End stone and dead coral in
    the dark would have become spawnable floors in the plains - a player's
    end-stone cellar - and blackstone or smooth basalt would have added every
    amethyst geode. The owner picked the rule over the two workarounds offered
    (let the Nether pair carry the End floors; accept the leak): a breed's floor
    now counts only in a vanilla Nether or End biome or a stand-in for one. With
    that in place the Enderpony takes all of Nullscape's surfaces - end stone,
    four dead corals, blackstone, basalt - and the End is Enderponies. The cost,
    written on breed-format.html#ground-off-world: no overworld breed can use
    spawn_ground, and none does.

    Regions Unexplored's Nether floors are set in code, not data. They were read
    by javap off RUSurfaceRuleBuilder: four nyliums, blackstone, netherrack, soul
    sand. The nyliums went onto the Netherhorse and the Nightmare.

    What is true now and was not: 161 modded biomes have wild breeds (153
    overworld, 5 Nether, 3 End), and the End spawns horses at all for the first
    time. It has no other creature spawn, so the breed-only rate is the whole
    draw there - flagged as the thing to watch.

    Checks. BiomeAnaloguesTest: every mapped biome has a wild breed, no analogue
    is a typo (mutated once to prove it fails), the pool is the analogue's whole
    pool, every modded Nether and End biome has a breed able to stand on its
    ground in the dark, no overworld biome gains a floor, and no mod-added
    dimension is on the table. Green with SpawnGroundTest, NetherBreedsTest,
    BreedFilesTest, BreedsTest, BreedSpawnSettingsTest, BreedSpecParserTest and
    RegionTest; :neoforge-26.1.2:build and creator parity green. The full
    :common:test suite was not run. Nothing was played: the owner's dev client
    was up the whole session, so the builds ran in a worktree at C:/phcwt (the
    scratchpad path is past Windows' filename limit for this repo) and no
    runServer was booted. The wasm was not rebuilt - nothing in web/ reaches the
    spawn lookups.

    Waiting: breeds.html#modded-biomes-0930 - where to look in each dimension,
    and the negative (a dark end-stone room in the overworld stays empty).
