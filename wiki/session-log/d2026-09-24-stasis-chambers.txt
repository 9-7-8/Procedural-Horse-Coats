2026-09-24 — the stasis chambers are built, and the plan's hardest piece turned out to exist already

    Stage one of the six-stage build order on wiki/horse-stasis.html: the four stasis-chamber
    items, their recipes, and the capture/release path every later stage stands on. The bank,
    the Browse tab, passive healing, the drop buffer, in-bank breeding and the emergency
    chamber are all still planned and are still on that page's Roadmap tab. Scope was the
    owner's call at the top of the session, against the page's own ordering.

The plan said capture/release was new work. It was not.

    wiki/horse-stasis.html asserted, in its lede and again in its footer, that freezing and
    later resuming one specific living horse had "no existing analog anywhere in the mod" —
    that transfer papers, breed spawn eggs and the stallion seed jar all name or spawn a horse
    but none of them resume one. That is true of those three, and the conclusion drawn from it
    was wrong: LycanthropyHandler has carried a whole horse through a night spent inside a
    wolf since the LYCAN gene shipped, with exactly the two calls stasis needs —
    horse.saveWithoutId into a CompoundTag then discard(), and EntityType.HORSE.create +
    horse.load + addFreshEntity coming back. It is proven across chunk unloads, restarts and
    deaths. So stasis reuses it rather than inventing a second way to freeze a horse, and most
    of what would have been the expensive stage was reading one file.

    That also settled the snapshot's shape against what the page had sketched. On NeoForge
    saveWithoutId carries the data attachments and stores the entity's UUID, so a released
    horse is the same horse down to the id the ancestry database, every pedigree, every stall
    sign and every transfer deed key on. The sketched alternative — a record enumerating
    horse_record, horse_gear, horse_care and the rest by hand — would have lost the id, lost
    every vanilla field nobody thought to list (age, custom name, leash, the vanilla saddle
    slot), and rotted the first time somebody added a fifteenth attachment. The cost of the
    tag is that StasisSnapshot cannot live in common/ the way the page wanted, because
    CompoundTag is a Minecraft type: common/ got StasisTier, the four tiers and their
    cumulative capability flags, with StasisTierTest pinning the ladder, and the snapshot
    stayed in the game module. Correct restoration beat portability; the portable half is the
    half that had something to port.

Two design calls the owner made, and one the session made

    The chamber is reusable — letting a horse out hands the same chamber back, empty. The word
    "single-use" was in the wiki lede but nowhere in the owner's own words in To do.txt, so it
    was put to her rather than built on: one chamber holds one horse at a time, and a Spacer
    chamber is not a diamond you spend twice. She also chose the custom recipe class over a
    plain tag-matched recipe for the water container, which is why StasisChamberRecipe exists
    at all.

    The session's own call was that the three tier upgrades must be a Java recipe too. A
    shapeless JSON recipe builds its result from scratch and copies no components, so upgrading
    an occupied chamber would have handed back an empty one and deleted the animal — silently,
    with nothing in the log. StasisUpgradeRecipe copies the stasis_snapshot across. The JSON
    version of that feature is not a cheaper version of it, it is a data-loss bug with a
    crafting recipe attached.

The water container is deliberately half-done, and says so

    The recipe matches c:buckets/water (Tags.Items.BUCKETS_WATER), so another mod's container
    crafts it without being named here, and getRemainingItems returns the input's own declared
    crafting remainder — which is how a vanilla water bucket comes back as an empty bucket.
    A modded container that declares no remainder is handed straight back still full, because
    "not consumed" is the rule that matters and returning nothing would break it. The correct
    answer is draining it through its fluid capability, which is the same
    match-by-tag-return-drained-container problem the water troughs on compatibility.html will
    have to solve, and the page already said to reuse that rather than invent a second
    remainder rule. The legacy NeoForge FluidUtil that would have done it is
    @Deprecated(forRemoval) in 26.1.2 and the transfer-API replacement cannot change a stack's
    Item through ItemAccess.forStack, so building it blind in a recipe path would have been a
    crash on craft for the sake of a bucket. It waits, and it is on the Verification tab.
