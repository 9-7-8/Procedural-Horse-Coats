2026-09-21, last of all — two tack slots become nineteen, and two documentation conventions fall out of it

    The horse gear overhaul, asked for directly: a Gear tab holding every
    slot. It shipped as the roster, the storage and the screen — and deliberately
    not the pieces, which the owner said up front were for later.
    Horse gear is the page, and it is the first one built under
    the two conventions this session established.

The ceiling was the design, and it had already been worked out

    EquipmentSlot has exactly two animal entries, BODY and
    SADDLE, and EquipmentClientInfo.LayerType is not extensible, so
    nineteen slots were never going to be nineteen equipment slots. The saddle and the barding
    keep the two real ones because vanilla draws those free; the other seventeen ride
    data/HorseGear, a new synced attachment. A container was the other
    option and is worse twice over — a widened container is reachable by
    hoppers, and AbstractMountInventoryMenu.quickMoveStack computes every
    shift-click range from hardcoded indices it shares with donkeys, mules and llamas, which is
    how items duplicate or vanish. None of that was discovered this session: the roadmap had
    written it down, and HorseTackSlot's own class comment had named
    “a bridle, a blanket and shoes” as the obvious next ones. The seam
    was built expecting this, and the work was smaller than the roster's length suggests.

The roster carries the layout, so the screen did not grow with it

    Each constant now holds its backing, its zone, its label, its hint and its position
    on the paper doll as a fraction of the doll's box. Adding a slot is a line in the
    enum plus a tag; the screen is not touched. The Gear tab draws the horse's own portrait and
    the slots over it in the place the gear actually sits, which needed a new
    HorsePortrait.drawPosed — the existing portrait swings to follow the
    cursor, and a doll whose horse moves would put the near fore leg somewhere different from
    the boot slot that belongs to it. Overview's old two-slot row is gone; it now reports a
    count and points here.

    What a slot accepts is an item tag, and all seventeen are empty. That is
    the correct refusal while the pieces do not exist — a slot with an empty tag takes
    nothing rather than taking dirt — and it is the seam each piece joins later, from a
    datapack if wanted. The tab says so on screen, because seventeen slots that silently refuse
    everything read as broken rather than as unfinished.

The roster checks itself at boot, because it could not be a test

    Every way a nineteen-row table of screen geometry can be wrong fails silently: two
    slots on one anchor draw exactly on top of each other and one becomes unclickable, an
    anchor outside 0..1 is invisible but still holds its click box, and a third constant handed
    a real EquipmentSlot would take one the barding needs. A unit test was written
    for exactly this and deleted — this module's test source set has no
    Minecraft on its classpath on purpose (MaterialScanTest imports none) and the
    enum cannot load without one. So it is HorseTackSlot.verify(), thrown from the
    mod constructor beside DietFoods.verify, which exists for the same stated
    reason.

Four calls from the owner, and one reversal

    - A bridle and a halter share one headstall slot, reversing the
        roadmap's split. The distinction that split protected is real — haltered means
        led and lunged, bridled means steered — but a horse cannot wear both at once, so
        they are two items competing for one slot. The distinction survives; it just lives in
        the items now. Recorded.

    - A blanket gets a slot, which the enum's own documentation had already
        named and the roster had not.

    - Legs and hooves are four slots each, not a set of four, so a horse can
        throw a single shoe.

    - Mane and tail go in with their utility owed rather than declared. They
        are the two slots that collide with the rule reversed earlier the same day —
        nothing purely aesthetic except breeding — so braids alone will not qualify to
        fill them. A neck slot was considered and dropped as already covered by barding.

Two conventions, and they are now hard rule 9

    Both came from the owner while the code was being written, and both are about the same
    failure: a subject's plans and its checklist were living on two enormous shared
    pages instead of beside the thing they are about.

    - A subject with a page keeps its plans on a Roadmap tab there. The
        accessories system, the slot roster, shoes, native tack, the saddlebags and the saddle
        pad moved off the roadmap whole — ids and all, so every
        inbound roadmap.html#anchor became horse-gear.html#anchor in
        one rewrite rather than two. The roadmap keeps one sentence pointing at them,
        because a summary would be the same content in two places again.

    - A new thing's verification checklist goes on a Verification tab of its own
        page, not in the one growing document, which
        now holds older entries only. A checklist beside the thing it tests is read by whoever
        opens that page and deleted by whoever confirms it.

    Both use wiki/tabs.js's existing page-local tab mechanism,
    which turned out to be designed for precisely this and is deliberately not a wiki-wide
    view, so no sidebar filter or index switcher had to learn about them. Four tabs on the gear
    page were confirmed rendering in headless Chrome, with no console errors.

What was not built, and what nobody has seen

    No render layer for any of the seventeen, because there is nothing to
    render; the saddle and the barding draw through vanilla as they always did. No items. And
    nothing here has been opened in a game — the checklist is on the
    page's own Verification tab, and the regression that matters most is that the saddle and
    the barding still work, since they changed screens and their write path moved from an
    inline setItemSlot to HorseTackSlot.set. A server boot was
    not run: another NeoForge instance of the owner's had held the run directory since
    02:01, and it was left alone.
