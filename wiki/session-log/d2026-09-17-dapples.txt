2026-09-17, later still — Silver draws its dapples, and a render that could not settle it

    Next on the natural list, one at a time. Silver had
    shipped as the dilution only — the dapples the gene is named for were not
    drawn — and the roadmap also recorded that its flaxen mane read a little
    gold rather than pale. Both fixed in one pass.

    The lattice is BodyNoise.cellDistance in body space, warped exactly as
    grey’s is, so that two kinds of dapple on one horse
    are the same shape of thing. The depth is 0.16 against grey’s 0.42:
    a grey dapple is the difference between pigment kept and pigment lost, while a silver
    dapple is a shading within one chocolate, and at grey’s depth it reads as
    spots. Body only; the mane and tail keep the flat flaxen path. The mane was pulled from
    HAIR_KEEP_RED 0.40 to 0.30 in the same change.

The seed lives on the allele, which was the owner’s correction

    The roadmap had specified a fixed-seed modulation “per-genotype rather than
    per-horse”, and the owner overrode it mid-session: the seed, spacing and
    strength are stored in the silver allele’s own epigenetics. That is the
    better call and it is worth writing down why. A genotype-derived hash would make every
    horse of a given genotype dapple identically and permanently; on the allele, the values
    inherit with the copy they sit on and drift each breeding, so two siblings can differ and
    a line can be bred toward heavy dappling. Strength runs down to zero, because a
    silver that barely dapples is a real and common horse.

The render confirmed two things and could not settle the third

    Checked with an offline render rather than by spending game time. It confirmed the
    dilution and the paler mane. It could not confirm the dappling either way
    — a 0.16-depth modulation is a few percent of brightness on a 128-texel sheet, which
    is below what can honestly be judged by eye, and the sample’s strength comes from its
    own seed, so the picture might not even have been a fair test of the feature.

    “I could not see it” is not evidence of absence, and would not have
    been evidence of presence either. So the eyeball was replaced with a measurement:
    SilverDappleTest paints silver horses across 64 seeds and measures the spread
    of surviving black over body texels. It asserts that a strongly dappled horse really is
    modulated, that a barely dappled one is not, that the long hair stays uniform, and —
    the one a picture can never show — that strength varies from horse to
    horse, which is the specific way this design breaks if the epigenetics are read
    from the wrong place. A render of one horse would look perfectly correct while every
    silver in the world wore the same coat.

The coat sample tool had been broken for months, silently

    Found while trying to render that probe. bakeCoatSamples was dying on its
    twelfth sample with “gene horsegenetics.grey has no allele 'G'”, so it
    had been producing 11 of its 122 renders and exiting non-zero. The cause is
    dated: the commit that reworked grey into a dosage locus left the sample rows asking for
    grey=G/g, an allele the locus no longer has. Silver’s samples sit below
    the break, which is why the probe produced nothing at all.

    Repaired to real dosage genotypes (G2/N steel, G2/G2 mid,
    G3/G3 nearly white) and all 122 samples render again. The file’s own
    comment already warned that “a sample list is a poor place to keep a claim about
    how a gene works” — it now carries the cost of having been right. The
    wider lesson is the one that closed gap 263 later the same day: a tool or test that
    nobody’s change happens to run can stay broken indefinitely with no session at fault.
