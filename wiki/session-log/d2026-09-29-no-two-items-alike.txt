        Can you recolor every single item we have, other than the breed eggs,
        which is currently identical to something else within this pack or

    Twenty-four sprites moved. The interesting part was not the recolouring; it
    was that the check written to find the duplicates was too weak twice, and
    passed work it was meant to reject both times.


1. WHAT WAS ACTUALLY DUPLICATED

    The answer had to be measured rather than remembered, because "identical"
    is a claim about pixels and about 792 vanilla sprites nobody has in their
    head. tools/check-duplicate-item-art.mjs, and tools/png.mjs under it — a
    PNG reader in about 80 lines, because comparing PICTURES means decoding
    them: two PNGs of one image written by different tools share no bytes, and
    a 32x32 sprite and the 16x16 it was scaled from are still the same art.

    First honest pass found 115 collisions, which was wrong — the reader threw
    `require is not defined` in an ESM module and reported 106 textures as
    unreadable. Fixed, and the real number was 10.

    Then two of those 10 were false, and both for reasons worth keeping:

        golden_carrot_seeds draws minecraft:item/wheat_seeds — and applies a
        horsegenetics:gold_tint over it. It is gold wheat seeds, which is the
        whole point of the item. A checker that reads models but not item
        definitions cannot see that.

        freedom_stick LAYERS minecraft stick and minecraft feather. The
        composite is a picture that does not exist in vanilla. Flagging each
        layer separately was the mistake.

    Both are now exclusions with the reason written next to them. A checker
    that cries wolf about art gets switched off, which costs more than the two
    false entries did.

    That left 7 real collisions over 15 items:

        vet_kit                                  = minecraft bundle
        custom_horse_spawn_egg, preset_...       = minecraft horse spawn egg, and each other
        stall_sign, bound_stall_sign             = minecraft oak sign, and each other
        holding_pen_sign                         = minecraft spruce sign
        5 tickets                                = one sprite between them
        4 whistles                               = one sprite between them
        blank_transfer_paper, signed_..., research_paper = identical pixels


2. THE CHECK WAS TOO WEAK, AND CERTIFIED THE BUG IT EXISTED TO CATCH

    THE ONE THING WORTH READING IN THIS FILE.

    First recolour pass ran, the check went green, and blank_transfer_paper was
    still visibly a research paper. Both are books; putting them side by side
    settled it in a second, which no amount of green output would have.

    Two separate faults, and they compounded:

        The recolour's 'flat' mode MULTIPLIED the source's saturation. The
        research paper sits at 0.40, the recipe asked for 0.45, and the result
        was 0.18 — a slightly different brown. 'flat' now SETS saturation
        absolutely; 'rotate' still multiplies, because keeping the source's own
        relationships is the point of rotating.

        The check compared HASHES, so it only ever answered identical or not.
        Every pixel had genuinely changed. It passed, correctly, on a question
        that was the wrong question.

    So artDistance: mean per-channel distance over the sampled grid, counting
    only cells where at least one sprite is opaque, so two sprites of the same
    SHAPE score on their colour rather than being flattered by the transparent
    surround they share. Threshold 12, empirical — the two books scored about 8
    while being indistinguishable, and every deliberate recolour scores above
    20.

    A checker that certifies the thing it was meant to catch is worse than no
    checker, because it ends the investigation.


3. WHAT THE STRONGER CHECK THEN FOUND

    38 more, of which 30 were the per-wood cart families: an acacia wagon and a
    jungle wagon are alike because acacia and jungle planks are alike. That is
    the contract — the sprite says what the thing is made of — and there is
    nothing to recolour one TO that another wood does not already own. Exempt
    from the NEAR test only; two of them being byte-identical would still be a
    bug and is still caught.

    The other 8 were real and are in the ask:

        4 splice carrots nearly indistinguishable from each other
        5 occupied stasis chambers nearly indistinguishable from each other

    The carrots are ROTATED, not flattened. A flattened carrot stops being a
    carrot — the whole sprite becomes one hue and the family loses the thing
    that says what it is.

    The chambers are a horse in a bottle, which is mostly pale glass whatever
    tier it is, so the tier's colour had almost no sprite left to live in. Each
    took its EMPTY twin's hue at a saturation that survives the glass.

    NOT DONE, DELIBERATELY: the empty chambers' own palette is crowded into a
    third of the wheel (0.27 to 0.55). They are distinguishable from each other
    and so were out of scope. If the occupied five still read as a coin-flip
    in-game, that palette is the thing to move, and it is written on the
    verification tab rather than done unasked.


4. TWO HAZARDS FOUND WHILE BUILDING IT

    IDEMPOTENCE. Several recipes derive a sprite from the art of the item
    itself — a splice carrot rotated away from its neighbours is still that
    carrot. Reading the live texture would have rotated the already-rotated
    output on every run, so a "no-op" re-bake would hand back a different
    palette each time and a checked-in generated asset would be untrustworthy.
    Sources are pristine copies under tools/item-art/, which nothing writes to.
    Verified by running the bake three times and hashing the whole folder: all
    three identical.

    PURPLE CUBES. Fourteen models were repointed at art that did not exist
    until that moment, which is exactly how a missing texture happens, and a
    missing item texture logs nothing at all. The checker gained a
    texture-exists test — the item-side twin of check-block-models.mjs — and it
    was confirmed to bite by pointing vet_kit at a typo and watching it fail.


5. WHAT IS LEFT
