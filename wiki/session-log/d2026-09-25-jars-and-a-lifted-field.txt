2026-09-25 - a horse you can see in the jar, and a field lifted out from under itself

    Two unrelated briefs in one session, queued one after another while the first was
    being built. The stasis half was asked as a sentence ("the stasis chamber should
    have little drawings of the horses, and hitting teleport to home should suck them
    out of the stasis chamber and leave the empty jar in there"); the realm half arrived
    as four more messages over the following hour, one of which turned out to be a bug
    report wearing a feature request.


PART ONE - THE STASIS CHAMBER

The bank's own page had already written the complaint down

    HorseStasisBankScreen's countLine carried the comment: "a bank with forty spare
    chambers in it and a bank with forty horses in it look identical otherwise, since
    every tier shares one item model". So the drawing half was not a new idea, it was a
    known gap with a Verification entry (verify-occupied-art) filed as "the fix is art,
    not a decision". That entry is now deleted and the decision is on the Coding tab,
    per the rule about where a closed check goes.

Everything needed already existed

    HorsePortrait draws one horse small, keeps two throwaway entities for a whole
    screen, and falls back to a flat coat swatch. ClientHorseCoats fetches a coat by
    RECORD uuid, answered from HorseAncestryData - so it works perfectly for an animal
    that has no entity anywhere, which is exactly what a chambered horse is. The two
    together were maybe fifteen lines at each of the two draw sites.

    What took the thinking was the size. A slot is sixteen pixels and the jar's body
    is eight of them; a portrait drawn into a five-pixel hole is a smudge. The answer
    was to draw the portrait at the slot's FULL size and scissor it to the hole, so what
    shows is five pixels OF a sixteen-pixel horse - its barrel, at the scale the rest of
    the mod draws a horse at. FamilyTreeScreen already draws entities inside a scissor,
    so the composition was proven rather than hoped for.

The first texture cut too much out

    The five chambers share one glass bottle and differ only in the colour of the
    opaque body, which is how a tier is read. The first occupied texture cut a window
    through the middle of that body, and the preview showed the obvious: the tier had
    become a one-pixel outline and every jar looked the same. The second cut the window
    out of the UPPER body instead and left the colour as a band of liquid in the base -
    which reads as a specimen jar, keeps the tier legible across a grid of fifty-four,
    and gives the horse a taller window than the first attempt did. Rendered both and
    looked at them before writing any screen code, which is the only reason the first
    one did not ship.

Send home had to find a jar, and the index already knew how

    A horse in a chamber defeats both of StallRecall's ways of finding a horse. It is
    not unloaded - it is not an entity - so waiting for a chunk will never produce it;
    and HorseWhereabouts recorded where it went IN, which is precisely a place it is
    not. The branch therefore had to come before the sighting is read, not after.

    Finding the jar was the part that looked expensive and was not. StasisBankIndex
    already publishes, per bank, whether it holds an empty emergency chamber - a flag
    that exists so the emergency chamber can consult a bank without searching the
    world. Asking it "which horses are in there" is the same trick with a subject: the
    ids come off the snapshot component, which StasisSnapshot unpacks for exactly this
    class of question, so publishing is a field read per slot and the common answer
    costs no chunk at all.

The order of operations is the whole feature

    TicketHandler.landingSpot measures a stall against the horse's own bounding box, so
    the animal has to exist before the destination can be checked - but it must not be
    in the world yet, because everything after that point can still refuse. So release
    was split into restore (build it, do not place it) and place, and the sequence is:
    find the chamber, check it is not already loose, resolve home, restore unplaced,
    check ownership, measure, choose a ticket, and only then place and swap the stack.

    The shape this replaces - release somewhere, then teleport - is a real horse
    standing in the room with the bank for a tick, and on a refusal a bottle opened for
    nothing. Every refusal here leaves the chamber as full as it was, which is the
    promise StasisChamberItem.useOn already made and which the new path had to keep
    rather than restate.

    The destination and its three sentences moved into one Home record when the second
    caller appeared, on the class's own existing doctrine about TicketHandler: the two
    paths look separable and are not.


PART TWO - THE HORSE REALM

"A horse ate the portal and now I'm trapped" is not a feature request

    The message asked for the portal to be cobblestone. The reason given in passing was
    the whole finding: a hay bale is the largest single meal in this mod and a horse
    eats it OUT OF THE WORLD (HayBales, DietFoods), so a portal framed in hay is a
    portal the animals standing beside it demolish - collapsing it through
    HayPortalBlock.updateShape and stranding whoever was on the far side. Neither
    feature is wrong. They simply meet, and nothing in either of them could have caught
    it, because each is behaving correctly.

    What a frame is made of was a bare Blocks.HAY_BLOCK test in FIVE places - the flood
    fill, the portal block's enclosure ray, the realm's two frame builders, and the
    right-click that lights one. It is one method now. The golden carrot is unchanged;
    it was never the part a horse could take off the wall.

    The block id stays hay_portal and the class stays HayPortalBlock. Renaming a
    registered block rewrites every world that has one placed, and there is one of those
    with people in it.

The floor moved to 64, and the rules had to bend once

    Every Y in Java was already derived from HorseRealm.GROUND_Y, so that half was one
    constant. The flat layers in dimension/horse_realm.json are NOT derived and had to
    move with it, and the game test had four bare literals, now derived.

    HorseRealmLift is back-compat code, which hard rule 6 forbids. The owner asked for
    it by name. The defensible reason is not that the owner overrode the rule but that
    the rule's PREMISE has expired: "no saves worth keeping" was true with one tester
    and a dev world, and there is now a shared server with other people on it and a
    field of horses living in this dimension.

    It stores nothing. A chunk built on the old floor has bedrock at y=-1; one generated
    since has air there. One block read, exact, idempotent by construction - a lifted
    chunk no longer answers yes - and silent on every world that never saw the old
    layout. That is HorseRealmTerrain's own argument against a "decorated" flag, used
    again, and it means there is no migration marker that can drift from the blocks it
    claims to describe.

    Source and destination ranges do not overlap, because the offset is larger than
    anything the realm builds, so one ascending pass is safe. Worth re-checking if the
    floor ever moves by a smaller step.

The horses are not moved by the lift, and that found an older bug

    An entity can be in an entity section that has not loaded when its blocks do, so
    releasing them from the lift would miss exactly the ones that matter. Flooring
    anything below the bedrock in HorseRealmRules catches them whenever they turn up -
    and closes a gap nobody had filed: the realm is a bedrock sheet with NOTHING under
    it and had no floor check at all, so anything through it fell forever. Quietly,
    since a horse in the realm cannot die.

Everybody arrives at the middle, and a test now asserts the reverse of itself

    The arrival hash was correct and the goal was wrong. Spreading arrivals across a
    hundred cells does not keep two players out of each other's way in a field sixteen
    thousand blocks across; it keeps their HORSES apart, in hundred-chunk pockets that
    never meet, in a dimension whose entire purpose is horses living together. Bands
    look thirty-two blocks.

    horse_realm_grid_is_stable used to assert the hash SPREAD - a collapse onto a
    handful of cells would have looked, from inside, exactly like a popular meeting
    spot. It now asserts every draw reaches the same middle cell. Kept rather than
    deleted, because "everybody lands together" is now the load-bearing property and a
    hash creeping back would otherwise be noticed only by a player wondering where the
    herd went.

Leaving is a condition, not an event

    The obvious build - hook leaving, find the player's horses, release them - is wrong
    in four ways at once, and each one is a horse that stays tame forever in a field it
    was turned out into. Logging out fires no dimension change. Dying respawns without
    one (the debug corridor needed a separate subscriber for exactly this). Being
    teleported out goes through nothing. And a horse in an unloaded chunk cannot be
    found by any hook - which, in a field this size, is the normal case rather than the
    corner.

    So the rule is a property of the horse: a tamed horse in the realm whose owner is
    not also in the realm is wild. All four cases satisfy it without being handled, and
    a horse in unloaded country is released the moment somebody loads it - the first
    moment it could possibly matter. The scan is half HorseRealmHerds' period so a
    horse is turned out before it is asked to join a band, and the few seconds of delay
    are also what keep the rule from being a trap.

    What "wild" means moved into HorseRelease, shared with the freedom stick. The stick
    stays (owner's call) as the deliberate, immediate version that hands the saddle back
    rather than dropping it in the grass.
