2026-09-24, after the death notice — a horse close to death takes itself over and carries its rider out

    One bullet, the first one left in the Bugs section of intake/To do.txt, in the
    owner's words:

        When a horse reaches 20% health (threshold configurable), it should buck off
        its owner, jump fences, and do whatever it can to escape damage and avoid
        dying.

    Dated first, as START HERE says to. The bullet went in on 2026-09-23 (27cd45aa)
    and the previous session had already named it as the next one. Nothing in the
    repository reads a health threshold to decide behaviour: the only panic goal is
    HorsePanicGoal, whose holdGround() asks about fire, freezing and whether the horse
    has a target, and has no health term at all. AvoidEntityGoal is used nowhere. The
    nearest existing thing is HerdRules.FIGHT_YIELD_HEALTH, which makes a stallion
    yield a takeover fight, and it is not a flee. So: open, and genuinely absent.

    The owning page is horse-care.html — the same page as the hurt and death notices,
    and roadmap-execution.html's own stage 3 already lists "low-health escape" beside
    fence avoidance as part of the behaviour pass it wants.

Why this could not be a health check inside HorsePanicGoal

    That was the first shape it took, and it is wrong in all three of the things the
    sentence asks for. Vanilla's panic NEVER RUNS WHILE THE HORSE IS RIDDEN, and that
    is correct of it: a mounted horse's movement belongs to its passenger, and a goal
    that threw riders off as a side effect of being startled would be a different
    feature. It also holds ground whenever the horse has a target — right for a
    gladiator at full health, suicidal at two hearts. And it is not gated on health,
    so it is the same behaviour for a scratch as for a mortal wound.

    Three divergences is a second goal, not a third branch in an existing one.
    server/HorseEscapeGoal, at priority 0, above panic at 1.

    The arithmetic went to common/care/Escape with EscapeTest on it, for HurtNotice's
    reason: three of the four decisions in the owner's sentence are a threshold and two
    durations. RECOVERY_MARGIN is the one that would have been wrong and invisible —
    without it, a horse that heals a single point stops dead in front of the thing
    killing it and starts again on the next hit, so what it takes to stop is
    deliberately more than what it took to start. THREAT_LINGERS_TICKS is the other
    half: damage is instantaneous and flight is not, and a horse that stops the tick
    the zombie stops swinging is standing next to a zombie.

Then the owner amended it, and the buck came out again

    First it bucked, every tick rather than once, on the reasoning that a horse
    carrying somebody is steered by that rider so one buck plus a remount is the same
    as the goal not running. The owner's amendment, within the hour:

        Instead of bucking the rider off, can you have the horse run to safety with
        the rider, removing the saddle (make sure to know how this interacts with
        bareback riding) and pathing itself to safety, getting a boost to speed and
        jump height while it's fleeing?

    and then, separately: the saddle should go into the rider's inventory.

    This is the better feature and it is also cheaper, because ONE ACT DOES BOTH
    HALVES. Vanilla keeps both answers in the saddle slot: getControllingPassenger()
    hands control to a player passenger only if isSaddled(), and isImmobile() stops the
    mob's own AI moving a horse only while it is ridden AND saddled. So taking the
    saddle off makes the rider a passenger and hands movement to the navigation in the
    same stroke — no rider-input suppression, no mixin. The buck was a way of reaching
    that state by throwing the rider away instead of carrying them.

    The saddle goes to rider.getInventory().add, and rider.drop when that fails, which
    is ModNetworking.handleTackSlot's existing idiom rather than a new one. An unridden
    horse keeps its tack: removing it is about who is steering. And unsaddle() is called
    again from tick(), because a rider sitting on a horse can put a saddle back on it —
    but only start() says anything, or re-saddling would cost a line of chat per tick.

Bareback riding was the right thing to be told to check

    The owner's parenthesis found a bug before it existed. BarebackSteeringHandler
    implements bareback steering by LENDING THE HORSE A REAL SADDLE carrying
    ModDataComponents.PHANTOM_SADDLE, and re-lending it every tick. Two separate
    failures follow, and the naive implementation has both:

    First, a phantom saddle handed to the rider is a free saddle per bolt — the same
    duplication hole reclaim() exists to close (gap 156), reached from a new direction.
    So the stack is checked for the component and a phantom is destroyed rather than
    given, and the rider is told nothing about a saddle they never owned.

    Second, and worse, the lender would put the reins back on the very next tick. That
    bug has the worst available shape: the escape behaviour would work in every test
    and fail only on a bond-81+ horse, which is to say only on the best horse on the
    server and the only kind anybody rides bare. eligibleRider now returns null while
    HorseEscapeGoal.bolting(horse) holds.

    bolting() is a scan of the goal selector rather than a flag on the horse. A static
    set keyed by UUID goes stale the moment a bolting horse is unloaded or killed
    mid-flight, and stop() is not guaranteed to run; a scan cannot. It is asked last in
    eligibleRider, after the cheap tests have ruled out all but a handful of animals.

The boosts, and why they are on JUMP_STRENGTH rather than the impulse

    Escape.SPEED_BOOST is 0.30 and Escape.JUMP_BOOST is 0.40, both transient
    ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL modifiers so a boost is a proportion of that animal rather
    than a flat amount that would be everything to a Falabella and nothing to a Shire.

    The ids live under escape/ and NOT under gene/, deliberately:
    GeneAbilityHandler.clearAttributes sweeps only ids it owns, which is itself the fix
    for a pregnant mare who lost repro/late_pregnancy to another system's tick. Outside
    gene/ nothing else will take these off, so stop() must — and does.

    JUMP_BOOST is the number that decides whether "jump fences" is true at all. A fence
    is a block and a half, which wants about 0.5 of jump strength, and the weakest
    horses sit at 0.4; 40% takes 0.4 to 0.56. That turns the earlier promise from "a
    good jumper escapes" into "a horse escapes, and a good one escapes further", and it
    is the one line of EscapeTest that is about the game rather than about arithmetic.

    Moving the attribute rather than the jump impulse is also deliberate.
    HorseInstantJumpMixin refuses to touch the impulse precisely because the
    information screen reads its metres off the attribute, so an impulse change would
    make that figure a lie. Boosting the attribute keeps it honest: a bolting horse
    shows more metres because it really does jump further.

MOVE and not JUMP, although it jumps

    Flags are declared as MOVE alone. Vanilla's FloatGoal is also at priority 0 and
    holds JUMP, and the goal selector needs every declared flag free to start a goal —
    so claiming JUMP would mean a horse swimming could not start this goal at all,
    which is the exact case (being shot at in deep water) where it most needs to move.
    Vanilla's own PanicGoal claims MOVE alone for the same reason and leaves jumping to
    the move control.

    Flagged in the class, because bake-behaviour-hierarchy.mjs reads the setFlags call:
    the generated page now says MOVE for a class that presses jump, and a later session
    reading only the page would be entitled to think the jumping was a bug.

    The re-bake also reports HorseEscapeGoal and InspectHoldGoal as a priority-0 MOVE
    conflict, correctly — neither can interrupt the other, so the insertion order in
    HorseCareHandler.onHorseJoin is what decides it, and the escape goal is added
    first. Somebody holding the information screen open does not get to pin a bleeding
    animal in place. Adding the goal also un-stuck the page's BondFollowGoal.Watch row,
    which had been missing from the generated span.

Fences are jumped at, not pathed over

    A fence is BLOCKED to a ground navigator, so no path over one is ever produced and
    moveTo simply refuses. SunShadeGoal met this in September and its answer is reused
    verbatim: on a refusal, drive getMoveControl().setWantedPosition straight at the
    escape point and press getJumpControl().jump() while horizontalCollision holds.
    Jump strength is a real attribute with a real range, so whether a horse clears the
    rail is a fact about the horse — which is the right answer to "jump fences" and
    also the thing most likely to disappoint.

    What it runs away from: the attacker's position when getLastHurtByMob() answers,
    otherwise the square it was standing on when the bolt began, which is the right
    answer for lava, a cactus or powder snow where nothing is chasing it and the place
    itself is the problem. When even that is where it still stands, it runs along its
    own view vector.
