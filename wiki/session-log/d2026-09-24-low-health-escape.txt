2026-09-24, after the death notice — a horse close to death bucks its rider and bolts

    One bullet, the first one left in the Bugs section of intake/To do.txt, in the
    owner's words:

        When a horse reaches 20% health (threshold configurable), it should buck off
        its owner, jump fences, and do whatever it can to escape damage and avoid
        dying.

    Dated first, as START HERE says to. The bullet went in on 2026-09-23 (27cd45aa)
    and the previous session had already named it as the next one. Nothing in the
    repository reads a health threshold to decide behaviour: the only panic goal is
    HorsePanicGoal, whose holdGround() asks about fire, freezing and whether the horse
    has a target, and has no health term at all. AvoidEntityGoal is used nowhere. The
    nearest existing thing is HerdRules.FIGHT_YIELD_HEALTH, which makes a stallion
    yield a takeover fight, and it is not a flee. So: open, and genuinely absent.

    The owning page is horse-care.html — the same page as the hurt and death notices,
    and roadmap-execution.html's own stage 3 already lists "low-health escape" beside
    fence avoidance as part of the behaviour pass it wants.

Why this could not be a health check inside HorsePanicGoal

    That was the first shape it took, and it is wrong in all three of the things the
    sentence asks for. Vanilla's panic NEVER RUNS WHILE THE HORSE IS RIDDEN, and that
    is correct of it: a mounted horse's movement belongs to its passenger, and a goal
    that threw riders off as a side effect of being startled would be a different
    feature. It also holds ground whenever the horse has a target — right for a
    gladiator at full health, suicidal at two hearts. And it is not gated on health,
    so it is the same behaviour for a scratch as for a mortal wound.

    Three divergences is a second goal, not a third branch in an existing one.
    server/HorseEscapeGoal, at priority 0, above panic at 1.

    The arithmetic went to common/care/Escape with EscapeTest on it, for HurtNotice's
    reason: three of the four decisions in the owner's sentence are a threshold and two
    durations. RECOVERY_MARGIN is the one that would have been wrong and invisible —
    without it, a horse that heals a single point stops dead in front of the thing
    killing it and starts again on the next hit, so what it takes to stop is
    deliberately more than what it took to start. THREAT_LINGERS_TICKS is the other
    half: damage is instantaneous and flight is not, and a horse that stops the tick
    the zombie stops swinging is standing next to a zombie.

The buck is every tick, which is a deliberate divergence from the advice

    intake/Implementation advice.txt asked for "a single buck-off". It is repeated
    instead, and the reason is the same sentence that made this a separate goal: a
    horse carrying somebody is steered by that rider rather than by its navigation, so
    one buck followed by a remount is the same as the goal not running. Climb back on
    inside the ten seconds and you come back off.

    That is the call most likely to be wrong about how it feels to play, so it is
    written as a pass condition on horse-care.html#escape-0924 rather than buried: if
    the owner would rather ride a bolting horse to safety, the fix is one line.

MOVE and not JUMP, although it jumps

    Flags are declared as MOVE alone. Vanilla's FloatGoal is also at priority 0 and
    holds JUMP, and the goal selector needs every declared flag free to start a goal —
    so claiming JUMP would mean a horse swimming could not start this goal at all,
    which is the exact case (being shot at in deep water) where it most needs to move.
    Vanilla's own PanicGoal claims MOVE alone for the same reason and leaves jumping to
    the move control.

    Flagged in the class, because bake-behaviour-hierarchy.mjs reads the setFlags call:
    the generated page now says MOVE for a class that presses jump, and a later session
    reading only the page would be entitled to think the jumping was a bug.

    The re-bake also reports HorseEscapeGoal and InspectHoldGoal as a priority-0 MOVE
    conflict, correctly — neither can interrupt the other, so the insertion order in
    HorseCareHandler.onHorseJoin is what decides it, and the escape goal is added
    first. Somebody holding the information screen open does not get to pin a bleeding
    animal in place. Adding the goal also un-stuck the page's BondFollowGoal.Watch row,
    which had been missing from the generated span.

Fences are jumped at, not pathed over

    A fence is BLOCKED to a ground navigator, so no path over one is ever produced and
    moveTo simply refuses. SunShadeGoal met this in September and its answer is reused
    verbatim: on a refusal, drive getMoveControl().setWantedPosition straight at the
    escape point and press getJumpControl().jump() while horizontalCollision holds.
    Jump strength is a real attribute with a real range, so whether a horse clears the
    rail is a fact about the horse — which is the right answer to "jump fences" and
    also the thing most likely to disappoint.

    What it runs away from: the attacker's position when getLastHurtByMob() answers,
    otherwise the square it was standing on when the bolt began, which is the right
    answer for lava, a cactus or powder snow where nothing is chasing it and the place
    itself is the problem. When even that is where it still stands, it runs along its
    own view vector.

Not played

    EscapeTest covers the rules. Nothing about the behaviour is tested because all of
    it is NeoForge, and the checks are on horse-care.html#escape-0924. Two of them
    matter more than the rest. The fence one cannot be read off a single horse — a
    poor jumper failing to leave a pen is the honest outcome and a good one failing is
    a bug, so it wants two horses with very different jump stats in the same pen. And
    the water one is the only thing that would show the JUMP-flag reasoning to be
    wrong: a swimming horse that drowns or stands still means FloatGoal and this goal
    are fighting after all.
