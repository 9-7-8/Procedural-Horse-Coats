2026-09-25 - the horse realm is built, and the hay portal now goes there

    The treatment written earlier the same day (d2026-09-25-horse-realm.txt) was
    implemented. Four questions were put to the owner before any code: pool size (2x2
    blocks), where a portal arrives (hashed from the Overworld portal, so the same one
    every time), whether players may build there (no), and how many uses the freedom
    stick has (64). Everything else came off the treatment unchanged.

A second dimension, sharing nothing with the first

    horsegenetics:horse_realm sits beside horsegenetics:debug_pens. The hay-bale portal
    now arrives in the realm; the debug corridor and its test yard are reached by F6
    and by nothing else.

    Every rule is keyed to the realm's level and the corridor reads none of them, which
    is the constraint that shaped the whole implementation rather than a nicety. The
    corridor is an unrestricted test bench on purpose: its blanket "no horse damage"
    rule was deleted on 2026-09-13 because it made six genes untestable, and its
    horses-only entity filter was switched off the day before that because it deleted
    the zombies and cows two gene tests were about. Both are exactly what the public
    realm needs. They were therefore written fresh in server/HorseRealmRules rather
    than generalised out of the corridor, which would have quietly undone two
    deliberate decisions.

    Because F6 became the corridor's only door, the keybind is now registered in every
    build including release jars, and the server decides who may use it:
    debug.tools on, or LEVEL_GAMEMASTERS - the same bar /testkit already sets. An
    ordinary player is told where the hay portal goes instead of getting silence.

The field is arithmetic; nothing about it is saved

    1,000 by 1,000 chunks, chunk 0 to 999 on both axes. The ground is three lines of
    minecraft:flat in dimension/horse_realm.json - fifteen air layers, bedrock, dirt -
    which with min_y: -16 puts bedrock at Y=-1 and dirt at Y=0. min_y has to be a
    multiple of sixteen, which is the only reason it is not simply -1.

    server/HorseRealmTerrain adds the rest on chunk load, deferred one tick and
    idempotent: a 2x2 water pool at every fifth chunk's origin, a lit hay portal at
    every hundredth chunk's origin (a hundred of them), and a sixteen-high wall of
    barrier blocks one block outside the field. A custom ChunkGenerator was the
    obvious answer and was not used: flat is a path this mod already runs on and a
    codec it does not have to register, and what flat cannot do - a fixed grid, and a
    block with a block entity - is trivial against a live ServerLevel. Most chunks do
    no block access at all.

    The water is not decoration. Healing is gated on standing near water, so a dry
    field is one where a hurt horse never recovers - the mistake the test yard made for
    weeks (gap 258).

    Eternal day is an omission rather than a setting: the dimension type carries
    default_clock and no timelines field, and an empty timelines means sky darkness
    never varies. That is the same 26.1.2 fact debug_pens was debugged FOR on
    2026-09-12; here it is the feature.

    Arrival is a hash of the Overworld portal's block position onto one of the hundred
    exits, not a scaled coordinate - the Overworld is millions of blocks wide and the
    realm is sixteen thousand, so any linear mapping puts every portal anyone will ever
    build into one cell. Both of its properties fail silently when broken, which is why
    they are a test rather than a comment.

The breeding rate is a clock, and that is the part worth reading

    Nothing in common.repro counts down. Pregnancy says so in its own header: every
    time is an absolute game tick and every state is derived from an anchor and the
    current tick. There is no timer to divide by four. There is only the reading of
    "now" that each derivation is handed.

    So each horse keeps an offset in its persistent data - ticks it lived through that
    its reproduction did not - and HorseRealmRepro.reproTime(horse) is game time minus
    that offset. Every repro read and write for that horse goes through it. One number,
    one place, and a horse that has never been in the realm has an offset of zero and
    behaves exactly as before.

    That one framing buys all four requirements: the rate (25% credited, 75% deferred,
    so everything takes four times as long), a true pause at zero including existing
    pregnancies, no progress at all while the realm is empty (the horse is not ticking,
    so nothing is credited and all of it is deferred), and correct behaviour on the way
    home (the offset belongs to the horse, so it stops growing and is never taken away;
    clearing it on exit would make a mare instantly, retroactively overdue).

    NaturalBreedingHandler is the one place both clocks appear. Her cycle and her one
    try this heat use her repro clock; the three-second courtship, the chat throttles
    and the log stamps use the world's, because a message is something a player reads in
    real time whatever the realm's pacing.

The freedom stick, and the call that is easy to miss

    A feather, a stick and one horse hair, shapeless, 64 uses, and a refusal costs
    nothing. Used on your own horse in the realm it heals it, returns its tack, and
    un-tames it.

    Un-taming is three calls and the third is the one that matters: setTamed(false),
    setOwner(null), setTemper(0). Without the temper reset the release is a formality -
    vanilla's RunAroundLikeCrazyGoal rolls against temper and a horse that WAS tame sits
    at maximum, so the first player to mount it tames it on the first roll. Nobody would
    notice that was broken.

    Re-taming needed no code. Nothing on HorseRecord or in HorseAncestryData is derived
    from ownership, and HorseOwnerTrackingHandler reconciles the owner mirror every
    forty ticks and accepts null - its javadoc already named "a horse going wild again"
    as a case it handles, long before there was one.

    The saddle has to come off for a structural reason rather than a generous one:
    AbstractHorse.canUseSlot(SADDLE) asks isTamed(), so a saddle left on a horse that
    has gone wild is a saddle quietly destroyed.

    Two design calls were taken rather than asked, both recorded on the realm's coding
    tab: the stick refuses a horse you do not own (a shared field where anyone can
    unclaim anyone's horse is the opposite of a safe one), and a horse standing in a
    realm portal alone does not travel (a released horse surfacing in a stranger's back
    garden is the outcome the whole feature exists to prevent). Players enter with their
    horses; only players, and their own tamed horses within fifty blocks, leave.

Released horses had to be taught to band

    server/HorseRealmHerds exists because a released horse falls through every gate the
    herd system has. HerdManager.assignFounder returns immediately for anything that
    already has a record, and the wild-herd path is gated on the wild-spawn mark that
    HorseFoundingTickHandler strips from every horse loading with one. A released horse
    is the opposite case in every respect. Its band is labelled Feral Mixed rather than
    the lead's breed, because whoever gathers there is whoever was released nearby and
    FERAL is already the mod's word for no herd identity.

What was proven, and what was not

    runServer boots clean and its log names ServerLevel[world]/horsegenetics:horse_realm,
    which is the only available proof that the dimension JSON loads at all.

    Three new gametests, and all fifteen pass: horse_realm_is_built (pools, a lit exit,
    the perimeter), horse_realm_grid_is_stable (four thousand arrival hashes: always a
    real exit, always the same one twice, and spread over at least 75 of the hundred),
    and freedom_stick_crafts.

    horse_realm_is_built usually runs in the Overworld and that is not a mistake:
    GameTestServer builds its world from a hand-written three-dimension list and never
    reads the datapack's dimension/ folder, so the realm cannot exist in that harness
    however correct the JSON is - the runner saves the overworld, the nether and the end
    and nothing else. The geometry does not care which level it writes into, so it is
    asserted wherever it can be, and the two ground assertions, which belong to the flat
    generator rather than to any Java, are asked only when the real dimension is there.

    Nothing has been played. Nobody has walked into the realm, released a horse, come
    back for one, or watched a foal born in it. The full list is the Verification tab of
    wiki/horse-realm.html; the item's half of it is on wiki/item-freedom-stick.html.
    The one check that cannot be made any other way, and is most likely to be wrong, is
    leaving a pregnant mare in an empty realm for a long while and confirming her
    pregnancy did not move.
