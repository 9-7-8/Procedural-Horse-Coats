2026-09-15, later — five more breeds, the duplicate drafts, and a height bug that shipped

    Three pieces, in the order they happened: the third intake batch, then a pass over the drafts that turned out
    to be re-supplies of breeds that already ship, then an audit that started as a one-line check and found a
    mistake in seven breed files — three of them written the same day, four of them shipped earlier.

The batch: Colorado Ranger, Criollo, Croatian Coldblood, Danubian, Domestic Mountain Pony

    - The Criollo is the first breed ever to name manchado, and
        it is carriers only. The locus has always existed and no breed had ever used it, so the pattern was
        reachable solely from the wild founder table; the Criollo is the population it is most reported in, which
        makes it the obvious first. The 6% carrier rate is a design call and says so — there is no
        test for manchado, so there is no frequency to copy. The gene page had no Breeds section at all to
        contradict, which is why this needed adding rather than correcting.

    - The Domestic Mountain Pony is the best test the mod has of the no-At decision.
        It is the rare breed with real coat data: Cotman etal. genotyped 313 horses and found dark
        bay 44%, black 36%, ordinary bay 11%, and the true-dun D allele at 0.09. Dark bay is carried
        by a deliberately ShD-skewed shade pool
        rather than by an agouti allele, because At was dropped as unvalidated — so here is a
        population whose target proportions are measured instead of guessed, and whether it reads as dark
        bay in the world is the thing to look at. It is also the only breed whose dun rate is a published allele
        frequency, and it inherits that study's argument with itself: D at 0.09 makes about 17% of
        founders dun where the same study counted 8% by eye. The genotype was followed over the
        eyeball, deliberately, and that is the call to revisit if duns look too common.

    - The Colorado Ranger is the first breed whose strain forces loci wild rather than
        naming a pool to draw from. Its registry certifies a pedigree and not a colour, so eleven founders in
        twenty are solid with lp/lp and both PATN modifiers pinned off, and nine carry the leopard
        complex — deliberately lighter than the Appaloosa's, which stays the defining leopard breed. Pinto is
        barred by that registry within five generations, so no tobiano, frame, splash or dominant white in either
        strain.

    - Two more breeds that never run wild. The Croatian Coldblood and the Danubian are the
        second and third to use the spawn checklist after the Budyonny — both managed working
        breeds with no feral population. The path is still unexercised in a real world.

    - One intake file did not hold the breed its name promised.
        intake/Croatian warmblood.md was a complete, well-sourced draft of the Croatian
        Coldblood, naming it throughout and contrasting it with the Posavac. The coldblood shipped and the
        file was consumed, so if a Croatian Warmblood was wanted, no draft for it has ever arrived.
        Recorded on the checklist because deleting the file removed
        the only trace of that queue entry.

The duplicates: four drafts for breeds that already ship

    The owner's next drop included drafts for the American Cream Draft, Irish Draught, SA Boerperd and the Bosnian
    Mountain Horse — the last being the breed shipped hours earlier as the Domestic Mountain Pony. Each one's
    new science went into the breed it duplicates and into the gene page that owns the locus, then the draft was
    deleted. A breed has nowhere of its own to put science: neither
    breeds.html nor the book carries tab panels at all, so
    locus-level findings went to the science tabs of the gene pages and breed-level facts to the breed's own
    notes.

    - JEB1 has no locus, and it is half of a shipped breed's real test panel. The American Cream
        Draft's association tests exactly two things — PSSM1, which ships, and junctional epidermolysis
        bullosa, which does not exist here at all. That is the most conspicuous gap on any shipped breed's own
        panel, so it went to §12 rather than being stood in with an
        unrelated recessive lethal (LAMC2 has since shipped). Both loci are left clear on the breed: presence is documented and no frequency
        is, and for a dominant that distinction decides everything, because a guessed rate would show on
        every copy instead of hiding in carriers. GYS1 now names both breeds
        whose panels include it and says why neither carries a pool.

    - The studbook colour list is why dun is in the Irish Draught's pool. Horse Sport Ireland
        names six whole colours and calls excessive white undesirable. Two shipped pools sit outside that list
        — roan, and a trace of cream — and were kept as shipped and written down as the open
        question rather than quietly deleted, because re-tuning a breed that already ships is a different
        decision from writing a new one.

    - The Boerperd bars a double-cream foal from registration, which is the same
        registry-rule-not-disorder shape as the Chilean Corralero: cream stays high enough for buckskin, palomino
        and smoky black, low enough that a double dilute is an uncommon founder, and reachable by breeding.

    - The Bosnian draft's real gift was a size finding. Schrimpf etal.'s
        homozygosity scan shows long-term selection toward roughly 135cm at the withers and small-allele
        homozygosity at the body-size loci — so the breed is small because it was selected small,
        not because it carries the pony allele. It is not the p.G28E test, so
        HMGA2 records it with that caveat and the breed names no pool.
        Dun also gained the only real breed-wide D figure on its
        page.

The height bug, and why it had been “verified”

    - BreedStatCurve triples the part of a size ratio above 1.0
        (BIG_GAMMA), so above the 15.75hh baseline
        hands = 15.75 × (1 + (s−1)/3) and a file written with the obvious
        hands / 15.75 comes out short — only at the top of its band, which is why it
        looks right. Three breeds in this batch had it. Auditing all 65 found four more already shipped: the
        Anglo-Arabian, Australian Stock Horse, Azteca and Budyonny were each shorter than their own table
        row claimed, the Budyonny by most of a hand. Seven files corrected, and all 129 height strings
        across the two breed pages regenerated from the files.

    - The reason it survived a check is the useful part. The rule had been confirmed against the
        Mustang and recorded as sound — but the Mustang's band lies entirely below 1.0, where both
        the linear and the real curve give the same answer. Verifying the arithmetic on a pony proves
        nothing whatever about a draught horse. The Percheron (1.38  17.7hh) and
        the Irish Draught (1.238  17.0hh) are the discriminating cases.

    - A third error mode turned up on the way: the Dhampir's row read 17.3 because
        a decimal number had been typed into the stable-notation column. The two pages use different
        notations — the table is stable, where 16.3 is
        sixteen hands and three inches, and the book is decimal — so the same three digits are two different
        horses and copying a height between them is silently wrong.

    - Nothing asserts any of it, which is gap 257: no test
        reads a breed file's size against the height printed for it. A BreedFilesTest case parsing the
        two pages would close it for good.

    - A test-runner trap worth knowing: :common:test does not
        dependsOn :common:bakeBreedFiles, so asking for both in one Gradle command races
        — the test reads the browser bundle while the bake is still writing it and fails with “the
        browser bundle is stale— run bakeBreedFiles”, which is a lie. Run the bake as its own
        invocation, then the tests. The same message for real means a breed file was edited after the
        bake.

    Green: :neoforge-26.1.2:build, BreedFilesTest, BreedSpecParserTest,
    check-parity, check-links, check-gene-tabs and the designer boot check;
    every pool of the five new breeds was dumped back out of the baked files pair by pair, because a wrong
    allele token is warned and dropped rather than thrown. None of the five has been seen in a world, and
    four already-shipped breeds are now visibly taller —
    §0-EG says what to look at, and the dark-bay check on the
