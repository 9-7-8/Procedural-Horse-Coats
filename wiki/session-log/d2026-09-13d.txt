2026-09-13 — reading the yard back, and a leak that was hiding inside the number meant to detect it

    The first run of the new yard, read off the log. Most of the pens answered
    their own questions; one was lying; and the census turned out to have been
    concealing a leak in the one figure that exists to find leaks.

What the log settled outright

    - Egg layer, all eight alleles. Every one dropped its own
        item — leather, wool, bone, slime, ink, feather, string, egg. The
        pen separates itself exactly as designed, because a feather can only
        have come from the feather horse.

    - The six deathbed kills dropped leather and nothing else.
        Killed inside thirty seconds, and the only items to appear were plain
        vanilla horse drops. That is precisely the signature of a handler that
        returns before getDrops().clear(), and it is how
        the seventh protection was confirmed
        rather than merely suspected.

    - The gladiator's death, to the tick. Seven consecutive
        zombie hits, twenty ticks apart, three damage each, into twenty-two
        health: dead in 6.4 seconds with no misses. Nothing was malfunctioning
        — a horse and a zombie both swing every twenty ticks for three
        damage, which is exact parity, and exact parity is a coin flip.
        Worth stating because it is the opposite of the intuitive reading: the
        fix is not "make the horse work", it is "stop making it a fair fight".

    - Bone-meal dryad honours the crop carve-out. One
        gene fertilised | grass_block and eighteen wheat standing
        untouched all run. The carve-out cannot be observed directly - a crop
        nobody fertilised grows anyway - so the evidence is the list of what it
        did touch, which is why that line exists.

The pen that was lying, and why the lie is the more useful finding

    The eyesight pen reported movement_speed 0.165-0.221 in the lit
    half and 0.165-0.221 in the dark half, census after census.
    Identical to three decimals reads as a dead gene.

    It is very probably the opposite. Caveborn is faster in the dark and
    daywalker slower, so the two horses' values swap when the
    condition flips — and a set whose members have exchanged places has
    exactly the same minimum and maximum as one that never moved. The tell was
    in the outliers: three censuses read 0.165-0.165 and four read
    0.221-0.221, which are the moments mid-swap with one horse
    flipped and the other not. A measurement aggregated over the things
    being compared cannot see the comparison — the same sentence
    that gap 211 has been making about the
    spread counter. The readout names each horse now.

    Beside it, 7 480 heal lines in eighteen minutes, almost all
    of them ordinary gated regen ticking the hundred and forty gallery horses
    back to full. The healer pen's own readings were in there and unfindable.
    Healing is scoped to a watched pen now; damage stays dimension-wide, because
    a horse dying anywhere is worth a line and this log is the argument for it.

The leak, which took the owner's own account to identify

    The census went from 205 horses to 290 across one plot
    rebuild. Two independent bugs on the same path, and the second one is the
    interesting half.

    The teardown sweep was blind. It was a single
    getEntities(plotBox(plot)), and an entity query returns only
    what is in a loaded chunk — while a plot is a corridor
    thousands of blocks long whose far end unloads the moment nobody is standing
    in it. Every horse past the player's chunk radius survived, and the teardown
    said nothing about it because it had nothing to say. It walks the built
    range chunk by chunk now, forcing each and releasing it immediately, bounded
    by highestIndex rather than the declared box. It also releases
    the watch's forced chunks after the sweep rather than before, which
    was the same mistake in miniature.

    And dying is a third way out of the dimension that nothing was
    watching. PlayerChangedDimensionEvent does not fire for
    a respawn - a respawn builds a new player entity rather than travelling - so
    a player who died in a plot left it claimed, built, and holding forced
    chunks for the life of the world. The owner supplied that half herself:
    “I did die and have to respawn and go back in, if that could be
    it.” It was.

    The reason this one is worth a section rather than a line:
    the entity count is the single number in the census that says whether this
    dimension is leaking, and for as long as this was open that number was
    measuring the leak rather than reporting it. An instrument that is
    itself downstream of the fault is the hardest kind to distrust, and the only
    thing that broke it open was a figure that moved when nothing should have
    moved. The teardown now logs what it removed, so the next one is checkable
    instead of inferred.
