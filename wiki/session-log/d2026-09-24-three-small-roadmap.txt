The subagent's report was wrong about the one thing that mattered, and checking cost a minute

    An Explore agent was used to verify the five claims against the source before recommending
    them. It reported that WhistleItem "silently destroys" the lead — no item drop. That is
    not what vanilla does. Leashable.dropLeash() is dropLeash(this, true, true), and the
    second true IS the ground drop; the lead was being dropped where the horse stood, not
    destroyed. The fix changes where the lead ends up, not whether it exists.

    Read out of the decompiled Leashable.java in the neoformruntime cache rather than
    believed. This is the memory "verify a prescribed fix first" earning its keep on a
    report that was otherwise accurate on all six points.

Item 1 — the area whistles had no tooltip at all

    EnderWhistleItem has had appendHoverText since it was built; WhistleItem never did. So
    three tiers, one shared icon, three names, and the numbers 16 / 32 / 64 appeared NOWHERE
    a player could read them — the recipe blurbs say "further than the basic one" without
    ever saying how far. The only way to learn a radius was to walk out and count.

    It reads the radius field rather than taking a fourth constructor argument or a per-tier
    lang key, for the same reason WhistleItem.task() does: the radius is already the only
    thing distinguishing the three, and a second parallel field is a second thing that can
    disagree with it.

    Second line is "A ridden horse stays put; a leashed one is untied" — the two outcomes the
    verification tab says get reported as bugs. It deliberately stops short of saying where
    the lead GOES, because that became a server config in item 4 and a client on a dedicated
    server has no copy of the server's config to read.

    The whistles page's Roadmap tab had a bullet reading "A tooltip naming the horse, and a
    texture that tells a bound whistle from an unbound one". The tooltip half was ALREADY
    BUILT (the ender whistle names its horse) and had been sitting there as a plan. Deleted
    that half and left the texture half, which is still real: the four are now only
    indistinguishable in the hotbar, where nothing draws a tooltip.

Item 2 — "only the first time" meant "never again this session"

    HorseBrowserScreen.init() asked for the roster only if !ClientHorseRoster.received().
    That reads as a lazy first fetch. It is not: ClientHorseRoster.clear() runs on disconnect
    and nowhere else, so received() stays true for the whole login. A player who bred a foal,
    tamed a horse or sold one saw the stable as it was the first time they pressed H, until
    they found the Refresh button. The same guard was on the tab-switch path, so switching
    onto My horses did not fix it either.

    The roadmap called this "trivial — drop the !received() guard". Dropping it outright is
    not quite right, because HorseRoster.gather walks the whole ancestry table plus the allele
    and breed sweeps, and clicking across the tab strip is a casual gesture in a way pressing
    Refresh is not. Added requestRosterUnlessJustAsked(), which skips a sweep within two
    seconds of the last. The cooldown is static so closing and reopening the screen does not
    reset it — that is exactly the gesture it exists to soak up. The Refresh button is
    deliberately NOT throttled: it exists to be believed.

    received() is still read, and now only for what it was always good for: telling an empty
    stable from "the answer has not arrived yet", in three places including
    HorseStasisBankScreen.

    Birth and death pushes are still owed and stay on the roadmap tab. They are the actual
    work — a hook in the foaling and death paths and a payload that says what changed.

Item 4 — the lead was being left behind, and in one case in another dimension

    Six places in the mod call dropLeash(). Every one of them is untying a horse before
    teleporting it, because a leash across a teleport is a leash to nowhere. Vanilla's
    dropLeash() drops the lead at the animal's position, which is right for a leash that
    snapped and wrong for one we cut: the horse arrives beside you and the lead stays put.
    Up to 64 blocks for an echo whistle. In ANOTHER DIMENSION for an ender whistle or an
    interdimensional ticket, where it is simply gone.

    server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player) uses removeLeash() — the same untie with the
    drop suppressed — and hands the lead to the player who caused the move, falling back to
    a drop at their feet when their pack is full. Under behaviour.leads_return, default on,
    off restores vanilla exactly.

    Three of the six sites converted: WhistleItem, EnderWhistleCalls.deliver, and
    TicketHandler.arrive (which grew a Player parameter, the only signature this touched).

    The two portal paths were left alone ON PURPOSE and that is the interesting call.
    PortalEventHandler and HorsePortalManager untie a horse that walked into a portal itself,
    with whoever is holding the lead standing right next to it — so the lead is already at
    their feet and vanilla's behaviour is the legible one. Converting them would have moved a
    lead from "on the ground in front of you" to "in your pack" for no gain.

    The sixth is DebugPenManager, a debug tool, left alone.

    untieFor is safe on an unleashed horse, so the call sites dropped their own isLeashed()
    guards rather than keeping a second copy of the check.

    NOTE for whoever finishes the rider-comfort bullet: the GENERAL Horse Tweaks behaviour —
    a lead returning whenever VANILLA drops one, e.g. a leash snapping at range — has no
    clean seam. NeoForge 26.1.2 publishes no leash event at all (grepped its sources jar:
    zero hits for leash/leashable outside a recipe file), and dropLeash spawns its item
    through Entity.spawnAtLocation with nothing to hook. The options are a mixin or an
    EntityJoinLevelEvent filter that would have to tell a snapped lead from one a player
    threw on the floor. That is written onto the rider-comfort Roadmap tab so it is not
    rediscovered.

The gametest, and confirming it can fail

    whistled_lead_comes_back in gametest/ModGameTests, the eleventh. In a gametest rather
    than JUnit for the usual reason: the NeoForge module's test classpath carries no
    Minecraft, and the assertion is about a real Leashable, a real inventory, and whether an
    ItemEntity appeared in a real level.

    It asserts the two halves SEPARATELY — no lead on the ground, and a lead in the player's
    pack. Passing the first while failing the second is the shape of the bug and would be
    worse than the behaviour it replaced, so one combined assertion would have been the wrong
    test.

    Then the step that is easy to skip: the helper was temporarily reverted to always call
    dropLeash() and the suite re-run. It went red with exactly the intended message ("the
    lead did not reach the player who whistled — it has been destroyed rather than
    returned"), and green again on restore. A test that has never been seen to fail is not
    yet a test.

    Two things worth knowing for the next gametest:

        makeMockPlayer(GameType) is the one to use, not makeMockServerPlayerInLevel(), which
        is @Deprecated(forRemoval) and really joins the player list. A leash holder only has
        to be an Entity — Leashable.setLeashedTo never asks whether it is in the level — and
        a mock player's inventory is a real one.

        The "no item on the ground" assertion runs five ticks later via runAfterDelay. An
        assertion that something is ABSENT is the one that passes wrongly if asked too early.

What the next session should pick up

    Item 3 from the shortlist is still the cheapest thing on compatibility.html and is
    untouched: a load-time warning when Realistic Horse Genetics is installed. Both mods
    replace the horse and both own coat generation, so whoever registers last wins, and a
    player currently discovers that as a half-broken world. compat/HorsePoweredCompat is the
    pattern to clone almost exactly — a list of candidate mod ids, ModList.get().isLoaded,
    one log line — and the only open question is whether a log line is enough for a player
    who does not read logs.
