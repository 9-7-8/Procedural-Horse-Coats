2026-09-29 - giving the blocks and items art of their own


    An art session. It started as an intake job for third-party texture packs
    and turned into the mod drawing its own furniture, because the audit the
    packs prompted found more borrowed art than anyone had counted.

    THE PACKS, AND THEIR TERMS

    Four art packs are now on the machine under intake/potential-assets/, one
    folder each with its licence. Only pixel-art-textures (FlakDeau, CC0) has
    its images in git; the Raven icons, the pixelated patterns and the Internet
    Pattern Book are on disk and ignored, by per-pack .gitignore rules. Every
    pack's notes record what the terms actually are rather than what a store
    page implies:

      - Raven Fantasy HD. A real licence, with a MODS clause that permits use
        in a free mod provided users are told they may not take the assets on.
      - pixelated-patterns. The store page states NO terms at all - fetched
        again to check - only a "Royalty Free" tag, which covers fees and not
        redistribution. Treated as all rights reserved.
      - Internet Pattern Book. The uploader says outright that the Geocities
        tiles are not theirs. The owner took them anyway, knowingly; her
        reasoning is written in the pack's notes so no later session reopens it.
      - piiixl. Queued, opened, then DELETED at the owner's call: the author
        stated the terms inconsistently across their own comment thread, and a
        pack whose licence moves depending on which reply you read is not worth
        building art on. The README says so, so nobody helpfully re-adds it.

    Three traps found by opening them rather than trusting the pages: 116 of
    the Internet Pattern Book's 331 files are not the format their extension
    claims, 195 of the 1043 CC0 tiles are 16-bit PNGs that the repo's own
    png.mjs refuses, and the Raven pack's separated files are NOT laid out
    linearly on its spritesheet - the rows have trailing gaps, so the obvious
    index arithmetic picks the wrong icon. Hashing all 806 files against all
    896 cells is what got the right ones.

    WHAT THE AUDIT FOUND

    Eight of twelve blockstates had no art of their own. Five job-site posts
    shared ONE texture set, so a player could not tell the leatherworker's post
    from the scientist's; three more wore vanilla furniture unchanged - the
    bench was the smithing table, the shelf the bookshelf, the stasis bank the
    barrel. On the item side 111 of 164 sprites share a silhouette, but only 39
    are a defect: 72 are the per-wood cart families, which is vanilla's own
    idiom for boats and doors.

    The measurements that ranked the work: the ten stasis chambers own seven of
    the ten closest pairs in the roster, and the closest pair anywhere is 13.6
    against a checker that fails under 12.0. The breed eggs, never audited
    before, turn out to be the best art in the mod - 1154 to 2128 colours each,
    closest pair 39.0.

    WHAT SHIPPED

    Five posts, each carrying its trade on the same barrel carpentry, with the
    hoops in the colour of that equestrian's hat so the two identities are one
    thing to learn. The Tack Dyeing Bench, the Equine Research Shelf and the
    Horse Stasis Bank, all drawn. The golden carrot crop in gold art instead of
    a gold filter over Mojang's carrots. Three stall signs that are three
    different drawings rather than one drawing in three hues. And three
    licensed Raven icons - the vet's kit, the freedom stick, the golden carrot
    seeds - with the credit, the users-may-not-redistribute line and an
    explicit CC BY-NC carve-back for exactly those three files in
    THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.

    Every block and item in the mod now has art of its own except
    breed_spawn_egg, which is vanilla's horse spawn egg recoloured - the
    correct idiom for a spawn egg.

    THE WIKI SIDE

    Item pages show their items: 55 sprites across 18 pages, captioned from the
    mod's own lang file. Blocks with their own art get a rotatable 3D preview in
    a gameplay-tab infobox - the owner's correction, because the models are for
    players to see and were first put on the Coding tab.

    The rule that only own-art blocks may be previewed is ENFORCED, not
    remembered: both bakes hard-fail on a minecraft: texture, naming the block
    and the face. Verified by pointing the block one at the bench, the stasis
    bank and a jump on purpose - three errors, exit 1. The item bake also fails
    on any shippable item that no page shows, which is the only version of
    "every item page has that item" that survives new items being added.

    Gates and jumps are settled the other way: their vanilla planks are the
    point, a birch gate should look like birch, and the consequence - that they
    can never have a preview - is written on both pages so the absence reads as
    a decision.

    THINGS THAT WENT WRONG, AND WHAT THEY TAUGHT

    The block preview first used fetch() for its shape data. That works on a
    server and breaks silently for anyone who clones the repo and double-clicks
    a page, because file:// refuses to fetch a sibling. It ships as a script
    assigning a global. chrome.mjs opening pages over file:// is what caught it.

    Keying the wiki's item strips on TEXTURE names rather than item ids failed
    on three sprites, and the failure was the useful kind: horse_ticket.png is
    the blank ticket's art and horse_whistle.png is the basic whistle's, so
    neither texture name has a lang entry. The bake refused to caption them.

    Four superseded recipes had to LEAVE bake-item-recolours.mjs rather than be
    ignored - it writes straight over textures/item/, so a recipe left behind
    would have repainted the new art on the next re-bake.

    Two renames in one day: horse_traders_post_bottom -> post_bottom ->
    plank_bottom. The first was right for a block family of one and wrong the
    moment a second family shared the file. Recorded rather than hidden.

    NONE OF IT HAS BEEN PLAYED. Every page that gained art gained an open check
    with something specific to look at, chosen because each fails quietly: a
    model that lost its top slot wears side art on the lid; a renamed shared
    texture leaves a magenta underside; hoop colours can only be judged with the
    villager actually standing at the post.
