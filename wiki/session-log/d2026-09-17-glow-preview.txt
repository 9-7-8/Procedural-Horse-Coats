2026-09-17, after the regions work — A glow can be seen without starting the game

    §10’s last entry was “the creator
    cannot show a glow”, and the roadmap framed the fix as shading in the
    preview. Asked for it across the horse designer, the breed designer, the custom
    spawn egg and the gene pages, two of those four turned out not to be the job described.

Two findings that changed the work before it started

    The spawn egg already renders glows.
    CustomHorseSpawnScreen.drawPreview builds a real
    EntityRenderState and goes through GeneticHorseRenderer, which
    carries EmissiveCoatLayer — so a glowing gene has been emitting there
    all along. What it cannot do is dim: vanilla’s
    g.entity(state, scale, translation, rot, camRot, x0,y0,x1,y1) takes no
    lighting argument. Owner’s call was to look first and build a
    darkened-texture variant only if the glow does not read against the GUI’s
    directional shading — it is the most expensive item in the job and it is for one
    screen.

    The gene creator cannot have this, and it is a hard rule rather than a cost.
    Its preview is the one engine that never touches the wasm —
    spec-engine.js / fields.js, the hand-written port, which has no
    emissive pass at all. Writing one is exactly what hard rule 3 forbids. So the creator was
    left out and the roadmap entry rewritten to the job that would actually close it: move
    that preview onto the wasm so it can bake an unsaved spec, which would also end
    the port’s reason to exist.

One fold, in common/, because the browser draws the same glow

    The interesting part was not the toggle. “The level becomes the texel’s
    alpha, the RGB stays the coat’s own colour” lived inside
    GeneticCoatTextureFactory.buildGlow, and giving the browser a glow meant
    either calling it or copying it. It moved to
    CoatTextureComposer.Baked.glowSheet, and the whole-part set a
    glow effect lights moved with it as
    CoatTextureComposer.glowParts — that second one mattered, because
    three shipped genes (glow, nebula points, suntouched) get
    their light from the effect rather than from an emissive layer, and leaving the resolver
    behind in the renderer would have made exactly those three the ones that did not light in
    the designer. Both modules now call one implementation; the NeoForge side got
    smaller.

    Alpha is the level because the emissive pass blends: 0.4 is four tenths of the
    full-bright colour over six tenths of the world-lit texel, a dimmer colour rather than a
    darker one. The browser cannot express that the same way — three reads an emissive
    map’s RGB and has no alpha to honour — so setGlow premultiplies
    (RGB × level, opaque) and reaches the same answer by the route that
    renderer has. That is the one deliberate divergence, noted on both files.

The lights go down, not the glow up

    A glow is always drawn; in daylight it is simply lost in an already-bright coat, exactly
    as in game. So the toggle dims the scene — lights, sky and fog — and leaves
    the emissive map alone, which is what an emissive map is for. It does not go to black:
    a horse lit by nothing is a silhouette, and you could not tell whether the unlit half of
    a marking is the shape you drew.

    On the gene pages the button is hidden until a bake proves this horse lights
    up. Asking the pipeline beats a per-gene flag: it is right for a built-in Java
    gene painting emissive texels in overlay(), for a spec gene’s emissive
    layer and for a glow effect alike — and right per outcome, so a
    heterozygote under its minDose offers no dead button. Changing to an outcome
    that does not glow puts the lights back on rather than stranding the reader in the dark
    with the way out hidden.

What was measured, including one guess that had to stop being one

    emissiveMap on the pinned three r128 was an assumption, and
    the failure mode is invisible — a perfectly good horse that never lights. It is
    asserted now by wiki/tools/check-glow-render.mjs, on both material types
    (Lambert for the horse designer, Standard for everything else), along with the white
    emissive that multiplies the map. The same check drives the button on a real
    gene page: tron reveals it, agouti leaves it hidden — the whole chain, export to
    DOM, in a browser. GlowSheetTest pins the arithmetic (0.4  alpha 102,
    a transparent texel never lit, an ordinary horse  null rather than a
    sheet of zeroes). Both coat goldens came through untouched, which is the
    evidence that the fold was lifted rather than rewritten.

    Not done, on purpose: the LUT lab shares
    viewport.js and so inherits the capability, but was given no control —
    it was not asked for and its question is the gradient, not the dark. The breed
    designer’s six-founder herd strip is six coat sheets at 76px rather than
    six horses, and a flat sheet has no lighting for a glow to be brighter than, so only its
    3D founder lights.
