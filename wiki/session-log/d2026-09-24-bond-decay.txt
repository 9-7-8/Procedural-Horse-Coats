The one number that was hers

    The rate and the floor were asked as options. She took 31 — the bottom of behaviour
    tier 1 — and both numbers in the config rather than as constants.

    31 is the interesting half. It means the worst neglect can do is take back the tiers
    where a horse walks to you (61) and steers bareback (81), and it can never take back
    the one where the horse lifts its head as you walk past. A horse you raised from a
    foal does not become a stranger. That is the same correction foals-start-at-zero
    already got, and the punishing-husbandry line in philosophy.html rules out the
    alternative anyway.

Three decisions inside a fifteen-line block

    Elapsed days, not a tick rate. A horse spends most of its life in an unloaded chunk,
    so anything ticked is a rate on *attention*: the horse nobody visits keeps its bond
    forever and the one in the pasture you ride through loses it. Charging for every whole
    day since the stamp is the only version of "over time" that means the same thing to
    both horses. This is the persistence question Implementation advice.txt section 2
    raised, and the answer is that the marker already existed — dayStamp, which the daily
    gain cap rolls over on. Sharing it means whichever of the two moves it first, the other
    reads that day as already counted.

    A stamp of zero is stamped, not charged. This is the one that would have shipped a
    disaster. Bond can be written straight onto the attachment without going through
    awardBond — HorseBreedingHandler.inheritBond (a foal's birthright), /bond, the debug
    yards — and all of those leave dayStamp at its default of 0. In an established world
    that reads as hundreds of days owing, so every such horse would have been slammed to
    the floor on its first tick. An unstamped horse is now stamped with today and charged
    nothing. It is in the in-game checklist as the check that would be a disaster.

    The floor is asked about before the subtraction. A stamp old enough to overflow
    days * perDay comes back negative, which reads as bond owed *back*. decayed() compares
    days against the days-to-floor instead, and BondTest passes Long.MAX_VALUE/2 at it.
