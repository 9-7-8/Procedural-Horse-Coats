2026-09-24, after the three roadmap items — the stasis bank holds two hundred horses, which meant
teaching a container grid to scroll

    The owner asked:

        Update the horse stasis bank to hold 200 horses, then create a new release

    One sentence, and almost none of the work was the number.


The constant was the small half

    HorseStasisBankBlockEntity.SLOTS was 54 — "a double chest's worth", six rows of nine, and
    the grid was drawn by the screen and placed by the menu at exactly those coordinates. Only
    six places in the codebase read SLOTS at all, so changing the number compiles in a minute.

    What does not work is drawing it. Two hundred slots is about twenty-three rows of nine,
    which is roughly four hundred pixels of grid in a window that is two hundred and twenty-two
    pixels tall, over a player inventory that has to stay on screen. So the real question put to
    the owner was how the Chambers tab should show a grid it cannot fit, and the answers were:

        scroll it (not page it), and 207 rather than 200

    207 is twenty-three clean rows. 200 would have left a last row of two slots and seven gaps,
    which reads as a bug rather than as a limit. The gameplay prose says "two hundred and seven"
    once and "two hundred" thereafter.


Scrolling a container grid: two designs, and the one that cannot desync

    There are two ways to scroll slots in an AbstractContainerMenu, and they are not equally
    safe.

        Scroll a VIEW. Keep 54 slots at fixed positions and have them read a moving window of
        the 207-slot container. This is what it sounds like it should be. It is wrong here:
        the slot-to-container mapping now depends on the scroll offset, so the client and the
        server must agree on that offset before every click. A scroll packet overtaking a click
        packet files the player's horse in the wrong chamber, or takes the wrong one out. These
        are pedigreed animals; "usually right" is not a specification.

        Scroll the SLOTS. Keep all 207 slots, each permanently bound to its own container
        index, and move where the client draws them. Slot index to container index never
        changes, on either side. The offset is client-only, like the tab already is.

    The second one is right, and it is right for a reason that is worth writing down: a Slot's
    position is not shared state. The server places its copy of the menu once and never reads
    x or y again, because a click travels as an index. So the two sides being at different
    scroll offsets is not a desync — it is not information the server has or wants.

    The catch is that Slot.x and Slot.y are `public final int`, and AbstractContainerScreen
    reads them AS FIELDS — renderSlot, renderSlotContents and findSlot all say `slot.x`. So a
    Slot subclass cannot make itself movable: there is no accessor to override, and overriding
    one would not help, because nothing calls it. Two `public-f` lines in
    accesstransformer.cfg, with the safety argument above written into the comment. Full
    reasoning is now on wiki/api-notes.html#slot-position-final.

    The other half is isActive(), which vanilla honours in BOTH findSlot and the render loop.
    A slot scrolled out of the window returns false and is neither drawn nor hit-tested. This
    was already the idiom in this exact file — it is how the three tabs hide each other's slots
    — including the rule that the server returns true unconditionally, because the server
    cannot know what the player is looking at and does not need to. Scrolling reuses it rather
    than inventing anything.


The scrollbar cost the window nothing

    The Browse tab already draws a three-pixel wheel-only thumb, so the grid got the same one
    rather than a new idiom. It fits in four pixels that were already spare: the last column's
    well ends at MARGIN + 8*18 + 17 = 169, and the window's own shadow starts at WIDTH - 3 =
    173. SCROLL_X and SCROLL_W are written as that arithmetic rather than as 169 and 4, so the
    bar cannot drift off the gutter if the grid ever moves. The window is still 176 x 222, the
    player inventory did not move, and nothing else on any of the three tabs was relaid out.

    Neither bar can be dragged, on purpose and consistently: the thumb's job is to say there is
    more below, and the wheel is how a player would reach it anyway.


What got worse, and was shipped anyway

    The bank's known-unmeasured bandwidth check got about four times bigger. Every chamber's
    component carries the horse's WHOLE entity tag — deliberately, because a lossy stream codec
    is how a creative pick-block silently erases a horse — and the entire grid goes to the
    client in one ClientboundContainerSetContentPacket on open. Scrolling changes which rows are
    DRAWN, never which are SENT.

    So verify-bank-bandwidth was rewritten rather than left alone, and it now says two things it
    did not say before. First, that the honest fix if it is bad is no longer "a smaller grid or a
    paged one" — the grid is deliberately not smaller now — but sending only the rows the player
    is looking at, which is real work and not a constant to turn down. Second, that nobody has
    established what size ClientboundContainerSetContentPacket a vanilla client will refuse: at
    54 chambers this was a performance question, and at 207 it might be a hard failure on open.
    That is the first thing to look at in the next play session.

    The healing rotation also got slower, and was left alone. The bank mends one chamber per
    turn every StasisUpkeep.HEAL_INTERVAL ticks, so a full bank now takes the better part of
    five minutes to come back round to any given horse. Speeding it up to compensate would mean
    a full bank doing four times the work every tick, which is the one thing the whole feature
    is built not to do — the promise is that a bank costs the server the same whether it holds
    one chamber or all of them. The gameplay tab now says so plainly, including the advice that
    a horse which actually needs mending mends faster in a field.

    Nothing about the grid has been seen in a game. wiki/horse-stasis.html#verify-bank-scroll is
    the new check list, and the click-through-the-frame case is the one that matters: click the
    slots, do not judge the offset by eye.
