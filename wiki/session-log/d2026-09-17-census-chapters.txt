2026-09-17, last of the day — A census of every allele, and the wild put first

    Two pieces, asked for together: the Getting started book gained a chapter on wild horses
    and one on magic, and the wiki gained a gene census —
    one page listing what every combination of every gene's alleles actually does. The census
    is meant to be handed to an AI as much as read by a person, which is why it is a plain
    semantic table with its text in the source rather than injected by script.

The census could not be assembled from anything that already existed

    Three baked assets look like they should back it and none can.
    genes.json carries a description per outcome but only for the data-driven
    magical genes; marking-facts.json has the family and pair spine for the
    painting genes but no descriptions at all, and it drops every non-painting gene
    and every wild type. Their union still misses the behaviour, yield, health and sex loci
    entirely. So GeneCensusTool walks Genes.codeOrder() and
    GenotypeCatalog.allPairsOf and reads Expression#description(),
    which exists for every gene whether or not it paints. A new gene reaches the census
    by existing, the way a gene page does — the only way to make it
    stale is not re-running the bake, which is why it is in the regeneration table rather
    than in anybody's memory.

Expression#wildType() means two different things, and it broke the same instruction twice

    The owner asked for wild-type rows to go, keeping agouti, extension and shade. That reads
    as a one-line filter and is not, because Expression.wildType(…) is also
    the only constructor that paints in neither phase — so a gene with no coat
    effect uses it for real outcomes. Culling literally on the flag dropped 917
    rows and deleted 85 whole genes: body size, magic speed, diet, fireproof
    and sex all went to zero rows, which is precisely the population the census was built to
    cover.

    The second attempt was wildType() && affectsCoat(), which is right for
    painting genes and spares every non-painting gene's baseline too — 80 rows
    still reading "Wild type". The third attempt, suggested from outside the code, was "the
    expression of the gene's baseline pair", and that over-selects: SexGene's
    default allele is X, so its baseline pair X/X is Mare.
    What shipped tests Expression#id() against the "wild" id that
    Expression.wildType assigns — stable identity rather than display prose,
    so a renamed outcome cannot silently change what gets deleted. Eight rows still carry the
    label, and three of those are genes whose outcome is named "Wild type" while carrying a
    custom id (gap 267).

    Two requirements genuinely conflicted at patn1 and
    patn2: both declare one unnamed expression answering for every pair, so "drop
    unnamed baselines" removes the only row the locus has and the census stops mentioning it.
    The tool now decides a gene's rows before writing any, and a gene that would lose all of
    them keeps all of them; if that rescue ever fails it refuses to write the page. The bake
    failed loudly on exactly those two genes before the rescue existed, which is the shape of
    failure this wanted.

The breed column, and the trap a string comparison would have passed

    The census says which built-in breeds naturally carry each presentation, so a blank cell is
    a blank spot — and 1014 of the rows are blank, because only 51
    of the registered genes are named by any breed at all. Attribution is per presentation
    rather than per gene, which gives the carrier-versus-shows split for nothing: a breed
    holding Agt/n lands on the wild row, one holding Agt/Agt on the
    variant row.

    The trap is that breed files store pairs as authored while the census prints them
    canonicalised. akhal_teke.json writes ShL/Sh; the page
    prints Sh/ShL, because AllelePair orders by
    Gene#alleles() declaration order. A token-string comparison would have quietly
    credited nobody on that row and on its Cr/N and G3/N rows, and
    the column would have looked like it worked. Credits are built by resolving tokens to
    Alleles and comparing pairs. Every credit is also checked against the pairs the
    row actually prints, and the bake throws rather than emitting a breed name beside a pair
    list that does not contain the pair which earned it.

    Excluded deliberately, all of which would have made the column useless rather than wrong:
    Feral Mixed (its sheet is empty — it rolls everything in the founder instead), magical
    herds (they bolt a random magical gene onto 5% of wild herds, so Friesians would appear
    against genes Friesians do not carry), splices, carrots and the editors. The four body-stat
    loci are not in any gene pool — they come from the breed's stat scores — so they
    read set by stat scores rather than a false blank, and feralOnly loci
    read feral only for the same reason.

The wild comes first, because that is what a player meets

    Getting started went from six chapters to seven and from 66 tasks to 90. Horses in the
    wild moved to the front and from two sub-chapters to eleven, and taming moved into it:
    every horse a player meets is wild until they tame one, so husbandry now begins where a
    horse is already yours. The chapter describes behaviour that was fully implemented
    and completely undocumented — sparring that settles rank without damage, a
    band stallion coming round behind a stray mare to drive her in, takeover fights
    that stop at two-fifths health, displacement at water but never at plain grass, grooming
    partners standing head to tail. A new Magical horses chapter covers what a magical
    gene is, the dhampir's two strains and why a white one cannot be hand-tamed at all, and
    magical herds.

    Thirteen of the 24 new tasks are things you watch rather than do, which no
    existing hook shape could express, so HorseProgress.completeForWatcher credits
    the nearest player within 24 blocks with line of sight. The line-of-sight test is
    the point: a box that ticked for a fight behind a hill would be claiming the player had seen
    something they had not. Four hooks needed a firing guard because they sit inside goals that
    tick, and those are the ones that can stick on or off —
    §0-EO says which.

    One near-miss worth recording: the first draft of the enum renamed
    NAME_HORSE. Task ids are what a save stores, so that would have
    orphaned every player's completed task and broken a live call site, while compiling
    perfectly in between. The enum's ordering was changed freely and its constant names were
    not.

What is not verified

    None of the checklist has been played. Every one of the 24 new tasks is
    unseen in game and the watcher mechanism is the part able to fail silently in both
    directions at once. The census, by contrast, is verified by construction and by 26 headless
    Chrome checks, including that the collapsed mobile pair cell still has its full pair list in
    the file on disk — the AI-readability requirement depends on that and it is invisible
    in a browser. Also fixed in passing, because the census put it on a page built to be read:
    night watch's "close behind" description carried a stranded "silently, all night."
