2026-09-17, and then — Flaxen fades from root to tip

    The last shovel-ready item on the natural list. Flaxen
    computed one flat score per part and applied it to every texel of the mane and
    tail, so its only variation was the strand mixing. A real flaxen mane is darkest at the
    crest and palest at the ends, and that is the one thing a uniform wash cannot look like
    however good the strands are.

    It was a one-line insertion, because the seam was already there. The
    painter modulates its per-part score once already —
    s * (1 - STRAND_DEPTH * strands) — so the gradient is a second factor at
    exactly that point, and the two compose without either knowing about the other. No new
    machinery at all.

The coordinate was already solved, twice

    The roadmap said the gradient “wants a coordinate along the hair rather than across
    it” and noted HairPattern.axesBySpan as the seam. It was more solved
    than that: grey’s hairRootLead already
    uses axesBySpan(skin, part)[1] to let a mane grey ahead of the body, and its
    comment records that this axis runs root to tip on both parts with the middle of
    the box as the root — the same axis the midtstol is
    centred in. So flaxen reads the existing convention rather than deriving its own.

    That was a deliberate choice and worth the sentence: a second way of finding the
    same coordinate is how two genes come to disagree about where a horse’s mane
    begins, and this repository has already paid for that class of drift today, when
    CoatSampleTool kept asking grey for an allele the locus had dropped. Three
    genes and one test now move together if the convention ever changes.

Depth on the allele, and a measurement rather than a look

    How deep the fade runs is epigenetic on the copy, following the precedent set for
    silver’s dapples and
    roan’s corn spots: it inherits, drifts, and runs down to
    zero — a horse at zero draws the flat mane the gene drew before, which is a real coat
    rather than a failure.

    FlaxenRootToTipTest compares mean surviving red in a band near the root
    against a band near the tip on the same horse, which the roan test could not do and
    so had to go statistical. It also checks that depth varies across horses and that nothing
    outside the mane and tail is touched. All three passed first run, and the goldens moved by
    24 of 1092 rows, every one carrying a flaxen allele.

    The direction is the thing most worth checking in game, and the reason it
    is first on the checklist: a reversed gradient still looks
    like a gradient in a screenshot, so the test asserts which end is darker rather than that
    the two differ.
