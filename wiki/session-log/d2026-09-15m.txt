2026-09-15, later — a cowboy comes from somewhere, and two “patches” turn out to need no patch

    A large batch was asked for — two new genes, weather-gated spawning, spawning in
    visited chunks, regional cowboys, a Getting Started rewrite, horseman stock, and six mod
    investigations. Far more than one session, so it was cut to two: the
    compatibility work and the cowboy’s regions. The rest is untouched and
    still wanted; the two genes in particular were researched in full and not built.

The research changed the brief twice

    Two of the requested patches do not exist as patches. Saturated Saddles adds no
    items at all — it is minecraft:crafting_dye recipes onto the
    vanilla saddle, producing the vanilla dyed_color component, with no
    namespace of its own. So “sell a coloured saddle if that datapack is
    installed” became sell one always, with no detection, no optional
    dependency and no third-party id to break. That is strictly better than what was asked
    for, and it is the reason there is still no ModList.isLoaded call anywhere
    in this repo.

    Universal Dyeing does not touch horses. Its store page advertises
    leather horse armour, wolf armour, bundles and bleaching; both published artefacts were
    downloaded and enumerated in full, and they contain exactly 176 recipes over eleven
    block categories and nothing else — no match for horse,
    saddle, leather or dyed_color in either. It was
    called a very high priority patch; there is nothing to patch. The negative is written up
    at length here precisely so the next person does not re-investigate on the strength of
    the description. (It sat on the compatibility page too until 2026-09-17, when that page
    was audited down to mods there is actually something to say about.)
    The player’s actual wish still lands, because vanilla leather horse armour has
    always been dyeable.

    Of the other four: Bridle is Fabric-only on 26.2 and cannot be
    co-installed at all; Realistic Horse Genetics is not a patch target but
    a competitor occupying the same design space and replacing the horse entity as we do,
    stopped at 1.20.4; Horse Tack is blocked transitively by requiring RHG,
    and its code is MIT while its 381 textures are All Rights Reserved; BHAR
    is a clean recommendation whose only friction is a licence forbidding reuse of any part.

The cowboy’s region — and the field that was waiting for it

    country has been on every breed from the start and nothing read
    it. The parser has always thrown on a capital letter rather than forgiving one,
    on the stated grounds that a country is meant to be keyed on. Region is the
    first thing that keys on it: ten pools of neighbouring countries, from which a cowboy
    draws his whole string and his name.

    The rule was that a cowboy must be able to stock five breeds from his own
    region, and a census said four sensible regions cannot. Counting only
    cowboy-sellable breeds, the Middle East had three, Central America two, North Africa one
    and Sub-Saharan Africa one. Rather than let those spawn a trader at an empty rail, each
    was folded into its nearest neighbour — Africa with the Near East, Central with
    South America. Two regions run the other way and are far larger than the rest, and there
    is no honest fix for that short of inventing countries. Australia and New Zealand, which
    the brief did not mention, went in with East Asia because nothing else is near them.

    The structural change was an ordering one.
    CowboyHandler.found named the man at line 253 and did not pick his breed
    until line 277, so his name could not possibly have reflected his trade. The pick now
    comes first and the region is derived from it rather than stored —
    PreferredBreed is already persisted, so a second field would be one more
    thing that can disagree with it. The horseman, who has no breed, takes the region from
    the nearest cowboy who has one, and founds his own when there is no cowboy at all.

    The old cowboy-names-*.txt were already American frontier names, so they
    were moved into the new layout as people/north_america-*.txt rather than
    duplicated, and nine more pairs written. They deliberately do not join the
    designer asset bundle: the copy task globs names/*.txt, which does not
    descend into people/, and the designer names horses and never people —
    the old flat tables had been copied into it and never read.

A trade shipped in the morning and withdrawn in the afternoon

    Vanilla does not tint the saddle, and this session sold one anyway for
    about an hour. A follow-up look at tack feasibility read
    EquipmentAssetProvider out of the patched sources jar and found the leather
    asset built with Layer.leatherDyeable(…) and the saddle asset built
    with the plain single-argument Layer, dyeable Optional.empty().
    So dyed_color on a saddle is set, correct and invisible — the tinted
    model lives in Saturated Saddles' resource pack, which is the half of that
    project everyone forgets, this session included. The trade sold a fourteen-emerald
    saddle indistinguishable from the eight-emerald one, so it was deleted rather than
    documented as a quirk. The dyed leather horse armour, which vanilla genuinely tints,
    stayed.

    Worth recording as a process note: the original javadoc said “checked in game: not
    yet”, and that hedge was doing real work — but the answer was available from
    the sources jar the whole time, for the cost of reading one provider class. An
    unverified-API comment is a placeholder for a lookup, not a substitute for one.

Tack, assessed and not built

    The owner asked mid-session how feasible native tack would be. Verdict:
    feasible in code, unaffordable in art, and blocked on an existing
    defect. Every seam already exists — CutieMarkLayer is a
    proven precedent for geometry anchored to an animated bone, a new item is one line, and
    the synced-attachment pattern has ten instances. Two hard constraints turned up:
    EquipmentSlot has exactly two animal slots and both are taken, and
    EquipmentClientInfo.LayerType is not extensible, so tack past two pieces
    must ride on an attachment. The blocker is that the saddle already renders at vanilla
    scale over a genetically resized body, so tack would be visibly broken on arrival;
    fixing state.scale inheritance is a prerequisite. Written up at
    §23, with the honest note that shipping tack
    contradicts the compatibility page's current claim that tack is what this mod leaves to
    other people.

The names became drop-in, on the owner’s point

    The owner asked where the regional names were stored so she could expand them, and said
    they should not be in a Java file. They already were not — twenty plain UTF-8
    tables under names/people/ — but the question exposed a real gap:
    unlike breeds and genes, names had no drop-in folder, so widening one
    meant editing the repo and rebuilding. phc/names/ now exists beside
    phc/breeds/ and phc/genes/, made with a README on first launch.

    It adds rather than replaces, and that is the deliberate half. A
    drop-in holding ten names would otherwise make every trader in that region draw from
    ten; appending cannot make a region worse, and widening is what the request actually
    was. Removing a shipped name still means editing the shipped file, which is the right
    trade. Region ids are validated by the NeoForge side, which knows Region,
    so common/name stays decoupled from common/breed and a typo in
    a file name is reported in the log rather than silently doing nothing.

    The tables carry diacritics, so a test now fails on a replacement character anywhere in
    them. That guard exists precisely because the files are about to be hand-edited on
    Windows: an editor saving one as Windows-1252 produces
    Bj�rnstad with nothing logged and nothing to notice. The test itself
    uses the code point 0xFFFD rather than a literal character, because a test that
    catches encoding damage should not itself contain a byte that can be damaged.

    The offer to move the region definitions out of Java as well was declined —
    the pools change rarely, and the enum keeps them type-safe and test-enforced.

Two disciplines held on purpose

    No per-region breed counts were written down anywhere. They are
    Region.breeds(BreedSource.COWBOY).size(), they move whenever a breed is
    added or drops its cowboy source, and the floor of five is held by
    RegionTest.everyRegionCanSeatACowboy failing the build instead. A second
    test catches a breed whose country no region claims, so a drop-in from a new country is
    a build failure rather than a cowboy who never appears. While in the area, the stale
    “forty-nine breeds” claims on the landing page and the releases page were
    fixed by removing the number rather than updating it to 122.

    The designer’s web.wasm was reverted rather than
    committed. The bake rewrote it to a byte-identical size with no functional
    change — neither Region nor PersonNameGenerator is
    reachable from DesignerApi, and TeaVM compiles only the reachable graph —
    so committing it would have been 2MB of build noise. The boot check was run against
    the rebuilt file before reverting.
