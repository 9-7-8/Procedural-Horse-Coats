2026-09-17, after that — Roan grows corn spots

    Back to the natural queue. Roan’s per-texel dither had
    no notion of corn spots — the islands of solid base colour that sit
    inside a roan field — and the roadmap wanted a second low-frequency mask for them.

    The insight that made it small: roan already computes a whitening weight
    w per texel and calls PigmentField.whiten. A corn spot is
    therefore a suppression of w, not a new thing painted over
    the top — one insertion at one point rather than surgery on the existing dither
    maths, and it falls out correctly that a spot is base coat at full strength with a hard
    edge. Two fields decide them: a low-frequency cellDistance lattice for where
    the islands sit, and a lower one still gating which cells become spots at all.
    Without that gate every cell centre becomes one and the horse reads as polka-dotted.

    Seed and propensity live on the Rn copy, following the precedent the owner
    set for silver’s dapples earlier in the day, and
    reusing roan’s own existing idiom — the forehead-white roll, a propensity
    tested against a chance so that most horses get none. Roan went from three knobs off the
    expressing copy to five, and the class javadoc says so.

The test is statistical, and says so

    RoanCornSpotTest could not do what
    SilverDappleTest does, and the reason is worth recording. A corn spot cannot
    be isolated on one horse: the painter reads its epigenetics from the build context, so
    there is no way to paint the same roan with spots off and on, and
    density varies enough between horses to move total whiteness far more than
    the spots do. So the whiteness assertion compares means over 240 horses,
    where the density spread averages out and a systematic suppression does not. The crisp
    assertion is the other one — that corn spots are a minority trait —
    because that is the claim the javadoc makes and the one that would be silently wrong if
    the propensity roll were ignored.

    Both passed first run, and the goldens moved by 8 of 1092 rows, all of them roan
    horses — the same tightly-scoped result as the dapples, and the same method:
    decompose the diff by column before copying anything back.
