2026-09-17, later still — One layer, a different colour per region

    The owner asked what was next on the roadmap. The top of
    the roadmap is play-testing, which is hers rather than
    mine, so the next buildable item was §10’s last unbuilt piece:
    per-region colour.

Two thirds of it already existed. Again.

    That is the third time this section has given that answer, after the dial and the colour
    control. SpecPainter already varies a colour by region:
    applyColour computes leg = legIndex(part) and every knob read
    goes through v.get(value, leg), so a perLeg hue knob
    paints four legs four hues today. LEGS and BARREL have
    been first-class names in PartGroups all along. What was missing was not
    per-region variation but an authored regioncolour mapping,
    and that distinction was the whole decision: three of the four shapes on offer — a
    perPart knob flag generalising perLeg, a partId axis
    mirroring RAMP’s cellId, or a creator gesture writing the
    layer-per-region idiom — deliver variation, and none of them removes the duplication
    the roadmap was actually complaining about. The owner took the authored map.

The shape, and the refusals that replace a precedence rule

    A regions entry carries the op’s four colour parameters again,
    sentinel and all, so it can be a literal color or a hue
    pointed at a knob. A part no entry names falls through to the op’s own colour, which
    is what makes the field additive: adding one cannot move a texel it does not name, and
    that is why no shipped gene changed. solidColour is the single funnel for
    TOWARD and FLAT, and applyColour already held
    part at both call sites, so the engine change was one parameter and a loop.

    Three load errors rather than a precedence rule: a region naming no parts, a
    region carrying no colour, and a part claimed by two entries. Refusing
    the third is what lets there be no “first entry wins” for an author to find
    out about by experiment — and it matters because the groups overlap, so
    POINTS beside LEGS claims all four legs twice without either
    name looking wrong. The corpus scan the refusal discipline asks for was free here: the
    parameter is new, so zero of 195 genes can trip any of the three.

The regression that proved nothing, and had to be thrown away

    Parity came back green at 7171 checks, which on this page means nothing until the
    gate has been watched going red. The first attempt at that was void and was
    discarded: the anchor indexOf(part) >= 0 matched
    two sites, the new region lookup and spec-engine.js:414 — the
    PARTS mask’s own coverage — so the 984 failures it produced were
    mostly a different code path, and the first one reported was a pigment mismatch
    on silver.json, a natural gene the change cannot touch. A regression that
    breaks two things measures neither.

    Re-run against one site, the numbers are real and they are two different numbers:
    480 checks see the region lookup, and 138 see a 0.05
    lightness difference inside a region, confined to mane and tail. That second one
    is the one that mattered, because it answers the question
    the contract raises about
    pointsMin: the probes sample four texels per part, and a feature that lands
    on a flank slips between them. This one does not, so no answer table was
    needed — the pathSection treatment does not apply.

What was deliberately not done

    - RAMP and PALETTE do not take the field. Both
        bypass solidColour for a colour source of their own — a stop list
        walked along an axis, a colour drawn per cell — so scoping them is a second
        change rather than the same one. It is the remainder of
        §10 now.
        (Built the same day — the next entry
        up.)

    - Gap 164’s hueOffset
        stays open. It was floated as a cheap addition once the colour ops were open
        and then left out on purpose, to keep the change one feature.

    - No MarkingFactsTool change was needed, contrary to the
        first reading. It does not enumerate a gene’s declared colour fields — it
        renders the coat and measures what moved, so a per-region colour reaches the
        breed designer’s markings filters on its own.

    One trap closed on the way past: the spread-out
    contracts list did not mention the creator’s exporter, where an unknown key is
    dropped silently. The over flag hit that same omission and recorded
    it only in its own log entry; it is in the list itself now.
