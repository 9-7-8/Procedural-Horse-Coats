2026-09-24, after the last stand — a horse can no longer eat a crop through the fence it is
standing at

    One bullet, the next in the Bugs section of intake/To do.txt, in the owner's words:

        Horses are eating crops through a fence or wall.

    Dated first. The bullet went into To do.txt on 2026-09-23; horse-care.html's verification
    summary already claimed a hungry horse no longer walks at crops it cannot path to, which is
    exactly the shape START HERE's step 1 warns about. But the session that made that claim
    (d2026-09-24-pasture-edge) scoped this out in its own words — "the goal's eating radius is
    2.5 blocks and does not ask what is in between... its own bullet, deliberately left alone" —
    so it was open before a line of code was read, and the class was already named.

Why fixing the first half is what let the second half happen

    HungerFoodGoal.search asks createPath/canReach before committing to food. That settled the
    horses leaning on the rail, because the crops in that report were behind a fence with no way
    round at all.

    A real farm has a gate somewhere. So canReach says yes, the horse commits to the crop, and
    the way it is meant to get there is the long walk round. Then tick's eating test fires: a
    plain distanceToSqr against 2.5 blocks, centre to centre. A horse that happened to be stood
    at the rail was already inside that radius on the first tick of the pursuit, and ate the
    crop where it stood. The better the pathing check got, the more crops passed it.

    The same held for a dropped carrot or a carcass over a rail, for grazeInPlace's two-block
    dropped mouthful, and for a carnivore's swing at a cow through a fence.

What changed

    tick asks one question before the mouthful: for a block, HungerFoodGoal.exposed; for an item
    or prey, vanilla's own hasLineOfSight, which is the identical clip and was already used in
    five places here. A blocked target is not given up on — tick just returns, the repath keeps
    running, and the horse walks the way round that search already proved exists. The ordinary
    pursuit timeout is the backstop if it never arrives.

    The only real difficulty was where to aim the ray.

    Eye to block centre — the obvious test — would have stopped grazing dead. A grass block sits
    below the ground the horse walks on, so a ray from a horse's eye to the middle of one five
    blocks away dives through the turf in between and hits it. Every distant grass block would
    have read as barred, on flat open pasture, with nothing in the way at all. exposed therefore
    aims at the nearest point of the block's box to the eye, clamped axis by axis: for a grass
    block that is the top edge facing the horse, the part it actually puts its head on, and the
    ray skims the surface rather than burrowing under it.

    What the predicate stops is then set by collision height rather than by what a block is
    called. A fence and a cobble wall both collide to a block and a half, above a grazing
    horse's eye line, so both stop the ray — which is the two things the owner named. A full
    one-block wall does not, and that is deliberate: a horse can put its head over one and can
    jump it, so the pathfinder was going to walk it there anyway. It is written up as a
    judgement call that can be reversed by lowering the aim point, not as a defect.

Not played, and no test

    No unit test. It is one clip call into NeoForge's collision shapes end to end, so per the
    session contract the production path stays narrow and the runtime reproduction is written
    instead.

    wiki/horse-care.html grew #mouth-reach on the Coding tab and #mouth-reach-0924 on the
    Verification tab with five checks. The one that matters most is not the fix: it is that an
    ordinary pen still grazes its own grass. If grazing has stopped, the aim point is wrong in
    the way described above and every tuft five paces off is being refused.

    The fence's 1.5-block collision height is a reading of vanilla, not a measurement, and the
    class javadoc now says so beside the other two unverified readings in it.

    intake/Implementation advice.txt lost its line asking for one reachability/boundary
    predicate across pasture crowding, crop eating and food selection: all three now have one.
