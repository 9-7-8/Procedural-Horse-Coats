2026-09-13 — three fixes that were never reached, and a freeze that needed a reboot

    A long afternoon of live testing with a log watch running beside the game. The
    through-line: three separate "fixes" were written, documented and
    committed, and not one of them ever ran — and each was caught by a
    measurement, never by reading the code that contained it.

The fixes nobody reached

    - The eyesight readout. DebugWorldWatch.attributeReadout,
        which names each horse instead of printing a min–max, was private and called
        from nowhere. Wired in, then extended to print each horse against its own base and
        its block light — which answered eyesight in four censuses (gap 216,
        219).

    - The horse attack interval. HorseMeleeGoal overrode
        getAttackInterval() to 12 ticks; vanilla's resetAttackCooldown
        writes a hard-coded 20 and never asks. Found in bytecode after a guardian died one
        second before its zombie. The goal owns its cooldown now, at 10 (gap 222, since verified).

    - The stall detector's fallback box. Built to "never refuse", it
        turned every failed search into a plausible stall — which hid that the yard's
        stalls had a hole for a doorway instead of a gate. It refuses now.

A regression I shipped, caught the same way

    The world-flag cache answered flags it had never computed, and my fix stamped
    "never sampled" as Long.MIN_VALUE — so now - sampledAt
    overflowed, and dark, snowing, near_jukebox and
    hostile_near went permanently off for every gene for about an hour. The
    census line DAYWALKER (dark)=0.221 … light=1 made it undeniable. The
    sentinel is compared with == now.

The freeze

    Walking toward a herd froze the owner's whole machine, needing a reboot. A thread dump
    put it on the render thread (173s of CPU against the server's 32s): every
    newly-seen horse was baked twice inside the frame that first drew it. Now one bake per
    horse, a 4ms-per-50ms time budget with a shared stand-in coat, a 32-block
    detail distance, and a founding budget of 15ms per server tick. Owner:
    “Much, MUCH better feeling performance.”
    Pipeline  when a coat is baked,
    gap 221. My first two diagnoses were both wrong
    within the hour; cumulative CPU per thread settled it, a single stack sample did not.

Stalls, tickets and ownership

    - The horse in the roof was the stall's own sign being measured as a
        one-tile room: signs are force-solid, so a sign hung outside with sky above closed
        as a room, and at ticket time it was smaller than the real stall and won. Diagnosed
        on the first use of a trace line added for the purpose (gap 223).
        An L-shaped stall also needed the search to start from the nearest floor along the
        wall, not only the square straight behind the sign.

    - Owner rules: nothing binds to a horse you do not own, creative
        included; a seed jar is filled only from your stallion and used on any mare you own;
        one stall sign per horse, with a second placement moving the stall and popping the
        old sign off.

    - Lycanthropy was deleting horses. A were-animal's death removed the
        horse with no death at all. The first fix — refusing aquatic forms on land
        — was overruled: “It should absolutely still shift and then just
        die.” The animal's death now rebuilds the horse and kills it with the same
        damage source (gap 220).

Confirmed and retired from the yard

    Gladiator; dhampir (all three questions — its pen was wrongly deleted once and
    restored); eyesight; bone meal and the mixed dryad pair; ender echo's on-foot blink;
    shears; milking for milk and potion; the carrot breeding mechanism; the horseman, the
    cowboy and the transfer papers; every ticket stall; one stall sign per horse. Rows G and
    H are gone and the yard is seventeen blocks shorter.

Process, written down so it is not paid for twice

    - Kill every client before relaunching — a
        MinecraftMain command-line filter misses the dev client, two clients
        ran at once and interleaved the log; fml.modFolders is the marker that
        works.

    - Check what else is in a room before retiring it — the
        horseman's study also held the untested transfer-paper chest.

    - Untracked files in intake/ are the owner's drop-ins, not strays.
