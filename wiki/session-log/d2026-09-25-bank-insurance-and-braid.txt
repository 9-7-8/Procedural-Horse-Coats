2026-09-25 — insurance you can leave at home, and the first thing that goes in a mane

    Two asks, the second arriving mid-session. The first was three small-sounding
    additions to the emergency stasis chamber; the second was "before you cut a new
    version, can you implement the rescue braid too?" — a page that had been Roadmap-only
    since it was written, with four open design questions on it.

    Both shipped. Neither has been played.


PART ONE — the emergency chamber

    The ask, verbatim:

        You should be able to put empty emergency stasis chambers into your stasis bank,
        and they'll get used automatically if a horse triggers one, printing a message
        both to your horse log in the H menu and to your chat that they've been used.
        You'll be bucked off a tick before the horse gets sucked into the chamber in this
        case. Also, in the H menu, for location, it should say "in a stasis chamber" for
        all horses in stasis chambers.

    The hard part was the first sentence, and it was not the part that looked hard.

A bank is a block somewhere, and the whole feature exists to avoid looking for blocks

    An emergency chamber in an inventory is found by walking 41 slots. One in a bank is
    not: a bank is an unowned block in some dimension, quite possibly in a chunk nobody
    has loaded for a week, and "find this player's banks" is either a world search or a
    chunk load per bank per blow. Either would have contradicted the one promise the
    stasis feature makes about itself.

    Two questions went to the owner with options rather than being guessed at:

        Q. How does the rescue find your bank?
        A. Banks you placed, anywhere — the chunk is loaded for the instant it takes.
           (Over: only while loaded, which stops working the moment you ride away from
           base; or any loaded bank, which spends a stranger's chamber on a server.)

        Q. Bank first or inventory first?
        A. Inventory first, bank as fallback.

    What made "anywhere" affordable is StasisBankIndex's second field. A row is a bank's
    position and its owner — that much is only a phone book — plus `armed`: whether the
    block was last seen holding an EMPTY emergency chamber. The block entity publishes
    that every time its chamber grid changes and again on onLoad. So the usual answer,
    that none of your banks can help, is a walk of a short list in memory with no chunk
    touched at all. A chunk is loaded only for a bank that says it is holding one, which
    is a horse's life against one chunk load. Paying that to learn the answer is "no"
    would not have been worth it.

    The flag is last-known rather than live, and the reader copes: it is re-checked
    against the real container, so a stale row costs one wasted chunk load and finds
    nothing. That is the right way round for a cache deciding whether a horse lives.

    Three details that are easy to get wrong and were nearly got wrong:

      - The search shares the refusal's clock. StasisRescue.dueAgain already throttled
        the "your chamber is full" line to ten seconds per horse; it now gates the bank
        walk too, and a FAILED search starts the quiet period even though it says
        nothing. Without that, a horse burning to death with a stale index row against it
        re-opens the same chunk every half-second.

      - MAX_BANKS_CONSULTED is 8. Three banks never reaches it; two hundred banks does
        not get to turn a wolf bite into two hundred chunk loads.

      - A bank had no owner, because nothing had ever needed one. It now records its
        placer — and claims an unowned one the first time somebody opens it, which is the
        entire migration story for a world that predates this and has exactly one door.
        Owning a bank grants nothing else; anyone who can reach it can still open it.

The log row was not scope creep, it was the same argument the Log tab is made of

    HorseEvent gained RESCUE, and then HOMED for the braid. Nine kinds now. Both are
    there for the tab's own reason, harder than the rest of it: a chamber and a braid
    both fire BY THEMSELVES, with nobody watching, possibly a thousand blocks away. The
    chat line is the only thing that ever says so and it scrolls past while the player is
    busy with whatever was killing the horse.

    Two kinds rather than one, because they leave the player in different places — a
    rescued horse is in a bottle you have to go and open, a homed one is standing in its
    stall — and a filter that could not separate those is not a filter. Same argument the
    three ownership kinds were split on.

    RESCUE's line() defers to StasisRescue.saved / savedInBank rather than writing its
    own wording, the way a cover defers to CoverNotice. The log is where you look when
    you missed the chat line; two different sentences about one event would leave the
    reader wondering whether they were two events.

The tick before the bottle closes

    ejectPassengers then swallow used to happen inside one event. Now, when there is a
    rider, the player is set down, the blow is refunded up front, and the capture is
    deferred one server tick.

    The blow has to be refunded UP FRONT in that path — unlike the immediate one, where
    it is refused after the capture so a horse that could not be stored still takes its
    damage. A horse left standing for a tick still holding the blow that triggered all
    this would simply die of it.

    And the horse is held harmless for that tick: a CAUGHT set of ids is checked on the
    first line of onBlow and zeroes any damage to a horse mid-grace. The courtesy of
    setting the player down first must not be the thing that kills the horse.

"in a stasis chamber" — and the defect underneath it

    A horse in a chamber is discard()ed, not killed. It fires no LivingDeathEvent, it is
    never sighted again, and it therefore sat in the browser reading "not loaded" for
    ever. That is the wrong claim twice over — "not loaded" means "somewhere you cannot
    see right now", which reads as a horse you could go and fetch, and no amount of
    walking towards a bottle will ever load it. Exactly the wrong claim a dead horse used
    to make in the same column before the roster started dropping them.

    HorseWhereabouts now records stasis the way it already records death: written by
    swallow BEFORE the discard, while the horse still has a position, and cleared by
    release once the entity is really in the world. Two moments that are certain; nothing
    inferred.

    The real defect was next to it. The whereabouts column was drawn in two places and
    both wrote `loaded ? where : "not loaded"` by hand — so the rule lived twice, and the
    second copy was always going to be the one that got missed. It is HorseListing
    .whereLabel(fallback) now, in common/, with the string as HorseListing.IN_STASIS and
    a test. Free side effect: whereabouts are in the search haystack, so "stasis" is now
    a word the filter box finds horses by.


PART TWO — the rescuing braid

    Four open questions on the page, answered by the owner:

        Order against last stand   -> rides on top of the immunity window
        Binding UX                 -> no binding at all; resolve at trigger time
        Both hair slots worn       -> two independent saves
        Render the streak now      -> yes, both

    "No binding" was the answer that removed the most code. The plan wanted a bound-stall
    reference like BoundHorse and an interaction to set it. Resolving at trigger time
    means the item has NO data component at all, a braid can never point at a stall that
    has been pulled down, and a spare in a chest works for whichever horse you put it on.
    The price — the item cannot say in advance where it will send a horse — is paid by
    the one refusal line, which does not spend the braid.

The trap: it would have shipped green, and never fired

    The obvious condition for a braid is "did this blow kill the horse". It is wrong, and
    nothing in a test suite would have said so.

    By the time the braid is asked, HorseLastStandHandler at NORMAL has already set the
    damage to ZERO. So `damage >= health` answers NO on precisely the horses a braid
    exists for. Every test passes, the item crafts, the layer draws, and the braid never
    goes off.

    RescuingBraid.fires therefore asks where the horse IS: the blow is fatal, OR the
    horse is already on the last stand's floor. Same conclusion StasisRescue reached from
    the other end of the same event, for the same reason. RescuingBraidTest pins both
    halves, including one assertion that is really about two features at once — a horse
    on that floor must still be below Escape.DEFAULT_THRESHOLD, or the promise that it
    arrives bolting is not kept by anything.

Three subscribers on LivingDamageEvent.Pre, and the priorities are the feature

        NORMAL   HorseLastStandHandler   refuses the blow, holds it at healthLeft
        LOW      RescuingBraidHandler    sees a horse on that floor, takes it home
        LOWEST   EmergencyStasisHandler  the general net, behind both

    The chamber dropped from LOW to LOWEST to make room. The braid goes first because it
    is the MORE SPECIFIC instrument — worn on one horse on purpose, where a chamber covers
    every horse its carrier owns. Nothing is wasted by that ordering, which is what makes
    it safe: a horse the braid has moved is out of the fight and takes no further blows,
    so the chamber is never asked and never spent.

What was reused rather than rebuilt

      - TicketHandler.arrive is CALLED, not copied: portal particles, enderman sound and
        HorseLeads.untieFor for free, and a future change to how a horse travels reaches
        this without anyone remembering it exists.
      - TicketHandler.landingSpot is what says yes. It re-reads the sign and re-runs the
        stall detection live, so a stall somebody has filled with hay bales refuses here
        rather than suffocating an animal.
      - Stall first, then the holding pen — StallRecall's rule verbatim, because the Send
        home button already makes exactly this choice.
      - Nothing forces the escape behaviour. HorseEscapeGoal reads the health bar and
        recent harm on its own canUse tick and both survive the teleport
        (Escape.THREAT_LINGERS_TICKS outlasts the trip), so the horse comes up bolting
        through the goal's own door rather than a forceStart written for this.

The rider is the genuinely new part

    Within one level teleportTo carries passengers — the property GeneReactionHandler's
    blink already relies on. ACROSS levels it does not: the entity is re-created in the
    target and its passengers are put down. Every other teleport in the mod dodges this
    by refusing a ridden horse outright (TicketHandler, StallRecall, EnderWhistleCalls).
    A braid cannot, since being ridden is the situation it is bought for.

    So reseat() looks the horse up again by id in the world it landed in, sends the rider
    after it, and remounts. UNVERIFIED — nothing in this repository has watched a
    passenger survive a cross-level teleport, and EnderWhistleCalls says the same about
    its own version.

The streak: a mask, and the mask is baked

    BraidLayer is EmissiveCoatLayer's structural twin — one submitModel of the whole
    horse through a texture that is transparent everywhere it must not paint. What
    differs: the texture is a MASK (flat white over the mane or the tail) and the colour
    is the submit's own tint, so one pair of files covers every colour a player can mix
    rather than sixteen baked sheets.

    The submit's colour parameter had to be found by probe: a deliberately-wrong call
    made javac print all four overloads, and the ten-argument one carries an `int color`
    after a nullable sprite. Used here for the first time in this mod.

    The masks are baked from HorseSkinGeometry, not painted. The two patches are at
    texOffs(56,36) and texOffs(42,36) doubled onto the 128px sheet — and hand-painting
    two rectangles at those coordinates would work today and silently paint a horse's
    neck the day somebody moves a cube. HairMaskTool asks the mesh, and HARD-FAILS on a
    part covering no texel, because a mask nothing draws through is a braid nobody can
    see and it would ship green. :common:bakeHairMasks.

Two deviations from the plan, both stated on the page

      - The Tack Dyeing Bench does not dye a braid. The bench is built around tack_tint,
        which is three zones (seat, bridle, fittings) because a saddle has three; a braid
        is one plait of one colour. Forcing it through would mean a zone-names case, a
        tooltip saying the same word three times, and a screen with two slots that do
        nothing. Instead the braid is in #minecraft:dyeable and carries vanilla's
        dyed_color, which buys dyeing in a grid, mixing dyes, cauldron washing, the
        tooltip line and the tinted icon for one tag file.
      - The wool does not decide the colour. That needs a custom recipe to copy the dye
        across, and a CustomRecipe is invisible to JEI and the recipe book unless both
        defaults are overridden — the exact trap the stasis chamber recipes spent a day
        in yesterday. Plain JSON recipe, and the dye does the colour. Both are on the
        page's Roadmap tab.


What was checked, and what was not

    Green: :neoforge-26.1.2:build, the four affected common/ test classes, check-recipes,
    check-links, check-designer-boots, and a dedicated server that loaded 1651 recipes
    and 1729 advancements with no error in the log. (It never reached Done( — port 25565
    was already held by something else on the machine. Mod load, registry freeze, datapack
    load and world load all completed before that, which is the half that mattered.)

    Not checked, and all of it on the two Verification tabs: every one of these behaviours
    in a running game. Nothing has worn a braid, no braid has broken, no rider has crossed
    a world boundary on one, no chamber has been spent out of a bank, and nobody has
    looked at either the streak or the item icon — both of which were generated rather
    than drawn.
