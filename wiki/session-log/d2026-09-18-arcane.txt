2026-09-18 — The horse wizard, and the three things he needed that did not exist

    One request: a cowboy who stocks no breeds, only Mixed horses carrying a showing gene
    from every magical family, no two alike, priced up. The interesting part was that it
    was not really a request for a villager. Magical genes sit near a 0.1% founder weight,
    so most of the registry has been unobtainable in practice since it was written, and the
    dealer is the answer to that — which is why three quite separate features had to
    be built before buying from him was a thing a player could do at all.

The pools are generated, which was the whole brief

    ArcaneStock builds every family's pool by walking
    Genes.magicalOrder() and asking GeneFamily.of, so a gene from
    a data pack is in his string the moment it registers. The only hard-coded thing is
    which families a horse must cover. The first version of the draw order was
    hand-written, smallest family first, with a test asserting the order was right —
    and the test failed on one swap. That was the useful signal: a hand-written
    order that needs re-sorting whenever somebody adds a mane gene is the same maintenance
    burden the generated pools exist to avoid. It is computed per horse now,
    scarcest first, and re-sorts itself.

    Before committing to per-gene-and-pair uniqueness across a herd of ten, the question
    was whether the registry could even supply it. A scratch census said the tightest
    family holds nineteen showing pairs against a maximum herd of ten — comfortable,
    and now asserted by a test rather than remembered.

Three things discovered by building it

    - You could not read a horse you did not own. The info screen was
        reachable only through the vanilla horse inventory, which only opens on a tamed
        horse — so a player was being asked for up to twenty-eight emeralds for an
        animal whose genes are the entire product, sight unseen. Sneak-and-use now opens it
        on any untamed horse. Which immediately raised a second thing: renaming had
        never been gated on ownership, because nothing could reach a stranger's
        horse before.

    - A name tells you nothing about a horse. Fine for six horses of one
        breed; useless for ten horses carrying a hundred loci between them. Hence the filter
        box — and the reason it filters server-side is the sharp edge of the
        day: a trade is an index into the merchant's offer list, so a client drawing fewer
        rows would sell you a different horse from the one you clicked, silently.

    - The owner's premise that monsters ignore cowboys was wrong.
        Cowboy extends AbstractVillager, which zombies target by
        class — his two hundred health exists precisely because of it. Survivable in a
        village with golems and walls; not on a hillside. Checked rather than taken on
        trust, and now made true rather than assumed.

What the yard was for

    One in twelve barns is the right rate for a world and a miserable one for looking at
    him, so row AL-west of the debug yard founds one on the spot. It calls the real
    founding path with only the paddock walk disabled, because a fixture that assembled its
    own herd would go on passing after the real one broke. The owner confirmed the string in
    game within minutes — no geldings, eleven or twelve genes each, no repeats, prices
    right — which is a soft verification: the yard cannot show the one-in-twelve roll
    firing at a real barn, and it cannot show restock keeping the string distinct over time.
