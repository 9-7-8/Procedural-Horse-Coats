2026-09-26 — four asks from the field, and two bugs nobody reported


    A queue session. Four requests arrived one after another while work was
    already running, none of them related, and all four shipped. Nothing was
    played: everything below is build-green and written up as unplayed.

        1. the reaper should only break fully-grown crops, and ignore
           melon/pumpkin stalks
        2. a supply cart behind a reaper should pick up what it cuts
        3. how do you attach two carts together?
        4. the reaper kept getting stuck underground on elevation changes
        5. you should be able to search on pulling ability
        6. filter the Genes tab to specific genes, remembered between openings
        7. wandering traders buy horses at double the rate of a cowboy

    Two of these turned out to be documentation failures rather than code
    failures, and two turned up defects that were never reported because they
    are invisible when they happen.


1. THE REAPER, AND THE FENCE GATES NOBODY MISSED

    The ripeness bug was already known — carts.html open check 286, found
    2026-09-19 — and its note prescribed the fix. Two things in that note were
    wrong, and one of them had been the reason it was left alone:

        "the tag is shared with the plow's clearing lists and the change wants
         its own look"

    It is not shared. horsegenetics:reaper_harvestable is read from exactly one
    line (ReaperCartEntity:152) and the plow reads three separate
    plow_breakable/* tags. A sentence written to defer a change had deferred it
    for a week on a fact that was never true. Verified by grep before writing
    anything, which is the only reason it was caught.

    The real find was underneath. The tag included #minecraft:maintains_farmland,
    and in 26.1.2 that vanilla tag contains:

        pumpkin_stem, attached_pumpkin_stem, melon_stem, attached_melon_stem,
        beetroots, carrots, potatoes, torchflower_crop, torchflower,
        pitcher_crop, wheat, moving_piston, #minecraft:fence_gates

    #minecraft:fence_gates. THE REAPER HAS BEEN DESTROYING FENCE GATES it was
    driven past, since the carts landed, silently — a broken gate logs nothing
    and looks like something else did it. Nobody reported it because there is no
    way to notice it as a reaper problem. Removing the tag entry costs nothing:
    everything else it contributed is already in #minecraft:crops or
    #minecraft:flowers.

    The ripeness gate itself is ReaperCartEntity.cuttable, three answers for the
    three kinds of thing in the tag:

        stem or attached stem  -> never cut
        CropBlock              -> only at isMaxAge
        has an "age" property  -> only at its maximum   (pitcher crop, modded)
        anything else          -> cut on sight          (grass, flowers, saplings)

    The stem case has to be explicit and first, because StemBlock extends
    VegetationBlock and NOT CropBlock — so the generic age test would have
    happily cut a fully-grown stem, which is the exact thing being fixed. The
    owner's reasoning for ignoring stems is better than "it is unripe": the
    melon itself is not in the tag, so the reaper was cutting down the vine and
    leaving the fruit — destroying the one part of the field you were growing.

    The item tooltip has said "Harvests mature crops it is drawn over" the whole
    time. It is now true.


2. THE SUPPLY CART GLEANS, AND ONLY BEHIND A REAPER

    SupplyCartEntity.pulledTick picks up ItemEntities within two blocks, gated
    on getPulling() instanceof ReaperCartEntity.

    The gate is the design, not an optimisation. A cart that hoovered up every
    drop wherever it went would be a cart you cannot park next to your things,
    and every death near one would feed it your inventory. The behaviour belongs
    to the PAIRING — reaper in front, cart behind — and so it is tested on the
    puller rather than granted to the cart. A third vehicle further down a train
    is pulled by the cart rather than the reaper, so it does nothing.

    Overflow stays on the ground: you come back to a full cart and a field that
    is not clear, which is the readable version of running out of room.


3. "HOW DO YOU ATTACH TWO CARTS" WAS A DOCUMENTATION BUG

    It already worked. carts.html §3 said "Hitch a second vehicle to the back of
    the first and you have a train" and never said how, which is not an
    instruction.

    The mechanism is in ActionKeyPayload: R hitches the nearest cart to the
    player OR TO THE PLAYER'S VEHICLE. So a train is made by sitting on the lead
    cart and pressing R. Two consequences worth writing down, and now written:

      - the lead cart must be one you can sit on, so the plow and the seed drill
        can never head a train;
      - the reaper needs a rider anyway, so for the reaper + supply cart pairing
        you are already sitting in the seat the coupling is made from.


4. THE UNDERGROUND CART — A RECOVERY, NOT A DIAGNOSIS

    Not reproduced from the code, and the writeup says so. What WAS confirmed is
    a real defect one level up: a hitched cart has no vertical steering at all.
    pulledTick zeroes the vertical component of the target vector whenever the
    puller is on the ground (AbstractDrawnEntity:164-166) — deliberately, it is
    what stops a jumping horse yanking the cart into the air — with the
    consequence that NOTHING EVER PULLS A CART UP. Once its box is inside
    terrain the only exits are the 1.2-block step assist, which needs a
    horizontal collision to fire, and the hitch breaking.

    A first theory was wrong and was dropped: that tick()'s gravity accumulator
    (deltaMovement.y -= 0.08 every tick, X and Z hard-zeroed) was never reset.
    Reading the decompiled Entity.move settled it — vertical collision does not
    zero deltaMovement there, but it calls updateEntityMovementAfterFallOn,
    whose default Block implementation multiplies Y by zero. Gravity is reset on
    landing. Checking beat guessing by a wide margin; the "fix" for that theory
    would have been a no-op at best.

    AbstractDrawnEntity.climbOut lifts a cart whose box intersects blocks, by
    the smallest step that frees it, up to 1.25. The box is deflated 1/16 first
    so that a cart resting against a fence — a legitimate touch — is not read as
    stuck and set bobbing. That guard is itself untested.

    If it still buries after this, the next suspect is named on the check:
    CartInterpolationHandler applies its final lerped position with a bare
    setPos and no collision test.


5. PULLING ABILITY IS A COLUMN AND A SEARCH TERM

    pull is now a HorseQuery key with draft and draught as aliases, and
    Sort.PULL is a sortable column in My horses.

    The interesting part was that HorseListing — the row model the whole filter
    language reads — resolved traits.pull() and then THREW IT AWAY, keeping only
    speed/health/jump/scale. So "search on pulling ability" was one component on
    a record away the whole time. No packet change: HorseRosterPayload sends the
    genetic code and the client rebuilds the listing, so traits are recomputed
    client-side and pull came along for free.

    TOTAL_WEIGHT in HorseBrowserScreen is hand-summed from the COLUMNS weights
    and had to move 626 -> 656. It is commented as a known trap and it is the
    second time it has been paid; worth deriving one day.


6. THE GENES TAB PINS TO NAMED LOCI

    "just show me what alleles the horse has for Flight, Healer, Waterborn."

    GeneFilterPicker, built like SavedSearchPicker and driven by the existing
    TypeAhead. Multi-select, so a click toggles and the menu STAYS OPEN — three
    genes is three clicks, not three round trips, which is the one way this
    differs from every other dropdown in the mod.

    Three decisions worth keeping:

      - A pin overrides both existing rules INCLUDING the extension/agouti/shade
        exemption. Being asked for Flight and given the base colour as well is
        the filter declining to do the one thing it was asked; the base colour
        is on the Coat tab anyway.
      - The offered list is RosterGenetics.genesPresent(herd) UNIONED with the
        viewed horse's own variant genes. Every horse carries every registered
        gene, so the honest full list is the whole registry and is unusable; but
        this screen opens on strangers' horses and the cowboy's stock, which are
        in no roster, and a picker that cannot offer the gene on the horse in
        front of you is broken in the case you are looking at it.
      - The selection is on DISK, ClientConfig genes.filter, as gene KEYS. On
        disk because "remember it between me closing" was the request. Keys
        because a name is a label and horsegenetics.flight is the identity; an
        unresolvable key is dropped on read, so uninstalling a datapack costs
        the pin and nothing else.

    This is the first thing HorseInfoScreen writes to disk, and the first time
    it has needed the roster — so it now requests it in init() the way it
    already requested descendants. Without that, the menu came up empty on any
    session where the browser had not been opened.


7. THE WANDERING TRADER BUYS, AT DOUBLE

    HorsePrices.buyPriceFor gained a rate parameter, mirroring the existing
    emeraldsFor(record, arcane) split: the markup is on the man, not the animal,
    which is what §4 of the philosophy requires. The cowboy's rate is 1 and
    TraderSale.RATE is 2.

    Two owner calls shaped it:

      - THE HORSE IS LEASHED TO THE TRADER and walks off, as his trader llamas
        already do. He has no herd and nothing to re-list from, so there is no
        buying it back — which is the risk that makes the better price a
        decision rather than free money.
      - NO CAP. buyPriceFor used to clamp to MAX_EMERALDS. That was the SELL
        side's constraint borrowed where it does not apply: emeralds paid TO a
        player go into an inventory, while emeralds a player is CHARGED are an
        ItemCost a merchant screen cannot draw above 64. The clamp stays in
        roll(), which feeds the merchant offers, and is gone from the payout.

    That last change had a tail nobody asked about: CowboySale.pay built a
    single ItemStack(EMERALD, n). Above 64 that is not a valid stack and would
    have been silently trimmed on the way in — the player would simply have been
    short, with no error. pay() now hands the money over in batches of 64.

    Also: the trader handler cancels the interact event on a completed sale, so
    his trade screen does not open on top of it.


8. A BAKE THAT OWNS A FILE YOU HAND-EDITED

    The supply cart's tooltip was edited directly in lang/en_us.json. That key
    is owned by tools/bake-carts.mjs, which merges into the same merged lang
    file — so the edit would have been silently reverted by the next bake of
    anything cart-shaped. Caught by the end-of-session regeneration table, not
    by anything failing.

    Fixed at source and re-baked. The bake then rewrote 78 lines, which looked
    alarming; comparing the KEY SETS before and after showed them identical and
    exactly one VALUE changed. The file had simply drifted out of the bake's
    canonical sort order at some earlier point. Worth remembering that a big
    diff from a bake is not by itself evidence of a big change — and that
    checking is two minutes.
