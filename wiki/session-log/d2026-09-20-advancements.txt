2026-09-20, later still — The checklist becomes advancements, and the horse menu opens on a click

    Two asks, one build. Shipped as v0.5.021.

Every checklist task is now a real advancement

    The Getting Started book’s boxes were a private list the mod drew for itself and
    announced with its own green chat line. They are now also Minecraft
    advancements: a Horse Genetics tab, a root, eight chapter nodes and a
    card per task, with toasts, chat, /advancement and the gamerule that
    silences it. The mod’s own chat line is gone — two announcements of one
    event is one too many, and vanilla’s can be turned off by the player, which a
    hand-rolled line never could. Full writeup:
    progression.

    The shape is one trigger, not a hundred.
    horsegenetics:progress_task fires once from
    HorseProgressData.complete, and the condition names which task; a
    chapter condition matches anything in a chapter and an empty one matches
    anything at all, which is how the chapter nodes and the root are earned without a
    hundred-entry requirements array. A trigger per task would have been a hundred classes
    saying the same thing.

    The JSON is baked, not written.
    tools/bake-advancements.mjs reads the enum and rewrites the whole
    directory, so a deleted task loses its card too. Text is literal rather than a
    translation key, because the English already lives in the enum and a lang key would be
    a second copy of it. The one thing it cannot derive is the icon, so those are a
    hand-written map in the script — validated against the mod’s item
    definitions and against vanilla’s item list read out of the patched jar, because
    an icon naming an item that does not exist makes the advancement fail to load
    silently.

    The owner asked that future checklist items become advancements too, so that is now a
    check rather than a habit: check-progress-tasks.mjs fails on a task with
    no advancement and on an advancement no task backs, beside its original sweep for a
    task nothing can complete. And ProgressHooks.onLogin awards everything
    already ticked, which is what lets the advancements arrive after the fact on a save
    that was played without them — at the cost of one flood of toasts, once.

A right-click opens the horse menu

    Empty-handed, no sneaking, on any horse. It is a client setting,
    horseScreen.rightClickOpens, on by default. Sneak-and-use is deliberately
    not taken over, so vanilla’s inventory stays one gesture away, and an
    empty hand is required, so food, leads, name tags and papers all still mean what they
    meant.

    The click it takes over is the one that mounts — which is also
    how a wild horse is tamed. That is the whole risk in this change, and the answer is a
    Ride button on Overview doing exactly what the click did, bucking
    included. Anything less would have quietly cost the game its taming.

And the menu grew the tack

    Saddle and body armour, as two real slots on Overview, built from
    HorseTackSlot.values() so a bridle or a blanket is a line in that enum
    rather than a screen rewrite — which is what the owner asked for. They are
    entity EquipmentSlots, not container slots, so no menu had to be opened
    on the server and the client already had them off synced equipment.
    One click is a swap with your hand: this is a plain
    Screen with no carried stack, so the hand is the only cursor there is,
    and the whole interaction is one packet the server checks in one place.

    An access transformer widens a declaration, not its overrides

        The Ride button first tried to call AbstractHorse.doPlayerRide through
        an AT. That broke the Minecraft recompile outright:
        TraderLlama overrides it and stayed protected, which is “weaker
        access privileges” and 6882 source files that no longer compile. The
        four lines were copied instead. Worth remembering before widening anything a
        subclass overrides — the failure is not in your module and does not mention
        your name.
