Magical gene / recessive / grows geometry, paints nothing

Unicorn horn

A horse with two copies grows a single tapering horn on its forehead — and it is the first thing in this mod that is a piece of the horse rather than a picture on it. Nothing about the coat changes. How long the horn is, how thick, how tightly it spirals, how far it leans and what colour it is are all written on the allele copy and inherited with it, so a horn you like breeds true and a line selected for length keeps getting longer.

Crossing two of them

Finding one

Recessive and rare: about one wild horse in four hundred is a unicorn, and roughly one in twenty carries a copy without showing it. So the realistic route is not to wait for one but to notice you have carriers — cross two and about one foal in four grows a horn. A known gene splice carrot for the locus makes each step of that deterministic rather than a coin flip.

Two unicorns are not alike

The gene grants a horn. Everything about which horn is epigenetic, and the horn is redrawn from those numbers rather than chosen from a list:

WhatRangeHow it inherits
Length A nub barely out of the skull, through short, standard (about twice the height of the head) and long, to a narwhal tusk longer than the whole skull Continuous. Drifts a hair at every breeding, so a line creeps up or down the scale over generations rather than jumping
Thickness A needle to a stout cone Continuous, as above
Twist Four shapes: smooth (the only one that curves forward), loose, spiral, tight A category. Almost never changes; when it does it changes outright, so a spiral-horned line stays spiral-horned
Lean Mostly forward over the face; a few horses carry one raked back over the poll instead Continuous
Colour Pale keratin — ivory, pearl, bone, a warm gold Three channels, each drifting on its own, so a family's horn colour wanders gradually instead of being re-invented
Glow About one horned horse in eight has a horn that shines in the dark A category, like the twist — so it breeds true, and a luminous foal out of two dull parents is a real event

A foal born with the gene wears its own small horn from the day it is born; it grows into the full one.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing at all, for good or ill. A horn moves no stat, grants no ability, changes no hitbox, and does not affect what the horse can be ridden through or how it is fed. It is worth exactly what you think it looks worth.

If it costs you frames

A horn is roughly as much geometry again as the horse carrying it, so a large herd of unicorns is the case worth a setting. Three live in this client’s own config file:

  • parts.enabled — draw grown parts at all.
  • parts.glow — draw the extra full-bright pass a luminous horn needs. Off, it still draws, lit like the rest of the horse. This is the first thing to try if a shader pack makes horns look wrong.
  • parts.detailDistance — how close a horse must be for its horn to be drawn.
Turning it off does not take the horn away

Every one of those settings gates a drawing and nothing else. A horse with a horn still has it: it breeds it, passes it to its foals, and shows it in the gene list and the browser, and it is back on screen the moment the setting is. Two players on one server may disagree about all three and still be looking at the same horses.

It paints nothing, and that is the novelty

All three outcomes are wild types, so the baked coat texture is untouched and two horses that differ only here share one coat. That much it has in common with cutie mark and particle. What is new is what shows instead: not an overlay on the finished sheet but separate geometry — the first visual in the mod that is not a texture. See attached model parts for the machinery, which this gene is the first and so far only user of.

Practically, that means neither coat golden should move for a change to this gene, and when the gene was added neither did: the pipeline golden's keys all grew a horsegenetics.unicorn_horn=n/n segment and not one of the coat hashes changed, and the bake golden gained exactly two rows that hash identically to each other. If a future edit here moves a coat hash, something is wrong that a golden update would hide.

The epigenome, sorted by what it costs to change

UnicornHornGene.hornFor(genotype, epigenome) reads the expressing copy's values and returns an AttachedPart. The interesting design decision is that the six numbers are grouped by what changing them costs rather than by what they mean:

ValueKindReachesCost
lengthuniform 0–1 the PartShape size bucket and the axial scale Mesh, plus free
twistcategory, one per HornGenerator style the PartShape styleMesh
girthuniformcross-axis scaleFree — a pose transform
tiltuniform, a little into the negativerotation about xFree

Why length is in both columns

Length is not only a scale. A three-unit nub and a twenty-four-unit tusk want different segment counts: squashing fifteen boxes into three units leaves boxes a fraction of a texel wide, which shimmer as the camera moves, and stretching three boxes to twenty-four units gives a horn made of three visible blocks. So the segment count steps, and the steps are PartShape.SIZE_BUCKETS geometric buckets.

That would leave a seam — a lineage's horns popping as they grew through a boundary — so the same number also drives a scale along the horn's own axis, chosen so the drawn length is exactly what the epigenome asked for. Because the buckets are geometric and there are sixteen of them, that scale is never more than about 7% from 1, so it stretches the mesh without visibly distorting the taper. PartGeneratorTest pins both halves: the drawn length matches the ladder at two hundred points, and the stretch never leaves that band.

The bound on cost is structural, not a budget

The whole set of meshes this mod can bake is PartKind.styles() × PartShape.SIZE_BUCKETS per kind — a fixed, small number, however many unicorns exist and whatever a drifted epigenome asks for. That is why PartMeshes has no eviction policy and no bake budget, where GeneticCoatTextureFactory needs both: a coat is a per-pixel loop and there are unboundedly many of them, and a part mesh is a dozen boxes of allocation with a hard ceiling on how many can ever exist.

How the horn reaches the screen

  1. GrownParts.of(genotype, epigenome) — the one door the client uses, so the renderer never learns which loci grant parts and the next one is a line in that file.
  2. GeneticHorseRenderer.extractRenderState resolves it once per frame per horse onto GeneticHorseRenderState.parts, along with the three config flags. Deliberately not in the layer: CutieMarkLayer asks the genotype afresh on every submit and folds over the whole registry each time, which is affordable for one emblem and wrong for a pen of twenty horses.
  3. AttachedPartLayer walks the list. A horse with no parts returns on the first line, the way BraidLayer does for a horse wearing no braid.
  4. PartMeshes.get(shape) bakes the mesh the first time any horse needs it and keeps it.

A layer definition can be baked at run time

Every other model in the mod is registered during EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterLayerDefinitions, which would have forced attached parts into a fixed set declared at startup — and that would have made a horn's length one of five presets instead of a real number. It is not so constrained: LayerDefinition.bakeRoot() is public and pure (mesh.getRoot().bake(xTexSize, yTexSize), touching no registry, no event and no resource manager), so a mesh can be built the first time a horse wants one. Read out of the 26.1.2 sources, not assumed. See API notes.

The deferred-submit trap, and why the model is typed on the part

submitModel stores a copy of the pose and a reference to the model, and the batch renderer calls setupAnim(state) immediately before drawing each submit. So the obvious way to vary one shared mesh per horse — set visible or xScale on its parts and then submit — is a trap: the second horse in a frame overwrites the first horse's mesh before either is drawn, and the bug is one horse wearing another's horn.

PartModel is therefore a Model<AttachedPart> rather than an EntityModel of the horse's state. The type parameter of submitModel is free, and choosing the part means anything that must be applied by mutation can be applied in setupAnim, where it runs per submit with that submit's own numbers. Nothing needs that yet — a horn's length, girth, lean, colour and glow are all a pose transform or a draw argument, and a pose is copied rather than shared — so setupAnim is empty. That emptiness is the interesting thing about the class: it is where an antler's tine count will have to go.

The anchor, and the two models that do not agree

PartAnchor is an enum and not a bone name, so common/ never learns Minecraft's part names and retargeting an anchor is a client edit rather than a gene edit. AttachedPartLayer is the only place in the mod that knows one.

It walks the chain from the root part down — root, head_parts, head — where CutieMarkLayer reaches straight for body and hand-tunes its offsets to compensate. That difference is not tidiness: HdHorseModel.createHdLayer applies MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F), and that transformer rewrites the root part's own pose (a 1.1 scale and a −2.4016 y offset), so a layer that skips the root works in a space 10% too small and two and a half units too high. Walking from the root means the number never appears in the layer, and means the same code is correct for the foal, whose layer carries no such transform at all.

The two models share the bone names and nothing else. The adult head box spans y −11..−6, z −2..5; the foal's spans y −3.95..0.05, z −6.71..2.30, on a head part with its own offset. So the layer keeps one offset per model, and a foal's horn additionally carries a scale of its own: vanilla's foal is a big-headed thing whose skull is nearly the adult's, so matching the head would have given a newborn a full-length tusk.

Seen, 2026-09-30. Both computed offsets land: the owner watched a horn stay on the head in the dev yard and found it exactly placed on an adult, and in the right spot on a foal. The parenting through root → head_parts → head therefore does what the sources said it would. The foal's FOAL_SCALE errs the other way from the tusk it was guarding against: the horn comes out comically small.

One greyscale sheet for every colour

part_sheet.png is 64×64, cut into a 4×4 grid of 16×16 regions; two are painted (horn grain, and a denser darker tip) and the rest are room for antlers, crystals and feathering. It is greyscale on purpose — colour is the per-horse tint, the same method a dyed braid uses — so one file serves every horn a lineage ever drifts to, and no new art is needed when it does.

A box reads the corner of its region rather than all of it: a cube's UV rectangle is its own unwrap, and the largest box any part generates is about 10×4 texels inside a 16-texel region. That is what lets a part's proportions change with an epigenetic number without needing a new sheet, and it is why the regions are a uniform tileable grain rather than a picture with a composition. Re-bake with node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-part-sheet.mjs; the art is procedural and original, so nothing of anybody else's is in it.

Why it is not filed with the coat genes

GeneFamily.MAGIC_PARTS — “Grown parts” — is a new family, and the one enum constant is what the wiki sidebar, the custom spawn egg's picker and the browser designer's family filter all read. Cutie mark is overridden into the coat family because it is unambiguously a marking; a horn is not. It has a length in model units, it catches the light, it follows the head when the horse grazes, and none of those are things a coat gene can say. Filing the first one under “genes that put colour on the horse” would have been cheaper by one constant and would have given antlers, spines, crystals and wings the wrong home to be born into.

There is no real locus here

Obviously. This is one of the frankly magical genes and it makes no claim on horse genetics at all. What is worth saying is where the shape of the gene came from, because it was not arbitrary.

Why recessive, and why carriers

A dominant horn would be a horn you find rather than a horn you breed. One copy showing means the first unicorn you meet is the whole story, and any line descended from it is visibly unicorn from the first cross — there is nothing to work toward. Recessive puts the interesting object one step behind a thing you can notice and act on: I have two carriers. It is the same reason cutie mark is recessive, and it is the shape almost every showpiece locus in this mod takes.

The frequency is then a balance between two failures. Too rare and the gene is content nobody ever sees, which the mod already has too much of; too common and a unicorn is not a unicorn. One in four hundred wild horses, with one in twenty carrying, puts an unbred horn somewhere between “a genuine find” and “something that has happened to you”, and makes the carrier route clearly the sensible one.

Why the horn varies at all

It would have been less work to ship one horn. The reason not to is the premise of the whole mod (philosophy): a coat is not chosen from a catalogue, and a horn drawn from a list of five would be the first thing here that was. A gene that grants the same object to every horse carrying it is a switch, and a switch has nothing to breed toward — you either have it or you do not, and the second unicorn is identical to the first.

The epigenome is what makes the difference, and it needed no new machinery: a gene declares named numbers on each allele copy, founders roll them, and every breeding nudges them. So “a line bred for length” is a real activity with a real result, and two unicorns are no more alike than two bay horses are.

Why the horn facets, and why that is the right trade

A horn is a stack of small square prisms, not a smooth cone, so it has visible facets. A genuinely smooth horn would need custom vertex geometry rather than boxes — possible (the hay portal already does it) at the price of owning the vertex code. It was not taken, for two reasons. The first is that blocky is the game's own look and a faceted, carved-looking horn is not a compromise anybody has to apologise for. The second is more interesting: everything about the boxes is plain arithmetic in common/, which is what lets a unit test walk every mesh the mod can build with no game running, and what would let the browser designer preview a horn's silhouette from the same code rather than a second implementation of it in JavaScript.

The one piece of geometry that is a trap

Vanilla composes a model part's rotation as rotationZYX, so a segment is turned about x first and then about the parent's y. On a segment with no bend, those are the same axis as the horn's own, and the y rotation is a clean roll — a square section rolled a little per segment reads as a twisted horn, because the corners trace a helix down the length. On a segment that is also bent, the same rotation sweeps the bend sideways instead, and fifteen segments of that is a corkscrew whose tip points somewhere behind the horse's ear.

So the four styles split cleanly: the smooth one is the only one that curves, and the three twisted ones are dead straight. That is not a concession — a straight tapered spiral is the storybook horn and a narwhal tusk both, and the gentle forward curve is what an untwisted horn needs to stop reading as a spike. PartGeneratorTest asserts that no box ever carries both, because it is the sort of invariant a later tidy-up removes without anything going red.

What needs verifying here

The horn follows the head and sits right on an adult, but a foal's horn is comically small, randomizing the spawn egg's epigenetics has only just been made to reach it, and nubs, narwhals, size extremes, tack and a pen's frame cost are all unwatched.

A foal's horn is comically small — keep it?

The owner found it in the right spot on a foal but tiny, and called it funny rather than necessarily a bug. FOAL_SCALE was a guess made to avoid giving a newborn a full-length tusk, and it overshot the other way. Whether to raise it, or keep the joke, is the owner's call.

Nubs, narwhals, and the size extremes

Placement is confirmed on an ordinary adult and foal. Still to look at: a nub and a narwhal both, since the base girth changes with length — no gap at the base, nothing through the jaw. Then a tiny and a huge horse from the magical size locus: the horn should inherit the horse's scale from the pose stack, and if it does not this is where it shows.

Randomize epigenetics reaches the horn

The owner found that the spawn egg's Rnd epigen. changed nothing about the horn. The cause was that both editors only re-roll genes that change how a horse looks unless Rnd health is on, and the horn paints nothing, so it counted as invisible. Fixed 2026-09-30 by EditorRules.changesLooks, which also counts a gene that grows a part; UnicornHornGeneTest pins it. To check in the game: on a Horn/Horn horse with Rnd health off, Rnd epigen. should visibly change the horn's length, lean or colour on the preview.

A pen of unicorns

A horn is about as much geometry again as the horse wearing it, and nothing here has been measured. Twenty Horn/Horn horses in one pen against the same pen with parts.enabled off: look for a frame-time cliff, not a percentage. Then parts.glow off with a glowing horse in frame, and parts.detailDistance walked across. The default detail distance was picked by reasoning rather than from that measurement and should be re-set from it.

The switches are render-only

The claim is that all three settings gate a draw and touch nothing else. Check that with parts.enabled off a horned horse still lists the gene in the horse screen and the browser, still passes the horn to its foals, and saves and reloads unchanged — and that all three apply as soon as the config screen closes, with no world reload.

Against everything else that draws on a horse

Nothing on the head competes with the tack today, which is an argument and not an observation. Check a horned horse wearing body armour and a saddle, with a braid and a cutie mark, under dominant white, and while ridden — the horn should fade with the horse rather than hanging solid in the air, which is the one case the layer changes render type for. Also an invisible horse (potion): no horn should draw at all.

The designer does not draw the horn

The game's spawn egg draws the horn on its preview, as does every other in-game screen that shows a horse model. The browser designer previews a flat coat and cannot, so a Horn/Horn horse shows there with its gene row and no horn. Whether the designer should say so in words, draw it, or something else is a design call rather than a defect. See attached model parts.

Should a glow come from the light locus instead?

A glowing horn is an epigenetic category on this gene. The alternative — and it is what cutie mark does — is a contribution hook, so that a horse whose mane and hooves already glow grows a glowing horn as a matter of course rather than by an independent one-in-eight roll. The hook was not built because it would have had no implementor on the day, and an interface nothing implements is worse than none. Whether to add it, and whether the two should then interact or the light locus should simply win, is unresolved.
