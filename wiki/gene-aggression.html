Magical gene / no coat effect

Aggression

What a horse attacks, and when. One locus, one grid: a time of day crossed with what it goes for. Being in the saddle is not protection — a horse that hunts riders throws you off first.

Built, and not yet seen in game

The locus replaced three older ones on 2026-09-17 and nothing on this page has been watched happening since. Two of the records below were earned by the genes it absorbed and are kept because the machinery they tested is the same machinery. What to look at is on To be verified.

The grid

Every allele is a when and a who. The prefix is the time — Ad by day, An after dark, Aa always — and the last letter is what it goes for.

Two copies of the same allele, or nothing

Every allele here is recessive, and recessive to the others as well as to the wild type. Adh/n is a calm horse. Adh/Anh is also a calm horse — “hunts monsters by day” and “hunts monsters after dark” are two instructions and the animal settles on neither.

That is what makes a locus this wide a search rather than a lottery: you must find the same allele twice. Two aggressive horses caught in different places will usually throw perfectly pleasant foals, and the disappointment is the game.

What happens if you are on it

Nothing about being ridden calms an aggressive horse. Which of two things happens depends on what it wants:

  • if it is aggressive toward riders (or everything), it bucks you out of the saddle and comes for you;
  • if it is aggressive toward anything else, it ignores you and deals with the target.

The horse that absorbed this locus used to stop dead the moment anybody mounted it. That was a deliberate design and it is deliberately gone: a horse that hunts people is not a horse you can simply sit on.

Health

It picks fights, so it takes damage. This locus only makes the horse willing; how hard it hits is magic fighter, and how much it can survive is magic health. An aggressive horse with neither bravely inconveniences a zombie and then dies of it.

If it hunts riders, you may not be able to keep it

A wild horse that attacks players on sight cannot be approached, so it cannot be fed, so it cannot be tamed. The way in is passification — a separate locus that says what you can offer the animal to make it stop. A horse with an Aap pair and no passification allele is, in practice, a monster that looks like a horse.

The time of day is a condition, not a verb

There were once two temperament verbs for this, night_temper and day_temper, on the argument that a gate written onto every allele by hand would be wrong the first time somebody forgot it. That argument is about a gene file. This locus generates its alleles from a table in Java, so the gate is written once and cannot drift between them — and the general temper verb already carried a when. Both time verbs were deleted.

It holds a radius. It must never chase

A horse that pursues is a horse that dies forty blocks from its owner, and the owner blames the gene — correctly. Attack what comes within reach and return. Full pursuit and owner-leashed pursuit were both specified and both rejected in design; if a later session wants either, it is reopening a settled call.

“Everything” includes other horses, and “herds” does not

passive deliberately excludes every equine in MobGroups, because a horse is an Animal and a foal that flees or fights “herds” would otherwise turn on its own band — which is exactly what gap 241 was. That exclusion used to live in the night handler's private matcher and was nearly lost when fleeing moved onto the shared verb. A gene that really means other horses says horses.

Half-built: a ridden horse will not pursue

The buck works — GeneAbilityHandler.buckIfItIsTheRider ejects the passenger the moment the horse targets them. “Ignores its rider and goes for the target” does not. A ridden horse's movement is driven by its passenger rather than by its navigation, so the melee goal cannot walk it anywhere while somebody is aboard: it will turn and swing at whatever comes to it, and no more. Making it genuinely ignore the reins needs rider control suppressed while it has a target, in the shape of mixin.HorseRearFightMixin. See gap 268.

Some of it can be caught

Unlike the night locus it replaced — whose founder table was entirely heterozygous, so no wild horse ever expressed one — this one puts expressing combinations in the wild, and the nastier the target the rarer they are. A horse that hunts monsters is something to find; a horse that hunts everything is something somebody bred. That is not only flavour: passification exists to rescue a wild horse that is aggressive to players, and there has to be one. (Owner's call.)

Verified

All eight forms of the locus this replaced were confirmed (2026-09-13, as magic_night_temper). They were never broken, only unwatchable, for three reasons that each looked like “the gene does nothing”: the horse dimension had no night (a dimension_type needs timelines as well as a default_clock); the biome was the_void, so the monster-targeted forms had nothing to be about; and the fleeing forms were in 4 × 7 stalls, which confirmed the hunting forms at once and hid the flee forms completely. Moved to 19 × 30 paddocks, the log filled with 48 lines, every one path accepted. The flee half of that record now belongs to skittishness.

A horse swings every ten ticks once a rear no longer freezes it (2026-09-15, formerly gap 222). HorseMeleeGoal keeps its own ten-tick cooldown, because vanilla's reset hard-codes twenty (API notes). The yard's kick pen puts an aggressive horse in with two husks. Its first run (07:10) swung at ticks 84 and 143. The cause was the rear after each blow: a standing horse is immobile, and an immobile mob runs no goals (API notes). With HorseRearFightMixin (the owner's call: rear, and keep fighting), the 07:33 run swung at ticks 56, 70 and 80, killing the first husk in four blows. The control horse, which has no temper, never swung at its husks. What that run also showed, the horse not turning on the second husk, is the next record. Earned while this was the gladiator locus, on the same temper machinery this locus now uses.

A horse that kills one attacker turns on the next (2026-09-15, formerly gap 248). In the 07:33 run it killed the first husk at tick 130 and then never struck again. The second husk hit it four times in 3.6 seconds and killed it. GeneAbilityHandler.temper took the first matching entity in its box each beat: dead or alive, near or far, seen or not, while the neighbouring pen's husks sat inside that radius behind a wall. The fix keeps a live target it can see. Otherwise it takes whatever last hurt the horse, if that is alive, in range and in sight, and failing that the nearest live, visible match. A dead target is dropped. Guardian is untouched, because its owner-hurt trigger fires from GeneReactionHandler. Evidence, 07:44 run: the first husk died at tick 135. The next horse hurt line read no target, and the horse struck the second husk at tick 162, 27 ticks later. It killed that husk at tick 213 and lived, on 1 of 22 health.

Why a grid, and not a switch

Naming the target is what makes this a breeding problem rather than a toggle. A horse that hunts monsters and a horse that hunts you are the same gene one allele apart, and they look identical in daylight. Nothing about the animal tells you which you have until the conditions are right, and by then you are either delighted or on foot.

Why the change is total rather than gradual

A horse does not become slightly aggressive at dusk. A ramp would read as a mood — something the animal is working through — and the point is the discovery that the animal you ride all week is not the animal in your yard at night. A threshold is a fact about the creature; a gradient is weather.

Why aggression and fear are two loci

They were one locus once, and it could not say the interesting thing. With hunting and fleeing sharing a set of alleles, a horse could be a monster-hunter or shy of people but never both. Splitting them means “goes for monsters, runs from riders” is a single animal — a herd-guard nobody can catch — and that horse is worth breeding for. See skittishness.

What needs verifying here

Day temper inverts the night locus and exists only in unit tests — a Flp/Flp horse should run from you at noon and let you ride it at midnight; the older questions are whether Aah/Aah holds its ground instead of chasing, and whether Aap/Aap really throws its rider.
