Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing

Brow ridge

One copy and the horse grows a low wide ridge of bone across its brow, above and in front of the eyes. Plain, notched into three bumps or set with three short spines, and how tall and wide it is and how tall its bumps stand, is written on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own, brow ridge colour.

Crossing two of them

One copy, either sex

Dominant: Brw/n and Brw/Brw both grow a ridge, so a ridged horse bred to a plain one gives a ridged foal at least half the time. It is not sex-limited. Foals grow none; the ridge comes in when the horse grows up, and stays.

Where it sits

Across the top of the face, in front of the eyes. It is lower down the face than a unicorn horn, so a horse with both wears the horn just behind the ridge.

Finding one

Rare in the wild: about three horses in a thousand have a brow ridge, and because one copy is enough, every horse that carries the gene shows it. The skeleton horses can grow one in bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts.

What varies

  • Form: plain (a bar with a narrower step along its top), notched (three bumps) or spined (a row of three short spines).
  • Height: three classes, low, heavy and towering. Low to heavy in the wild; a line bred for height reaches towering and stops.
  • Width: how far across the head it reaches. A wild ridge stays inside the skull; the widest reaches its edges.
  • Bump: how tall a notched ridge’s bumps or a spined one’s spines stand.

Everything stacks: a horse can wear a brow ridge with every other part.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no armour value, no hitbox. It is a look.

The mesh: a bar laid on its side

A box hangs along its own -y and may be thinner across x than it is deep, never wider (PartGeneratorTest holds that). So a bar wider than it is tall is a box turned a quarter round z: rooted at the right end of the brow, five units long, running across to the left. Its square section is the ridge’s height and its depth front to back. It sinks a fifth of a unit into the skull so a stretched ridge shows no gap.

At draw time the part’s stretch is along the anchor’s up, which is the ridge’s height, and its girth is across it, which is the bar’s own length. That is why width on the copy is the part’s girth, and why a wider ridge is also a little deeper. What stands on the bar is turned a quarter back to stand up: a plain ridge’s step lies along the top, a notched one’s three bumps and a spined one’s three two-box spines stand at a fifth, a half and four fifths of the way across.

The epigenome, by cost

  • height: the class is the mesh’s size bucket (three), the remainder a stretch along the anchor’s up.
  • width: free, the part’s girth.
  • form and bump: baked, BrowRidgeGenerator.style(form, bump). A plain ridge has no bumps and is one style whatever its bucket, a trim to fit the box budget.

What the first photo shoot changed

The first build’s bumps stood as tall as the horse’s ears at wild sizes, which is a crest and not a low ridge. The ladder came down from 0.9–2.2 units to 0.7–1.7 and the bump shares from 0.45–1.2 of the bar’s height to 0.3–0.75 (2026-10-09).

The bone version

Bon/Bon on the colour locus. A ridge is all bone, so nothing is left off. Both skeleton breeds carry brow_ridge in their horn count group as a slot of its own, with the colour pinned to bone (owner, 2026-10-09).

Invented

No horse has a bony brow ridge that stands off the skull. The look borrows from the brow bosses and horn cores of ceratopsians and pachycephalosaurs, the heavy brow of a gorilla, and the notched frontal crests of some chameleons. This is fantasy and makes no claim about any real gene. It is dominant (the treatment’s call), so it shows on every carrier.

What needs verifying here

The brow ridge has been photographed standing still in all three forms, under a unicorn horn and in bone; whether it sits where wanted against the eyes, how the horn meets it, and how it looks in motion and at towering size are still open.

What a photo shoot showed, and the placement

2026-10-09, -PphotoShoot, five still frames, re-rolled for a heavy ridge with tall bumps. A bar sits across the top of the face in front of the eyes, in all three forms. A Wht/Blu notched ridge is a white bar with blue bumps, and a Blk/Red spined one a red bar with dark spines. A unicorn horn stands directly behind the ridge. A skeleton’s is bone.

For the owner to judge. The horn’s root touches the back of the ridge, so the two read as one piece; the treatment wanted the horn able to stand on top of it, and this may be that or may want a unit more gap (AttachedPartLayer.ADULT_BROW). A notched ridge’s three bumps stand close together and read as one grooved block. The spines are square columns, not cones.

In motion, and towering

Each form at low and at towering height, from the front and the side, while the horse walks, grazes and rears. The ridge should ride the head with no gap under it. At the widest width it should reach the edges of the skull and not hang past them.

Against a bridle and horse armour

Put horse armour on a ridged horse. The armour’s face plate covers the brow; say whether the ridge should show through it, as it does today, or hide.
