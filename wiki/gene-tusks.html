Magical gene / dominant / grows geometry, paints nothing

Tusks

One gene for the growths of the mouth and muzzle, with a form for each allele. Today it has one form, the narwhal horn: one long spiral tusk growing straight forward out of the front of the muzzle. Its length, thickness, twist and lift are written on the allele. Its colour is a gene of its own, tusk colour. Boar tusks and sabre fangs are the next two forms (see the Roadmap tab).

Crossing two of them

One copy, either sex

Dominant: Nar/n and Nar/Nar both grow the horn, and a second copy does not grow a second horn or a bigger one. It is not sex-limited: unlike the antlers, no allele grows only on stallions. Foals grow none; the horn comes in when the horse grows up, and stays.

Where it sits

On the front of the nose, in the middle, pointing forward along the line of the face. It moves with the head, so it dips when the horse grazes. It does not sit where a unicorn’s horn does, so one horse can grow both.

Finding one

Very rare in the wild: about one horse in a thousand has a narwhal horn, and because one copy is enough, every horse that carries the gene shows it. The skeleton horses can grow one in bare bone, as one of their horn-like parts.

What varies

  • Length: from a short tusk just past the end of the face, through long, to a great one longer than the horse’s body is deep. Wild horses are short to long; a line bred for length keeps growing it, up to the ceiling.
  • Twist: a spiral or a tight spiral. A narwhal horn is always straight; it never curves.
  • Thickness.
  • Lift: how far the point is raised above the line of the face.

Everything stacks: a horse can wear a narwhal horn, a unicorn horn, antlers and every body part at once.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no ability, no hitbox. It is a look.

Slice A of the tusks treatment

The narwhal horn is the cheapest form, so it came first and proved the gene, the new anchor and the colour locus. There is no new generator. PartKind.NARWHAL is HornGenerator in its two twisted styles (NARWHAL_STYLES: spiral and tight, never the bending smooth style) on its own ladder, NarwhalSize, from 10 to 30 model units, geometric, with the horn’s sixteen size buckets. No new render path either: the client change is the SNOUT anchor’s case in AttachedPartLayer.anchor. One call in GrownParts.of, dressed by its colour locus; the granting locus is in GrownParts.shapes’s every-part baseline.

Each form reads its own copy

Every other part gene reads the expressed copy. This one reads the copy that carries the form, through TusksGene.copyFor. That is for slice B: a Sab/Nar horse will grow its fangs from the Sab copy’s numbers and its horn from the Nar copy’s, so the two are bred apart though they share a locus. Two copies of one form read the first copy (the antlers’ symmetric rule), not the higher-priority one.

A copy follows its own allele because of Epigenome’s alignment invariant: Copies.first() belongs to pair.first(), and Genome keeps them aligned when breeding. The treatment marked this unverified. TusksGeneTest now breeds Nar/n with n/n both ways round and checks that every horned foal’s horn has its Nar copy’s length, never the empty wild copy’s. A mutant that always read the second slot turned that test and the homozygote test red.

The numbers are generic

Every copy carries the same five numbers whatever its form, and each form reads the ones it uses. The narwhal horn reads four: length (its place on NarwhalSize), girth, curve as a lift of up to NARWHAL_LIFT (0.45 rad) above the head’s axis, and style % 2 as its twist. style is a category of six so that each form’s own count (two now, three for the tusks and fangs) divides it: every style equally likely, and two forms on one horse independent. The seed is for the fangs’ serration and the tusks’ hook. Both are fixed now, so slice B changes no saved schema.

Why only Nar is declared

A Tsk or Sab allele declared today would grow nothing, which is a promise the game would not keep. Alleles are saved by token, and slice B appends them after n. Appending never changes the canonical order of a pair that already exists, so no saved horse’s copies move to the other allele.

Why priority 334

The part loci fill 227–246, and 247 is the only slot left beside them. The next free run of two was 334/335. The MAGIC_PARTS override files the pair with the parts whatever band the number falls in, and nothing requires slots to be unique.

Invented

No horse grows tusks. The narwhal’s tusk is a real thing: a single spiralled canine tooth that grows forward through the upper lip, almost always on the left and almost always in males. This horn takes the look and not the biology. It is centred on the muzzle, not offset to one side (owner, tusks treatment), and mares grow it as often as stallions. It is fantasy and makes no claim about any real gene. The forms combine (a horse with two different forms grows both) because the parts already stack, so there is nothing to suppress.

What needs verifying here

The narwhal horn has been photographed standing still at wild lengths (2026-10-05); how far down it points, its length ceiling, grazing, the size gene, a foal, and head gear are still to be seen.

What a photo shoot showed, and the look

2026-10-05, -PphotoShoot, four still frames of wild-length founders: ivory, Red/Blu, black beside a white unicorn horn, and a bone horn on a great valley skeleton. The horn roots in the middle of the front of the muzzle with no gap, runs straight along the line of the face and tapers to a point. The two-tone horn is red at the root fading to blue at the tip. Beside a unicorn horn the two never touch.

Two things for the owner to judge. Because the neck holds the head nose-down, a horn along the face points well below level; the lift number raises it by up to 0.45 rad, and a wider lift (or a fixed one) would change that. And the ceiling, a great horn of 30 units, has not been seen: the treatment asks for it to read as a long tusk without the horse looking like a unicorn on its face.

Grazing, the head, and the size gene

A horse with a great narwhal horn while it walks, tosses its head and grazes. The horn rides the head bone, so it should dip with the head; when the head is down to graze, say whether the horn driving into the ground is acceptable. Then a tiny and a huge horse (the size gene): the horn should scale with the head, through the pose scale already on the stack, as the page records from reading the code.

A foal, and head gear

A foal with Nar/n should show no horn, and grow one when it grows up. A bridle (and head armour, if any is worn) on a horse with a narwhal horn: the horn should not hide inside the gear or float clear of the face.

What is still planned here

Slice B (#161): boar tusks and sabre fangs, the gene’s other two forms, each on a new pair of anchors with a new generator, and the mixed pairs that grow two forms at once.

Boar tusks and sabre fangs

Two alleles appended after n: Tsk, boar tusks, short and curved, rising from the lower jaw at each side (anchors JAW_RIGHT/JAW_LEFT); and Sab, sabre fangs, long flat blades hanging from the upper lip past the chin (anchors LIP_RIGHT/LIP_LEFT). Each needs a generator of its own (a tapering curved chain; a flat tapering blade), three styles each read as style % 3, the curve number as the tusk’s sweep or the fang’s lean, and the seed for the hook or the serration. The new expressions are the two single forms and the three combinations (Tsk+Sab, Tsk+Nar, Sab+Nar), each grown from its own copy, which this slice already does through copyFor. The boar tusks and fangs are bone like the horn, so the skeleton breeds’ count group would decide whether they carry those forms too.
