Magical gene / no coat effect

Verdant

A horse that changes the ground it walks on: mycelium, moss or grass, creeping outward from wherever it stands. Three variants at one locus, every one of them needing two of itself — so a mycelium copy beside a moss copy is not half of each, it is nothing at all.

Crossing two of them

It paints nothing, and that is a gap

All five outcomes are wild types, so affectsCoat() is false, the locus is out of the texture key, and the gallery collapses it to one entry — four alleles for no catalogue growth at all, the same trick sex and the non-coat loci use.

The cost is real, and it is the same one milk has: a verdant horse is indistinguishable from an ordinary one until you watch the floor. It sits awkwardly beside healer, which draws a stripe precisely so a player can see what it does. A marking would be the fix, and it would want to be part of the locus rather than bolted on.

Three recessives at one locus

Every variant needs two of itself. That is a genuinely different shape from the rest of the mod, and it is worth sitting next to its nearest relative: milk’s two variants are also mutually recessive, but the pairing of them is lethal where this one is merely inert. Both are combination tables. Neither needs a word for what it is doing, and no dominance ranking could tell the two situations apart.

The consequence is that a verdant horse takes real work. With the commonest variant at 8%, about one wild horse in a hundred and fifty spreads grass, and the two carriers who could throw one look exactly like every other horse. Three variants at one locus also means a line bred toward one of them is drifting away from the other two — which is the sort of decision this model exists to create.

A cover, not a block id

The gene names one of three covers — mycelium, moss, grass — and stops there. It does not name a block, because “spreading moss” is not one conversion: it is a small family of them, plus a rule about what it will and will not eat. That judgement needs the game’s block registry, so it lives in the translator, where the lists are deliberately narrow:

Every entry is ground a player would expect to green over, and nothing on the lists is a block anyone builds with. Grass is the gentlest of the three and the only one that undoes damage rather than doing it.

One block a beat, on purpose

The translator converts at most one eligible block per beat, and picks a random candidate near the horse rather than the nearest one. Both are design rather than performance dodges: this is a horse changing the ground it walks over, and a player who leaves one standing in a field should come back to a patch rather than to a new biome. Picking randomly also makes the edge of a spread ragged instead of a perfect expanding disc.

It used to be skipped entirely in the horse dimension, for the same reason the light block was: nothing there should be able to rewrite the floor. That guard made every spreading gene a silent no-op in the one place built for watching them (“dryad does not work”, owner 2026-09-12), so it is commented out in GeneAbilityHandler.spread — marked RESTORE BEFORE 1.0, and the version that comes back is meant to tell the corridor from the test yard rather than treat the whole dimension as a display case.

What needs verifying here

Nothing has watched a verdant horse change a floor, and the gene paints nothing at all, so every check here is a block count off a log — starting with the one the warn box is actually worried about: moss eats stone, cobblestone and andesite, which are things players build with, so a moss horse stabled on a cobbled floor may well eat it.

Still open (NOT played)

Verdant has no coat effect whatsoever, so there is almost no “does it look right” here at all — the verdict on nearly every item is a count of blocks in a pen. The dryad pens are the worked example of how to get one: a pen with a known floor, a stated watch list, and an unattended run long enough that SPREAD_CHANCE stops mattering. A verdant row would want the same.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • The carrier does nothing, and that is the control. A mush/moss horse is verdant-carrier: two variants that are mutually recessive, so it is inert and passes both on. Stand one on its own ground for hours. Pass: not one block converted. This is the check that tells a working gene from one that never runs, because a gene that converts nothing looks identical either way.
  • A moss horse on a cobblestone floor — the one worth doing first. moss’s list is stone, cobblestone, andesite, gravel, dirt, coarse dirt and grass block. Pass, as the page claims it: nothing a player built with is eaten. What the code will actually do: convert the floor, because cobblestone and stone are on the list. Stable a moss/moss horse on a cobbled floor for an hour and count the moss blocks. If it eats the floor, the list is what is wrong, not the horse — and this page’s claim that “nothing on the lists is a block anyone builds with” needs correcting either way.
  • Each cover eats only its own list. Give three matched pairs a floor that is half something on their list and half something that is not. Pass: mush/mush takes podzol and rooted dirt; grass/grass takes dirt, coarse dirt and rooted dirt and leaves podzol alone; neither touches stone. The podzol split between mycelium and grass is the sharpest pair, because the two lists differ on exactly that block.
  • It is one block a beat and it stays a patch. SPREAD_RADIUS 2, SPREAD_INTERVAL_TICKS 60, SPREAD_CHANCE 0.35, and at most one conversion per beat. Pass: a horse left in a field for a real session leaves a patch a few blocks across, not a new biome, and the count over a measured hour comes out well under the theoretical ceiling — the way gap 208 predicts for every spreading gene.
  • About one wild horse in a hundred and fifty spreads grass. grass is the commonest variant at 8% per allele, and a matched pair is what it takes. Pass: the gene row reads grass, moss or mycelium at roughly that rate over a lot of wild horses, and verdant-carrier far more often — carriers look exactly like everything else, so the row is the only way to see them.
  • It spreads in the horse dimension now the guard is off. The dimension check in GeneAbilityHandler.spread is commented out (RESTORE BEFORE 1.0), which is what made dryad visible there on 2026-09-13 — but no verdant horse has been stood in the gallery since. Pass: a moss/moss horse rewrites the floor it stands on, the same as it would in the overworld. Fail: nothing happens, which means the spreading genes differ in a way nothing in the code says they should.
