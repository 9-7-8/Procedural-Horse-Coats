Magical gene / no coat effect

Skittishness

What a horse runs from, and when. The mirror of aggression, allele for allele — and being ridden makes no difference to it at all.

Built, and not yet seen in game

The locus was split out of the two temper genes on 2026-09-17. The record below was earned by the allele it replaces, on the same flee machinery. What to look at is on To be verified.

The grid

The same three times crossed with the same five targets as aggression, on F-prefixed tokens so the two loci read alike: Fd by day, Fn after dark, Fa always.

Two copies of the same allele, exactly as on the aggression locus. Two different shy alleles show neither.

It does not stop when you get on

Aggression pauses nothing for a rider and neither does this, but the consequence is different and worse to own: a mount that bolts when a creeper comes round the corner is a horse you cannot take anywhere. That is the version of this gene a rider actually meets, and it is deliberately not gated away.

The one that is genuinely hard to keep

Fnp — flees riders after dark — is the difficult one. By day it is a perfectly ordinary horse that can be caught, tamed and ridden. After dark it will not let you near it. Nothing is wrong with the animal; you simply own a horse that is only yours for half of each day.

Health

Running is the safest thing on either temperament locus, and a shy horse generally outlives a brave one. The cost is where it ends up: a fleeing horse paths twelve blocks away from whatever frightened it and does not consider what is in that direction.

Fleeing was once implemented somewhere else entirely

Until 2026-09-17 the temper verb returned early on any mood that was not aggressive, and fleeing was implemented in NightBehaviourHandler against the night verb instead. When this locus moved onto the shared verb the flee path had to move with it, into GeneAbilityHandler.flee. Had it not, this gene would have loaded, registered, baked, appeared in the census and done nothing whatsoever — which is the failure mode this codebase keeps paying for. If you add a mood, follow it all the way into the translator.

“Herds” must not include the horse's own band

passive excludes every equine in MobGroups. It is the whole of gap 241 below, and the exclusion moved from the night handler's private matcher into the shared group resolver when this locus was built — where every caller gets it rather than one.

There is no vanilla avoid-goal to borrow

Fleeing is a navigation call rather than a goal: the horse paths to a point FLEE_DISTANCE directly away from the nearest match and drops any target it had. Log whether the path was accepted. From outside, a flee that never fired and a flee that fired into a fence four blocks away look exactly alike, and Navigation.moveTo's return is the only thing that tells them apart.

Verified

A night-shy horse leaves its own herd alone (2026-09-15, formerly gap 241). The bug: a Flc/Flc foal born into a wild band logged 1,578 night flee | ... from minecraft:horse lines in eight hours, because NightBehaviourHandler.matches never left equines out of “passive” despite a comment saying it must. The fix excludes equines and has the flee trace compare UUIDs with equals. Evidence: the yard's NIGHT SHY pen (row X) put a Flc/Flc mare in with a stallion, two mares and two cows for the 06:49 run's whole first night: 96 night flee | ... "NIGHT SHY" from minecraft:cow - path accepted lines and none from anything else. The allele is Fnc now, and the exclusion it tests lives in MobGroups.

Fear is not negative aggression

It would have been cheaper to keep one locus with a mood axis, and it would have been wrong. Temperament is not a line with hunting at one end and bolting at the other: a great many real animals are aggressive toward one thing and frightened of another, and the same animal is often both at once. Two loci say that. One could not.

Shy is commoner than dangerous

A frightened horse costs its owner a fence and an afternoon; an aggressive one costs them the horse. So the expressing combinations here are several times commoner in the wild than aggression's, and a wild herd that scatters when you approach is meant to be an ordinary thing to meet rather than a find.
