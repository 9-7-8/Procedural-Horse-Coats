Magical gene / no coat effect

Magic health

The magical version of the hearts the natural loci trade around — MSTN spends them for speed, HMGA2 gives them for being small. One of the five magical body-stat genes — size, speed, health, jump and pull — and like the other three multipliers, most wild horses carry a copy, worth about a tenth either way. Two copies of the same kind add, and no wild horse is ever born with two. (Pull is the odd one out: an additive score rather than a multiplier, and with no vampiric allele.)

Crossing two of them

Written out rather than derived from Hardy-Weinberg, because random mating is exactly what this table is not: about 82% of wild horses carry one copy, and no wild horse carries two of anything. Every doubled horse in the world, in either direction, is something somebody bred — the health loci’s rule, pointed at something worth having.

The vampiric allele

A third kind of copy, Vmp, and the Dhampir’s strength. Unlike Hardy it is worth a fixed amount, the same on every horse: one copy gives twice the hearts, two copies three times. Beside a Hardy or Frail copy, both count. About two wild horses in a hundred carry one; none carry two. A seal brown Dhampir has one, a white Dhampir two.

One of a family of five

This gene is a thin subclass of AbstractMagicStatGene, which is the magic body size pattern generalised to the three additive stats. Everything on this page is true of magic speed, magic health and magic jump alike; only the stat it moves and the allele names change. Magic body size is the odd one out — it multiplies scale, which carries its own two-stage natural clamp, so it kept its own class.

Magic pull is the fifth axis, and it is not this class either. The locus has the same shape — codominant, one allele up, one down, a per-copy number inherited with the copy, both copies added — but its sum is added to a 1–10 score rather than multiplied into a game unit, and it has no Vmp allele (the Dhampir gets its pull from a per-strain score on the breed sheet instead). So the multiplier arithmetic, the vampiric rows and the × tables below are this gene’s and its two AbstractMagicStatGene siblings’, not pull’s.

Because most wild horses carry a copy of all five, a wild-caught horse has a quiet, independent wobble on every axis at once, and a horse that is remarkable on any one of them is a breeding result rather than a catch.

Ten combinations, ten outcomes. Vmp adds vampiricPerCopy() into the same sum as the other copy rather than an epigenetic percentage, so it is identical on every horse — it is the old dhampir gene’s triple health, split in half across the two copies. A codominant locus is the case where every combination genuinely differs, so collapsing any two rows would be a lie about the gene. The contribution is signed — Hardy positive, Frail negative — so the whole table falls out of one line and Hardy/Frail cancels because a positive and a negative are added, not because anything checks for it.

It multiplies, it does not add

The natural health loci add a weight per copy. This gene takes the sum of its two copies’ percentages and multiplies whatever those loci settled on, through TraitBuilder.multiplyHealthUnclamped, applied after every addition and bounded only by HorseTraits.MAGICAL_MIN_FACTOR / MAGICAL_MAX_FACTOR (ten times either way).

common/genetics/genes/AbstractMagicStatGene.java
@Override
public void contribute(AllelePair pair, Genotype genotype, GeneEpigenetics epigenetics,
                       TraitBuilder out) {
    double sum = signedDelta(pair.first(),  epigenetics.copy(0))
               + signedDelta(pair.second(), epigenetics.copy(1));
    if (sum != 0.0) {
        applyMagic(out, 1.0 + sum);   // multiplyHealthUnclamped(1.0 + sum)
    }
}

Multiplying is what makes it compose the way you would want: a magically hardy pony is still frailer than a magically hardy draught horse, because the natural loci settle the horse’s own health first and the magic scales whatever that turned out to be. The MIN_HEALTH floor (half a heart) is still applied last, so a doubled Frail horse stacked with disorders cannot resolve to zero hearts — killing a horse is the damage path’s job.

It needs both copies’ epigenetics

Every non-codominant epigenetic gene asks for “the copy that expresses”, because one locus produces one result. A codominant one cannot: asking for the expressed copy would count one allele twice and the other not at all. So this gene takes an GeneEpigenetics and asks it for copy(0) and copy(1) — the same accessor magic body size and mane colour need, for the same reason.

Each copy’s percentage is a bounded normal draw (mean 10%, σ 7%) off that copy’s stored epigenetic seed, floored at 1% so a Hardy allele can never come out subtracting. The seed is rolled once for a founder and inherited verbatim with the allele, so a foal that inherits the copy inherits the exact percentage — which is what makes breeding two good copies together a real project.

The bounded Gaussian keeps the real reach near ×2 even at two maximal copies, so the ten-times MAGICAL_*_FACTOR clamp is a guard that never actually fires — the same story as magic body size. Asked about a genotype with no epigenome to read (a wiki table, a punnett display), the gene reports the midpoint: exactly ×1.10 for one copy, ×1.20 for two.

Not a health disorder

It is not a HealthContribution. That marker is what the server config’s “off” position switches off, and it is meant for disorders — the dwarfisms, the lethal foals. Turning those off must not also quietly strip a horse of its magical vigour. A Frail/Frail horse here is a horse with fewer hearts, not a sick one; it never reports a Condition and is never suppressed.

Deliberately health only

It moves no other number. Not speed, not jump, not size, not pull. The natural loci already own the trade-offs between the stats; this gene is a clean multiplier on one axis so that the five can be bred independently.

Verified

The multiplier lands on the attribute (2026-09-14, the yard’s MAGIC HEALTH pen, read all day): max health 27.0 and 27.4 against 22. Not verified: how it feels in play, the mirror allele, the config switch and inheritance (the body stats verification tab).

What needs verifying here

Magic health’s upward multiplier has been read off the attribute in the yard, but the Frail mirror, the Vmp allele’s fixed doubling and tripling, and the claim that health.mode = off leaves magical vigour alone have all never been in a world.

Still open (NOT played)

The MAGIC HEALTH pen settled the half that was hardest to see — the multiplier reaches MAX_HEALTH, and two horses read differently, so the magnitude really is per allele copy. Everything below is a genotype that pen does not hold, and every verdict is a number: spawn from the custom horse spawn egg and read /attribute <horse> minecraft:max_health base get, always against a plain horse of the same breed, because the natural loci settle the baseline first.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • The mirror allele takes hearts off. A Frail/Frail horse. Pass: clearly below the plain control — about four fifths of it — and never below half a heart, which is MIN_HEALTH’s job. A Frail horse reading the same as the control means only the positive direction is wired.
  • Vmp is fixed, and the same on every horse. Vmp/n should be exactly twice the control and Vmp/Vmp exactly three times it. Pass: both numbers land on the multiple, and two Vmp/Vmp horses read identically — unlike two Hardy/Hardy horses, which must not.
  • health.mode = off does not strip it. Set the server config to off, reload the world — each horse re-resolves its body once per level load — and read a Hardy/Hardy horse again. Pass: it keeps every extra heart, while a dwarf in the same world loses its disorder. This locus is not a HealthContribution, and the switch turning it off with the disorders is exactly the kind of failure nobody would notice for weeks.
  • The percentage is inherited, not re-rolled. Breed two Hardy/n horses whose own multipliers you have read. Pass: a Hardy/Hardy foal’s multiplier is close to the sum of its parents’ two single-copy ones, and two full siblings that inherited the same pair of copies read the same number. A fresh roll per foal would make the whole breeding project meaningless and would look perfectly healthy.
