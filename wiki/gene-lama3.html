LAMA3 (junctional epidermolysis bullosa, Saddlebred)

The same disorder as LAMC2, in a different gene and a different breed — and the best-measured disorder frequency in this mod.

Crossing two of them

The one number here that was not chosen

Almost every carrier rate in this mod’s health layer is a judgement call — picked so a wild horse is healthy and an inbred line is not. This one is not picked. A random 2007 foal-crop sample found 9 carriers in 175 foals, a mutant allele frequency of 0.026, and that is the figure the American Saddlebred ships with.

The word doing the work is random. Most disorder frequencies in horses come from animals tested because somebody suspected something, which inflates the number in ways nobody can measure afterwards. A random foal crop has no such bias, which makes this a better piece of evidence than most of what the rest of the health layer stands on.

No founder is ever affected

The founder table lists only the clear horse and the carrier. A wild-caught horse is an adult that survived, so it can carry this and never have it, and the homozygote’s weight is simply absent from the table. The only way to produce an affected foal is to breed two carriers.

Not interchangeable with LAMC2

A Saddlebred carrying this is clear of the draft variant, and vice versa. The two are separate loci that inherit independently, so breeding a carrier of one to a carrier of the other cannot produce an affected foal at all.

Two loci for one condition — the reasoning is on the sibling page

Why JEB is two genes here rather than one gene with two alleles is set out on LAMC2, along with what that buys a breeder. The short version: they are genuinely two different genes, and a locus with two variant alleles would wrongly make a horse carrying one of each an affected horse.

The same structure, a different chain

Laminin-332 is a trimer of three chains — alpha 3, beta 3 and gamma 2 — and it is the central anchoring protein holding the epidermis to the basement membrane beneath it. LAMC2 supplies the gamma chain; this locus supplies the alpha.

Break either and the same assembly fails, which is why the two produce a clinically indistinguishable disease: blistering and skin loss at every point of mechanical stress, and detachment of the hoof capsules, from birth. The mechanism is set out in more detail on the LAMC2 page and is not repeated here.

The variants themselves are not alike. The Saddlebred form is a partial deletion of LAMA3; the draft form is a separate mutation in a separate gene. Two horses can each be “a JEB carrier” and have nothing genetically in common beyond the sentence.

Why the breed societies test

Because the carrier is invisible and the homozygote is unsalvageable, the only useful intervention happens before the mating. An allele frequency of 0.026 sounds small, but it means roughly one Saddlebred in twenty carries a copy — so two carriers meeting is not a freak event in a population that breeds within itself, and one in four of their foals would be affected.

That is the same argument the mod makes with its own machinery: the carrier row costs nothing at all, and the entire cost of the locus sits behind a pairing. It is what makes a pedigree and a gene database entry worth keeping rather than flavour text.
