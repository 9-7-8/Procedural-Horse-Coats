Cream

The dose-dependent dilution: one Cr gives palomino / buckskin, two give cremello / perlino. Real-horse SLC45A2, so it is allelic with pearl — modelled here as two genes whose combined effect is computed once, in CreamPearlDilution.

The combined dose table

Both genes call the same resolver, and it reads both doses. Cream is the driver whenever a Cr is present; pearl drives only the no-cream double-pearl case. Either way CreamPearlDilution.apply runs at most once.

“Keep” values multiply the existing pigment; tint is the share of a texel’s black added back as red (see PigmentField.dilute). Red is always restricted harder than black, so a diluted bay’s body fades to cream while the points hold smoky colour — agouti still says where they are.

House rule, owner, 2026-09-01

No cream horse keeps a pitch-black point. A single-cream point may be a very dark brown but never a void — so single cream carries a full tint, not the token amount a real-world buckskin’s black points would argue for. Classic pearl gets sepia points, perlino rusty ones.

Why single cream touches black at all

A real single cream barely affects eumelanin. But bay never adds black anywhere — the points are just black it declined to restrict — so a genuine pigment dilution has to reach them too. That is a smoky / sooty buckskin, and it is much better than leaving them jet.

Buckskin, perlino and pearl bay all render brown / rusty / sepia points over their diluted bodies.

Not modelled yet

  • Blue eyes on the double dilutes — the classic hook, and the first thing that would make a gene read the cream/pearl dose for something other than pigment. Blocked on there being an eye-colour channel at all; CoatRegions.redrawEyes copies eye texels verbatim from the template.
  • The MATP merge. Cream and pearl being two genes makes Cr/Cr and prl/prl expressible, which is biologically impossible. The planned fix is one five-allele MATP locus (Cr, prl, sun, sno, wild), with this dose table moving onto it.
