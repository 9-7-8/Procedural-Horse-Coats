Breeding & pedigree

The single source of truth for the breeding, horse-record, pedigree and stat-inheritance system. Layer 1 (common/, pure Java) owns the rules; layer 2 (neoforge-26.1.2/) only translates — it never decides how codes combine, what an ancestor is, or the band a foal’s stats come from.

Breeding, as a player sees it

Feed a mare and a stallion the usual golden food and they make a foal, the vanilla way. What is different is what the foal is: every gene is inherited, one copy from each parent, so its coat, its eyes, its speed, health, jump and size all come from its parents rather than from a dice roll. Two horses of the same sex will not breed.

  • Stats are genetic. A fast pair tends to make fast foals, and the horse information screen colours each number green or red against the parents’.
  • Carriers are real. A plain-looking horse can hand on something it does not show; the Genes tab lists every locus a horse is carrying anything at.
  • Breed labels combine. Two of the same breed breed true, two breeds make a named cross, and anything messier is Mixed.
  • A pairing can fail. Some pairs share a hidden lethal: the mare miscarries and loses half a heart, with a line saying what it looked like but never which gene. Other foals are born with a disorder and die within seconds.
  • Magic turns up by itself, very occasionally. About one foal in a thousand is born carrying a magical gene neither parent had — a mutation, out of nowhere — and one in a million takes two copies. Nothing you feed or pair makes it likelier, and it only ever happens to a foal somebody bred: a horse the world spawned is exactly its breed.
  • Twins can be identical. About one twin pregnancy in ten is one egg that split, so the two foals are a single draw carried twice and are alike down to the last streak. See pregnancy.
  • Names follow the family. A pair’s first foal takes the dam’s first name and the sire’s last; later foals drift toward fresh names.
  • Everything is remembered. The family tree shows great-grandparents with their coats, and the Offspring tab reads the pedigree downward. Selling a horse does not take it out of the family: it stays where it was, greyed out, marked sold if it was one of yours, and still clickable.
  • A foal of your tamed mare is yours, tamed to you and starting at a quarter of her bond.

What “breeding” covers

  1. Genetic-code combination — two parents’ codes produce a child’s.
  2. Stat inheritance — a foal’s speed and health, rolled from the parents. Random for now; Mendelian later.
  3. The horse record — the per-horse data bag: identity, sex, name, genetic code, parents, stats.
  4. The ancestry database — a server-wide store of every record, queryable for a horse’s ancestors.
  5. Breed inheritance — a foal’s breed label: same-breed parents breed true, two breeds make a named cross, and messier pairings collapse to “Mixed”.
  6. Integration — attaching a record to a live Horse, persisting it, pushing stats onto attributes, syncing to clients, and the in-game surfaces.

Breed labels combine (BreedLineage.combine)

Every horse carries a breed token on its HorseRecord (breed, optional). A wild herd is a pure breed; a lone spawn is unknown. HorseBreedingHandler.applyBredFoal sets the foal’s from its two parents’:

  • Pure A × pure A → A.
  • Pure A × pure B → A × B cross (components sorted, so order never matters).
  • The same cross × itself, or a cross × one of its own two breeds → that same cross.
  • A cross × anything else (a third breed, a different cross) → Mixed. Mixed × anything → Mixed.
  • Feral Mixed — a lone wild spawn, a /summon, a spawn-egg horse — is absorbing, exactly like Mixed: any cross involving one produces a plain Mixed foal, feral × feral included. See why.
  • Spliced (A) — a line a gene splice carrot has been let into — is the pair {A, splice} and combines by the rules above with no exception: spliced A × A stays spliced A, spliced A × B is Mixed, and the mark never washes out. See spliced lines.

A pure breed also pins the five magical body-stat genes to the breed’s target bands, so record.traits() and HorseRecords.traitsOf resolve a Thoroughbred near ×2 speed and a Falabella near ×0.45 scale. A cross averages its two parents’ bands per axis (only where both pin it); Mixed / Feral Mixed pin nothing and fall back to the ordinary bounded-Gaussian draw. Full detail: the breeds page.

Layer 1 — the domain model

Genotype.breedWith(Genotype other, Rng)

Mendelian segregation: for each gene the child takes one allele from each parent, drawn 50/50 within that parent’s pair. That is 2 nextBoolean() per gene, genes in codeOrder() — 96 draws for the 48 built-in genes; true means that parent’s first allele.

Each resulting pair is put in AllelePair’s canonical slot order, so breedWith is symmetric: E/E-A/A-… × e/e-a/a-… always gives E/e-A/a-….

Sex-linked loci are the one exception

A gene that declares X_LINKED or Y_LINKED (the mode; today only brindle) does not get the sire’s copy by a coin flip — it is decided by the foal’s own sex. On an X-linked locus the sire gives his X-borne allele to a filly and his Y — i.e. nothing — to a colt; Y-linked is the mirror. The dam is unchanged in both. The sex locus is priority 1, so the foal’s sex is always already drawn by the time any other gene is reached.

The second coin is still flipped and thrown away. Two nextBoolean() per gene is an invariant a great deal leans on — the golden coats, the gamete-bias equivalence test, every claim that adding a gene shifts the random stream by a known amount — and a locus that quietly consumed one would make the stream depend on a foal’s sex. Which parent is the dam is read off the parents’ own sex loci, since breedWith is otherwise symmetric and nothing else has ever needed to know.

What the foal then looks like is its genes’ combination tables, nothing more: one W22 gives a white foal because every combination carrying it maps to KIT’s masking dominant-white expression, and one Hlr/n gives an ordinary-looking foal because that combination maps to a wild type. There is no dominance rule in the middle.

The homozygous-only magical loci

A single Hlr is an invisible carrier; only Hlr/Hlr shows, and milk, the LUT locus and the five magical body-stat genes are built the same way. Those are the genes you have to breed for rather than spot, and each page has the frequencies.

Seal brown has no allele — it is a high roll of bay’s epigenetic leg and face heights.

Genome.breedWith(Genome other, Rng) — the one a foal actually uses

A genotype says which alleles a horse carries. It does not say what those particular copies carry, and every copy carries a priority and a set of literal numbers — one per value the gene declares. Those live in an Epigenome, aligned slot-for-slot with the genotype’s pairs. Genome is the two together, and it exists because only a breeding pass that draws both at once can keep that alignment true.

Per gene, in codeOrder():

  1. One nextBoolean() picks which of this parent’s two copies is passed on, one more picks the other parent’s — the same 2 draws Genotype.breedWith makes, so the allele half is bit-for-bit unchanged.
  2. Each chosen allele arrives carrying that parent copy’s priority verbatim and its numbers nudged by drift. A foal that inherits its dam’s A inherits her bay shade — the polygenic half of how dark a bay she is — to well inside what anyone could see.
  3. The two copies are re-aligned to AllelePair’s canonical slot order; the epigenetics follow their own allele across the swap.
  4. Priority tie-break: if both copies arrived holding the same priority, one extra nextBoolean() bumps the second copy a single step up or down (AlleleEpigenetics.deconflict), clamped inside [1, MAX]. A horse never carries the same priority twice at one gene.
Two passes, and why

Pass 1 locks every allele — two nextBoolean() per gene, in a fixed order, whatever the carrots say. Pass 2 then does all the epigenetic work: drift, and the fresh roll a splice carrot’s substituted gamete needs. Nothing in pass 2 can move a foal’s genotype, which is the guarantee that matters.

There is no longer an exact draw count. It used to be “2 nextBoolean() per gene plus 1 per tie”, which let an unfed carrot be bit-for-bit a plain breeding; drift spends a couple of draws per stored value on top, so that guarantee cannot hold and is not claimed.

Genetic drift

Inheritance used to be exactly verbatim, which meant a lineage’s numbers were frozen the day its founder was caught and no amount of breeding could produce a horse outside what the wild population already contained. Every scalar now takes a tiny nudge on its way to a foal.

The magnitude is drawn from a two-sided exponential whose scale is EpiDrift.SCALE of the value’s design span — so the larger the drift, the exponentially less likely it is:

Of every scalar passed on…moves less than
half0.10% of its range
90%0.35%
99%0.70%
all but 1 in 10 0001.4%
all but 1 in 1 000 0002.1%

So the overwhelming majority of foals are, to the eye and to the stat readout, exactly their parents — “breeds true” here means imperceptible, not bit-identical — while the tail stays open. A horse carries a few hundred of these numbers, so roughly one foal in thirty has one value that moved a visible amount. Most pairs breed true; chaos stays possible.

Drift may leave the range founders are rolled in. A value is held only inside its hard safety bound — the one that stops a horse breaking the model — never inside its design range. That is deliberate: the design range is where wild horses start, not a ceiling a breeder can never pass. A line bred for three hundred generations is allowed to be somewhere no wild horse has ever been.

Noise seeds and categories do not nudge. There is no “slightly different” splash shape or body site — nudging either replaces the thing rather than adjusting it. Both instead get a EpiDrift.REPLACE_CHANCE chance per breeding of being re-rolled outright, so a lineage keeps its exact markings essentially forever and a foal that does change is a real event rather than a boundary accident.

Priority does not drift either — it is an ordinal deciding which copy expresses, and a horse whose expressed copy silently swapped between generations would be inexplicable.

What epigenetic priority is for

This is AlleleEpigenetics.priority — not Gene.priority(), which is the fixed per-gene constant Genes sorts codeOrder() on (so breeding rolls each gene in a stable order and a gene only ever sees lower-priority genes). When the two copies are different alleles the expressed one is the canonically first — an arbitrary but fixed choice, not a claim that one allele wins. When they are the same both express, so the tie goes to the higher epigenetic priority. That is its only job; it is a full-range int for headroom. It selects a copy, never an order.

Founders

A founder — wild spawn, /summon, a dimension pen horse — gets a fresh Epigenome.random: an independent priority and an independent roll of every declared value on every copy, from that value’s own declared distribution, deconflicted the same way. That is the only place epigenetics are ever invented. CoatGenerator.generate is the founder path, and a foal must not go through it. A horse of a breed goes through BreedFounder, which is the same path plus the breed’s stat targets written onto the copies — and the only place a breed ever touches a number.

Stats are inherited, not rolled

HorseStats.rollFoalStat is gone

A foal’s speed and health used to be drawn uniformly from [0.75 × min(parents), 1.5 × max(parents)] — an uncapped random walk with no genetics in it at all. Two full siblings could differ by a factor of two, and “breeding for speed” was breeding for luck. The class is deleted.

A horse’s body — speed, max health, jump strength, body size and the disorders it expresses — is now a pure function of its genotype, resolved by HorseTraits.resolve. So a foal inherits its stats the same way it inherits its colour: through the alleles, and nowhere else. There are no speed or health fields on HorseRecord at all, the same way there is no sex field — a stored derived value can only ever go stale against the alleles beside it.

Full machinery, the baselines, the per-gene weights and the two kinds of lethal: the horse’s body. What matters on this page is that breeding draws nothing extra — the same two nextBoolean() per gene that always decided the coat now decide the body too.

Breeding can now fail, or produce a foal that dies

Two of the new health loci change the shape of a pairing:

  • Embryonic lethal (MET, and the milk locus's incompatible humours): the Mendelian draw runs as usual, applyBredFoal reads the genotype it produced, returns false, and the caller cancels the BabyEntitySpawnEvent. No foal at all — the mare miscarries. Genotype.breedWith is untouched, so the odds stay the ordinary one-in-four for two carriers.
  • Lethal at birth (overo lethal white and four recessives): the foal is born, named, and filed in the pedigree, and then dies over a few seconds. The pedigree is fine with it — ancestorsOf already tolerates ancestors it cannot find.

Both are governed by the server’s health.mode setting, which never changes what a horse carries or passes on.

The miscarriage — and why it does not name the gene

An embryonic lethal used to be a silent non-event: two horses that would breed with anything except each other, for no visible reason, which is indistinguishable from a bug. It is now something that happens to the mare, in LethalFoalHandler.announceMiscarriage, and in every build — it is gameplay, not a diagnostic.

  • Half a heart off the dam, through the same genetic_defect damage type a lethal foal dies of. Enough to see on the health bar if the chat has scrolled away. It can kill a mare already on her last half heart, and that is left alone: breeding an animal that close to death is a decision.
  • A line to everyone within 32 blocks, not only to whoever held the wheat. The fact belongs to the pairing.
  • The line describes the sign, never the disorder. MiscarriageSigns holds one sentence per embryonic-lethal Condition, written so that two different lethals read differently without either naming a gene. That is the whole point: a pairing that comes to nothing is the only evidence a player ever gets that two of their horses share a recessive lethal, and printing the answer would retire the pedigree that exists to find it. A dozen pairings sorts a herd into two groups; nothing hands anyone the group's name.

A condition with no sign written for it falls back to a generic sentence rather than failing, so a new lethal gene works the day it is added — it simply reads like every other one until somebody writes it a line. The log always names the condition, because a dev reading a log is debugging rather than playing. Condition.description() also still names it, which is what the horse information screen shows for a horse you already have in front of you: an animal you can inspect tells you everything, an event you witnessed tells you what it looked like.

Every draw, written down (dev builds)

A Mendelian draw is invisible, and the only evidence afterwards is one foal — which is a single sample of a distribution. “Every foal came out chestnut” and “five foals happened to come out chestnut” look identical from the paddock. BreedingDebug.reportDraw runs on every pairing, before the viability check so a miscarriage still leaves a record, and writes:

  • to the log, always (whenever DebugAnnounce is on — which since 0.3.2 means the debug.announce server config, on in dev and off in a normal install): both parents' and the foal's short forms, the foal's full code, its four resolved body numbers and viability, every condition, and then BreedingReport.full — one line per registered gene giving dam A/B x sire C/D -> X/Y, whether that combination is new, what it expresses, and which parent supplied which copy where that can be said at all;
  • to chat, in a dev build only (!FMLEnvironment.isProduction()): the body numbers and BreedingReport.notable — the loci the foal expresses or landed on a combination neither parent had. Past a dozen of those it prints a count and points at the log.

BreedingReport lives in common/ and recomputes nothing: it reads the three genotypes that already exist, so it cannot disagree with what was actually bred. It deliberately does not claim a copy's parent when both parents could have supplied it — the draw records nothing about that, and a guess dressed as a fact is worse than a question mark.

Sex

MALE / FEMALE. Vanilla horses have no sex; this is mod-assigned, used to decide dam and sire, to gate breeding (one of each), and for display. Sex.label(boolean adult) gives the horse term: stallion / mare for adults, colt / filly for foals.

Sex is a gene, and the enum is derived

The enum is now a reading of the sex locus (horsegenetics.sex, X/Y, priority 1), not a stored field: HorseRecord has no sex component and the codec has no "sex" key. HorseRecord.sex() reads it out of geneticCode, via Genotype.sexOf(String) (which skips a full parse, because the info panel asks per frame).

So a foal’s sex is inherited, not rolled — the dam is X/X and gives an X, the sire is X/Y and gives one or the other 50/50, all of it through the same breedWith draw as every other gene. The only place sex is chosen is a founder, via Genotype.withSex(Sex).

ancestorsOf(id, depth)

A breadth-first walk up the mother/father links:

  • Nearest generation first — parents, then grandparents.
  • depth is how many generations: 1 = parents only. depth ≤ 0 returns empty.
  • The horse itself is never included.
  • Each ancestor appears at most once, even under inbreeding.
  • An ancestor referenced by id but absent from the database is skipped, and that branch is not explored further.

offspringCount(a, b)

Scans every stored record for one whose two parents are exactly a and b, order-independent. It fed the old foal-name schedule and is now a plain query on the database: nothing in the mod calls it, and the HorseRecords wrapper that existed only to reach it is gone with the schedule. It stays on HorseDatabase because “how many foals has this pair had” is a question the browser and the papers will want asked.

forget(id)

For a horse deleted outright rather than dying: the horse dimension clears its pens on exit, and without this every visit would leave hundreds of throwaway records in the save forever. It deliberately does not repair records naming the forgotten horse as a parent — ancestorsOf already skips ancestors it cannot find, so a foal bred in the dimension and brought home keeps working with a missing parent node.

Layer 2 — integration

data/HorseRecordCodecs

MapCodec / Codec for HorseRecord, kept out of the layer-1 type so it stays free of DataFixerUpper. NBT keys: id, first_name, last_name, barn_name, genetic_code, mother_id, father_id, tamed_by, bred_by, generation, speed, health, parent_stats.

There is no sex key any more: sex is a gene, so it is already inside genetic_code. The record has 14 components — still under RecordCodecBuilder.group’s limit of 16. Also exposes STREAM_CODEC / LIST_STREAM_CODEC for the sync payloads.

data/ModAttachments.HORSE_RECORD

A NeoForge Data Attachment whose value type is the layer-1 HorseRecord itself, registered with the map codec, so it persists in entity NBT with no hand-rolled save/load.

Its default is a sentinel: HorseRecord.founder(holder.getUUID(), FEMALE, "", "", "eeaa") — built via the AttachmentType.builder(Function<IAttachmentHolder, T>) overload so the default can read the holder’s own UUID. Its blank name is how the code distinguishes “never assigned” from a real record.

data/HorseAncestryData

extends SavedData implements HorseDatabase — a thin wrapper holding an InMemoryHorseDatabase. What it adds:

  • Dirty-tracking: record(...) only calls setDirty() when the record actually changed (HorseRecord is a value type, so an unchanged re-record compares equal and is a no-op).
  • Persistence: SavedDataType id horsegenetics:horse_ancestry.
  • Scope: server-global, not per-level — server.getDataStorage(), so a parent stays lookup-able even when unloaded, in another dimension, or dead.

Runtime flows

A. Wild spawn → founder record

HorseGeneticsEventHandler.onHorseJoin on EntityJoinLevelEvent (server side, any Horse) calls ensureRecordAndCoat(horse):

  1. rng from horse.getRandom().
  2. No real record → apply(horse, newFounder(...)): random genotype (the sex locus among it), generated name, no parents.
  3. Otherwise (bred or reloaded) → re-record into the global DB (idempotent) and re-set the custom name if missing.
  4. Every horse, every join → applyTraitsToEntity(...). Vanilla has just randomised this horse’s speed, health and jump; this is where that gets overwritten with what its alleles say. Doing it on every join rather than storing it is what lets a re-tuned gene, or a change to health.mode, reach horses that already exist. A newly founded horse is healed to its new max; a reloaded one is only clamped down.
  5. Coat: if the coat attachment is still UNASSIGNED, CoatGenerator.generate rolls a founder epigenome, stores {genotype code, epigenome code}, and sends the coat sync packet.

Step 4 means the coat is always derived from the record’s genetic code plus the persisted epigenome — a bred foal’s coat matches the genes it actually inherited and stays the same across reloads. The pixels are generated client-side; see the pipeline.

The horse-dimension pens pre-apply a founder record before adding the entity, so each pen ends up with exactly one mare and one stallion; onHorseJoin then sees a real record and only fills in the coat.

B. Real breeding → combined record

HorseBreedingHandler.onBabySpawn on BabyEntitySpawnEvent (fired before the foal is added to the world). This is golden-carrot breeding, the only path that makes a foal at once - and it always makes one. Two adults of opposite sex is the whole of it (owner): no cooldown, no taming, no full health, no gelding check, no subfertility roll, no heat cycle, and a pregnant mare breeds anyway - her pregnancy is untouched, because nothing here conceives. A parent armed with a breeding carrot no longer diverts to a pregnancy attempt either: the carrot’s bias is folded into this foal and spent on it. The one thing that can still stop a foal is the draw itself being an embryonic lethal, which is genetics rather than a gate. What follows is the instant foal:

  1. Require both parents and the child to be Horse.
  2. ensureParentRecord(parent) for each — a parent that predates the mod gets a founder record now, and its stats are backfilled from its entity so the roll has real numbers.
  3. Sex gate: same-sex parents → event.setCanceled(true), no foal.
  4. The FEMALE parent is the dam, the MALE the sire.
  5. childGenome = GeneticCodeCombiner.combine(genomeOf(dam), genomeOf(sire), rng). genomeOf reads the parent’s coat attachment; a parent whose attachment is missing or has drifted founds a fresh epigenome now and keeps it, so the foal still inherits real allele copies.
  6. childGeneration = 1 + max(dam, sire).
  7. Viability: the drawn genotype is resolved to Traits. An embryonic lethal stops here — applyBredFoal returns false and the event is cancelled, so there is no foal.
  8. Stats: nothing is rolled. ParentStats.of(damTraits, sireTraits) is captured so the UI can colour the foal’s numbers against its parents’.
  9. Name — see the schedule below.
  10. bredBy = the causing player’s username, if any.
  11. Auto-tame: if the dam is tamed, the foal is tamed to the dam’s owner, and tamedBy is that owner’s username.
  12. apply(child, record) then applyTraitsToEntity(child, childTraits, fullHeal=true) so the foal spawns at the max health its own genotype gives it — which for an affected foal is not many hearts.
  13. If the foal is lethal at birth, a chat line names the disorder. LethalFoalHandler does the rest.
  14. The foal’s coat attachment is written here, not at join time. It has to be: the inherited epigenetics can only be read while both parents are in hand, and flow A’s founder path would re-roll them. Flow A then sees an assigned attachment and leaves it alone.

Steps 5–7 are HorseBreedingHandler.applyBredFoal; steps 8–13 are populateFoal, which birth from a pregnancy shares (bornFromPregnancy). Every other path — the stallion seed jar, a carrot-armed pair, a stallion left with mares — draws the genome at conception through common.repro.Conception, stores it on the mare as an embryo, and finishes the foal at the due date. The jar passes a synthetic sire HorseRecord reconstructed from its stored_genome component (a GenomeSample + the donor’s UUID / name — his sex is in the stored genotype). Same Mendelian + carrier-faithful draw everywhere — no gamete is frozen at collection. See fertility & gestation.

The foal’s name

HorseNames.foal(dam, sire, foalSex, policy, generator, rng). A foal keeps one half of its name from a parent and rolls the other, every time. The default NamingPolicy inherits the last name and takes it down the same-sex line — a filly carries her dam’s, a colt his sire’s — so the surname reads as a family while the rolled first name is what keeps two foals of one pairing apart. The foal’s sex comes out of the same Mendelian draw as everything else, so the surname line is genetic too.

This replaced a schedule keyed on how many foals a pairing had already produced: foal 1 took dam.first + sire.last, foal 2 the other combination, foals 3–6 one parent half plus a rolled word, and foal 7 onward a wholly rolled name. It read well for a single pair and was wrong for a herd — the first two foals of every pairing are built only out of words already in use, so names collided constantly on the owner’s server and a surname meant nothing. Rolling one half every time puts the whole of one word table (the files HorseNameGenerator.fromResources() reads) between two full siblings, and leaves the inherited half to do the one job it is good at.

Whose rule, and where it lives

NamingPolicy is two choices — which half is inherited (LAST, FIRST) and which parent supplies it (BY_SEX, DAM, SIRE) — and it belongs to a player, not to a world. Two constraints fix where it can be stored, and between them they rule out both obvious homes:

  • Not a server config. On a shared server each player names their own horses their own way, and a server config holds one answer for everybody.
  • Not applied on the client. A foal is named on the server, at a due date that routinely arrives while its owner is offline and their client is not running.

So the client suggests and the server decides. The player sets naming.inheritedHalf and naming.parentSource in phc/client.toml; ClientLifecycleHandler sends both on every login as NamingPolicyPayload (enum names, not ordinals, so reordering an enum cannot relabel somebody’s setting); HorseNamingData, a server-global SavedData keyed by player UUID, files it. Nothing else names a foal. An unknown player is not an error anywhere on that path — policyFor(null), a player who has never sent one, and an unreadable enum name all answer NamingPolicy.DEFAULT, because naming a foal must not be a path that can fail.

The policy applied is the dam owner’s, not the breeder’s, and the two are not always the same player: a foal is born where its dam is, to whoever owns her, and that is whose herd the name has to sit in. The breeder is the fallback for a tamed stallion put to an unowned mare; a wild birth has neither and gets the default.

C. Paper inspector

Holding paper and right-clicking a horse dumps to chat: name, id, the horse term (stallion / mare / colt / filly), generation, genetic code, speed, health, jump and size — all resolved from the genotype — any conditions it expresses with their descriptions, a vs parents line (above both / between / below both) when parentStats is present, bred by, tamed by, sire and dam resolved to names, and ancestors (3 gen).

D. Name-tag rename

Right-clicking a horse with a real record with any name tag — enchanted, anvil-renamed, or plain — opens the rename window (client/HorseRenameScreen): two fields, first name and last name, prefilled with the horse’s current registered name. Confirming sends RenameHorsePayload; the server re-checks range, that a name tag is still in hand and that the two parts are not both blank, then applies the name and consumes one tag (unless creative). Cancelling consumes nothing. The interaction is cancelled on both sides so vanilla does not also set the entity’s custom name.

It paints its panel first and calls super last, which is not decoration. Screen’s default order draws the widgets and then hands over, and this window fills a 94%-opaque panel across its whole area — so in the default order both name boxes and both buttons were drawn and then painted over. They were still focused and still taking keystrokes; you simply could not see them, the caret, or the letters you typed, which is why testers reported a window they could neither type into nor leave (0.5.004; Esc did still close it). HorseInfoScreen and HorseBrowserScreen had already hit this and already inverted the order — this screen was the one left on the old one.

E. Barn name

A free-form display override, editable from the horse inventory screen (an edit box plus a “Set” button). The server checks the player is within 8 blocks, then sets it — blank clears, otherwise clamped to 16 characters. When set, it is what displayName() returns.

The box is offered only on Overview, and only for a horse you own: a field you can type in but not submit is worse than no field, and the server refuses a stranger's anyway. That ownership test is why nobody could set a barn name at all until 2026-09-24. It asked the entity — AbstractHorse.getOwnerReference() — and that is a server-only answer: defineSynchedData registers exactly one value on a horse, the flags byte behind isTamed(), and the owner lives in a plain field that only ever goes to NBT. On a client it is always null, so the test returned false for every player including the owner and the box and its button were hidden on every horse in the game. The feature had been built on 2026-09-07 and was unreachable for seventeen days, which is why the report against it said “still”.

The fix carries ownership where the client can see it. HorseRecord gained an Optional<UUID> ownerId and a ownedBy(viewer) test; the record already syncs per entity, so the screen has the answer before it draws. HorseOwnerTrackingHandler — the same 2-second pass that already existed to fill tamedBy late — mirrors vanilla's owner onto the record, and unlike tamedBy it reconciles on every pass rather than filling a blank once, because ownership moves: a transfer paper, a cowboy sale, or a horse going wild again all change it, and a mirror that only filled a blank would keep showing the old owner. The record stays a mirror, not a second authority — HorseOwnership.isOwner on the server is still the thing that refuses an action. The screen also re-applies its widgets when the answer changes, so a horse tamed or handed over with the screen already open shows the box without a tab switch.

Worth generalising: any client-side “is this horse mine” test has to read the record, never the entity. The one other caller, WhistleItem.ownedBy, is server-side and correct as written.

Confirmed working 2026-09-25, on the owner's own modded dedicated server — which is the environment that matters, the original bug having been in client-side state that a server round trip is the only honest test of. A barn name typed and set stuck. The erase path, the ownership gate's refusal on somebody else's horse, and the two-second mirror catching a horse tamed with the screen already open are still unwatched; they are on the Verification tab.

A horse you sold is greyed out, not gone

Selling has never removed a horse from a pedigree, and no code path ever did: a transfer paper sets the new owner and clears the brand, the cowboy's trade hands over a signed paper, and the record itself is only rewritten with a new ownerId. The one caller of HorseAncestryData.forget is the debug pen cleaner. What was missing was any sign of it on the chart, so a stable you had sold out of looked exactly like one you had not.

A box whose horse is owned by somebody who is not you now draws dim — darker panel, dim border, and a scrim over the inside of the box, because the coat comes from an entity renderer that takes no tint and would otherwise sit bright inside a grey frame. It keeps its place and stays clickable. The attribution line reads sold - by <you> when the horse is credited to you and owned by someone else.

The test is HorseRecord.ownedByAnother, and it is deliberately not !ownedBy: a founder, a wild ancestor and any horse whose owner was never mirrored are owned by nobody, and reading those as another player's would grey out most of a pedigree. HorseRecord.soldOnBy is the narrower question — bred or tamed by the viewer, owned by somebody else — since a stallion borrowed for one covering was never yours to sell. Ownership is a UUID and attribution is a username, so the two comparisons are made on different things by necessity. The family overview — every horse at once

One row per generation, founders along the top, a line from every horse down to every foal it got. Drag it about, scroll to zoom (about the cursor, so the horse you are pointing at stays put), Fit to put the whole thing back on screen.

  • The row is the horse's own generation, which HorseBreedingHandler sets to one more than the higher of its two parents. So a foal out of a founder mare by a fourth-generation stallion is a fifth-generation horse, sitting five rows down with one long line reaching up past four rows to its dam. That is what a line-bred family honestly looks like, and it is why this chart is a thicket rather than a pyramid. Generations nobody has a horse on are skipped rather than drawn as empty bands.
  • The gutter down the left is the generation axis, pinned to the window rather than drawn on the map — pan two thousand pixels sideways and you still want to know which row you are reading. Within a row, horses are in name order, tie-broken by id so nothing swaps places between two rebuilds.
  • Clicking a horse hides everything it has nothing to do with, leaving that horse, everything it produced, and whoever it produced them with. On a chart this crowded that is the only way to answer “who did this mare actually breed with”. Nothing moves when the focus changes — the horses keep their places, so the map does not jump under the cursor. Show everyone, on the right, puts the world back; so does a click on empty chart.
  • The dam's line is drawn in her colour and the sire's in his, which is most of what makes a crowded patch legible. The elbows are axis-aligned because fill is the only line a screen has — and because a thicket of diagonals would not read as a pedigree at all.
Light entries, not records

PopulationDataPayload sends a name, a sex, a generation and two parent ids per horse — not HorseRecords. The family tree and the Offspring tab send whole records because they draw each horse in its real coat, and a record carries a genetic code and an epigenome: thousands of characters of per-allele numbers. Affordable for a dozen horses, absurd for every horse a world has ever bred. The overview draws named boxes, so it is sent named boxes, and a horse worth knowing more about is one click from its own screen.

The cap is PopulationDataPayload.MAX_ENTRIES and the cut is made oldest first: entries are sorted by generation before it is applied, so what survives is a complete map from the founders down to wherever it stops rather than a scatter of horses whose parents are missing. The screen says so in its hint line instead of presenting part of a world as the whole of it.

Ancestor coats are the real ones

HorseRecord carries both the genetic code and the epigenome code, so a dead or never-loaded ancestor draws the coat it actually wore. There is deliberately no stand-in: a record without a genome draws a blank box rather than a plausible invention, because a pedigree showing a horse that never existed is worse than a pedigree with a gap in it.

Known limitations

  • No environmental noise on the stats. They are now purely genetic, so two horses with the same genotype are the same horse. That is deliberate; if the numbers ever feel too tidy, the place to reopen it is epigenetic variation (inherited with the allele, still deterministic), not a fresh die roll. See the horse’s body.
  • Drift is untuned against real play. The curve is what the owner specified and the distribution is unit-tested, but nobody has yet bred thirty generations of anything to see whether a line actually develops character at the rate it should. The two numbers to turn are EpiDrift.SCALE and EpiDrift.REPLACE_CHANCE.
  • No inbreeding prevention and no generational effects. Nothing computes how closely two horses are related, nothing warns, and no foal is born weaker for it. This is a decision and not an omission (owner, 2026-09-24): no mechanic anywhere in this mod may stop line breeding, in a wild band as much as in a paddock. There was one for nine days — a dispersing filly in BandLife refused a bachelor who shared her dam or her sire, so that her own brother could not recruit her — and it is gone; she takes the nearest bachelor there is, brother or not. Line breeding is a practice, not a mechanic, and what it costs a breeder is the recessives they did not want, nothing the game adds. Sex-linked inheritance, by contrast, is built: the sex locus and X- and Y-linked transmission both, the one exception to independent assortment. What is left there (the roadmap) is that a punnett square draws a sex-linked locus wrongly rather than refusing to.
  • Name-generation output is rough and slated for a rework. The rule — <alpha> <space> <beta> — is fixed.
  • HorseRecord.geneticCode and the coat attachment’s genotype code both hold the genotype string. The coat derives from the record so they stay consistent; the redundancy is a likely future consolidation.
  • ancestorsOf only walks records present in the database; a gap in the chain ends that branch.
  • The family tree needs a fresh server request per re-centre; there is no local caching of “the whole tree”.
Literal epigenetics and drift are the newest things here

Not seen in-game at all. Unit tests cover the mechanism — an inherited allele keeps its copy’s numbers to within one generation of drift, the epigenetics follow their allele when the pair is reordered, a tie gets deconflicted, and the drift distribution matches its declared curve. What is unproven is the live path: that a foal really looks like the parent it took the allele from, and that a cross of two extreme breeds really does spread rather than average. The checks are on the Verification tab.

The filter language: a SQL WHERE clause

Every horse search box in the mod is a SQL WHERE clause — My horses, Horse realm, the breeding pickers and the stasis bank, because all four run through HorseQuery. There is no SELECT or FROM: there is one table and you always want every column of it, so the ceremony would be something you typed into a one-line box before every search, for ever. The box is the clause. (Owner, 2026-09-26.) 

healer = hom AND flyer IN (het, hom)
breed LIKE 'Arab%' AND generation > 2 AND NOT lethal
mare AND (bond >= 50 OR tamed)

AND, OR, NOT and brackets, with SQL's precedence (NOT tightest, then AND, then OR); = != <> > >= < <=, IN (a, b) and LIKE '%x%'. Keywords fold case; quote a word to use one as a value (name = 'or').

Everything written before this still works, because it is a subset

Two terms side by side are implicitly ANDed, which is exactly what the box did when it had no operators — so mare gen>2 -lethal still means what it always meant. -term survives as shorthand for NOT term (owner's call) and : as a synonym for =. That is what let a parser go in under a screenful of existing tests without changing one of them: HorseQueryTest is unaltered and still green, and QueryExprTest is the grammar laid on top.

A gene name is a column, which is the part that is new

healer = hom. Before this there was no way to ask about zygosity without already knowing the answer: you could write genotype:Fly/Fly, which requires you to know and spell both allele tokens, so you could only ask it about genes you had already looked up. hom, het, none and any ask it by gene name, and any is the old gene: key exactly.

hom counts real alleles, not slots. The reserved slot in a stallion's X-linked pair is not an allele he has, so comparing the two slots directly with AllelePair.homozygous() would read every stallion alive as homozygous at every sex-linked locus — the same trap carries already carried a comment about.

It never throws. A search box is typed one character at a time, so every prefix of every valid query is a query somebody is one keystroke away from having — a dangling AND, an unclosed bracket, an operator with nothing after it. All of them parse to something that matches nothing, and a dangling AND deliberately keeps filtering on what came before it, because a table that empties itself the moment you type AND reads as broken. QueryExprTest walks every prefix of a real query to pin it.

Saved searches

Three keys ask three different questions about one locus, and a breeder wants all three:

  • gene:e (alias carries:) — the horse holds a copy of that allele, carrier and homozygote alike. Also takes a gene name or key, meaning “anything off-baseline here”.
  • genotype:E/e (alias pair:) — the pair is exactly those two, either way round, written the way AllelePair.toTokens() writes it. This is the question the other two cannot ask: gene:e is every horse with a red copy, genotype:E/e the carriers only, genotype:e/e the chestnuts only.
  • expresses:chestnut — the resolved Expression, so what the horse actually shows rather than what it holds.

Allele tokens are case-sensitive; nothing else is. Extension’s alleles are E and e, and agouti’s A and a — at those loci case is the allele. Until 2026-09-24 HorseQuery.terms() lower-cased every term before it compared and carries() matched with equalsIgnoreCase, so gene:e quietly meant “carries E or e” — which is every horse alive — and no combination could be spelled at all. Case now survives terms() and the folding happens at each point of use instead: names, breeds, coats, flags, gene names and the keys themselves still ignore case, so MARE, BREED:Shir and gene:EXTENSION all work, while gene:E and gene:e are two different questions. HorseQueryTest pins both halves.

A malformed term matches nothing rather than throwing — genotype:E/ is a half-typed term in a box that re-filters every keystroke, not an error, and an exception into a render loop is not an option.

How horses actually reproduce

The two sexes run on completely different clocks. Mares cycle seasonally and have a short fertile window; stallions are fertile all year. And a mare can conceive again while still nursing, often within about a week of foaling.

Mares: a 21-day cycle, in season

Mares are long-day seasonal breeders: they begin cycling as spring days lengthen, cycle through summer, and mostly stop in winter, so that an eleven-month pregnancy lands a foal in the next spring.

Estrus (“heat”)2–8 daysA follicle matures; ovulation near the endReceptive; most fertile in the last day or two
Diestrus14–16 daysProgesterone from the corpus luteumNo

A mare in heat approaches the stallion, raises her tail, “winks”, urinates in small amounts and stands to be mounted. Those signs mean receptive, not ovulating — breeders confirm timing with ultrasound.

Stallions: no cycle

A mature, sound stallion can breed year-round. He is still somewhat seasonal — semen output and libido peak in the long days and dip in winter — but it is a quality modifier, not an on/off state. Fertility also depends on semen quality and on how often he is used.

Pregnancy, foal heat and nursing

  • Gestation averages about 340 days, commonly 320 to 370. Almost always one foal; twins are high-risk.
  • Foal heat — the first estrus after birth — starts about 5 to 12 days after foaling, with ovulation around day 7 to 10.
  • Nursing does not prevent conception. A healthy mare can foal in spring, conceive on foal heat, and spend most of the next year both pregnant and nursing. That is how annual foaling happens.
  • Breeders sometimes skip foal heat after a hard birth, a retained placenta, infection, poor body condition or an older mare — because of the uterus’s recovery and her condition, not because of the milk.

The problem with putting this in a game

Vanilla Minecraft produces a foal the instant two horses are fed. A realistic model separates courtship, mating, conception, pregnancy and birth — which is also what would let a foal’s genes be drawn at birth from a stored sire. The hard part is time:

  • Straight compression breaks the short stages. Squeeze a 340-day pregnancy into one Minecraft day (20 minutes) and the whole 21-day cycle becomes 74 seconds, with heat lasting 18 seconds — accurate and unplayable.
  • The usual fix is a floor. Scale every stage from one gestation setting, then clamp each independent stage (heat, diestrus, recovery, foal heat, lactation) to a minimum such as one Minecraft day. The proportions come back as the gestation setting grows.
  • Nested windows are not stages. Peak fertility is the end of heat, not a day after it, and a cycle is heat plus diestrus — clamping those separately double-counts.
  • Lactation is a condition, not a lock. The biologically honest version lets a nursing mare conceive and charges her body condition for it instead.
  • Clock choice matters. Counting world ticks means sleep does not skip a pregnancy; counting world days means it does. A hybrid (pregnancy follows world time, heat follows active ticks) stops a player sleeping through every breeding window.
Most of this is built now

Heat, natural covering, conception odds that vary by phase, pregnancy, twins, foal heat and nursing are all implemented — common/repro and server/ReproHandler — and are described on fertility & gestation. Golden-carrot breeding still exists alongside them, is still an instant foal, and is now the deliberate override: it always works, whatever the cycle says and whatever either parent is carrying.

What is not built is the list immediately above this note: a body condition model, a real lactation lock, and the clock-choice question.

Two things here do, and neither can be seen in a single-player test of one pairing: that the policy reaching the naming call is the dam owner's, and that two players on one server keep different ones. The mechanism is on the Coding tab.

  • The default, read off a herd rather than a pair. Breed one pair three or four times over. Every filly should carry her dam's last name and every colt his sire's, and no two foals should share a full name — that second half is the whole point of the change, and it is what the old schedule failed at only once a herd existed.
  • Both settings do what they say. In phc/client.toml set naming.parentSource = DAM, rejoin, breed: colts now take the dam's last name too. Then naming.inheritedHalf = FIRST: the foal keeps the parent's first name and the last name is rolled.
  • It has to be a rejoin. Editing the file mid-session must change nothing — the server holds the copy that names foals and is only told on login. If a live edit takes effect, the policy is being read on the wrong side.
  • Two players, two policies. The real check, and it needs a second client on a dedicated server. Give each player a different naming.parentSource and let both breed their own mares at once: each player's foals follow that player's setting, and neither setting touches the other's herd.
  • Born while the owner is offline. Put a mare in foal with a breeding carrot, log out, let the gestation come due, log back in. The foal should be named to the owner's policy, not the default — this is the case that the whole HorseNamingData detour exists for, and the one a client-side setting would silently get wrong.
  • A wild birth still gets a name. A foal born to an unowned mare in a wild band takes the default policy and nothing throws.

Open, found 2026-09-14: the lethal ratio pens draw far too few, then far too many, homozygous foals, one run at a time. RATIO HYPP and RATIO LETHAL WHITE are carrier-by-carrier crosses and should throw one lethal foal in four. The 17:36 run threw too many (HYPP 10 H/H in 20). Ruled out: the parents (every run's stud and mare were the pen's own H/N and O/N founders, and every foal's father is the pen's stud); the tallies (they equal the foaled lines); early losses (none in either pen, either run); carrot bias (none armed, and an empty list folds to GameteBias.NONE); the birth path (bornFromPregnancy carries the stored embryo straight through); a stuck sire coin (foal sex ratios are not consistently skewed); and the draw and generator offline. 40 000 draws a case through the real Genome.breedWith on the yard's founders gave 25 : 50 : 25 from one stream and after a passing conception roll at chances from 0.46 to 0.85. 400 simulated runs with the generator jumped a fixed distance between conceptions spread exactly as binomial.
