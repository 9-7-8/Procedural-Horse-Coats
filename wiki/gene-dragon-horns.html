Magical gene / recessive / grows geometry, paints nothing

Dragon horns

Two copies and the horse grows a pair of horns behind its ears, swept back over the neck. Whether they curve, stand straight or curl, and how long, thick, swept and splayed they are, is written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, dragon horn colour. The first of the head parts, and the fourth grown part after the unicorn horn, antlers and ram horns.

Crossing two of them

Two copies, either sex

Recessive, like the unicorn horn: Drg/Drg grows the pair, and a Drg/n carrier looks like any other horse. Two carriers bred together are the reliable way to get one. It is not sex-limited — mares and stallions grow them alike, unlike the stallion-only antler and ram horn alleles. Foals grow none; the pair comes in when the horse grows up, and stays.

Finding them

Rare in the wild — about one horse in four hundred has a pair, and about one in ten carries a copy without showing it. No breed carries them yet.

What varies

  • Form — swept (raked back, curving along the neck), straight (an angled spike) or curled (a ram-style curl that opens outward).
  • Length — five classes, budding to great; budding to long in the wild, and a line bred for length reaches great and stops.
  • Sweep — how far a swept horn bends back (from still pointing up to tipping down past the neck), how far a spike leans, how far round a curl goes.
  • Splay — how wide the pair angle out from the skull.
  • Girth — thicker or thinner.

Both horns of a pair are the same: the numbers come from one copy. Everything stacks — a horse can wear a unicorn horn, antlers, ram’s horns and dragon horns at once, each at its own place on the head.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no attack, no hitbox. It is a look.

The ram horn pattern, with the unicorn’s inheritance

Slice A of the head parts treatment: one part end to end, to settle the pattern the other five head parts reuse. Two kinds, one mesh per side (a mirrored copy by negative scale turns culled boxes inside out); a new pair of anchors, NAPE_RIGHT and NAPE_LEFT, with one offset per horse model in AttachedPartLayer.anchor; DragonHornGenerator and DragonHornSize beside the ram horn’s; and one line in GrownParts.of, which dresses the pair with its colour locus. No new layer, render-state field or config key. The granting locus is in GrownParts.shapes’s every-part baseline, so the editors see the colour locus as changing the look.

The epigenome, by cost

  • length: the class is the mesh’s size bucket (five; a longer horn is more segments), the remainder a uniform stretch, the ram horn’s rule.
  • form, sweep and splay: all baked into the mesh — DragonHornGenerator.style(form, sweep, splay), three forms by four sweep buckets by three splay buckets. Splay is baked rather than a transform because the layer’s only free rotation is the lean about x; an outward angle on a pair needs a z rotation that differs by side, and adding one was not worth a new field for a number that drifts slowly.
  • girth: a draw-time scale across the horn.

Thirty-six styles by five classes by two sides is 360 meshes at most, each at most DragonHornGenerator.MAX_NODES boxes, inside PartGeneratorTest’s box budget; baked lazily, so a world with no dragon horns bakes none.

The nape anchor

Head-local, on the adult: x ±2.6 (just outside the ear, which spans x 0.55..2.55), y -9.5, z 4.5 (half a unit inside the back of the skull). That keeps the root two units behind the antlers’ CROWN root and well behind the horn’s FOREHEAD. The foal’s offset exists because the switch must answer for every anchor, and is never drawn.

Invented

No horse, and no real animal, has a locus that grows horns behind the ears; this is fantasy, and makes no claim about any real gene. The inheritance is the unicorn horn’s on purpose — rare and recessive — so that a pair is something a breeder works toward by crossing carriers, rather than something that turns up in every second foal.

What needs verifying here

No dragon horn has been seen in a running game: where the nape anchor puts the root, and how each form, sweep and size reads against the ears and neck, are unchecked.

Where the pair roots, and whether it clears the head

The nape anchor was placed from the vanilla head and ear boxes, not by eye. Spawn a Drg/Drg adult and look from the side and from behind: the roots should sit behind and just outside the ears, clear of an antler rack’s roots. Check every form at standard and great length, walking, grazing and rearing: a great swept horn at the highest sweep tips down past the crest and may cut into the neck or the mane; a curl may cut the neck. Clipping at the extremes is accepted (owner); clipping at the defaults is not.

All four parts at once

A horse with a unicorn horn, antlers, ram’s horns and dragon horns: each should sit at its own place. The ram’s horns share the antlers’ crown anchor, so the crowded one is crown against nape.

The foal gate and the size gene

A Drg/Drg foal should be bare until it grows up. A tiny and a huge adult should carry horns scaled with them — the size gene scales parts through the pose stack, a claim from reading the code.
