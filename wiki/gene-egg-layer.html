Magical gene / no coat effect

Egg layer

The horse lays. An egg, at first — but the locus is built to take an allele for any item in the game, and the egg is only the one everybody thinks of first. It drops on the ground where the horse is standing, on a timer.

Built, and not yet seen in game

The gene, its inheritance and its founder table exist and are covered by the test suite; the translator that turns it into behaviour was written against the 26.1.2 sources and is flagged unverified in its own comments. Nothing on this page has been watched happening to a horse. What to look at, and what is most likely to be wrong, is on the Verification tab.

One of the alleles is worth having

Eight of the items are things a chicken could be jealous of — an egg, a feather, wool, a slime ball, an ink sac, string, a bone, leather. The ninth is an ender pearl, and it is the reason the Enderpony is worth catching: every horse of that breed is Endp/Endp and lays them in a paddock for nothing. It is in the wild carrier spread with the other eight, so a pair of ordinary carriers from opposite ends of a world can also throw a pearl layer — the breed is the reliable way to one, not the only way.

The third way a horse gives you something

Item drop is what it leaves when it dies. Milk and potion milk are what it gives when you ask. This is what it produces while you are not watching, and it is the only one of the three that needs nothing from you at all.

Health

None. It lays whether or not it is well, tamed, or paying attention.

Item entities accumulate, and that is a real server cost

A timer that drops an item and never checks is the classic way to fill a chunk with entities. Skip the drop when enough of that item is already lying nearby, and never extend the despawn timer on what it produces. A pasture of layers left running overnight is the case to design for, not a single horse.

Open item ids mean a diamond-laying horse is expressible

The locus deliberately accepts any item id. That is the right call for expressiveness and it puts the balance burden entirely on the founder table, so what the wild may roll is a design decision every time an allele is added — not something the format decides for you.

The rule used to be absolute: nothing worth having was rollable at all, and anything valuable had to come through the splice layer. The ender pearl is a deliberate exception to it (owner's call, 2026-09-24), because the Enderpony is a whole breed built on laying pearls and the allele had to exist somewhere. It sits in the ordinary carrier spread, which is what makes a pearl layer breedable without ever meeting an Enderpony. Nothing further up the value scale has been let in, and the next one is the same decision again rather than a precedent already set.

Unknown ids do nothing, loudly once

Following lycan: an item id this game version has never heard of simply never lays. Resolve against the live registry, warn once through warnUntranslated, and never log per firing.

The new verb, and why it is shaped like this

produce completes a pattern that was already two thirds built: item drop produces on death, yield produces on interaction, and this produces on a clock. That symmetry is the argument that it is a real verb rather than a special case — it is the missing corner of a table, not a new table.

On the ground, not into a chest

Laying into the horse’s inventory was considered. It is tidier and it makes the accumulation hazard disappear, and it was rejected because it silently does nothing on a horse with no chest — and a gene you cannot see working is the failure mode this mod already tracks.

Verified in the test yard

The despawn timer bounds the eggs; nearby_cap never fires (overnight 2026-09-14 to 15, unattended). The EGG LAYER pen packs four layers of one allele into a corner, so their six-block circles overlap and the pen total is the local count. It never had more than 5 eggs on the ground against a cap of 8, and read 1 to 4 most hours. A copy's interval is 4 000 to 14 000 ticks and a dropped item despawns at 6 000, so the floor clears faster than the layers fill it. The night of 2026-09-13 agreed: a peak of 5, and all 57 eggs removed had despawned. Nothing further needs bounding. The cap stays as a guard for an interval short enough to outpace the despawn.

A measuring trap: the first attempt used eight layers spread over nineteen blocks, which read 3 to 11 items. That says nothing about the cap, because the cap is counted per laying horse and eight horses that far apart each have their own neighbourhood. Clustering the layers is what made the pen readable.

Opening the vocabulary

Every other effect that names a thing — item_drop, spread — uses a closed vocabulary, deliberately, so the judgement about what a word means stays with the translator that knows the game. This locus is the first to take raw ids instead, and the precedent that made it safe is lycan, which has stored mob ids as plain strings since it shipped. The rule that makes it work is the same one: an id the game does not know does nothing, quietly.

What needs verifying here

Egg layer is confirmed laying and confirmed bounded, but only for the egg and only for the matched pair: nobody has checked that a mismatched pair and a single copy lay nothing, that an allele other than the egg lays its own item, or that the new ender pearl allele the Enderpony is built on lays anything at all.

Still open (NOT played)

What the overnight pens settled is on the Coding tab: the horses lay, the despawn timer bounds the eggs, and nearby_cap never has to fire. What they did not test is the locus — they were four horses of one allele, all expressing. Every check below is about the combinations that must produce nothing, which is the half that fails without telling anybody.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • A mismatched pair lays nothing. Spawn a horse with two different laying alleles — say egg and leather — from the custom horse spawn egg, and leave it on a swept floor for an hour of game time. Pass: the ground stays empty, and its gene row reads the mismatched expression rather than either allele. This is most crosses between two carriers, so it is the common case and not the corner.
  • One copy lays nothing. The same, with an Egg/n horse. Pass: nothing on the ground and a carrier row.
  • An allele that is not the egg lays its own item. Spawn a matched pair of one of the other alleles — leather or slime ball, say — and wait out one interval. Pass: that item appears, not eggs. The item id is resolved against the live registry per allele and only the egg has ever been watched working; a wrong id warns once through warnUntranslated and then lays nothing for ever.
  • The ender pearl allele lays a pearl. Endp/Endp is the newest allele and the only one anybody has a reason to breed for, so it is the one a wrong id would be most embarrassing on. Spawn one from the custom horse spawn egg and wait out an interval. Pass: an ender pearl on the floor, and nothing in the log from warnUntranslated. Then do the same by catching an Enderpony, which is the route a player takes.
  • No wild horse lays, and an Enderpony does. The founder table for a matched-pair locus puts carriers in the wild and never the pair, but a breed pool overrides the founder table and the Enderpony's names Endp/Endp. Pass: over a lot of ordinary wild horses you find carriers at roughly EggLayerGene.WILD_CARRIER_PERCENT spread over the alleles and never one that drops anything — while every wild Enderpony does. The first layer in a world is either one somebody bred or one somebody caught.
  • Two layers in one pen keep their own clocks. The interval is written on the allele copy, so two expressing horses should not lay in step. Pass: over a night their drops are not simultaneous, and a foal bred from two layers lays at a rate that is neither parent’s exactly — which is the only thing the epigenetic range is for and the only part of it still unread.
