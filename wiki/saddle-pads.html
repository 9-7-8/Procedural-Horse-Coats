Saddle pads

A fitted pad beneath the saddle whose surface the player designs from a banner. The planned editor should control not only the pattern but how much of the horse the pad covers, then let the player zoom, centre and tile a banner across that area. None of this page is built yet; it is the single home for the saddle-pad plan.

What is still planned here

A fitted saddle pad with a banner-to-saddlepad editor that controls coverage, zoom, centring and tiling, then saves reusable designs to apply across a player's horses.

The pad

The pad rides its own horse-gear attachment rather than consuming an equipment slot. Vanilla gives an animal only BODY and SADDLE, and armour already needs one of those, so the pad must coexist with armour without replacing it. It gets one render layer over the horse body and does not need a hand-modelled mesh: the first proof can reuse the coverage and alpha-cutout approach of the vanilla horse-armour layer.

Author the pad at maximum coverage, then store the chosen bounds with the design. A small saddle pad and a full caparison are therefore the same item at different coverage settings, rather than two unrelated item types. Foals do not wear pads until an adult layout exists; their UV layout is different and should not be quietly stretched from the adult one.

The banner-to-saddlepad editor

The planned UI is a dedicated editor opened from the equestrian bench or the pad itself. Its main preview is the pad's banner area, with controls that make the result legible before it is applied to a horse:

  • Shape and coverage: choose the pad's outline and resize it in each direction to decide whether it covers only the saddle area, extends down the flank, or becomes a larger caparison.
  • Zoom: magnify or reduce the banner beneath that outline without changing the pad's coverage.
  • Centre and position: move the banner horizontally and vertically so the chosen motif lands where the player intends on the pad.
  • Tile: repeat the banner across the pad when one crop is not enough, independently of the pad's shape and size.
  • Apply and reuse: save the finished design as a named, portable design that can be duplicated across all of the player's horses, rather than rebuilding the same pad one horse at a time.

The saved design needs to contain the pad shape and coverage, the banner source, its two-axis offset and scale, and the tiling choice. It should be an ordinary serializable design value, not client-only GUI state. Applying it to another horse copies the design data; it does not make the horses share mutable state, so editing one horse's pad cannot unexpectedly repaint every other horse.

Making and rendering it

The Tack Dyeing Bench already owns the dyeing half; the pad editor is the unbuilt half. The banner pattern should resolve to the same ordinary resource image used by the existing banner rendering path, then composite as base × base colour followed by each pattern layer and colour. The editor preview and the horse render must consume the same saved values, so a design cannot look one way in the bench and another way on the horse.

The first implementation should preview the transformed banner in the editor before tackling a live 3D horse preview. If a horse preview is added, dragging must not rebake a genetic coat every frame: preview the transform over an existing sheet and bake on release, or only re-composite when the pattern set changes. The existing texture cache, texel budget and reload invalidation are the machinery to connect rather than a second rendering pipeline.

Attachment, slot and failure edges

  • Keep the pad in the horse gear attachment as the authority and copy item/design data on every write. Do not mirror it into a widened horse container where hoppers or shared quickMoveStack ranges could reach it.
  • Expose it beside saddle and armour where players already look for tack, but do not put it on EquipmentSlot.SADDLE. The bareback steering path writes that slot and can reclaim stacks carrying its phantom-saddle marker.
  • Consume the source banner when making a design. Washing a pad may return it to a blank design, but it should not silently make the banner free.
  • Keep saved designs portable between a player's horses while retaining each horse's own copied instance, and reject adult pads on foals until a foal-specific layout is designed.
