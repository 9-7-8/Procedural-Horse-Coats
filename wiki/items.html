Gameplay / items

Items & recipes

Every item the mod adds, and where to read about it. They live in three creative tabs — Horse Genetics, then Horse Breeds for the one spawn egg per breed, then Horse Carts — and nearly all of them are built on one material: horse hair, which you get by shearing a horse.

The items

ItemWhat it is for
Horse hair The material everything else is made from, taken raw by every recipe that wants it. Shear a horse for it.
Breeding carrots — stabilizer, magnifier, epigenetic splice, gene splice Fed to a parent before breeding; changes what it passes on to the foal.
Splice carrots — known, unknown, and five themed Add a gene to a foal, either one you chose or one at random.
Research papers One gene and one allele pair — Agouti: A/a. Spend it on a splice carrot and that is the pair a foal gets.
Equine Research Shelf Files research papers — one per allele pair — and copies them onto blank books, for as long as you keep the original.
Tack Dyeing Bench Dyes a saddle’s seat, bridle and metal fittings separately — a dye in each leather row, a material in the fittings row. Any dye, and any ingot or gem, including ones another mod added.
Seed jars Store a stallion’s genetics and breed a mare with him later.
Vet’s kit Geld your stallion, or examine a mare’s heat and pregnancy.
Whistles — basic, golden, echo, ender Call every horse you own nearby — or, with an ender whistle bound to one horse, call that horse from anywhere.
Double gates — one per wood, including other mods’ woods A fence gate two blocks wide, both leaves on one click. The doorway you can lead a horse through rather than shove it through.
Jumps — one item, in any wood including modded ones A showjumping fence in three styles — vertical, oxer, crossrails. Side by side to widen it, stacked to raise it; it stops a horse like a fence does, and every block you add is a block more clearance asked for. The wood is carried on the item rather than being an item of its own, and the rails and the uprights can differ — both are set in the jump’s screen.
Modded horse armour — one per metal another mod added A horse armour in that metal, tinted its own colour. You get them by installing a mod that adds an ingot or a gem; nothing here ships one.
Stall signs Mark a room as one horse’s stall.
Tickets — four tiers Send a horse to its stall. One use; the tier is how far it reaches.
Holding pen sign & ticket Your one general pen: send any horse you own there, stall or no stall.
Transfer papers How a horse changes hands. A signed one is drawn as the horse itself.
The four posts — leatherworker’s, scientist’s, supplier’s, metalsmith’s Makes a villager into that one of the four equestrians. One post, one trade, one villager.
Cowboy Hitch The posts’ fifth twin: makes a villager into a cowboy. Written up with the villagers.
Spawn eggs — custom, breed, preset Build a horse gene by gene, get a foundation horse of a breed, or keep an exact horse in an item.
Stasis chambers — four tiers Put one of your horses inside and it stops being an entity at all — no ticking, no hunger, no growing up, no cost to the server — until you let it out again, unchanged. Reusable. The tiers buy what the bank may do with a stored horse, not the storing itself: a Basic is only kept, an Intermediate is searchable and mended, an Advanced has its drops collected, and a Spacer can be put to stud without leaving the bank.
Emergency Horse Stasis Chamber A Basic chamber surrounded by eight ender pearls, and the only item here you never use. Carry it and every horse you own is insured: the instant one falls below a tenth of its health it is in the bottle, wherever you and it happen to be, and nothing can hurt it in there. One chamber is one life — a second horse is refused out loud, and the answer is to carry two. A spare empty one filed in a stasis bank you placed insures your horses too.
Horse Stasis Bank A chest that takes stasis chambers and nothing else — any tier, full or empty — so the horses you have shelved live somewhere you can find them. Six rows of nine, and the title bar counts the horses in it. Three tabs: the grid, a searchable list of the horses inside, and a supply room that feeds and mends them, collects what they drop, and breeds a pair you turn out at stud.
Rescuing braid Any wool, a horse hair and an ender pearl, worked into a horse's mane or tail. It breaks the instant that horse would die and puts it — and you, if you were riding — back in its own stall, bolting. One use per braid, and a horse has two hair slots. Dyed like leather armour.
Two rows of that table are not a fixed length

The double gates and the modded horse armours are generated from whatever other mods are installed, so the roster you actually have is longer than the one above in exactly the measure your pack is. Vanilla’s twelve woods ship as files; every other wood, and every ingot or gem any mod added, is found by reading those mods’ jars at startup and written into a pack in .minecraft/phc/generated/. With no such mods installed — the common case — nothing is generated and no folder is left behind. The whole mechanism, and what about it is still unproven, is on the compatibility page.

House rules for every recipe

Three things that are true of all of them
  • Shapeless, with exactly one exception: the blank transfer paper is a ring of eight paper around a horse hair, and the ring is the idea. Everywhere else the shapes are not the puzzle.
  • At least one modded ingredient in every recipe, and every output is a modded item, so nothing can collide with a vanilla or third-party recipe. That holds for the generated ones too — a modded metal’s armour takes a horse hair for its padding, and outputs an item in this mod’s own namespace, which is what keeps it off BHAR’s recipe space and everyone else’s. The one exception is the double fence gates, which are two vanilla gates and nothing else — see below.
  • Horse hair goes in raw. There is no material chain to climb: whatever a recipe wants hair for, it takes horse hair. Nothing is a trap because there is nothing to convert and no conversion to undo.

The double gates are outside the modded-ingredient rule

Every other recipe in the mod carries something of ours. A double fence gate is two vanilla fence gates of the wood, shapeless, and that is the whole recipe. It took a braided rope for the hinge until the rope was removed; the owner’s call was that a gate should not want horse hair at all, and a fence line is the one thing you build dozens of.

Why the risk is judged acceptable: nothing in vanilla consumes a fence gate — every vanilla recipe that mentions one produces it — so there is no collision to have today. What remains is a third-party mod shipping its own two-gate shapeless recipe, which would make one of the two uncraftable with nothing logged. check-recipes.mjs reads only this mod’s folder and cannot see it. If a modded pack ever reports a double gate that will not craft, this is the first place to look.

What does not work yet

Worth knowing before you spend materials on one:

  • Tickets and stalls shipped together in 0.3.7 and neither has been played.
  • A sheared horse still looks unsheared, so you cannot tell by eye which of your horses you have already done today.

Tack: the saddle is dyeable

A saddle carries three colours — seat, bridle and metal — and an undyed one looks exactly as it always has. That second half is the point: the change reaches donkeys, mules, skeleton horses and zombie horses too, and none of them should look any different from the day before it landed.

Vanilla does not tint saddles — it builds the saddle’s equipment asset with the plain, non-dyeable layer, which is why a dyed-saddle trade was written and withdrawn before this existed. This mod ships its own asset in the vanilla pattern instead: three disjoint layers, each taking its own colour, so the hardware can stay metal whatever the leather does or be dyed with it. Vanilla’s dyed_color could not have done it at any number of layers — it holds one value per stack and is read once, outside the layer loop — so the three live in the mod’s own tack_tint component instead. That is also what brought the trade back: the leatherworker’s dyed tack writes the mod’s own three-zone tack_tint rather than vanilla’s dyed_color, so it reaches the saddle and the fittings both.

The textures are derived, not drawn

Tinting multiplies, so a brown base has no headroom and most dyes collapse into mud. The base therefore has to be desaturated — and rather than redraw vanilla’s saddle by hand, neoforge-26.1.2/tools/tack/bake-saddle-textures.ps1 derives it. The split needs no artistic judgement, which is why it can be a script: vanilla’s sheet holds exactly nine colours, six saturated browns and three pure greys, so leather is saturated and hardware is R = G = B. That rule is asserted in the bake, so a future vanilla retexture that breaks it fails loudly instead of shipping a muddy saddle.

Each leather pixel is vanilla’s colour divided by the tint applied when undyed, so base × undyed reproduces vanilla exactly. Measured worst error is zero — the shipped files were read back and recomposed against vanilla pixel by pixel. The inventory icon carries its own constant, because its palette is genuinely brighter than the sheet’s and the entity’s divisor would clamp it.

What it deliberately does not touch

  • Camels, pigs, striders and nautiluses. The asset covers all ten saddle layer types because it replaces vanilla’s whole file, but those five carry vanilla’s entry verbatim. A horse mod has no business repainting camels.
  • The Tack Dyeing Bench is what dyes one. Craft it, or use the one standing at a cowboy’s homestead. It colours the seat, the bridle and the fittings independently, and a saddle you already own can be brought to it — this rides on minecraft:saddle rather than on an item of our own.
  • It conflicts with Saturated Saddles. Both replace the same vanilla file, so whichever loads later wins. They do the same job, so the result is not broken either way — but running both is pointless, and ours needs no resource pack.

Tack fits a resized horse now — verified

Confirmed in a world on 2026-09-16: “the saddle now fits resized breeds correctly”. This had been open long enough to be written up as a prerequisite for any further tack work, and the recorded diagnosis was wrong: it said the equipment layers did not inherit the horse’s genetic scale. They always did — LivingEntityRenderer scales the pose stack and then iterates the layers, inside the same push and pop.

The real cause was a missing mesh transform. Vanilla builds three horse layers with MeshTransformer.scaling(1.1F) applied — the body, the armour and the saddle — and applies it in LayerDefinitions, one level above the mesh function. HdHorseModel is a copy of that mesh function, so the transform was never in the part that was copied and its absence is invisible on inspection. The body therefore drew at 1.0 while tack baked from vanilla’s layers drew at 1.1 — and since the transform is scaled(f).translated(0, 24.016 × (1 − f), 0), the tack sat 2.4 units high as well as 10 % large.

So it was never really about resizing. The bug was on every horse including scale 1.0; the size locus multiplied the visible gap rather than causing it, which is exactly why it read as “worse on big horses” and sent the original diagnosis looking in the renderer. One line in createHdLayer() fixed it, and the side effect is that every horse is now about 10 % larger than it used to render, because the HD body had been the small one all along.

Somebody else’s tack, in a chest

Chests now hold horse tack. About one chest in five — any chest, vanilla or another mod’s — carries one piece: a leather horse armour, a saddle, or an iron, golden or diamond horse armour, plus one of every modded metal’s armour you happen to have. Leather is the commonest and diamond the rarest, so a diamond armour in a chest is still the best thing in it.

The leather pieces come already dyed, in the mod’s own three zones, with the two leather zones rolled separately — an unrelated pair, because a piece somebody lost in a dungeon should not look like it came off a shop rack. The fittings are deliberately cheap: iron, copper, bone, coal, basalt or gold, never a gem. The leatherworker’s five tiers are a ladder ending in lapis-dyed tack on diamond, and that is only worth climbing if a chest cannot hand you the top rung.

Golden carrot seeds — a crop, and not a gold farm

A golden carrot normally costs eight gold nuggets, which puts a hard ceiling on how many horses you can afford to work with. Golden carrot seeds grow them instead: an ordinary crop on ordinary farmland, eight ages, bone-mealable, exactly like vanilla’s carrot in every mechanical respect.

No gold comes out of it at any point. The loot table drops golden carrots and its own seeds and nothing else — no nugget, no ingot — and a golden carrot cannot be turned back into gold by any vanilla recipe. It is a way to grow horse food, and a player who plants a field of it ends up with a field of horse food.

The gate is getting the first seeds, and there are exactly two ways:

  • The equestrian supplier’s top rank, at 38 emeralds for one, twice per restock.
  • Chest loot — 20% in any chest, one to three seeds when it hits. Not a list of dungeons any more: it was 7% across eight named vanilla tables, and the owner played a heavily modded server for weeks without ever finding one, because nearly every chest she opened belonged to somebody else’s structure. See every chest, any mod on the Coding tab.

They are not craftable from anything, and a golden carrot itself will not plant. That second point is the load-bearing one: if a golden carrot were the seed, every golden carrot ever crafted from eight nuggets would be plantable and the rarity would be worth nothing. A mature plant gives one golden carrot and two seeds, so a field doubles and pays for itself once you have started it.

No art ships for it

The crop wears vanilla’s own carrot stage textures, tinted gold; the seed icon is vanilla’s wheat seeds, tinted the same gold. Two routes to one colour, because an item tint cannot reach a block in the world and a block tint cannot reach an item in a slot — so the constant lives once, on client/GoldTintSource.GOLD, and both read it.

This is also the mod’s first block tint, and 26.1.2 does not have the lambda-per-tint-index block colour handler older versions did: RegisterColorHandlersEvent.BlockTintSources takes a list of one-method BlockTintSource, indexed by the model’s tint index. The four stage models therefore parent a template of ours carrying tintindex: 0, because minecraft:block/crop has none and a model without one renders plain orange however loud the handler shouts.

Source: block/GoldenCarrotCropBlock, data/loot/AddGoldenCarrotSeedsModifier, server/GoldenCarrotHandler (the two checklist boxes). Taught in Getting Started → Farming and haulage, beside the carts.

The creative tab

item/ModCreativeTabs registers one Horse Genetics tab holding every item in ModItems.TAB_ITEMS, plus one filled breed spawn egg per breed that has one. The blank breed egg is not listed anywhere: its whole content is the breed component, and one without a breed is a puzzle rather than an item. The custom spawn egg and the filled breed eggs additionally appear in vanilla’s Spawn Eggs tab. In a dev build, the Spawn Test Horse World button’s starting inventory contains one of every item.

Chest loot — every chest, any mod

Four global loot modifiers put this mod’s things in chests: data/loot/AddResearchPaperModifier, AddGoldenCarrotSeedsModifier, AddBreedSpawnEggModifier and AddHorseTackModifier. Three of the four reach every chest table in every namespace; the breed spawn egg still names its ten, because a foundation horse turning up in an arbitrary modded chest is a different question from a saddle doing so.

Why a regex condition and not NeoForge’s

neoforge:loot_table_id tests one exact id, so “put this in chest loot” was written as an any_of of however many ids somebody thought to list — eight to ten vanilla chests apiece. On a heavily modded server that is close to nothing: most of the chests a player opens belong to another mod’s structures, and none of those were ever in the list. That is why the owner had never once found a golden carrot seed.

data/loot/LootTableMatchesCondition (horsegenetics:loot_table_matches) matches the queried table id against a regular expression instead, and [^:]+:chests/.+ says the whole thing in one line. It matches the path rather than the namespace, because vanilla and nearly every structure mod file their chest tables under chests/; it deliberately does not match block, entity, fishing or archaeology tables, since a horse armour falling out of a sheep is not what was asked for. A malformed pattern is a DataResult error rather than a thrown exception, so a datapack that mistypes one loses that modifier instead of the whole pack.

26.1.2 has no LootItemConditionType class. The registry holds the MapCodec itself, exactly as the loot-function registry does — see data/loot/ModLootConditions, and the API notes.

AddHorseTackModifier draws one piece from a weighted pool: leather horse armour 10, saddle 8, iron 6, gold 4, diamond 1. Every modded armour shares a single share of 5 and is drawn uniformly inside it. That is not tidiness — compat/ModdedArmour registers one armour per metal any installed mod adds, and a 319-jar pack brings dozens, so a weight each would make vanilla’s four the rare ones and turn the pool into a survey of the player’s modlist. With no such mods installed the bucket is not in the draw at all.

The saddle and the leather armour get a tack_tint, not vanilla’s dyed_color — same reason as the bench and SetTackTintFunction: dyed_color holds one value per stack, reaches only the blanket, and does nothing at all to a saddle.

Known gaps, across the roster

  • Art. The four tickets share one texture, the four whistles share one, both stall signs borrow minecraft:item/oak_sign. The blank transfer paper reuses the research-paper texture.
  • A stall is only ever read by a ticket. There is no auto-return and no “assigned pen” behaviour, and only breaking the exact sign block releases one.
  • The sheared look is unbuilt — see horse hair.
  • Nothing generated for another mod’s materials has ever been drawn — not a modded double gate, not a modded horse armour, not either one’s icon — and no generated recipe has ever successfully parsed. Gaps 271–275.
  • The known_gene_splice_carrot is one generic item parameterised by a component, so every gene’s carrot looks the same in the inventory.

Adding an item

Recipes live in neoforge-26.1.2/src/main/resources/data/horsegenetics/recipe/; the item objects in neoforge-26.1.2/…/item/ModItems.java. When you add an item or change a recipe, give it a page here in the same change and register that page in wiki/pages.js — the roster above is a table of links, so an unlisted item is an item nobody can find.

What is still planned here

The roster wants an audit of item/ModItems against the recipe folder — an item with no recipe and no page is invisible to anyone not reading the source, and a 1.0 blocker — plus real textures for the tickets, whistles and stall signs that currently share one.

  • Keep items.html current. It is hand-written against the recipe JSON; generating it, or checking it in a test, would stop it rotting.
What needs verifying here

The quiet failures are here: golden carrot seeds carry this mod's first block tint and fail invisibly — a crop frozen at age 0, a gold item dropping where nothing gold should, a tint that misses the model — and chest loot now rides on a regex that has never had anything taken out of a chest to prove it.

Golden carrot seeds (2026-09-18, NOT played)

  • The crop is gold, not orange. It is vanilla's carrot sheets under a block tint, and this mod's first block tint - if the tint does not reach it, it grows as plain carrots and looks like a bug in the loot table rather than in the model. The seed icon should be gold too, by a different mechanism.
  • It grows at all. Plant on farmland, wait, or bone-meal it. A crop that sits at age 0 forever means the randomTicks property did not take.
  • Breaking it early returns the seed, and a mature one gives one golden carrot and two seeds. Nothing gold ever drops.
  • The seed will not plant on anything but farmland, and a golden carrot still will not plant at all.
  • The supplier sells them at rank 5, 38 emeralds, twice. Level him up and look. He has four other rank-5 trades and rolls three of the five.
  • They turn up in chests - now 20% in any chest rather than 7% across eight named ones, so a handful of chests should do it where it used to need a creative check of the loot modifier.
  • The two checklist boxes tick - plant one, harvest one.

Tack in chest loot (2026-09-24, NOT played)

Proven only to the depth a server boot can prove it: a clean runServer logs nothing, and the same boot with a deliberately invalid regex logs the decode error from our own codec - which shows the folder is scanned, add_horse_tack resolves and horsegenetics:loot_table_matches is registered. Nothing has been taken out of a chest.

  • The regex reaches modded chests, and only chests. Open another mod’s structure chest and tack should turn up at about one in five; open a block drop, a fishing catch or a mob drop and it must not. The pattern matches the path, so a mod that files its chest tables somewhere other than chests/ is invisible to it and would be worth knowing about.
  • A found saddle and leather armour are visibly dyed, in two unrelated leather colours with cheap fittings - and never a gem fitting, which would undercut the leatherworker’s top tier.
  • No gem fitting, and no diamond flood. Diamond horse armour is weight 1 of 29; several chests in a row producing one means the weighted chain is wrong.
  • With another mod’s metals installed, its armours appear - and do not swamp vanilla’s four. This is the one branch that cannot be checked in a vanilla dev world at all; it needs the synthetic compat mod, and nothing generated for another mod’s materials has ever been drawn.
  • Rates feel right rather than merely different. 20% seeds, 30% research papers and 22% tack per chest were picked against “much more common” and nothing else; a stronghold is a lot of chests.
  • Dyed leather horse armour specifically is “very common”. The owner asked for that word for this one piece, separately from the rest of the tack. It is the heaviest entry in the pool, which puts it at 10 of 29 of a 22% chest — about one chest in thirteen, or one in fifteen once another mod’s metals join the draw. That is the number to judge: if a session of chest-opening does not turn up two or three dyed leather armours, W_LEATHER_ARMOUR in AddHorseTackModifier is the dial, not the 22%.
