Fertility & gestation

Mares come into heat, and breeding through this mod’s own tools makes a pregnancy. The foal arrives when it ends, not the moment the pair is bred. Plain golden carrots still give a foal at once, exactly as in vanilla. Fertility is inherited: the fertility gene decides how readily a horse breeds and how often a mare carries twins.

Two kinds of breeding

You do thisYou get
Feed two horses golden carrots (neither has eaten a breeding carrot) A foal at once, as in vanilla. Heat does not matter.
Feed golden carrots to a pair where either horse has eaten a breeding carrot A pregnancy, if the mare is in heat and it takes.
Use a seed jar on your mare A pregnancy, if she is in heat and it takes.
Leave a stallion with mares He covers each mare once per heat, and she may become pregnant. See below.

Heat

A mare is in heat for about one Minecraft day, then out of it for about one day, and so on. No two mares share the same timing. Her information screen shows it on the Breeding line in the Body section: In heat, In heat – best time now, or Not in heat – about 12 min to go. A mare in heat and a nearby stallion also walk toward each other.

The second half of a heat is the best time. Out of heat, a mare refuses a seed jar and the jar is kept. Stallions have no heat.

Will it take?

WhenChance
Second half of a heat80%
First half of a heat50%
Foal heat, the first heat after she foals60%
Not in heat, pregnant, or just foaledno chance

Her own fertility moves these a little either way, and a subfertile horse halves them. A stallion can cover three mares, or fill three jars, in one Minecraft day; after that his odds are halved for the rest of the day. Nothing is ever more certain than 95%. The action bar says straight away whether it took.

Pregnancy

  • It lasts one Minecraft day (twenty minutes) by default. A server can make it longer.
  • Her Breeding line says Pregnant – about 14 min to go. In the last third she is 15% slower, and the line adds heavy and slow.
  • A pregnant mare refuses golden carrots.
  • She foals where she stands. The foal belongs to whoever owns her then, and is credited as bred by whoever arranged the breeding.
  • If she is somewhere unloaded when she is due, she foals as soon as you come back.
  • If she dies, the pregnancy is lost.
  • Twins are rare, and more common with the twinning allele. Usually both are born alive; sometimes only one, and occasionally neither.
  • About one twin pregnancy in ten is identical. One egg that split, so the two are a single foal drawn once: the same sex, the same colour, and the same markings down to the last streak — genuinely indistinguishable, because they share their epigenetics as well as their genes. The other nine pairs in ten are fraternal, two ordinary draws that are no more alike than any brother and sister. Two things follow from an identical pair being one genome rather than two: they can never be a colt and a filly, and if the draw was a lethal one the pregnancy loses both or neither.
  • Two parents that both carry a lethal like MET sometimes make a pregnancy that is lost early. She comes back into heat afterwards.

Pregnancy does not affect her health.

After the birth

  • For about a day she cannot be bred. Then comes foal heat, a heat at slightly lower odds, and then her normal cycle again.
  • She is nursing (shown on the Breeding line) until her foal grows up, or until it has been more than 32 blocks from her for a whole day. Nursing does not stop her being bred again.
  • Foals from plain golden carrots do none of this. Their dam is not nursing and her cycle carries on.

A stallion left with mares

Every adult horse, tamed or wild, breeds on its own. When a mare is in heat and a stallion stays within three blocks of her for a few seconds, as any vanilla animal must before breeding, he covers her once that heat (a burst of hearts), at the odds above. If it does not take, nothing more happens until her next heat. The foal is credited to her owner. A fence between them does not stop it, just as it does not stop two vanilla cows.

It does not happen if:

  • either horse is badly hurt — below 90% of its health; a scratch does not stop it (why) — ridden, or on a lead;
  • he is a gelding, or has already covered three mares today;
  • eight or more other horses, foals included, are within 16 blocks of her;
  • the horses are a cowboy's, whose string never breeds;
  • nobody is near enough for that area to be loaded.

Geldings and the vet’s kit

A vet’s kit, used while sneaking on your own stallion or colt, gelds him for good. A gelding never breeds, never fills a seed jar, and sires nothing from golden carrots. He does not spar, guard mares or go looking for a mare in heat, and he bonds with you a quarter faster. His information screen says Gelding and his nameplate symbol is grey. About half a cowboy’s males are geldings, and they cost a little less.

Used without sneaking, the kit examines a horse: where a mare is in her heat and when it ends, whether a pregnant mare is carrying twins, and how many covers a stallion has made today.

The setting

fertility.gestation_days in the server config, from 1 to 340. Every other stage grows with it in proportion, but is never shorter than one day, so at 340 a mare keeps a realistic three-week cycle. The clock is game time: sleeping and /time set do not move anything. With the server’s testing tools on, each of those “days” is one real minute.

The hooks

  • HorseBreedingHandler.onBabySpawn: a pregnant dam cancels the event. An armed parent sends the pair to ReproHandler.breed and cancels. The patched Animal.spawnChildFromBreeding resets love and sets both 6000-tick cooldowns on a cancel. Otherwise the instant foal goes through applyBredFoal, after one roll against vanillaFoalChance made only when it is below 1, so a wild-type pair draws exactly what it used to.
  • StallionSeedJarHandler: filling counts a cover and moves the stallion’s armed carrots into the jar. Using it requires ReproHandler.receptive instead of vanilla love.
  • NaturalBreedingHandler: every 40 ticks an adult mare asks ReproRules.mayTryNaturally (in heat, and her lastNaturalTry is before this heat began). She finds the nearest entire stallion within NATURAL_REACH who is under the cover limit, checks NATURAL_CAP, stamps the try, and calls breed with her owner as bredBy. Before that, the same stallion must stay in reach for NaturalCover.COURTSHIP_TICKS (a transient Courtship per mare), exactly as vanilla's BreedGoal waits out loveTime >= 60 inside distanceToSqr < 9. Like vanilla, there is no line of sight check. Cowboy-branded horses are skipped. It is an entity tick, so only loaded chunks ever breed.
  • Gelding is HorseRecord.gelded, and HorseRecord.entire() is what every stallion check asks: the herd goals, rivalry, the jar and onBabySpawn. VetKitHandler gelds and examines (ReproHandler.vetReport). See gap 229 for what still reads sex alone.
  • BreedingCooldownHandler refuses vanilla love items to a pregnant mare, server side only; see gap 226.
  • Birth is HorseBreedingHandler.bornFromPregnancy, which shares populateFoal with the instant foal. The breeder is looked up by name, and only gets checklist ticks and gene discovery if they are online.
  • HerdGoals.HeatAttraction at priority 6. The info-screen line rides HorseSocialSyncPayload.breeding.

Settled

debug.tools shortens the reproductive day (the owner, 2026-09-13). With the testing tools on, ServerConfig.reproTiming() uses a one-minute day, so a heat and a pregnancy each last a minute. That is deliberate, including on a real server where the owner tests release builds with the switch on, where every mare runs about twenty times fast. It is the only thing behind that switch that changes how the game plays for everyone.

A natural cover needs a healthy horse, not an untouched one (2026-09-16, closing gap 258). The rule was full health exactly — getHealth() >= getMaxHealth() — and that turned a single point of damage into permanent infertility, because healing is gated on standing near water (HorseCareHandler.nearWater): a horse in a dry pen never got the point back. A miscarriage costs the dam half a heart, so a mare who lost a pregnancy to a lethal pairing was never covered again. She went on cycling and reading In heat, stallions ignored her, and nothing was logged, because that verdict (NOT_A_BREEDING_MARE) was the one the handler passed over in silence. A wolf bite or a lost fight did the same to any horse. It is now a fraction, ReproRules.COVER_HEALTH, tested by ReproRulesTest at exactly the health a miscarriage leaves her; and the refusal now logs once a minute per mare, naming her health. Why golden carrots stay instant

The owner’s standing rule is that this mod adds to vanilla and does not take vanilla away, and a pregnancy replaces the instant foal outright. So the pregnancy lives only on paths vanilla never had: the jar, the carrots and the gene. The one thing fertility may do to plain breeding is the subfertile allele, which takes two copies in a parent. An unbred horse is never sf/sf, so unbred stock breeds exactly as vanilla does. The epigenetic number is kept off that path for the same reason. The three options this was chosen from, and every other call, are under settled decisions.

What real mares do

The two sexes run on different clocks. A mare cycles seasonally and has a short fertile window. A mature stallion can breed all year. And a mare can conceive again while she is still nursing, often within about a week of foaling (MSD Veterinary Manual). Breeding’s science tab gives the same biology in brief, for the pedigree side.

A seasonal 21-day cycle

Mares are long-day, seasonally polyestrous breeders. Most begin cycling as the days lengthen in spring, keep cycling through summer, and stop or nearly stop in winter. That lands an eleven-month pregnancy on the next spring, when a newborn has the best conditions. During the season a cycling mare ovulates about every 21 days.

The practical breeding target is the day or two before ovulation, through ovulation itself. Sperm from a live cover or fresh semen survives long enough to meet the egg. Frozen semen does not last as long, so it is timed much closer to ovulation (PetMD).

What heat looks like

A mare in heat takes an interest in a stallion and stands near him. She raises her tail and repeatedly exposes and “winks” the vulva, urinates in small, frequent amounts, and stands still to be mounted. These signs mean she is receptive. They do not time ovulation, so breeding programmes add teasing, ultrasound and sometimes hormone tests to find the fertile window.

Stallions: no heat cycle

A stallion does not come into heat. Once he is mature and sound, he can make sperm and breed all year. He is still somewhat seasonal: semen output, libido and even testicular size peak in the long days and drop in winter, varying with the individual, latitude, management, nutrition, age and artificial light. His fertility is also more than willingness. It depends on semen concentration, motility and morphology, on reproductive health, and on how often he is used. So a stallion may show interest at almost any time, while a mare must be in heat and near ovulation for a good chance of conception.

Pregnancy and twins

Gestation averages about eleven months, usually given as 320–370 days (Wikipedia, Mare). Progesterone suppresses cycling, so a pregnant mare does not normally come back into heat. A mare usually carries one foal. Twins are a veterinary emergency in horses and far less likely to end in two normal foals.

Foal heat, and why breeders often skip it

After foaling the system restarts quickly. Most mares have a first postpartum heat, the foal heat, beginning about 5–12 days after birth, with ovulation often at 7–10 days. A mare can therefore be bred, and become pregnant, while a newborn is still at her side. The natural rhythm of a healthy mare is to foal in spring, come into foal heat a week later, conceive, nurse the foal through much of the next pregnancy, and foal again about a year on.

A foal-heat breeding can succeed, but a vet may advise waiting for the next cycle, about 25–35 days after foaling. Common reasons are a retained placenta or abnormal discharge, a difficult delivery, uterine injury or infection, a uterus slow to return to normal (delayed involution), poor body condition or a heavy lactation load, an older or less fertile mare, or a sick foal that needs more from her. The deciding factor is usually uterine recovery and overall condition, not the milk itself.

Nursing does not stop conception

Unlike many mammals, a nursing mare can cycle, ovulate and conceive. Lactation matters indirectly: a mare carrying a pregnancy and feeding a foal at once needs enough calories, protein, minerals and water, and a poor-condition mare conceives and carries less well. Some mares with a foal at foot also tease as unreceptive because their attention is on the foal, which does not mean they are not cycling (MSD Veterinary Manual, owners' edition).

Compressing 340 days

A normal Minecraft day is 24 000 ticks, about twenty minutes (Minecraft Wiki; also xgamingserver). Fitting a 340-day pregnancy into one day is a 340× compression, so one horse day becomes about 3.5 seconds. Applied evenly to everything, it gives this:

Accurate, and unplayable: a heat would be over before a player noticed it. The general form is stageTicks = gestationTicks × referenceDays / 340, and everything below is about what to do with the short end.

What the mod does: one-day floors, stages that nest

Each independent stage is scaled by that formula and then floored at one day: max(dayTicks, gestationTicks × referenceDays / 340) (ReproTiming, coding tab). The references are 5 days of estrus, 16 of diestrus and 7 from foaling to foal heat. Nested stages are not floored again, or they would double-count days:

  • The cycle is estrus plus diestrus. It is derived, not floored, so its minimum is two days, not three.
  • Peak fertility is a window inside estrus (its second half, 80% against 50%), not a stage after it.
  • Foal heat reuses the estrus length.

At the default setting everything collapses to “a day in heat, a day out”. The real ratios come back as fertility.gestation_days grows (the setting, capped at 340):

Calendars considered and not built

The one-day floor was one of several shapes. The others are kept here as options, and none of them is in the mod.

  • An abstract breeding year. An invisible “year” of 24 to 56 Minecraft days, with mares cycling only in its first two thirds and anestrus in the rest, and each mare given a private offset into a 21-day (or 7-day) cycle. Players would only see in season or out of season. The per-mare offset exists: every mare’s phase comes from her UUID, so no two share their timing (coding tab). The seasons and anestrus do not.
  • A 3× compression. Gestation of 16–20 Minecraft days (about 5⅓–6⅔ hours of play), a 7-day cycle with 2 days of heat, foal heat 2–4 days after birth, and lactation of 9–15 days, with small random variation on each. A reproductive year of 24 days, rather than 18–19, keeps every pregnancy from being exactly one year long. The mod gets close to this by setting fertility.gestation_days to 18, but it adds no variation.
  • Minimum floors in minutes instead of days. Keep a twenty-minute gestation but floor the short stages at gameplay lengths: a 10-minute cycle, 3 minutes of heat with a 1-minute peak, 4 minutes of recovery, foal heat 4 minutes after birth, and weaning available at 8 minutes and automatic at 12. Presets would range from instant testing (a 5-minute gestation) to long-form realism (120 minutes), with a strict_biological_scaling switch that removes the floors. The mod floors in days instead, and its testing tools make each day one real minute.

Which clock, and what sleep does

There are three ways to count reproductive time:

  • World time. Timers follow the calendar, so sleeping advances them. It is intuitive, but on a server where people sleep, animals race ahead.
  • Active ticks. Only ticks that actually run count. Sleeping, /time set and other time-altering mods move nothing. This is what the mod does: every deadline is compared against the level’s game time (below).
  • A hybrid. Pregnancy advances with world time, but heat, foal heat and recovery count active ticks, so a player can sleep through a pregnancy without sleeping through a mare’s one-day heat. It is not built.

Whichever clock is used, the implementation advice is the same, and the mod follows it. Store absolute tick deadlines (a due tick, a foaling tick), never a counter that pauses when a chunk unloads. Store a pregnancy the moment conception succeeds. Rank the states so a pregnant mare is never also in heat: PREGNANT, then postpartum, then heat, then diestrus. Keep nursing as a flag beside the state rather than a state of its own (state is derived, never stepped).

What the model leaves out

  • Seasons. There is no anestrus and no spring transition. A mare cycles all year.
  • Stallion seasonality and semen quality. A stallion’s only variables are his fertility alleles and how many covers he has made today.
  • Body condition. A 0–100 condition score was suggested, falling while a mare is pregnant or nursing, gating breeding below about 45, and turning dangerous below 25. The mod’s hunger is the nearest thing, but it pays for healing and has no effect on fertility, pregnancy or nursing.
  • A difficult foaling. There is no retained placenta or slow involution to delay foal heat. Every mare waits the same postpartum time, and recovery and the delay before foal heat are one stage, not two (7 and 10 real days in the reference).
  • Variation in gestation. Every pregnancy lasts exactly the configured length. Real ones range across 320–370 days.
  • Lactation as a timer. Nursing ends when the foal grows up or has been away from its dam for a day (after the birth), not on a lactation clock.
  • Geldings. The reference data model lists gelding as a sex. The mod keeps sex genetic and records gelding on the horse’s record (the hooks).

Why it is built this way

  • Absolute ticks, derived state. A countdown pauses when a chunk unloads, and a stored state has to be stepped through every transition it missed. A state computed from three anchors and the time has nothing to catch up on.
  • Drawn at conception. A carrot’s bias and both live genomes exist then and not at birth. It also means an embryo is a real object, which embryo extraction on the assisted-reproduction roadmap can read.
  • The sire’s whole genome in every embryo. Birth may come after he is sold, unloaded or dead. The parent-stats comparison needs his body, and a stored genome is the only honest source for it.
  • Carrots that wait. A 30-second carrot made sense when a carrot was fed at the moment of breeding. Once a mare has to be in heat, it would mostly have been wasted, so a carrot now waits on the horse until a conception uses it.
  • Every horse breeds, once per heat. The Spontaneous Breeding gene made automatic breeding a rare trait. The owner removed it: a stallion left with mares making foals is simply what horses do. Heat and pregnancy already slow the rate to roughly one foal per mare every few Minecraft days. The one-try-per-heat rule stops a pair rolling until it succeeds, and the local cap bounds what is left. It held a clustered herd at ten for five hours (verified); a band spread wider than sixteen blocks is untested.
  • Game time, not the day counter. The mod’s other day-based rules (a horse’s age, the daily bond cap) already count game time, and on a server where people sleep, day time would push pregnancies through in bursts.

One twin pregnancy in ten is identical (2026-09-17, NOT played)

Twins used to be two independent draws every time, so every pair was fraternal. About one pair in ten is now a single genome drawn once — true clones, epigenetics included. The rate is unit-tested; what is unplayed is whether an identical pair actually looks identical in the world.

Nothing here needs looking at to judge — do these first

  • An identical pair is indistinguishable. Breed a tw/tw mare repeatedly until twins arrive, and compare the two foals side by side. Pass: roughly one pair in ten matches in every respect — same sex, same colour, and the same markings down to the individual streaks and patches. Fail: a pair that is obviously the same colour but has different white markings, which would mean the epigenome is being redrawn rather than shared.
  • An identical pair is never a colt and a filly. Sex is a locus like any other, so it comes along with the rest. Pass: every matched pair is two colts or two fillies. A mixed pair that otherwise looks identical is a failure.
  • Most pairs are still fraternal. Pass: the other nine pairs in ten differ the way any two siblings do — often different sexes and plainly different coats.
