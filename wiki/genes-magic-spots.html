Spots and rings

Genes made of countable marks: spots, rosettes, annuli, crescents, chains of disks, and haloes with something dark inside them.

Angler

Strings of faintly glowing lights strung horizontally along the body, each light a pale core inside a deeper rim, like the lures of a deep-water fish.

Ang · n

AnglerRound and oval lights in strings that run horizontally along the body and bend as they go, never singly - a string is two lights at least. Each light is a pale core inside a deeper rim of the same hue, and one horse carries one design throughout. The strings turn vertical on the face and legs, where nothing else on this horse runs level. Mane, tail and hooves are untouched however close a string passes.
Angler carrierOne copy shows nothing. Two carriers bred together are the only way the lights appear.
Wild typeNo lights.

Aurorae

Luminescent spots scattered over the whole horse, in one hue the line carries, with the mane and tail fading into the same light toward their tips.

Aur · n

AuroraeRound and oval spots of cold light over the whole body, sparse in one copy and crowding together in two. Every spot on a horse is the same hue, and the mane and tail run into it toward their tips. A faint wash of the same colour lies over the coat between the spots.
Wild typeNo aurorae.

Crescents

One to four broad open arcs, the largest centred on the barrel, each thinning to nothing at both of its ends and keeping the coat colour inside its curve.

Crs · Crc · n

CrescentsWide arcs of a single band, a quarter to two-thirds of the way round a circle, with the coat inside the curve untouched. The largest sits on the mid-barrel and the smaller ones fall on the scapula, hip and caudal neck. Which way an arc opens is not fixed - a crescent horse has arcs facing several ways, never a row of them facing one.
Coloured CrescentsThe same marking in a colour the line carries rather than in white. Fully recessive: it takes two copies, and a single copy beside the white allele shows white. It never gives blue eyes, whatever the white form does.
Wild typeNo crescents.

Fawn

A single flowing line of soft round spots running neck to rump, a darkened topline above it, and a pale underside to the tail.

Fwn · Fwc · n

FawnRound, oval or tapered spots in one consecutive run somewhere between the neck and the rump - never two clusters with a gap between them, never a spiral or a pattern, just a flowing line of them. They keep off the face, the belly and the legs. Most fawn horses also carry a stylised white eyeliner, a slightly darkened topline in the manner of sooty, a pangare-like lightening underneath, and a pale underside to the tail. Every white on the horse is the same white. Blue eyes are possible.
Coloured FawnThe same run of spots in a colour the line carries rather than in white. Fully recessive: it takes two copies, and a single copy beside the white allele shows white. It never gives blue eyes, whatever the white form does.
Wild typeNo spots.

Glow

Small glowing speckles crowded along the topline, with the hooves lit to match. Every lit part of the horse shares one colour.

Gl · n

GlowFine luminescent speckles densest over the neck, shoulder, ribcage and hindquarter and thinning toward the belly, with all four hooves lit in the same colour. One hue per horse, with the speckles a shade paler than the hooves. The mane and tail are untouched.
Wild typeNo glow.

Integration

A spot field spreading outward from wherever the coat is already dark - merging into solid patches close in, thinning to isolated marks a foot or two out, and gone beyond that.

Int · Inc · n

IntegrationIt does not choose its own places; it grows out of the ones the horse already has. Anywhere the coat is dark - the crest and the base of the mane, a dorsal stripe, the distal limbs, the margin of any other marking - becomes a source, and elements propagate outward from it. Right at a source they merge into continuous patches with scalloped margins; a hand's span out they are separate circles and lobes with their long axis pointing the way they came; further still they thin to nothing. It reads as an appaloosa blanket at medium scale.
Coloured IntegrationThe same marking in a colour the line carries rather than in white. Fully recessive: it takes two copies, and a single copy beside a white allele shows white. It never gives blue eyes, whatever the white form does.
Wild typeNo spread.

Lantern

A soft line of bioluminescence running the length of the topline from poll to croup, broken here and there where the light fails to carry.

La · n

LanternA line of light along the spine, brightest at its core and dissipating into a soft bloom either side. It breaks in places - a lantern horse rarely carries an unbroken line - and the same colour shows faintly through the mane where the line passes under it. One copy is as lit as two: the horse cannot turn it off, and in the dark it is the only part of it you see.
Wild typeNo lantern line.

Lasertae

Two to five large ellipses on the scapula, barrel and hip, each a bright diffuse halo closing around a core that stays darker than the coat around it.

Lst · Lsc · n

LasertaeBig soft-edged ellipses, their long axis lying roughly nose to tail. The halo has no boundary worth the name on its outside and a firmer one inside, and within it the core sits a shade or two below the surrounding coat rather than above it - which is what makes a lasertae read as a hole with a light around it rather than as a spot.
Coloured LasertaeThe same marking in a colour the line carries rather than in white. Fully recessive: it takes two copies, and a single copy beside the white allele shows white. It never gives blue eyes, whatever the white form does.
Wild typeNo lasertae.

Pavonem

Peacock eyes - upright ovals filled paler than the coat, each with a bright spot low in it, and always a scatter of ordinary spots around them.

Pv · Pvc · n

PavonemA white paint marking rather than a coat colour, and it works on any base. The shape is an oval with a spot set into its lower part, filled with a lighter or roaned version of what is underneath, and it is never alone - ordinary spots always ring it. It stacks happily with tobiano and the rest.
Coloured PavonemThe same eyes in a colour the line carries rather than in white. Fully recessive: it takes two copies, and a single copy beside the white allele shows white. It never gives blue eyes, whatever the white form does.
Wild typeNo eyes.

Polymoon

A chain of pale disks running from the throatlatch back along the shoulder and onto the barrel, largest in the middle of its run and tapering at both ends.

Pmn · Pmc · n

PolymoonCircular disks laid nearly edge to edge along one curved path, sometimes overlapping a little, never scattered. The path leaves the throatlatch or the point of shoulder, follows the back edge of the scapula and runs out onto the barrel. Some horses carry a second, shorter chain on the hip. The disks are largest halfway along and smallest at the two ends.
Coloured PolymoonThe same marking in a colour the line carries rather than in white. Fully recessive: it takes two copies, and a single copy beside the white allele shows white. It never gives blue eyes, whatever the white form does.
Wild typeNo chain.

Rex

Open circles in a bright colour, at least three of them, anywhere on the horse - drawn over whatever else it is wearing, with the hooves in the same colour.

Rx · n

RexCircles - outlines, not filled disks - in the same colour the horse's speckling runs to. Three at minimum and no upper limit; they can land anywhere and they sit over any paint marking the horse already has rather than under it. They do not disturb the speckling itself, and the hooves take the same colour. The mane keeps its ordinary striping.
Wild typeNo circles.

Rosette

Leopard rosettes following the curve of the body - a dark ring around a centre that keeps the coat colour - sparse in one copy and blanketing the horse in two.

Rs · n

Blanket rosetteTwo copies crowd the rosettes together until they touch, and where they touch they read as broken striping rather than as rings. Coverage runs from average to the whole horse, and the mane picks up the tone wherever a rosette meets the topline.
Semi-rosetteOne copy scatters the rosettes over the upper half of the horse - clear and well-drawn, but selectively placed, and rarely reaching below the barrel.
Wild typeNo rosettes.

Wormholes

Small pale rings whose centres keep the coat underneath, gathered into two or three clusters over the shoulder, barrel and hip.

Wmh · Wmc · n

WormholesPale annuli with the base coat showing through the middle. Most of them close; a quarter or so are left open as arcs, and a few carry a second ring outside the first. They gather rather than spread - two to four clusters over the scapula, barrel, croup and lateral neck, with a scattering of loners between them.
Coloured WormholesThe same marking in a colour the line carries rather than in white. Fully recessive: it takes two copies, and a single copy beside the white allele shows white. It never gives blue eyes, whatever the white form does.
Wild typeNo wormholes.
