Horsemanship

What the rider brings to the ride, as something that can grow. A horse would go a little faster and jump a little higher while you are on its back, by how skilled a rider you are and how well that particular horse knows you — and for nobody else, because the bonus belongs to the pairing and is thrown away the moment you dismount. None of it is built. This page exists so that the plan has a home of its own rather than a section on a shared list, and so that when the first piece of it ships there is already a page to document it on.

What is still planned here

A rider-skill curve that moves speed and jump only — never health — as an AttributeModifier applied on mount and removed on dismount, with an uncapped, sharply diminishing shape, and an optional Pufferfish’s Skills tree shipped as pure data.

Horsemanship — the rider is half of what a horse can do

This reverses a position the roadmap held twice. A horsemanship skill sat on the survey backlog for months with an objection attached: it would be the first thing in the mod where the player progresses rather than the horse, which cuts against breeding being the progression — the same objection that still stands against a competition ladder. The owner reframed it on 2026-09-21 and the reframing is what makes it buildable: bonding makes the horse listen, and skill makes you a better rider. Neither makes the horse a better horse.

The bonus belongs to the pairing, and never to the animal

A horse's stored stats are always exactly its genome. The bonus is an AttributeModifier added when you mount and removed when you dismount, computed from your skill and your bond with that horse. Nothing is ever written to the horse.

That is the whole of why this is allowed and a carrot that adds a heart is not. The carrot changes the horse; this changes the ride. It also means HorseInfoScreen keeps telling the truth, a sold horse carries none of your skill with it, and the same horse under a novice performs at its genome exactly as it does today.

What it may move, and how far

  • Speed and jump only. Never health, never size — those are health-gene territory, and a rider adding hearts would make a genetically frail horse stop being legible at a glance, which is the whole reason frailty is expressed as fewer hearts.
  • Uncapped, with sharply diminishing returns. The owner's call, and it is the part of this section carrying the design risk: with no hard ceiling, the curve is the only thing keeping a well-ridden ordinary horse from overtaking a bred one. Pick the curve against real genome spread — the gap between a poor and a good horse's resolved speed — and not in the abstract. Write the chosen shape and the numbers it was measured against on this page, on a Coding tab when it grows one, because a curve nobody can re-derive is a curve the next session will quietly get wrong.
  • Two axes, multiplied. General skill carries between horses; bond is the per-horse half and already exists. A skilled rider on a stranger's horse is competent and not at their best, which is the behaviour worth having.

The hook: Pufferfish’s Skills, and a tree that is pure data

An optional dependency, never a requirement. Three skills mods were read from their jars on 2026-09-21 — Pufferfish's Skills, RPG Attribute System and Skill Tree (RPG Series) — and only the first is a usable seam.

  • The tree is shipped as data and needs no code at all. Pufferfish's PackConfigReader scans every namespace for data/<ns>/puffish_skills/config.json, so a category living at data/horsegenetics/puffish_skills/categories/horsemanship/ is read when the mod is installed and is inert when it is not. No registration, no ModList.isLoaded check, no presence probe — without the mod the tree simply does not exist. Skill Tree ships its own two categories exactly this way, which makes its jar the schema documentation.
  • The effect needs no branch either. The built-in puffish_skills:attribute reward applies a real vanilla AttributeModifier to any registered attribute, including one we register unconditionally. Register horsegenetics:horsemanship, read player.getAttributeValue(...) always, and with no skills mod present the value sits at its baseline forever. That is a genuine zero-branch soft dependency, and it is the shape to copy for any future optional integration.
  • Anything the built-in rewards cannot express — touching bond or genetics directly — goes through SkillsAPI.registerReward from a class in the NeoForge module that is only ever loaded behind ModList.get().isLoaded("puffish_skills"), with the mod as a compileOnly dependency. Standard idiom; keep it to the cases that earn it.
  • Reading a level is SkillsAPI.getCategory(id).map(c -> c.getSpentPoints(player)), or c.getExperience().map(e -> e.getLevel(player)). There are unlock and lock events, on Pufferfish's own pub/sub rather than the NeoForge bus.

Why not the other two. RPG Attribute System has an api package, but its isAvailable() is compiled as a literal return true and is useless as a presence check; it exposes no events, so any integration has to poll RasApi.getLevel(player) on mount; and its eight attributes are fixed with no room for a ninth. It is a reasonable secondary signal — overall player level gating how deep the tree can be bought into — and cannot host the tree. Skill Tree has no api package at all and hard-requires spell_engine and spell_power; it is content sitting on Pufferfish's, so any level worth reading from it is a Pufferfish query anyway. KubeJS is a real third seam for a server-specific tweak, and not a substitute for something we want to ship in the jar.

Most players have no skills mod, and the feature still exists

Bond alone carries it, at a lower ceiling. Nobody is locked out, and installing a skills mod raises your ceiling rather than switching a feature on. This is also what makes the thing buildable in the first place: the whole system can be written, tuned and tested against bond before any hook exists, and the Pufferfish tree is then additive on top of something already working.
