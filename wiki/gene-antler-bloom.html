Magical gene / recessive / grows leaves on the antlers, paints nothing

Blooming antlers

Two copies and leaves, moss or blossom grow on a horse's antlers — a clump on every tine and a few along each beam. Which of the three is inherited. On a horse without antlers this gene does nothing at all.

Crossing two of them

Finding one

Recessive: about one wild horse in twelve has two copies, and most of those have no antlers to bloom. The Rimehart carries it, and every Rimehart that blooms grows moss.

What it looks like

Small clumps on the tines and the beams, in leaf green, moss olive or blossom pink. A tine the horse has not fully grown carries its clump at the same partial size. The growth is written on the allele and almost never changes between parent and foal.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill. Nothing can be harvested from the leaves.

It costs boxes, not a system

Every antler mesh AntlerGenerator builds already carries its clumps, in the PartSheet.BLOOM region: one on each tine's point, in that tine's group so it hides and shrinks with it, and a few on the beam. An ordinary rack's submits simply never draw that region. GrownParts.of sets the part's bloomTint, and the layer adds one submit drawing only BLOOM in that colour. That is the treatment's "a per-tine tint override plus a small second box set", done as a region of the mesh rather than a second mesh, so it adds no axis to the part cache.

There is no real locus here

A magical gene with no real counterpart. Deer do rub velvet and vegetation off their antlers, and an old stag's rack can come away with moss and grass tangled in it; this is that image, made heritable.

What needs verifying here

Blooming antlers have not been seen in game, so whether the clumps read as foliage rather than blocks is unchecked.

Leaves, moss and blossom on a rack

One horse of each growth: the clumps should read as foliage in their colour, sit on the tine points and along the beam, and vanish with a hidden tine. The clump boxes stick out sideways from the beam and may need pulling in on a massive rack.
