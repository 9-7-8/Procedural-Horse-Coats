Breeds & spawning / built, not yet seen in game

Undead horses

Undeath is two genes, not two entity types. A skeleton horse is a horse with two copies of the skeleton allele, and a zombie horse one with two copies of zombie, so the genome, the coat pipeline, breeding, records, bond and the body stats all reach them because they reach every horse. Vanilla’s own zombie and skeleton horses — in old saves, from skeleton traps, from /summon and eggs — turn into this mod’s horses the first time they are ticked: a Graveborn Warmblood or a Great Valley Skeleton Horse — or, in the Nether, a Blackened Skeleton Horse, which also runs wild there.

What happens to your vanilla undead horses

Nothing happens to them until a server operator turns conversion on. It ships off, because it cannot be undone. An operator first runs /horseundead test: one vanilla skeleton horse and then one zombie horse are spawned beside them (tamed to them, named and saddled), converted by the real converter, and checked. Once that passes, /horseundead enable turns it on for the whole server (below). From then on, each vanilla undead horse becomes one of this mod’s horses the first time its chunk loads, with nothing more to do. It keeps its name, owner, tame state, saddle, armour, lead, age and the share of its health it had left, and gets a pedigree and a coat of its own. Its speed, jump and hearts are re-rolled from its new genes, so a zombie horse comes out slower and much tougher than it was, a skeleton horse quicker and frailer. A horse you already owned arrives calm toward you and stays that way; an untamed one is as hostile as the breed is (below) until it is offered its item. Its bond starts at zero.

  • A horse with you on it waits until you get off.
  • A skeleton trap is still a trap. It springs as vanilla’s does; the horse it leaves behind converts after.
  • Other mods’ undead horses are left alone.
  • It can be switched off again with /horseundead disable or undead.convert in the server config (below). Off, vanilla undead horses stay vanilla; ones already converted stay what they became.

The two breeds

Neither is sold, stabled or found in a wild herd: they come only from conversion and from their spawn eggs (the Nether’s Blackened is the wild one). Both are hostile to their rider after dark until passified, have black eyes, do not burn in daylight, and are drawn at the size of vanilla’s undead horses.

The Blackened Skeleton Horse

The Nether’s skeleton horse, and the commonest horse there — rare, but more common than the Netherhorse, the Nightmare or the Mirrorgazer. Everything the Great Valley horse is, charred black (its horns stay bone), fireproof, and wild: herds of it on the Nether’s floors, in the dark. A vanilla skeleton horse that converts in the Nether becomes one; anywhere else it becomes a Great Valley.

Horns of bone

Every horn-like part a horse can grow is in the skeleton’s pool — a unicorn horn, antlers, ram’s horns, dragon horns and, since 2026-10-05, a row of dorsal spines, a back sail of bare rays with no membrane, shoulder and hip plates and a narwhal horn — all in bone, none glowing or shedding. The one exception is crystal growths: a crystal has no bone, so a skeleton grows ordinary see-through crystals in any colour (owner, 2026-10-05). About one skeleton in ten wears one kind, one in twenty two kinds, one in eighty three, and about one in 640 four. None wears more than four, and the rest have none. Every part with bone in it has a bone version like this, so a part that ships later can join them.

Breeding them

They breed as any horse does. Two skeletons give skeleton foals; a skeleton and an ordinary horse give ordinary-looking foals that carry it, and two carriers can surprise you. The diet, temper and drops ride on their own genes, so a bred-down skeleton can have an ordinary appetite and a gentle temper and still be bones.

The switch

undead.convert (server config, default false since 0.5.042; the owner reversed treatment D2 on 2026-10-03, so a server opts in only after watching it work). /horseundead (operators, level 2) says whether it is on and whether a test has passed since the server started; test converts one skeleton and one zombie horse beside the operator; enable, refused until a test has passed, sets the key and saves it; disable clears it. Each test subject carries the persistent flag horsegenetics:convert_anyway, which lets the converter take that one horse on its own entity tick with the key off; the command never converts from its own call. Removing the mod, or turning this off, leaves any converted horse as a plain minecraft:horse, as every other horse of this mod is left.

The breed sets the bundle (settled 2026-10-01)

The question was whether one undead locus may bundle several effects, against “a concept is a breed, not a gene”. It does not need to: each undeath gene is only the visible undeath (the look, the black eyes, “undead” to the mob groups, and the skeleton’s breathing). Diet, temper, passification, drops, particles and stats are separate, existing loci the two breeds pin. The only new genes are the two undeath loci; the rest is new alleles on old ones: Dbone on diet, Bne/Rot on magic item drop, Blk on both iris loci, Bon on horn colour and dragon horn colour, and offerings 14 (minecraft:bone) and 15 (minecraft:rotten_flesh) on passification. Every one was appended, so no saved horse’s allele moved, and none has a wild share.

The converter — server/UndeadHorseConverter

On EntityTickEvent.Post, under HorseFoundingTickHandler’s budget (one budget for every founding), never on the join event: writing Attributes.SCALE from anywhere else has crashed the chunk system before. A small table maps an exact EntityType to a pool name — SKELETON_HORSE to skeleton, ZOMBIE_HORSE to zombie — and is the extension point for another mod’s undead horse; subclasses are not converted.

  1. Waits while a player rides it, while it is an armed skeleton trap (SkeletonHorse.isTrap(), public in 26.1.2), while it is dead or dying. Skips for good a horse whose persistent data carries horsegenetics:keep_vanilla.
  2. Saves the undead horse whole (saveWithoutId) and loads the tag into a new minecraft:horse created with EntitySpawnReason.CONVERSION. The two share AbstractHorse’s save format, so the UUID, position, age, custom name, tame flag and owner, saddle and armour, lead, tags, persistence and other mods’ persistent data all come across with no copying here.
  3. Founds it from a breed whose sheet says "undead_of" that pool (common/breed/UndeadPools.pick: a pool breed that lives in the horse’s biome first — the Blackened in the Nether — else one that lives nowhere wild), picked uniformly with SeededRng(uuid, "undead-conversion") — the same seed for the sex, genome and name, so a replay makes the same horse. A custom name becomes the record’s barn name over a rolled registered name. The record’s owner mirrors the entity’s. The body is re-resolved and the health fraction restored. A tamed horse has every passification route settled FOREVER for its owner (D25).
  4. Swaps: lets the old lead go without its item (the tag holds the same lead; dropping it too would duplicate it), ejects passengers, discard()s the old entity (no death, drops or notices), adds the new one, remounts the non-player passengers and syncs coat and care to trackers. Discard comes first because two live entities cannot share a UUID.
  5. Fails safe. If the level refuses the new horse, a vanilla copy is rebuilt from the same tag and put back. Any exception leaves the original untouched; after five tries it is left vanilla until the level reloads. The failure mode is the horse still being there, and still vanilla.

Gametests: undead_horses_convert (a tamed, named, saddled, half-health zombie and a plain skeleton: one entity per UUID, a real record, the gene, the breed, owner, name, saddle, health fraction and passification all kept), undead_trap_waits, undead_keep_vanilla.

The art — vanilla’s own sheets, converted

Vanilla’s zombie and skeleton horses are the horse mesh with another texture — one layer definition in LayerDefinitions, stored at 1/1.1 of the horse’s scale, which is why both breeds are size [0.86, 0.95]. Their sheets are copied into common/sheet-sources/minecraft/ and converted to this mod’s layout by coat/skin/HorseSheetConverter (./gradlew :common:convertHorseSheets, from common/sheet-sources/manifest.txt). It scales by an integer factor (nearest neighbour up, box average down), re-lays the adult’s shared leg and ear patches into the HD mesh’s own patches as read from HorseSkinGeometry.patches, and in vanilla mode applies vanilla’s cube mirror to the left legs (every face flipped and the two side strips swapped — read from 26.1.2’s ModelPart$Cube). In straight mode it reproduces the shipped white template to the pixel, which HorseSheetConverterTest holds it to. The notice is in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.

The undeath genes reach the art through CoatSheetContribution: each names its sheets (skeleton_adult …), every host loads them into the LutSet’s sheets beside the LUTs (the game’s texture factory, LutSet.fromRegistry for every tool, and DesignerApi.setSheet from java.js), and the composer runs the zombie’s seed and the skeleton’s shape pass. See the two gene pages.

Two breed-file fields

  • "undead_of": "skeleton" | "zombie" joins a breed to a pool. Joining a pool is editing that breed’s file; the converter needs no change.
  • "count_groups": a set of loci where the sheet weighs how many of them express and the founder then picks which — how the skeleton’s horns are drawn (weights [5350, 640, 320, 80, 10]). See the breed format.

Also built with it

  • MobGroups: the undead group adds a horse that Undeath.kindOf says is undead (two copies, never a carrier).
  • mixin/HorseUndeadVoiceMixin: vanilla’s undead horse sounds on an undead horse — a mixin because the sound verb plays beside a horse’s voice and cannot replace it.
  • Passification.onOffer runs at HIGH and lets go of an item once that player’s calm is permanent, so the skeleton’s bone is its offering until paid and its food after.
  • The bone diet, the two drops (bones, rotten_flesh on the item_drop verb), and the undead breeds’ black-eye request.

Owner’s calls (2026-10-02)

  • Every horn-like part in the skeleton’s pool, all bone, no glow or emission; a Bon allele for the two horn colour loci. Odds P(k) = P(k−1) / 2k−1 from ten percent, through a new count group rather than independent loci or strains.
  • “Cannot drown” is the absolute underwater_breathing flag on the skeleton gene, not the graded water-breathing locus.
  • Weathered is a dunalino — red dun with one cream — which took the pink out of the old bone in the preview (double cream washed out; champagne went grey).
  • The converter is Java in common/ with a Gradle task, not a script.
  • The Blackened Skeleton Horse: its own breed (spawning is a breed field, not a strain one), charred bone with bone horns, fireproof, wild in the Nether at spawn_weight 2 (“rare in general, but the most common type of nether horse”), and the breed a skeleton converting in the Nether becomes.
  • Every part with bone in it has a bone version (the rule); the ram’s horns gained theirs, a shade below the wild range.
What needs verifying here

Conversion is proved by gametests and the looks by side-view renders, but no undead horse of this mod has been seen in a running game - an old save converting, a ridden horse, a sprung trap, the sounds and the first-frame coat are all unwatched.

An old save

Load a world with a saddled, owned, named zombie horse and a saddled skeleton horse: both convert on first load with tack, name and owner intact, the owner can mount (the owner-only riding rule must see the owner), and the log has no duplicate-UUID or ProblemReporter lines.

Ridden, and across a chunk border

Ride a vanilla zombie horse, get off, and watch it convert; ride a converted one across a chunk border and log out and in on it.

A skeleton trap

Walk into one: it springs as vanilla’s does, and the horse converts afterwards with its skeleton rider still on.

A leashed horse

A vanilla undead horse on a lead to a fence converts still tied, and no second lead appears on the ground.

No white flash

The converted horse’s coat is right on its first frame for a player standing beside it.

A stable of them

A dozen undead horses loaded at once convert over a few ticks; DebugAnnounce reports the founding budget deferring rather than the tick stalling.

The sounds

A converted horse makes vanilla’s undead horse ambient, hurt and death sounds, on both sides of a dedicated server (which side plays each is unverified).

Passification and taming

An untamed skeleton turns on a rider after dark until given one bone, then never again for that player, and a second bone heals it. An untamed zombie calms for one to three minutes on two to four rotten flesh and can be tamed inside the calm. A converted horse its owner already had never turns on them.

The horns on a skeleton

Spawn skeleton eggs until one wears a horn: the part should be bone-coloured, unlit and shed nothing.

The Blackened in the Nether

Visit soul sand valleys and the wastes: Blackened herds should be the horses seen most there, not drowning out the others; one should stand in lava unharmed; and a vanilla skeleton horse taken through a portal should convert into a Blackened, not a Great Valley. Its charred bone has been seen in the side-view render only.

Numbers to tune by eye

The skeleton’s coat_opacity and shade_strength; the Weathered strain’s colour; both breeds’ stat scores (the zombie’s health 10 is about 2.2 times baseline, not a true doubling); the offering amounts and the zombie’s calm window; the drop counts; the size band. None has been looked at in a game.

The test command

Run /horseundead test as an operator: a tamed, named, saddled skeleton horse and then a zombie horse appear beside you, each converts within a second or two, chat says PASSED, and /horseundead enable is accepted only after that. Checked by the conversion gametests and undead_test_command, which runs the command through the real dispatcher; never run in a game.

Switched off

With undead.convert = false, vanilla undead horses stay vanilla and converted ones keep working as horses.

What is still planned here

The spawning overhaul - new undead horses founded as gene-carrying horses rather than converted a tick after vanilla spawns them - and the undeath loci joining other breeds' founder tables.

The spawning overhaul

A server option undead.suppressVanillaSpawns, separate from undead.convert: new undead horses founded as minecraft:horse carrying the gene rather than spawned vanilla and converted a tick later. Skeleton trap horses come from the thunderstorm tick, not FinalizeSpawnEvent; which event, if any, is cleanly cancellable for them in 26.1.2 is unread, and if none is, the converter already handles them and suppression stays an optimisation.

The founder distribution is where the flavour lives. Both genes are recessive, and the founder rule is that a recessive puts its expressing combinations into the wild, never carriers. Which other breeds carry an undeath pair, and how often, is one line per breed sheet — an undead Shire and an undead Falabella are different jokes. The owner’s call for the first release was none (“add per breed later”).

What does not become dead code. ModAttributes’s SKELETON_HORSE/ZOMBIE_HORSE entries and the two undead saddle assets stay even with suppression on: other mods’ undead horses (left alone) and a server with undead.convert off still reach them.

Smaller follow-ups

  • The breed designer offering undead_of and count_groups; today the game and the designer read both, and only a hand-edited file writes them.
  • An adapter for a specific mod’s undead horse (one table entry, behind its own switch).
  • Inverted healing and harming potions on an undead horse — a mixin or event, deliberately not promised.
  • Reading minecraft:’s undead sheets at run time instead of shipping copies, so a resource pack retexturing them reaches these horses — the converter is already the code that would do it.
