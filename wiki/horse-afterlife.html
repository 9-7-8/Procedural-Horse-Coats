Gameplay / operator tooling

Bringing a dead horse back

An operator's undo for the one loss nothing else can reverse. Every other way of losing a horse has a tool that finds it: sold, turned out, bottled, stabled, lost across a dimension. A horse that died had nothing, and the thing that dies is not a stack of items — it is an animal with a name somebody chose, a bond they spent weeks on and a pedigree three generations deep. So a dead horse is now kept whole for a while, and /horseresurrect spends that: the horse that stands back up is the same horse, same id, and every foal's pedigree still points at it. It is an operator's command and nothing a player can reach.

What happens when a horse dies

If the horse had an owner, the whole animal is kept — not a description of it, the animal: its name, its bond, its hunger, its pedigree, its breed, a pregnancy in progress, the lot. Its owner is told, if they are online, that it can still be brought back and that they should ask an operator soon.

Nothing else changes. It still drops what it was going to drop, the death notice still goes out, the pedigree still records it. A wild horse is not kept at all — there is nobody to ask for it, and between the horse realm and ordinary worldgen they die in numbers that would make this the largest thing in the save.

This is the last resort, and three things come first

A horse only reaches this page when everything else failed. The last stand refuses to let a single blow kill one; a rescuing braid sends a braided horse home; the Emergency Stasis Chamber bottles any horse its owner insured, before the blow lands. All three are better than this one, because the player keeps playing through them. Resurrection is what is left.

How long you have

Until the server runs out of room for it. A dead horse is kept however long ago it died, until the store of dead horses reaches ops.resurrect_budget_mb (100 MB by default). Past that the oldest deaths are let go first, and only as many as it takes to fit again. A horse let go can no longer be resurrected; its pedigree record is kept for ever regardless, so no family tree loses it. Set the budget to 0 for no cap at all — a real cost, since a kept horse is a whole entity in the world save. /horseresurrect list says how full the store is.

A server can add a time limit as well: ops.resurrect_grace_minutes, 0 (off) by default. It counts the owner's own time online, not wall clock. Somebody who logged off ten seconds before a creeper found their mare has not spent a minute of their window, because the window is their chance to notice and ask and they were not in the room. The elapsed count keeps running while it is 0, so turning it on later starts expiring the backlog rather than sparing it.

Until issue #15 (2026-10-01) the only limit was that window, at sixty minutes. A server whose config file was written by an older release keeps its 60 until an operator changes it.

The command

/horseresurrect, permission level 2 (game masters), like /horsebounty.

CommandWhat it does
/horseresurrect list [player] What is still recoverable, whose it was, how long ago it died and how much of its window is left. With no arguments, /horseresurrect does this.
/horseresurrect <horse> It stands up in front of you.
/horseresurrect <horse> at <player> In front of somebody else — the usual one, since the owner is generally stood there asking.
/horseresurrect <horse> site Where it died. Loads that chunk. This is the one that can put a horse back in the lava that killed it, which is why it is never the default.
/horseresurrect <horse> egg <player> Into their inventory as an egg, to place where they want it.

<horse> is the horse's name — what the death notice said and what its owner will call it — and tab-completes from what is actually being kept. Two dead horses called Pepper are refused with both ids listed rather than resolved to the more recent one: that is exactly the case where guessing wrong means resurrecting the wrong animal and spending the other one's window while its owner waits.

The resurrection egg

egg hands the player a preset horse spawn egg with their horse inside it, and the tooltip says “brought back whole — the same horse, not a copy” so nobody mistakes it for the ordinary genotype egg. Right-click the ground and the horse is there.

The horse moves out of the server's keeping and into the item at the moment the egg is made. So it stops expiring — it is no longer the save's problem, it is something somebody is holding — and there is only ever one copy. The egg is consumed even in creative, for that reason.

What comes back, and what does not

The same horse. Not a clone with the same coat: the same id, so its foals' family trees, its stall records and any whistle bound to it all still point at an animal that exists. Its name, its bond, its breed, its pedigree, its epigenome and its age come back exactly as they were.

Not its tack. The saddle and the armour dropped on the ground where it fell, and giving them back as well would mint a second set. They are where it died; the horse comes back bare.

Nor does whatever killed it come back on it. The fire is out, the effects are gone, its lungs are full and its health is refilled to what its genes say it should be.

The four pieces

ClassJob
data/HorseAfterlife SavedData, server-global, id horsegenetics:horse_afterlife. A map of horse id to Wake. Holds the snapshot, the owner, where and when it died, and the owner-online ticks spent so far.
server/HorseAfterlifeHandler The writing half: LivingDeathEvent takes the snapshot, and a ServerTickEvent.Post sweep spends the window.
server/HorseResurrection The one place a dead horse is put back on its feet, shared by the command and the egg so the two cannot disagree.
server/HorseResurrectCommand /horseresurrect, and nothing else reads the store.

It is the stasis chamber's payload, pointed at a death

A Wake carries a StasisSnapshot — the dead animal's complete Entity.saveWithoutId tag, which on NeoForge takes the data attachments with it. That is the same payload a stasis chamber holds and the same one the lycan gene has been carrying a horse through the night inside since it shipped, so this is a third caller on a proven mechanism rather than a fourth way to freeze a horse.

The tag is what keeps the UUID, and the UUID is the whole argument for this design. Rebuilding the horse from its HorseRecord instead would have been easier and would have produced an animal with the right coat, the right name, the right stats and the wrong identity — every foal it ever had orphaned, every stall sign pointing at a horse that no longer exists, and nothing anywhere saying so. The gametest exists because that failure is invisible.

It is not a second copy of the ancestry database. HorseAncestryData keeps a dead horse's record for ever on purpose, because the pedigree needs it, and nothing here duplicates that. What is kept here is the part the ancestry data deliberately does not hold: the live entity, as it stood.

The clock is the owner, and it counts up

Wake.ownerTicks counts up and is compared against the setting when it is read, rather than counting a budget down from whatever the setting was at death. That is what lets a config change apply to horses that are already dead — in both directions, including to “for ever” and back.

HorseAfterlife.spend is only ever called for a player who is logged in, which is what makes the clock theirs; the sweep walks the online players every 200 ticks. dropExpired runs in the same sweep and is not redundant with it: a horse belonging to somebody who never comes back would otherwise sit in the save for ever under a window it is already past, the moment an operator lowered the setting.

The consequence to know about

An owner who never logs in again never spends their window, so under the window alone their horses would be kept indefinitely. The size budget is what bounds that now.

The size budget (2026-10-01, issue #15)

The owner's call: the store is held to a size, not a clock, and past it the oldest death (Wake.deathGameTime) goes first. The rule is common/horse/AfterlifeBudget.overflow, pure and tested by AfterlifeBudgetTest; HorseAfterlife.trimTo applies it from the same 200-tick sweep as spend, never on a death, so a burst of deaths costs one trim.

How a horse is measured. Each Wake carries bytes: its entity tag written through NbtIo.writeCompressed into a counting stream, once, when it is kept. Not a stat of the .dat file, which exists only after a save while the sweep runs between saves. The real file is one gzip stream across every wake and compresses a little better than the sum, so the budget errs towards letting go slightly early. A wake saved before the budget has no bytes field; the store measures it as it loads, so an old save is budgeted from its first sweep. The gametest a_dead_horse_comes_back_whole checks both, and that trimTo keeps a horse at budget 0 and drops one a byte over.

Undoing the death, not just the absence

A stasis snapshot is of a healthy horse somebody chose to bottle. This one is of an animal in the instant it died, and the tag is honest about that. HorseResurrection.revive runs before the horse is placed, so it never exists in the world in the state it died in:

  • The gear is cleared, through the entity's own EquipmentSlots rather than by deleting NBT keys — the keys move between versions, and a rename would fail silently as a duplicated saddle rather than as a crash. The snapshot is taken before vanilla drops the tack, so it is in the tag and on the ground.
  • Effects, fire and air are reset. A horse that died of wither would otherwise stand up and die again, which reads as the command not working.
  • The health is refilled from the genotype, not from the tag — the tag says zero. The same applyTraitsToEntity(.., true) a newborn foal gets.

The corpse has to go first (2026-09-26)

A horse does not leave the world the instant it dies: it falls over for twenty ticks and is removed at the end of that. For the whole of that second the corpse still holds the UUID, so addFreshEntity refuses the resurrected horse with “UUID of added entity already exists” — a warning in the log, a null return, and an operator told the horse could not be read.

That is the most likely resurrection there is: the one typed while the owner is still shouting about it. HorseResurrection.clearCorpse discards any entity with that id that is not isAlive(), across every level, before restoring. Its loot has already dropped by then, so nothing is destroyed but the animation. A genuinely living horse with that id is a different matter and is refused by alreadyAlive instead.

This was found by the gametest below, on its first run, and not by reading the code.

The egg is the preset egg

ModDataComponents.RESURRECTION is a StasisSnapshot on a preset_horse_spawn_egg, and PresetHorseSpawnEggItem.useOn checks for it before STORED_GENOME. Reusing that item rather than adding one is the same call GeneDeathHandler.cloneEgg already makes for the death drop: the two are the same thing arrived at from different directions, and one of them already has a model, a tooltip and a spawner behind it.

It is a component of its own rather than STASIS_SNAPSHOT even though the type is identical, because the two mean different things to the item carrying them — a chamber holding a snapshot is a full chamber and its whole state machine turns on that. The saving would have been one registration line.

One exception, written down

HorseWhereabouts.seen refuses to un-kill a horse, on purpose, so that a stale sighting cannot. seenAlive is the single caller entitled to say otherwise, and exists so the browser and the ender whistle stop insisting a horse is dead while its owner is riding it.

What needs verifying here

Nothing on this page has been played: the round trip is proven by a gametest and by nothing else, so the first check is the plain one — own a horse, name it, get its bond up, let it die, and confirm /horseresurrect list shows it and /horseresurrect <name> at <player> hands back an animal whose bond, name and pedigree are all still there and whose foals still list it as a parent; the saddle is the one that would be a duplication bug rather than a disappointment, so kill a tacked horse and check the saddle is on the ground and not also on the horse; the owner-online clock has never been watched over a real hour and the thing to confirm is the half that is easy to get backwards — that a horse dies, its owner logs off for an hour, and it is still there when they come back; the site placement can put a horse back in the lava that killed it and wants somebody to try exactly that on purpose; and the size budget has only been proven on one test horse, so a real server should confirm that the sum of measured wakes tracks the real horse_afterlife.dat and that the oldest deaths go first once it fills.

The round trip, with a real horse (not played)

The gametest horsegenetics:a_dead_horse_comes_back_whole asserts the id, the health, the empty saddle slot and the record, and it is the only thing that has ever exercised any of this. It runs in a bare test level against a horse ten ticks old with a UUID.randomUUID() for an owner, which is not a player, not bonded, and not carrying anything.

  • Tame a horse, bond it past 50, breed a foal from it, then kill it and bring it back. Pass: same name, bond intact, and the foal's family tree still draws the parent box. Fail: if the family tree greys the parent out, the id did not survive and the gametest is asserting something narrower than it looks.
  • Kill a saddled, armoured horse and resurrect it. Pass: one saddle, on the ground. Fail: two saddles is the duplication bug revive exists to prevent.

The owner-online clock (not played)

  • Kill an owned horse, log its owner off for well over an hour, log back in. Pass: /horseresurrect list still shows it with its window untouched. Fail: if it has expired, the sweep is charging wall clock somewhere and the whole argument for the design is not being honoured.
  • Watch one expire. Set ops.resurrect_grace_minutes to 2, kill a horse, and stay online. Pass: it is gone from the listing at about two minutes and the save shrinks. Fail: the sweep interval or the tick arithmetic is wrong.
  • Lower the setting with a backlog waiting. Keep several, then drop the setting below their elapsed time. Pass: the next sweep drops them even for owners who are offline — that is what dropExpired is for.

The egg (not played)

  • Hand one over and place it. Pass: the tooltip names the horse, the horse appears, the egg is gone even in creative, and the horse is no longer in /horseresurrect list. Fail: an egg that survives its use is a UUID duplicator.
  • Copy the egg in creative and use both. Pass: the second one refuses, out loud, because the horse is already alive.

Placement (not played)

  • site into lava, on purpose. The command warns and then does it. Pass: the horse appears and burns, and the operator was told it might. Fail: if it silently fails instead, the chunk force-load is not doing what atSite assumes.
  • site into an unloaded chunk in another dimension. Pass: the horse is there when somebody walks to it.

The size budget against a real file (not played)

The budget counts each wake's own gzipped tag (Coding), which should slightly overstate the file. Only the gametest has exercised it, on one horse.

  • Fill a small budget. Set ops.resurrect_budget_mb to 1, kill owned horses one by one, and compare /horseresurrect list's “the store holds” line with the size of data/horsegenetics_horse_afterlife.dat after a save. Pass: the listed figure is at or a little above the file, never below it; once it passes 1 MB the oldest death leaves the listing and the log says let go. Fail: a file bigger than the listed figure means the measure undercounts and the budget will overshoot.
