Magic day temper

What the horse becomes while the sun is up — the daylight twin of magic night temper. The same eight variants: four that hunt and four that run, from riders, passive animals, monsters or everything. After dark every one of them is an ordinary horse.

It takes a matched pair, exactly as the night locus does. The tokens are the same, so a horse can be Agh/Agh at both loci — a monster-hunter at every hour — or hunt by day and flee by night.

Turning the night locus inside out

The night locus is about the animal you ride all week turning into something else after dark. This one is the reverse, and it is stranger for it: Flp is a horse you can only catch at night, and Agp is one that is perfectly calm when the monsters come out and dangerous in the middle of the afternoon.

Rare in the wild

Unlike the night locus, where every wild horse hides one allele, most wild horses carry nothing here. Each variant is carried silently by about one wild horse in a hundred, and none shows it. A day hunter is something you find, not something every herd is hiding.

Health

No disorder. The aggressive variants will pick fights in daylight and can get the horse killed, and Fla will not be where you left it at noon.

One handler for both halves of the day

NightBehaviourHandler runs the night pair and this pair. By day it takes the day_temper and day_watch a horse carries, by night the night ones, and everything after that — the supersede rule, the flee path, the watch goal — is shared code that never learns which half of the day it is: the day records are handed on as night records, because their fields are identical.

The scan used to return before reading the genome by day. It cannot now, so each horse’s four time-of-day abilities are cached against the genetic code they were resolved from, and a horse with none of them costs a map lookup.

Day and night animals

Horses are neither strictly diurnal nor nocturnal. Free-ranging horses graze and move in bouts around the clock, sleep in short stretches, and are often most active around dawn and dusk. So there is no real “day horse” for this locus to model — it is the night locus’s magic, reflected, and the interest is entirely in what a breeder can do with a horse that is one animal at noon and another at midnight.
