Jockey passes

Lending somebody your horse for an afternoon. A pass is made out to one animal by its owner, handed to whoever is going to ride it, and fed to that horse to buy a day in the saddle — and then it runs out on its own, which is the whole point of it.

How they work

  1. Craft a blank jockey pass: paper, a horse hair and a piece of leather, shapeless. It names nobody and does nothing.
  2. Right-click a horse you own with the blank. It becomes a pass made out to that animal by name.
  3. Give the pass to whoever is going to ride. It is an ordinary item from here on — hand it over, put it in a chest, sell it at the gate.
  4. They right-click that horse with it. The horse takes the pass and they may ride it for one Minecraft day.

Why this exists

A horse will not carry a stranger. The two standing ways round that — owning the horse, or being on the owner's team — are both permanent relationships, and neither of them can say this person, this horse, this afternoon. Putting a jockey on your team so they can ride one mount in one race hands them every horse you own, indefinitely, and taking them off again afterwards is a chore nobody is going to do. A race meeting needs something that expires by itself.

Things worth knowing

  • A pass is made out to the horse, not to the person. That is what makes it worth having as an object: a pass for Kestrel, one day is something you can hand to whoever turns up to ride, sell at the gate, or post to a friend. A pass made out to a person would have to be assigned when it was made, and a jockey who did not show up would take it out of the meeting with them.
  • Only the owner can make one out, and creative is no exception — the same rule every other item you bind to a horse follows. Anybody holding a made-out pass can feed it.
  • Feeding a second pass adds another day; it does not replace what is left. A three-day meeting is three passes, and a jockey handed two on the first morning is not quietly refunded one.
  • It only works on the horse it names. A pass for Kestrel does nothing at all to anything else, and says so.
  • The owner is told when somebody feeds a pass to their horse, if they are online. Lending a horse is not a secret, and an owner who finds a stranger on their mare should be able to remember why.
  • Selling the horse cancels every pass on it. A pass is permission the previous owner gave; carrying it across a sale would hand the new owner's horse to strangers they never agreed to and could not find out about.
  • You cannot feed one to your own horse. It would buy you nothing, so you are told rather than quietly charged a pass.

The command — /horsejockey

The same lend, without the crafting, for the case the item is clumsy at: a race steward at the gate with twelve riders to admit and no wish to make twelve passes. It acts on the horse you are riding, or the one you are looking at within eight blocks, and every form needs you to own that horse.

FormWhat it does
/horsejockey <player> One pass's worth, the same length an item buys.
/horsejockey <player> <days> That many Minecraft days, added to whatever is left.
/horsejockey <player> revoke Take it back now. If they are on the horse, they come off.
/horsejockey list Who is borrowing this horse, and for how much longer.
  • Revoking has no item, and should not: something you have handed to somebody cannot be un-handed, but the person who lent the horse ought to be able to change their mind.
  • No operator override. An operator settling a dispute can move ownership with /horsegive; being able to write yourself onto somebody else's horse for a fortnight is a different power and nobody asked for it.
  • commands.horse_jockey turns the command off — not registered, not tab-completed — and leaves the item alone. behaviour.jockey_pass_days is what one pass, or one bare /horsejockey, is worth.

Where it lives

  • data/RidingPassAttachment — one map, player UUID to the game time their pass runs out. Registered as horsegenetics:riding_pass, serialised, copyOnDeath, and not synced: the refusal happens on the server, and a client that knew would only be able to predict it wrong.
  • server/JockeyPassHandler — the item's three moments, on PlayerInteractEvent.EntityInteract.
  • server/HorseJockeyCommand — the four command forms.
  • item/JockeyPassItem — the tooltip, which is the only part that has to exist on the client. Two item ids sharing one class, reusing the bound_horse component the stall signs already defined.
  • server/HorseRiding.mayRide — where it is actually read.

The pass is asked before the teams, deliberately

mayRide asks, in order: the config flag, untamed, the owner, a live pass, then the teams. The pass sits above the teams so that it still works on a server which has set behaviour.riding_allows_teams = false. They are unrelated answers — one is a standing relationship and one is a receipt with a date on it — and one flag silently gating the other would be a trap rather than a setting.

A map of deadlines, not a set of names

The same call passification's attachment makes, for the same reason: one map with a time in it cannot disagree with itself, whereas a set of names beside a map of expiries eventually will. Reading it is one comparison.

Deadlines are game time, which does not advance while the server is down — so a pass bought for a race is not spent overnight while nobody is playing. grant counts from the existing deadline when one is still running and from now when it is not, which is what stops a second pass being worth less than the first, and every write prunes what has lapsed so a horse does not carry a dead jockey's name in the save for ever.

All three of those are invisible from inside the game if they are wrong: a second pass that overwrote the first would look identical on the day it was fed and would quietly have cost somebody a day. So they are the one part of this with a test — jockey_passes_add_up, which was checked against a deliberately broken grant to make sure it bites.

What needs verifying here

None of this has run: feeding a pass needs a second player, and so does every form of the command. The arithmetic is tested and the rest is not — the check that matters most is not the pass working but the pass expiring, because a pass that never runs out is indistinguishable from one that works for the whole of the day somebody tests it on.

The item (2026-09-29, NOT played)

  • Made out. Right-click your own horse with a blank. Pass: the blank becomes a pass naming her, and a stack of blanks loses exactly one. Fail looks like the whole stack turning into one pass, which is the swap in makeOut taking the wrong branch.
  • Refused on a horse you do not own, and on an untamed one, with the ordinary bind refusal.
  • Fed. Second player, your pass, your horse. Pass: the horse makes the eating sound, the pass is consumed, they are told how long they have, you are told who took it, and they can get on. Fail looks like them being bucked off anyway (the attachment write and mayRide's read disagree — most likely the level is not a ServerLevel where one of them looks), or the pass being eaten with nothing granted.
  • It expires. The one that actually matters and the one nobody will run. Feed a pass, then /time add 24000 — game time, not the day/night cycle. Pass: the horse throws them at the next check. Fail: they ride on, which means the deadline is being read against the wrong clock and every pass is permanent.
  • The wrong horse. A pass for one mare, fed to another: it should say so and be kept.
  • A sale cancels it. Lend a horse, then give it away with /horsegive or a redeemed paper. Pass: the borrower is off it and cannot get back on.

The command (2026-09-29, NOT played)

  • All four forms, on a horse you own: bare, with a day count, revoke, and list.
  • revoke takes somebody off mid-ride rather than leaving them to the sweep a second later.
  • list names an offline borrower. It goes through the server's name caches, the same route the scoreboard-team lookup uses, and a borrower neither cache has seen falls back to eight characters of their id.
  • It refuses a horse that is not yours, for every form including list — who is borrowing your horse is your business.
  • The switch. commands.horse_jockey = false, restart: the command should not exist, and the item should still work.
  • behaviour.jockey_pass_days moves both. Set it to 2 and check that a fed pass and a bare /horsejockey both report two days left — if only one moved, the command has a hard-coded 1 in it. (The item’s tooltip deliberately says for a while rather than naming a length: it is drawn on a client that may have no server behind it.)
