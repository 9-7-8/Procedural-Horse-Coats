Horse gear / hoof equipment

Horse shoes

A horse shoe is one item to a hoof: four separate stacks in four separate gear slots, each in its own metal, drawn under the foot it is on. Five ship (copper, iron, golden, diamond and netherite) and every metal another mod brings gets one of its own. They are bought from the equestrian metalsmith. Each worn shoe adds a little speed and jump from its own metal, and a horse with at least one shoe on stands on powder snow. A shoe can take one of two enchantments: Grip, for ice, or Roadster, for paths and roads.

What they are

A horse shoe is a real piece of gear worn on a hoof. Each hoof holds its own shoe, so a horse can be shod on one foot, or in four different metals, and nothing treats the four as a set.

  • One shoe per hoof. Near fore, off fore, near hind, off hind: the four Shoe slots at the bottom of the Gear tab's paper doll.
  • Each shoe is independent. One can be taken off or swapped without touching the other three.
  • Any shoe fits any hoof. There is no left or right shoe.
  • Foals are not shod. A foal wears no gear at all.

Getting them

Shoes are bought from the equestrian metalsmith, one metal at each of his five levels, cheapest first. There is no crafting recipe: tack is to be made at the tack bench, which is not built yet (owner, 2026-10-12), and the bench will add the making. All of them are in the mod's creative tab.

A metal or gem another mod adds (tin, steel, ruby) gets a shoe named after it, in that metal's own colour, sold at the level its horse armour is sold at. A mod's own copy of a metal above gets no second shoe.

Putting them on

Open the horse's menu, go to the Gear tab, and put a shoe in a hoof slot. Or right-click your own tamed horse with a shoe in hand: it goes on the first bare hoof, near fore first. Click a shod slot to take the shoe back.

What they look like

A shoe is a U of metal under the foot, open at the heel, so the frog shows inside it. From the side only a thin edge shows at the ground; the whole shoe shows under a lifted foot, on a rearing or galloping horse. It is the colour of its metal, and the icon in your inventory is the same colour.

What they do

Each shoe adds a little speed and a little jump, from its own metal. The four hooves are added up, so three shoes are three quarters of four, and four different metals are four different amounts. There is no bonus for a matching set. A shoe taken off takes its share with it.

MetalSpeed, one shoeSpeed, fourJump, four (about)
Copper+0.09 m/s+0.35 m/s+0.11 m
Iron+0.13 m/s+0.52 m/s+0.17 m
Golden+0.17 m/s+0.69 m/s+0.23 m
Diamond+0.22 m/s+0.86 m/s+0.28 m
Netherite+0.26 m/s+1.04 m/s+0.34 m

Those are the defaults, and they are small on purpose: an ordinary horse runs at about 5 to 14 m/s, so the best four shoes are worth roughly a tenth of an average horse and how fast a horse is stays a matter of breeding. Jump height is not a straight line in the number behind it, so the jump column is for an average jumper; a strong jumper gets a little more from the same shoe. A shoe's tooltip states what one of it adds on the server you are on, and the horse's own screen shows the total in its Speed and Jump lines.

One shoe is enough for powder snow. A horse with a shoe on any hoof, of any metal, stands on top of powder snow the way a player in leather boots does. An unshod horse sinks.

Another mod's metal gives what iron gives if it is an ingot and what diamond gives if it is a gem, the same rung its horse armour stands on.

A server can change every one of those numbers, or switch shoes off altogether (the settings).

Enchanting them

A horse shoe takes one of two enchantments, each with three levels. They are the only enchantments that go on a shoe, and they go on nothing else.

EnchantmentWhat it doesOne shoe, per levelFour shoes at III
GripThe horse slides less on ice, packed ice, blue ice and anything else slippery.8% of the slide gone96%: ice is very nearly ordinary ground
RoadsterThe horse is faster while it is on a path or a road.+0.09 m/s+1.04 m/s
  • Each shoe carries its own. The four hooves are added up, like the metals: two shoes of Grip II are the same as one of Grip III and one of Grip I.
  • One shoe, one enchantment. Grip and Roadster will not go on the same shoe. A horse can still wear both, on different feet: two Grip shoes and two Roadster shoes is a horse for a road that crosses a frozen river.
  • Grip never makes ice better than ground. It only takes the slide away, so a horse on grass is exactly as it was.
  • What counts as a road for Roadster: dirt paths (the paths a village is built on, and any grass you have run a shovel over), cobblestone and mossy cobblestone, stone bricks of every kind, bricks and smooth sandstone, and the slabs of those. The speed arrives within a stride of stepping on and leaves within a stride of stepping off; a jump does not lose it.
  • Getting one. Put a shoe in an enchanting table, or put a Grip or Roadster book on a shoe at an anvil. The books turn up wherever ordinary enchanted books do: the table, librarians and loot.
  • An enchanted shoe glints in your inventory and in the Gear tab, and its tooltip says what its enchantment is worth on the server you are on. The shoe drawn under the hoof does not glint.

Why they are different from boots

Boots are a leg item and sit in the four Boot slots above the shoes. A boot is about the leg as a whole and a shoe is about the ground under the hoof, so they are separate slots and a horse may wear both.

How it is built

A shoe is an ordinary stack in an ordinary gear slot. Nothing about the slots, the attachment, the sync or the Gear tab is shoe-specific (Horse gear: the slots); what this feature added is the items, their place in four tags, and the step that turns four worn stacks into four drawn parts. Built 2026-10-12 (#164).

The items

  • item/HorseShoeItem: one class, an item per metal. The metal is the item and not a component, so the colour is a constant of the item (tint()). It stacks to 1.
  • common/gear/HorseShoes.Metal lists the shipped metals, their ids and colours. ModItems.HORSE_SHOES registers one per entry. The ids use vanilla's spelling (golden_horse_shoe).
  • compat/ModdedShoes registers one per material out of ModdedMaterials.armourMetals(), the list generated horse armour uses, so several mods' steels are one shoe. HorseShoes.ships(material) skips a mod's own copper, iron, gold, diamond or netherite. A shoe whose mod was removed loads as an iron shoe (an alias, by droppedArmours()).
  • compat/GeneratedShoes writes each modded shoe's item definition, name, its entry in the four tags and its trade. It writes no texture and no model.

The slots and the tags

The four hoof slots are HorseTackSlot.SHOE_FRONT_LEFT, _FRONT_RIGHT, _HIND_LEFT and _HIND_RIGHT, and each accepts its item tag, horsegenetics:gear/shoe_*. All four tags list every shoe. The generated pack's copies say replace: false and list only the modded shoes, because tags merge across packs.

One icon, tinted

Every shoe, shipped or generated, uses the one greyscale sprite textures/item/horse_shoe.png (drawn by tools/bake-drawn-items.mjs; a placeholder for the owner to repaint) through the one model item/horse_shoe. Its item definition names the horsegenetics:metal_tint tint source, and client/MetalTintSource answers with HorseShoeItem.tint().

Drawing them

A worn shoe is not an item model. It is the box-built PartKind.SHOE (the worn side of the attached parts), one mesh for four feet and every metal. GeneticHorseRenderer reads the four slots once per frame and calls HorseShoes.worn(frontLeft, frontRight, hindLeft, hindRight) with each shoe's tint, 0 for a bare hoof. That returns one WornPart per shod hoof on that hoof's PartAnchor, and AttachedPartLayer draws them. An unshod horse allocates nothing. The icon and the worn shoe take their colour from the same method, so they cannot disagree.

The trades

The five shipped trades are files under villager_trade/equestrian_metalsmith/level_N/, listed in that level's trade_set, whose amount went from 3 to 4 so that a shoe never pushes an armour or a buying trade out of a level. A trade set is a registry and does not merge across packs, so the generated pack rewrites a level's file whole: compat/MetalsmithTrades collects what generated armour and generated shoes each add and writes each level once. A modded shoe is sold only where one of its metal's items is registered, the rule its armour follows (#14).

Adding shoes changed what the generated pack holds, so the scan's fingerprint rules number went up (MaterialScan.fingerprint, rules|4): a pack generated by an older jar is rebuilt on the first launch.

What a worn shoe does

Built 2026-10-12 (#247). The rule is pure and lives in common/gear/HorseShoes: bonus(rates, frontLeft, frontRight, hindLeft, hindRight) takes each hoof's Metal (or null for a bare one) and returns a Bonus, the plain sum of what each metal adds. shod(rates, shoes) is the powder snow rule: any one shoe, and it does not depend on a metal's numbers, so a metal set to zero still carries it. Since #248 the full form is bonus(rates, onRoad, Shoe, Shoe, Shoe, Shoe) (the enchantments); the form that takes metals is four plain shoes off a road.

  • The rung. HorseShoeItem.tier() is the Metal the bonus is read from: itself for a shipped shoe, and HorseShoes.tierOf(gem) for a modded one (a gem where diamond is, an ingot where iron is). That is the answer ModdedArmour.protectionFor gives and it is pure for the same reason: nothing about another mod's item can be read when ours is registered.
  • The modifiers. server/HorseShoeEffects.refresh reads the four hoof slots and holds the sum as one transient ADD_VALUE modifier on each attribute, horsegenetics:shoes/speed on MOVEMENT_SPEED and shoes/jump on JUMP_STRENGTH. A modifier is replaced only when its number differs and removed when the sum is zero, so an unshod horse carries none.
  • When it runs. Not every tick. Shoes change only when a hoof slot is written, so HorseTackSlot.set calls it for a slot in Zone.HOOVES, and EntityJoinLevelEvent calls it once for a shod horse, because a transient modifier is never in the save. This is HorsePackHandler.refresh's shape and not GeneAbilityHandler's per-tick reconcile. The price: a changed shoes.* number reaches a horse when it next loads or is next shod.
  • Saves. Nothing new is saved. The shoes are the stacks already in the gear attachment and the modifiers are rebuilt from them.
  • The pack's slowdown is ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL, so it scales the shod speed: a loaded horse loses the same share of its shoes' speed as of its own.

Powder snow

Vanilla asks one static question, PowderSnowBlock.canEntityWalkOnPowderSnow(Entity), from the block's collision shape and from LivingEntity's climb out of it. It answers yes for the powder_snow_walkable_mobs entity-type tag or for the item in the FEET slot. Neither fits: the tag would float every horse, shod or not, and a horse has no feet slot. There is no method on the entity to override (26.1.2 has no Entity.canStandOnPowderSnow(), which the treatment expected), so mixin/PowderSnowShoesMixin injects at RETURN of that static method and says yes for an AbstractHorse that HorseShoeEffects.shod, only when vanilla said no. It runs on both sides, because a ridden horse is moved by its rider's client; the gear attachment it reads is synced.

The enchantments

Built 2026-10-12 (#248), the mod's first. Grip (horsegenetics:grip) and Roadster (horsegenetics:roadster) are data, data/horsegenetics/enchantment/*.json, with no effects block at all: a horse's shoes are four gear slots of this mod's, which no vanilla equipment slot reaches, so nothing vanilla would ever run an effect component. The files only make the enchantment exist, say what it goes on and what it costs. What it does is read in Java.

  • What they go on. supported_items is the item tag horsegenetics:enchantable/horse_shoe, whose one entry is #horsegenetics:gear/shoe_front_left. Every shoe, shipped or generated for another mod's metal, is already in that tag, so the generated pack needed no change and its fingerprint did not move. Every shoe is registered enchantable (ShoeEnchantments.ENCHANTABILITY, iron armour's), which is what an enchanting table asks for.
  • Where they come from. Both are in minecraft:non_treasure (data/minecraft/tags/enchantment/non_treasure.json, replace: false), the one tag vanilla's table, librarian and loot tags all include.
  • The conflict. Both name the enchantment tag horsegenetics:exclusive_set/horse_shoe as their exclusive_set, so a table or an anvil puts one of the two on a shoe and never both. That is the whole conflict: it is per shoe, and the four hooves may mix them. A stack given both by a command gets both; nothing in Java second-guesses it.
  • The read. item/ShoeEnchantments.shoeOf(stack) turns a worn stack into a HorseShoes.Shoe(metal, grip, road), the two levels read off that stack's own ENCHANTMENTS component. It is the only place a shoe's enchantments are read, on both sides. A level above three counts as three.
  • One total. HorseShoes.bonus(rates, onRoad, ...) sums each shoe's metal, each shoe's roadster when onRoad, and each shoe's grip, into one Bonus(speed, jump, grip). Roadster's speed is part of the same shoes/speed modifier as the metal's; there is no second modifier. Grip is a share from 0 to 1 and stops at 1.
  • The road tick. The metal's bonus needs no tick, but Roadster depends on the block under the horse, which no event reports. HorseShoeEffects.refresh asks HorseShoes.watchesRoad, and only a horse some roadster would speed up is put in a weak map with what its ground last was. EntityTickEvent.Post looks at the block under those horses every ROAD_CHECK_TICKS (4), and calls the same refresh when the answer changed. With the map empty the handler is one isEmpty() per entity. Air underfoot (a jump, a ledge) is no answer, so the horse keeps what it had. A road is the block tag horsegenetics:shoe_roads, which a pack can add to.
  • Grip. How far a mob slides is decided in one line of LivingEntity.travelInAir: getBlockState(posBelow).getFriction(level, posBelow, this), stored in the local blockFriction. There is no event, and NeoForge's getFriction hook is for a mod's own blocks. mixin/ShoeGripMixin is a @ModifyVariable on that store (the method's first float local): for a block slicker than ordinary ground (0.6) under an AbstractHorse it returns HorseShoes.friction(blockFriction, grip), the block's friction brought that share of the way down to 0.6. Everything else returns at the first comparison. It is a ModifyVariable and not a Redirect so another mod changing the same number composes with it. Both sides, as powder snow is.
  • The worn shoe has no glint. The stack glints wherever an item is drawn (vanilla's foil); the box-built shoe on the hoof is drawn by AttachedPartLayer, which has no glint pass. That is on the Roadmap tab.

The settings

Server-side, in phc/server.toml, read together through ServerConfig.shoeRates(), which is the only thing that builds a HorseShoes.Rates.

  • shoes.enabled (default on): the family's one switch. Off, shoes are still bought, worn and drawn, add nothing, and do not hold a horse up on powder snow.
  • shoes.<metal>_speed and shoes.<metal>_jump, for copper, iron, gold, diamond and netherite: what one shoe of that metal adds, in the attribute's own units. The defaults are the DEFAULT_* constants in HorseShoes. Each is capped per shoe (MAX_SPEED, MAX_JUMP) and there is no cap across shoes (owner, 2026-10-09).
  • The iron pair is also what another mod's ingot metals give, and the diamond pair its gems.
  • shoes.grip_per_level (default 0.08, range 0 to 0.34): the share of the slide one level of Grip on one shoe takes away. It is read live, on both sides, so a change is felt at once. 0 turns Grip off.
  • shoes.road_speed_per_level (default 0.002, range 0 to 0.015): the movement speed one level of Roadster on one shoe adds on a road. A change reaches a horse when it next loads, is next shod, or next steps on or off a road. 0 turns Roadster off, and then no horse's ground is watched.
  • The top of each range is a constant in HorseShoes (MAX_GRIP_PER_LEVEL, MAX_ROAD_SPEED_PER_LEVEL), as the metals' caps are. shoes.enabled turns both enchantments off with everything else.

The tooltip reads the same Rates, so it states the server's numbers and not the defaults; with the family off it says shoes change nothing on this server.

What is tested

  • HorseShoesTest (common), the bonus: four metals each add their own amount and taking any one off loses exactly its share; a bare horse gets nothing and each better metal gives more, inside the cap; a server's numbers are capped per shoe and not across shoes, an unreadable one falls back to the default, and the switch turns off both the bonus and powder snow; one shoe is enough for powder snow even when its metal adds nothing; a modded gem takes diamond's rung and an ingot iron's. Replacing the sum with an assignment turns two red.
  • Gametest shoes_add_their_bonus: on a real horse, four metals put on one at a time each move both attributes by their own amount; one taken off takes its share; a refresh from bare modifiers puts them back (what a loading horse gets); all four off leaves the horse exactly what it was with no modifier on it. Removing the call in HorseTackSlot.set turns it red.
  • Gametest a_shod_horse_stands_on_powder_snow: an unshod horse is refused by canEntityWalkOnPowderSnow and handed an empty collision shape; with one copper shoe it is accepted and handed a solid one; with the shoe off, refused again. Making the mixin answer no turns it red.
  • HorseShoesTest (common), the enchantments (#248): grip and roadster on different shoes fold into one total with the metals, roadster only on a road and speed only; the same shoes on other hooves are the same total and two roadsters add; only a roadster horse has its ground watched; a level past three counts as three; grip takes its share of ice's slide, never past ordinary ground, and leaves ordinary ground exactly alone; a server's number is capped per level and the total stops at 1; the switch turns both off. Adding roadster off a road turns one red.
  • Gametest shoe_enchantments_fold_into_the_total: both enchantments are registered, refuse to share a shoe, are offered at the enchanting table, go on every shipped shoe and not on boots, a saddle, horse armour or a sword, and every shoe is enchantable. Four shoes with four different loads read back hoof by hoof, off the horse and again through the gear attachment's own save codec. One Grip II shoe makes ice less slick by exactly its share. On stone the speed modifier is the metals' alone; with a dirt path put under the horse and no call from the test it becomes metals plus roadsters, and on stone again it goes back. Disabling the tick's refresh turns it red (0.014 where 0.022 was wanted).
  • Gametest grip_shoes_slide_less_on_ice: two horses on one sheet of packed ice get the same shove; the unshod one must slide at least a block and a half and the one in four Grip III shoes at most half as far. Making the mixin return the block's own friction turns it red (both slid 2.79 blocks).
  • HorseShoesTest (common): an unshod horse draws nothing; one shoe is drawn on its own hoof; four metals stay apart, in hoof order; a black metal is still a shoe; shipped ids are unique and a mod's copy of a shipped metal gets no second shoe. Reversing the hoof order turns it red.
  • Gametest shoes_fit_their_hooves: every shipped shoe is accepted by all four hoof slots and fits no other slot; four metals on four feet read back as put on; taking one off hands it back and leaves the other three; a foal refuses. Removing one shoe from one tag turns it red.

Design reasoning

Shoes are a good fit for the mod because they are a piece of utility gear that is real and visible but still anchored to the horse rather than to a chest or a ride mode. They also create a clean dividing line between purely cosmetic tack and useful wearable items: a shoe is not decoration, it changes how the horse interacts with the world. That matches the mod's utility-over-decoration rule: what a shoe does (its metal's speed and jump, powder snow, grip on ice, speed on a road) is what makes it worth buying, and how it looks comes second.

The design keeps each shoe as a separate stack on a separate hoof, because a horse can throw one shoe and wear unevenly, and a single "shod" flag could never say that. It is also why the bonus is a flat amount per shoe and not a percentage: four shoes are four additions, and a horse with three is exactly three quarters shod.

Why a U under the foot

The owner's shape (2026-10-11): a shoe is seen on the underside of the foot with only the barest bit on the hoof wall, runs round the front and sides and not the back, and is a U underneath too, so the frog shows. A band round the whole hoof wall would read as a boot.

What needs verifying here

Horse shoes were built on 2026-10-12 with their slots, their speed and jump bonus, their powder snow rule and their two enchantments covered by gametests and their look by a photo shoot, and no player has bought, worn, enchanted or ridden with one; the modded-metal half has not been run beside a real mod.

The shipped shoes (2026-10-12, NOT played)

  • Buy one. A novice equestrian metalsmith should offer a Copper Horse Shoe for 2 emeralds beside his other three trades, and each later level the next metal.
  • The icons. Five shoes in the creative tab, each the colour of its metal, none grey or purple-and-black.
  • Shoe a horse in the Gear tab, one foot at a time. Each shoe should appear under the foot whose slot it went in: the near (left) side slots on the horse's left feet.
  • Right-click your own horse with a shoe: it should go on, near fore first, and a fifth shoe should be refused and stay in your hand.
  • Ride it. At a gallop the shoes should stay under the feet and not swim, lag or flicker; standing, only a thin edge should show.
  • Save, quit and reload. All four shoes still on, in their metals.
  • Another player on a server should see your horse's shoes.

What a shoe does (2026-10-12, NOT played)

  • The numbers move. Open a horse's screen, note Speed and Jump, shoe all four feet in netherite: Speed should rise by about 1.0 m/s and Jump by about 0.3 m. Take one off and a quarter of that goes.
  • It is felt. Ride the same horse bare and shod along a measured line. The ridden horse is moved by the rider's client, so this is the check that the modifier reaches it.
  • After a relog and after a server restart the shod horse still has its bonus (the modifiers are rebuilt when it joins the level; only the rebuild itself is tested).
  • Powder snow. Lead or ride an unshod horse onto powder snow: it sinks. Put one copper shoe on: it stands on top, ridden and unridden, and can walk off the edge and back on. Take the shoe off while it stands there: it sinks.
  • The tooltip on each shoe states its own speed and jump and the powder snow line; with shoes.enabled off it says shoes change nothing on this server.
  • The settings. Set shoes.iron_speed to 0.05, reload the horse: an iron shoe is now plainly fast. Set shoes.enabled to false: no bonus after the horse reloads, and it sinks in powder snow at once.
  • With a loaded pack the shod horse is still slowed, and still faster than the same horse unshod with the same load.

The two enchantments (2026-10-12, NOT played)

  • The table. Put a shoe in an enchanting table with bookshelves round it: it should offer Grip or Roadster and nothing else, and never both on one shoe.
  • The anvil. A Grip book goes on a shoe; a Roadster book is then refused by that shoe; neither book goes on boots or horse armour. Two Grip I shoes combine to Grip II.
  • It shows. An enchanted shoe glints in the inventory and in its Gear tab slot, lists its enchantment, and its last tooltip line states the percentage or the m/s.
  • Grip is felt. Ride a horse across a frozen lake bare, then in four Grip III shoes: it should turn and stop about as it does on grass. This is the ridden case, moved by the rider's client, which the gametest does not cover.
  • Roadster is felt. Ride a horse in four Roadster III shoes along a village path and off it: the Speed line on its screen should read about 1.0 m/s more on the path, and it should not stutter where path and grass alternate.
  • Mixed. Two Grip and two Roadster shoes on one horse: both work, each at half.
  • The settings. Set shoes.grip_per_level to 0: Grip shoes do nothing at once, and their tooltip loses its line.
  • Other mods' enchanting. Not looked at: a mod that lists or applies enchantments by its own rules (an enchantment-description mod reads the .desc lang keys, which are written).

Shoes for another mod's metals (2026-10-12)

  • Beside a real mod with a tin or steel ingot: a Tin Horse Shoe in the creative tab, tinted, wearable on a hoof, and sold by the metalsmith at the level its armour is.
  • A mod with its own copper ingot should add no second Copper Horse Shoe.
  • The rung. A tin shoe's tooltip should state iron's numbers, and a modded gem's shoe diamond's.
  • The alias. Shoe a horse in a modded metal, remove the mod, load the world: the shoe should load as an iron one. Not tried against a real save.
What is still planned here

Shoes are to be made at the tack bench, wear out with distance and turn up in loot, and an enchanted shoe is to glint on the hoof as it does in the hand.

What is left

The stat bonus, powder snow and the two enchantments shipped on 2026-10-12 (what a worn shoe does, the enchantments), which finishes the shoes' own stage. What is left belongs to later pieces of the equipment release.

  1. Made at the tack bench (#226): a shoe from a shape and a metal, replacing "bought only".
  2. Durability (#236): shoes wear with distance, off by default in config.
  3. Loot (#238): shoes in chests and on stable and villager horses.
  4. A glint on the worn shoe (with #228, enchanted tack): a glint pass in AttachedPartLayer for a worn part whose stack is enchanted. Today only the item glints. A shoe shows little more than an edge on a standing horse, so this was left for the piece that makes every worn piece enchantable.

Checks those pieces owe
