Fertility — how readily a horse breeds

Three alleles. n is ordinary. Two copies of sf make every breeding half as likely to take. tw makes a pregnancy more likely to be twins. Every copy also carries a small number that nudges a mare’s odds. What a pregnancy is, and when a mare can have one, is on fertility & gestation.

Crossing two of them

What each combination does

HorseShown asWhat it means
n/n, sf/nWild typeOrdinary. One sf copy is silent.
sf/sfSubfertileEvery breeding is half as likely to take. Even plain golden carrots sometimes give no foal, the only thing in the mod that can make them miss.
tw/n, tw/sfTwin-proneA pregnancy is twins about one time in ten.
tw/twStrongly twin-proneA pregnancy is twins about one time in four.

An ordinary mare’s pregnancy is twins about one time in fifty. Twins only happen in a pregnancy, never from plain golden carrots. It is the mare’s alleles that count for twins. For subfertility, either parent counts, and two subfertile parents make it a quarter.

It has no effect on health.

Where it turns up

Wild horses sometimes carry sf or tw, and a few are tw/tw, but no wild horse is subfertile. That is something you breed.

The class

common/genetics/genes/FertilityGene, registered as Genes.FERTILITY at priority 3, between sex and diet. It paints nothing, and GeneFamily files it under Other natural genes by override. Without that, the “invisible is health” rule would list it with the disorders.

  • alleleFactor(pair) — SUBFERTILE_FACTOR for sf/sf, else 1. Conception.vanillaFoalChance is the product of both parents’.
  • twinChance(pair) — by pair.count(tw).
  • fertility(genome) — the expressed copy’s fertility value (Epigenome.expressed).
  • mareFactor(genome) — the allele factor times that value; what Conception.attempt multiplies the stage’s odds by. The sire contributes only his allele factor and his covers today.

epiSchema() is one Gaussian, mean 1.0 and sigma 0.05, floored at 0.7. The design span is mean ± 6 sigma, so a founder sits in 0.7–1.3. Founders come from a table of carriers only (n/n, sf/n, tw/n, tw/tw). It is not feralOnly, so a breed file may name it. Tests: FertilityGeneTest, and the conception odds in ReproRulesTest.

There is no fertility locus

Nothing in a real horse’s genome matches this gene. Fertility in horses is polygenic and strongly shaped by age, condition and management. The part that is inherited is small and spread across many genes. This locus is a game abstraction of a real heritable trait: something a breeder can select for and see results from, which a diffuse polygenic score would not give a player. That is also why the epigenetic number exists: it drifts a little each generation, so fertility stays a matter of degree even between two horses with the same alleles.

Twins in real horses

A mare fairly often releases two eggs in one heat, more often in some breeds. But a live twin birth is rare. Most twin pregnancies end with one or both lost, and breeders usually reduce a twin pregnancy to one early. The mod’s twins are more forgiving than that on purpose. Offered a harsher, more realistic outcome table, the owner chose “rare, and usually both live” (2026-09-13).

Subfertility

A stallion or mare that is reliably hard to get in foal is a familiar problem in real breeding. Its causes range from semen quality to uterine health, and are rarely a single inherited cause. The allele here stands in for the heritable part only. Making it recessive and absent from the wild keeps it something a breeder produces by breeding, which matches how a closed line accumulates problems rather than how wild populations carry them.
