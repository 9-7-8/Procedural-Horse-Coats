Item / tool

Whistles

Three area whistles call every tamed horse you own nearby, each reaching further than the last. The fourth, the ender whistle, is bound to one horse for good and brings it to you from anywhere — even another dimension. A horse close enough to simply come walks in rather than appearing, the ender whistle asks for a bond before it will bind, and one key blows the lot, and a bound ender whistle glimmers so you can pick it out of a hotbar. The fifth, the command whistle, does not call at all: hold it on a horse and a wheel of standing orders opens.

What happens when you blow an area whistle

Every tamed horse you own within range, in the same dimension and not currently being ridden, teleports to a spot beside you. A leashed horse is untied and comes, and the lead goes into your pack rather than falling where the horse was standing. A chime plays and a chat line tells you how many answered.

Horses already within 3 blocks, and the one you are riding, are left where they are. There is a roughly 3 second cooldown between uses.

Each whistle says what it reaches. Hold one and the tooltip gives its radius in blocks, and reminds you that a ridden horse stays put and a leashed one is untied — the two outcomes that otherwise look like the whistle not working. The ender whistle’s tooltip names its horse instead.

Near horses walk; far ones appear

A whistle used to teleport everything it reached, at any distance — so blowing one at a horse standing ten blocks away in an open field made it vanish and pop back at your elbow, which looks like a bug even though it was the feature working.

Now a horse that can simply walk to you does. It sets off at a brisk trot and arrives on its own feet. Anything further away, or that cannot get to you, appears beside you exactly as before.

The route is what counts, not how far off it looks. A horse fifteen blocks away on the wrong side of a fence is a long walk round, so it is teleported. Open the gate four blocks along and the same horse walks. One blow can do both at once: near ones set off, far ones appear, and the chat line counts them all together.

  • If it gets stuck, it is teleported anyway. A horse that stops making headway, or takes too long, is brought the old way. A whistle never ends with your horse standing behind a fence.
  • A horse in trouble is left alone. One that is on fire, freezing or being hurt drops out of the call and is not teleported — it has something more urgent to do than come when called, and yanking it to your feet is not help.
  • The ender whistle still always teleports. Its whole point is “from anywhere”, and no horse can walk across a dimension.

One key blows every whistle you carry

Press ' and every whistle in your pack sounds at once — no need to find the right one in a hotbar of four identical icons. Rebindable in Controls, and selecting a whistle reminds you which key it is on.

It is not literally every whistle, because that would be silly: a bigger area whistle already covers a smaller one, so only the largest you are carrying is blown and the others are left unused, cooldown and all. Every ender whistle is blown, since each is bound to a different horse — except two bound to the same horse, which call it once between them.

A horse an area whistle has already brought is not called a second time by its own ender whistle. You get one line of chat for the whole press, and one chime per whistle actually blown. Whistles still cooling down sit the press out; the key is not a way round a cooldown.

Binding an ender whistle needs the horse's trust

An ender whistle will only bind to a horse that already knows you — a horse at the bond tier where it starts coming toward you unasked. Below that it refuses, and says the horse does not trust you enough yet.

The reasoning is that a whistle should be a reward for a bond you have already built, rather than something you craft in advance and use to skip building one. Ownership is still required as well: taming a horse is not the same as being trusted by it.

It is not retroactive. Nothing re-checks bond after binding, so a whistle bound before this existed stays bound, and a horse whose bond later decays keeps answering its own whistle. The gate is on earning the whistle, not on keeping it.

The command whistle: standing orders

Aim at a horse you own, hold right-click, and a wheel opens. Move the mouse over an order and let go to give it; let go in the middle to change nothing. A quick tap opens nothing. It reaches a horse up to 16 blocks away, never through a wall, and the horse is not mounted and its inventory does not open. Sneak and hold anywhere to give the order to every horse of yours within 16 blocks; chat then says how many obeyed and why the rest did not (“Stay: 3 obeyed; 1 not bonded enough”).

  • An order lasts until you change it. It pauses while the horse is ridden or on a lead, and while the player it follows is in another world or offline.
  • An order never starves a horse. A hungry horse still goes to eat, and a frightened one still bolts.
  • It is cleared by calling the horse with any whistle (calling a horse means you want it), by Send home, by putting it in a stasis chamber, by selling or giving it away, and when it changes dimension.
  • Refused by a horse on a lead, one pulling a cart, or one not bonded enough; the wheel greys an order the aimed horse cannot take and says why.
  • Getting one: crafted (above), or from an equestrian supplier at level 3 for 5 emeralds. Giving your first order ticks the “Give a horse an order” checklist task.

More orders are planned: Graze nearby, Guard here and Go home, then Hunt monsters and Defend me for horses bred to fight.

The ender whistle

  1. Bind it. Right-click one of your tamed horses with an unbound ender whistle. It hums, and it is bound to that horse for good — it can never be unbound or moved to another horse. Craft one per horse you want to call.
  2. Blow it anywhere. The horse appears in front of you in a puff of portal particles, however far away it was, whatever dimension it was in. If it was a long way off and nobody was near it, it takes a moment: the whistle says it “echoes a long way off”, and the horse arrives within a few seconds.
  3. Use it as often as you like, with a five-second cooldown.
  • Only the horse’s owner can call it. If you sell the horse with transfer papers, the whistle goes with it in spirit: blow it after that and you are told the horse no longer answers to you. Hand the whistle to the new owner and it works for them.
  • A horse with somebody else riding it stays put.
  • If the horse dies, the whistle turns to dust — the next time you are holding it, with a message saying so. One left in a chest crumbles the moment you pick it up.
  • If the horse was never seen where it was last recorded — it wandered off in a chunk nobody had loaded — nothing answers, and the whistle says so.

A bound whistle glimmers

An ender whistle that has been bound to a horse shimmers like an enchanted item. An unbound one does not. That is the whole of it — you can see at a glance, in a hotbar or a chest, which of your whistles are spoken for.

It costs nothing and needs no new art, which is why it won over the second texture that was planned: the shimmer is worked out from whether the whistle is bound, so whistles you bound before this update glimmer too, with nothing to migrate and nothing to go and find.

The three area tiers still look the same

Basic, golden and echo share one texture and one icon, so they are told apart only by their names and their tooltips — each states its radius, which is what the tooltip is for. In a hotbar, where nothing draws a tooltip, they are three identical icons: keep them in order, or rename them at an anvil. Only the ender whistle is now distinguishable on sight, and only once it is bound. The blow-everything key takes most of the sting out of this, since picking the right icon is no longer how you use them.

The area whistles

item/WhistleItem, one class and a radius per tier. Pathing instead of teleporting and the bond gate both shipped on 2026-09-29.

appendHoverText reads the radius field rather than a fourth constructor argument or a per-tier lang key, for the same reason WhistleItem.task() does: the radius is already the only thing that distinguishes the three, and a second parallel field is a second thing that can disagree with it. Added 2026-09-24 — until then the three tiers had no tooltip at all, and the numbers 16, 32 and 64 appeared nowhere a player could read them. The second line (“a ridden horse stays put; a leashed one is untied”) deliberately stops short of saying where the lead goes: that is behaviour.leads_return, a server option, and a client on a dedicated server has no copy of it to read.

The lead, when a whistle moves the horse

Neither whistle unties the horse itself any more. Both call server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player), which suppresses vanilla’s ground drop and hands the lead to whoever blew the whistle — written up, with the reasoning and the gametest that guards it, on rider comfort, which owns that behaviour. Tickets go through the same helper.

The ender whistle

  • item/EnderWhistleItem — use calls, inventoryTick checks for dust once a second. Stacks to one. appendHoverText names the bound horse, reading it off the component rather than the entity: the horse may be unloaded, in another dimension, or dead, and the client cannot reach it in any of those cases. Same shape as the bound stall sign, which is where the wording came from.
  • server/EnderWhistleCalls — binding (an EntityInteract handler using HorseOwnership.bindRefusal, the same refusal stall signs use), calling, and the dust. The binding is the existing BOUND_HORSE component.
  • data/HorseWhereabouts — a server-global SavedData of each tamed horse’s last dimension and block, and whether it died. Written by server/HorseWhereaboutsHandler on join, on leave, every ten seconds (only when it moved more than 16 blocks or changed dimension), and on death. The ancestry database keeps a dead horse’s record for the pedigree, so it cannot answer “is it alive” — this can.

How a call finds a horse

  1. Any level with the entity loaded: move it now.
  2. Otherwise the last sighting’s chunk gets a TicketType.PORTAL ticket (radius 2, which expires by itself) and the call waits on the server tick for up to five seconds for the entity section to load.
  3. Same dimension: snapTo. Another dimension: Entity.teleport(TeleportTransition), which re-creates the entity in the target level.
  4. Ownership is checked at call time, never at bind time, which is what makes the whistle follow a sale.

It moves an entity found by UUID and never creates one, so it cannot duplicate a horse. A horse deleted rather than killed — the horse dimension clearing its pens — is gone from the ancestry database, and that crumbles the whistle too.

Walking a near horse in (2026-09-29)

server/WhistleCalls holds the decision and every way a call can end; server/WhistleCallGoal does nothing but walk.

Decided once, at blow time. tryWalk asks the horse’s own navigator for a fresh path to the player and walks only if that path exists, actually arrives (canReach() — a path that stops short is a fence), and is within WALK_PATH_BLOCKS steps. Nothing is re-decided later: a horse either set off walking or it teleported, and there is no third state.

The route is measured, not the distance, because fifteen blocks away can be eighty blocks of switchback. Since a route is never shorter than the straight line between its ends, capping the route caps the distance for free — so there is one number and not two. The cap is counted in path nodes, which are block steps, and a diagonal step covers about 1.4 blocks; that approximation is why the tooltip says “about”, and the tooltip reads the constant rather than repeating the number.

Not BondFollowGoal’s ReachCheck. That caches for two seconds, which is right for a goal asking every tick and wrong for a whistle — a call is a single event and deserves a fresh answer. It also only answers yes/no, and this needs the length.

Why the state machine is not in the goal

Every ending is decided in WhistleCalls’s server-tick sweep, and the goal reads a marker. That is deliberate: a goal that never starts cannot end itself. If something at priority 0 holds MOVE, or the horse is in danger, the goal simply never runs — and a state machine living inside it would leave the horse neither walking nor teleported, which is the “the whistle ignored my horse” report with nothing in the log.

  • Arrived — within the same three blocks the teleport path calls “already here”.
  • Gave up — deadline passed, or no headway for two seconds. It is teleported. That fallback is the guarantee that a walked call is never worse than the old behaviour, and it is also what covers the goal never having started.
  • In danger — dropped, and deliberately not teleported.
  • Invalidated — player or horse gone, mounted, leashed. Dropped quietly.

The marker is in memory only, keyed by horse UUID. A call lasts seconds; a half-finished one restored from disk would be a bug, so a reload mid-walk just ends it.

Priority 1, and why not 2

The design note for this reasoned “below survival, above everything social” and concluded priority 2. Reading the generated hierarchy rather than that line shows why 2 is wrong: priority 2 is vanilla’s BreedGoal, which claims MOVE, and an equal priority never interrupts — WrappedGoal.canBeReplacedBy requires a strictly lower number. At 2, a horse in love mode would refuse the whistle outright: the exact failure the note was warning about, behind a different goal than it named.

There is no integer between 1 and 2, so the goal sits at 1 and gives up what arbitration was doing there — yielding to panic and sun-shade, which are also at 1. That is paid for by hand: inDanger is checked in both the goal and the sweep, and is deliberately coarser than PanicGoal’s own trigger (on fire, freezing, or a non-zero hurtTime) so it errs toward letting the horse look after itself. Escape and the inspect hold are at 0 and still interrupt this the ordinary way, so the two genuinely non-negotiable goals need no help.

The bond gate on binding (2026-09-29)

EnderWhistleCalls.bindRefusal, a new method wrapping HorseOwnership.bindRefusal and adding the tier test on top. Not added to the shared one: that is also what bound stall signs call, and a bound sign has no reason to want a bond. Putting the test there would have gated the sign silently.

The threshold is BIND_BOND_TIER, a named constant rather than a literal, so the next tuning session is one line. No server config: one threshold is cheap but it is a surface, and it has not been asked for.

Guarded by the whistle_needs_bond gametest — bond 60 refused, 61 accepted, the two values either side of the boundary and nothing else, since anything more would be testing behaviourTier(). It goes through bindRefusal rather than posting an EntityInteract, because that method is the seam the real handler uses and there is exactly one call to it. Confirmed to fail with BIND_BOND_TIER lowered to 1, which let bond 60 through.

One key, every whistle (2026-09-29)

common/care/WhistleSelection holds the rule, and it is pure: a list of radii and bound ids in, the picks out, with WhistleSelectionTest beside it. server/WhistleBlowing does what needs a world — the inventory scan, cooldowns, calling the items, one line of chat.

The payload carries no fields at all. The obvious design has the client work out which whistles to blow and name them, which is the TackSlotPayload lesson again: an index into a container is a claim about that container. So the client says only that the key was pressed and the server scans the sender’s real inventory. A tampered client can at most press its own key. StreamCodec.unit does the encoding — not a new trick here, DismountPayload already used it.

Both items’ use() now delegate to a shared blow() that owns the cooldown. Two implementations would have become a way round the cooldown the first time one of them forgot to set it.

The selection returns the area whistle first, and that ordering is a contract. A player can hold an echo whistle and an ender whistle bound to a horse twenty blocks away; both would call it, and it would teleport twice and chime twice. So the area whistle is blown first and reports which horses it handled, and an ender whistle bound to one of those is skipped. Returning the picks in inventory order would have made that work or not depending on which pocket the whistle was in — which is what the ordering test in WhistleSelectionTest pins.

The action-bar prompt uses sendOverlayMessage, not the displayClientMessage with a boolean flag that the design note assumed: 26.1.2 renamed it, as the comment on block/Jumps already recorded. The text is built from Component.keybind, so it resolves against the player’s own Controls and reads correctly after a rebind.

Telling a bound whistle apart, without art (2026-09-29)

EnderWhistleItem.isFoil(stack), returning whether BOUND_HORSE is present. Three lines.

This replaced a planned art job, and is a better answer than the one it replaced rather than just a cheaper one. The plan was a second sprite plus an item model conditional on the component; the owner asked for the enchantment glint instead. The reason it is better is that the glint is derived, not stored. Every whistle bound before this shipped glimmers the moment the class loads, because nothing was written to any stack — "is this bound" keeps exactly one answer, the component, and the look is computed from it.

The obvious alternative, setting ENCHANTMENT_GLINT_OVERRIDE on bind, would have worked for whistles bound afterwards and left every existing one dull with no way to find them. That is the general shape of the trap: a cosmetic written into saved data needs a migration, and a cosmetic computed from saved data does not.

ItemStack.hasFoil() consults isFoil unless that override component is present, and it is what CuboidItemModelWrapper and SpecialModelWrapper actually read — checked in the 26.1.2 sources rather than assumed, since overriding a hook nothing calls would look exactly like the feature failing. The whistle carries no enchantment, so the default (isEnchanted()) was false and nothing is being overridden away.

Two things deliberately not built (2026-09-29)

Both were on the Roadmap tab as open possibilities and are now closed, so that the list shrinks rather than carrying a maybe forever. They are here rather than on decisions because that page is a pointer index now and says in as many words not to add decisions to it; a settled call belongs on the Coding tab of the thing it settles.

  • Binding donkeys, mules and llamas to an ender whistle. No. The area whistles already call any AbstractHorse and are unchanged. The ender binding stays horse-only because BoundHorse, HorseWhereabouts, the crumble-to-dust path and the ancestry lookup all key on a genome-carrying Horse. Widening it would also drag in bound stall signs, which share the same component — so the cost is not the one class it looks like.
  • Bound modes on the three area whistles — no. The idea was a ladder: loaded chunk, then same dimension, then any dimension, by tier. The ender whistle is that ladder’s top rung built properly, and a second way to reach across a dimension would compete with it for no gain. The Roadmap tab used to hedge this with “unless someone wants the ladder back”; it does not any more.

The command whistle (2026-10-03)

Piece 1 of the command whistle treatment. The idea came from the Bedrock addon 1,000,000 Horses! (Cynosia; scripts by InnerTri), whose Horse Whistle cycles Stay / Follow / Wander on right-click and applies to every nearby tamed horse when sneaking. It was surveyed from its .mcaddon on 2026-10-03. Nothing is copied from it: the copy surveyed was a re-upload of a paid Marketplace pack, so no code, art or text from it is in this repository. The wheel, the bond gates and the order goals are this mod’s own.

  • common/: care/HorseOrder, the orders. The enum is append-only and saved by NAME, so an unknown name loads as Rejoin herd. care/HorseOrders holds the gate (lead, then cart, then bond; Rejoin herd never refused) and every word a player reads. care/CommandWheel is the slice geometry: slice 0 is centred straight up and the slices run clockwise, with a dead centre. Tests: HorseOrdersTest, CommandWheelGeometryTest.
  • The order is the attachment horsegenetics:horse_order (data/HorseOrderAttachment): the order, an anchor, the dimension it was given in, and who gave it. It is synced, and not copied on death. The default is no order, so every older world loads unchanged.
  • Clearing is lazy where it can be. server/HorseOrdering.current drops an order whose giver no longer owns the horse, or that was given in another dimension. No hook in the ownership or portal code is needed. The moves within one dimension clear it themselves: the area whistle’s recall, the ender whistle’s call, Send home (StallRecall), and HorseStasisHandler.swallow.
  • Opening the wheel (client/CommandWhistleClient) cancels InputEvent.InteractionKeyMappingTriggered. That event fires in Minecraft.startUseItem before MultiPlayerGameMode.interact sends the interact packet (read from the 26.1.2 sources), so the server never hears the click and nothing on the server has to be kept in step. The entity-interact events would not do: they fire after the packet has gone. The aimed horse is ray-cast out to 16 blocks and stopped at the first block.
  • Picking on release (client/CommandWheelScreen) works two ways. Opening a screen calls KeyMapping.releaseAll(), so keyUse.isDown() is useless from then on. The screen gets the right button’s mouseReleased, and tick() also polls GLFW. The poll catches a release that came before the screen opened, which is a tap: the cursor is still in the dead centre, so a tap picks nothing. The screen does not pause single-player.
  • The client decides nothing. CommandHorsePayload carries entity ids and the order’s name. HorseOrdering.handle re-finds the horses and re-checks ownership, reach (16 + 2 blocks of slack), lead, cart and bond.
  • Goals: OrderStayGoal and OrderFollowGoal, both at priority 4, not the 1 the treatment recommended. A goal at equal or worse priority is never even asked canUse while a better one runs, so at 1 a stayed horse would never start HungerFoodGoal (3), and the owner’s rule is that an order never starves a horse. At 4, every need pre-empts the order: escape (0), panic and a whistle call (1), and hunger, food, sparring and fighting (3). Idling is held off: herd life (5–7) and vanilla’s stroll (6). BondFollowGoal shares 4 and stands down under any order, and its tier-1 Watch under Wander. WildHerdGoal needs no hook, because it already refuses tamed horses and only tamed horses take orders.
  • Stay’s anchor is snapped to the ground (HorseOrdering.groundAt), and “at the spot” is judged across the ground. A horse ordered mid-jump, or still landing, was otherwise anchored in the air where no path reaches. The order_stay_walks_back gametest found this, because its horse had not landed yet.
  • The item is item/CommandWhistleItem, stack size 1, and does nothing by itself. Its art is a recolour of the basic whistle in bake-item-recolours.mjs: green, because the ender whistle already owns purple. The trade is level 3 at 5 emeralds. The checklist task is COMMAND_WHISTLE.
  • Gametests:
    • order_stay_walks_back: a stayed horse moved three blocks is steered back to its spot. It checks the navigation’s target, not only the distance, because the neighbouring tests’ horses pull it about. With the walk-back cut out, the horse still drifted home once.
    • order_yields_to_needs: the order goals sit below every need, and bond-follow stands down.
    • order_clears: an order is dropped on a new owner, in another dimension, and in stasis.
    The recall’s clear needs a real ServerPlayer, so it is a play check.

Known gaps

  • The three area whistles share one texture. A bound ender whistle is now told apart by its glint; the tiers are not told apart at all.
  • Nothing gates the area whistles on bond — deliberately; they call horses you already own. Binding an ender whistle is gated.
  • The far-chunk wait and the cross-dimension teleport are written against 26.1.2 sources and not seen working — see the verification tab.
  • A horse moved while its chunk was unloaded by something other than itself (a piston, another mod) is looked for where it was last seen, and not found.
What needs verifying here

Ender whistles have never been blown, and the far call is the one that fails invisibly — a wrong portal ticket or entity-section wait simply answers nothing across an unloaded chunk; and the three pieces that shipped on 2026-09-29 are navigation, goal arbitration and a keybind, none of which a test can judge, with the walk-in’s worst failure being a horse left standing behind a fence.

Walking a near horse in (2026-09-29, NOT played)

Navigation and goal arbitration, so none of this is unit-testable and all of it is here. Do these in an open flat area first, then break them on purpose.

  • One horse, ten blocks, open field. Pass: it walks to you and the chat says 1. Fail: it teleports, which means the path query is refusing something it should accept.
  • The same horse behind a fence with no gate. It cannot path, so it should teleport. A horse left standing at the fence is the worst outcome this change can produce and the reason the give-up fallback exists.
  • A gate ten blocks around — the route is too long, so it should teleport. Then move the gate to four blocks around: now it should walk. That pair is the cap doing its job; if both walk, the route is not being measured.
  • Across a river or a ravine — teleports.
  • A horse mid-meal, in heat, or in a herd. The call should win. Then watch what it does on arrival: it should stand near you for a moment, not turn round and rejoin the herd at once. If it does, that is a tuning finding — the herd goals may want a short “just called” grace — not a bug in the call.
  • A horse in vanilla love mode (fed a golden apple). This is the case that decided the goal’s priority, so it is worth one look: the call should interrupt it. If the horse carries on to its mate, the priority reasoning is wrong.
  • Set a horse on fire, then whistle. Pass: it is left alone — not walked and not teleported — and it panics normally. Fail: a burning horse walking calmly toward you, which means the hand-written danger yield is not firing and priority 1 is starving panic.
  • Panic mid-walk. Hit a horse while it is walking in. The call should drop and it should not be teleported.
  • Three horses at 5, 12 and 40 blocks, echo whistle. Two walk, one appears, chat says 3, and nothing stacks on top of anything on arrival.
  • Reload the world mid-walk. No error in the log; the horse just stops coming.
  • The ender whistle beside a walkable horse must still teleport it.

The ender whistle (built the evening of 2026-09-13, NOT played)

None of this has been in a game; everything below is covered by unit tests on the genetics side only. Test kit batch 2 is the ender whistle and the overworld half of band life.

  • Bind. Craft one (echo whistle + eye of ender + ender pearl) and right-click a tamed horse you own: “bound to … for good”. Try an untamed horse and someone else’s: both refused. Right-click another horse with the bound whistle: nothing rebinds.
  • The tooltip names the horse. An unbound whistle says how to bind it; a bound one says Bound to: <name>. The name rides the component, so check it on a whistle in a chest with the horse in another dimension, and on one carried across a /reload — a blank name falls back to eight characters of UUID, which means the bind read no horse record.
  • Far call — the one that can fail invisibly. Ride a thousand blocks away (so the horse’s chunk unloads), then blow it. Expect “echoes a long way off” and the horse within five seconds. If it always says nothing answered, the portal ticket or the entity-section wait is wrong.
  • Near call. Walk thirty blocks away and blow it: the horse appears in front of you.
  • Across dimensions. Leave the horse in the overworld, go through a Nether portal, blow it. Then the reverse. Check the horse that arrives still has its name, coat and genome (open its information screen) — a cross-dimension teleport re-creates the entity.
  • Sold. Transfer the horse to a second account with papers; the first account blowing the whistle should be told the horse no longer answers.
  • Dust. Put the whistle in a chest, kill the horse, take the whistle out: it crumbles with the message. Kill another while holding its whistle: crumbles within a second.
