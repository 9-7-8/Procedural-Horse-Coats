Freedom stick

Give a horse back. Used on your own horse in the horse realm, it heals it, unclaims it, and leaves it standing there with its name, its genes and its whole pedigree intact for whoever tames it next.

Making one

Shapeless: a feather, a stick, and one horse hair. It has 64 uses, and only a successful release costs one — every refusal is free and says why.

Using it

Lead the horse through a hay-bale portal into the horse realm, then right-click it with the stick. It refuses in three cases, all of them on purpose:

IfThen
You are not in the horse realm Nothing. A horse released in the Overworld is a horse abandoned, not a horse given away — it would be somebody’s problem by morning and dead by the next one.
The horse is not yours Nothing. The realm is shared; if anyone could unclaim anyone’s horse it would be the most dangerous place in the game to leave one.
The horse is already wild Nothing, and it says so.

What happens to the horse

  • It is healed to full. It is the last thing you will ever do for it, and a hurt horse in a field may never breed and may take a long time to find water.
  • Its tack comes back to you — saddle and body armour, into your inventory, or at your feet if it is full.
  • It is wild. Not yours, not anybody’s. It has to be tamed the ordinary Minecraft way: ride it until it stops throwing you, or feed it.
  • It is still itself. Same name, same genotype, same epigenome, same breed, same parents, same generation, same place in every family tree. Whoever tames it inherits all of that and becomes its owner.
  • It joins a band with other released horses nearby, and breeds with them — slowly. See the realm.

Where it lives

server/FreedomStickHandler, a PlayerInteractEvent.EntityInteract subscriber rather than an Item.interactLivingEntity override — the house rule, because vanilla turns any item used on a tamed horse into a mount, so the event has to be cancelled on both sides with the work done server-only. The item itself is one line in ModItems: a plain durable Item, like the vet’s kit.

Un-taming is three calls, and the third is the one that matters

setTamed(false), setOwner(null), setTemper(0).

The temper reset is what makes “tame it again” mean anything. Vanilla’s RunAroundLikeCrazyGoal rolls nextInt(maxTemper) < temper, and a horse that was tame is sitting at maximum — so without it the first player to sit on the horse tames it on the first roll and the release is a formality nobody would notice was broken.

The saddle has to come off for a structural reason rather than a generous one: AbstractHorse.canUseSlot(SADDLE) asks isTamed(), so a saddle left on a horse that has gone wild is a saddle quietly destroyed.

Re-taming needed no code

Nothing on HorseRecord or in HorseAncestryData is derived from ownership, so releasing touches none of it and there is nothing to put back. HorseOwnerTrackingHandler reconciles the record’s owner mirror every forty ticks and accepts null — its javadoc already listed “a horse going wild again” as a case it handles, long before one existed. When somebody tames it, tameWithName sets the owner and the same tracker mirrors it back.

tamedBy is deliberately not cleared. It records who first got a rope on this animal, it is written once, and a sale has never rewritten it either (transfer papers). The cost is that the re-tamer is not credited in attribution(); the alternative was losing a fact about the horse’s history to make a nicer line of text, which is the wrong trade on a page whose entire subject is that the history survives.

The wild-spawn mark

A release sets BreedSpawnHandler.WILD_SPAWN_KEY on the horse so the herd systems will look at it. That is not sufficient on its own — HorseFoundingTickHandler strips the mark from any horse that loads carrying a record, which a released horse always does. The band actually comes from server/HorseRealmHerds; see the realm’s note on it.

What needs verifying here

Never used in a game — and the assertion with teeth is that mounting a released horse throws you off, because a missed temper reset looks exactly like a working release right up until somebody re-tames it in one click.
