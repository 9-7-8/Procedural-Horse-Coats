Crystal growth colour

What colour a horse’s crystal growths are. The same colours and rules as horn colour (white, the rainbow, pink, black, grey or chaos), on a locus of its own. Two different copies make the crystals one colour and their points the other. On a horse without crystals it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

The colours, and two-tone crystals

Exactly the horn colour table. Two copies of one colour make every crystal that colour, see-through, with solid points of the same colour. Two different ones make the see-through crystal one colour and its solid point the other. The crystal takes whichever colour comes earlier in the list, so Red/Blu is red glass with blue points, either way round. Every copy carries a little shade of its own, so no two ruby-crystalled horses are quite the same red.

Bone

Two Bon copies are simply bone-coloured crystal, still see-through. A crystal has no bone in it, so unlike the other body parts there is no bare-bone version. No wild horse carries Bon. The skeleton horses grow crystals in ordinary colours, with this locus not pinned (owner, 2026-10-05).

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all. It is only the colour of the crystals.

One short subclass, the crystal antler’s split

A key, a name, a priority and the words its outcomes are written in. Everything else is AbstractPartColourGene’s. Its Words carry a split sentence (“the crystals are one, the base, and their points the other”). partGrows answers from the crystal locus, so the short form leaves the colour off a horse without crystals.

The split costs no new machinery. A see-through part is drawn in two passes. The blended pass draws its kind’s see-through regions in the base tint, which for the crystals is the CRYSTAL shafts. The solid pass draws everything else in the tip tint, which is the BONE_TIP points. This is the same path the sail takes, with the membrane as the base and the spines as the tip. GrownParts.of never calls boneOnly for the crystals: there is nothing soft to leave off.

Invented

Horses grow no crystals, so neither is their colour real. The table reads as gemstones (ruby, citrine, emerald, sapphire, amethyst, rose quartz, smoky and black quartz), which is exactly what the treatment asked of a crystal’s colour gene. It is a separate locus from every other part’s because the owner chose one colour gene per part.

What needs verifying here

The crystal-and-point split is unit-tested but has never been seen: whether a two-tone horse shows see-through crystals in one colour with solid points in the other, and whether dark colours still read as see-through, are open.

The crystal in one colour, its point in the other

A Red/Blu crystal horse close up: red see-through shafts, blue solid caps, the same on every crystal. Then Blk/Blk and Gry/Gry: a dark tint at the crystal’s alpha may read as a solid smoked block rather than glass, and if it does, that is a look for the owner to judge rather than a bug.
