Roadmap

Planned features that are not built. Ordered by priority — foundations before the features that sit on them, content before polish. That is the whole of what this page is; everything else that used to live here has a better home, listed below.

Nothing but planned features lives here

Four kinds of writing keep trying to settle on this page, and each has an owner that is not this one:

  • Something that shipped → delete it and write it up on its own page. “Marked shipped” is not “moved”, and only moving it keeps one source of truth.
  • A decision, open or settled → Design decisions. Including a decision not to build something: that is an answer, not a backlog item.
  • A defect, a gap or an unchecked assumption → Known gaps. A built thing behaving wrongly is not a planned feature.
  • Something to go and look at in-game → To be verified — built, not yet seen, which is a different and much longer list than this one.

Sections are named, never numbered: the page has been reordered twice and will be again. Link by anchor.

Foundations that unblock the rest

Group traits, and the rest of the effect architecture

Traits that come from a group of genes

Wanted, and distinct from the cut polygenic idea: two or more genes, each with their own alleles, that only together trigger an outcome. This is epistasis / complementation — still fully Mendelian — and it needs a home. The leopard complex got what it needed without one (expressionIn is handed the whole genotype), but that is a single-gene read, not a rule of its own.

  • It is not a gene, so it cannot live on the Gene interface. It wants a separately registered TraitRule that declares which gene keys it reads and is handed those pairs.
  • It is still Mendelian. Each gene inherits normally and independently; the rule only reads the result. No new inheritance code.
  • It may affect the coat (settled). So a coat-affecting rule needs everything a gene needs: a phase and a priority. Which argues strongly for modelling it as a pseudo-gene: same delta function, same ordering, same determinism rules, differing only in that its inputs are several pairs instead of one. Reusing the gene pipeline wholesale is much cheaper than a parallel one, and the spawner and the coat cache then need no special case.
  • The one thing it cannot inherit: it has no alleles, so it contributes nothing to the genotype code and never appears in an allele lookup. Keep it out of codeOrder().
  • Determinism: rules sort like genes, and a rule reading an unregistered gene must fail loudly rather than default.
  • Visibility to the player. A trait with no single gene behind it is invisible in a genotype code and unguessable in a punnett square. It needs a UI surface of its own, or it will read as randomness — exactly what the philosophy says not to do.

Cream + pearl looks like this case and is not: they are one physical locus, and MATP merges them rather than writing a rule.

The rest of the effect architecture

A first slice is built — effects verbs with boolean when conditions, usable from a gene file and from a built-in Java gene through the same vocabulary. What is left is everything else on the trait & effect architecture, which is the source of truth for the design and carries its own build order: triggers, selectors, auras, resource pools, goals, trait relations, on_change, and the 0–1 scalar conditions the built version approximates as booleans. The capability interfaces should not be considered final until that lands — a modder writing a coat gene is on solid ground today; one writing a behaviour gene is building on something that will move.

Sex-linked inheritance — a Y-linked gene, and a square that can draw one

The scaffolding ships and brindle proves it. What is planned on top:

  • Nothing is Y-linked yet. The mirror case is built and tested through Inheritance.Y_LINKED — a mare's pair is two reserved X placeholders — and is waiting for a gene that wants it. A Y-linked locus travels down the male line untouched by any mare, which is a different and equally legible pedigree shape.
  • A punnett square for a sex-linked gene is a different square, and neither of the two that exist draws one. The wiki's (wiki/gene-inheritance/, on every gene page) pairs the two copies of each parent as though both were autosomal, so brindle's square is drawn wrong rather than refused — it needs the sire's reserved Y slot and a foal's own sex on one of the axes. The browser's Breeding preview tab has the same hole.

The gene creator: a preview that runs the real pipeline, and a usability pass

Move the creator's preview onto the wasm

The creator is the one tool whose preview never touches the real pipeline. The horse designer, the breed designer and every gene-*.html preview run common/ compiled to WebAssembly; the creator runs the hand-written JS port, which has no emissive pass and is not getting one. So it cannot show a glow, and it is not to be extended to fake one.

Closing this means a @JSExport that bakes an unsaved spec, so the creator previews through DesignerApi like everything else. That would also end the port's reason to exist and change what wiki/gene-creator/tools/check-parity.mjs is comparing, which is why it is a job of its own rather than a finish to the last one.

“Covered by parity” has to be demonstrated, not assumed

Anything the editor previews, it previews through the port, or it will show a horse the game will not breed, convincingly. pointsMin is the case that proved the probe cases are not enough on their own: with a minimal shape on prismatic's crescent and nothing else, reversing the blend in the creator left parity green — a drawing on a flank is a few dozen texels and the fixtures sample four per part. The way that was established was to break the port on purpose and watch the check stay quiet. Do that with the next one too.

A usability pass on the creator

The tool is correct: it loads, it previews a horse, it writes every part of the format, and it proves its own engine against the game's on boot. What it has never had is a pass for whether it is pleasant to use. Nothing here is specified, deliberately — the point of the pass is to look at it with fresh eyes rather than to implement a list written in advance. Known rough edges to start from:

  • Effects and layers look alike. They share the .layer card, which is honest about them being peers but makes the right column read as one long undifferentiated stack.
  • The forms are flat. Every parameter of every mask, op and effect is always visible at the same weight; nothing is folded away behind “advanced”, and the common case is not faster than the rare one.
  • Errors are a list at the bottom. The validator names the problem well but does not point at the field that caused it.
  • No undo, and no warning before “New gene” discards what you have.

The constraint that does not move: it has to keep working from file://. That is why every script is a classic one and every asset is inlined — see the format. A UI framework that needs a bundler or a dev server is out.

Genes still to build

Finished genes are not listed here — they are rostered on the genetics model with a page each, and any residual tuning lives on the gene's own page. What follows is only what no gene does yet. Natural genes all exist in real life, so they belong in the 0–99 priority band and run before any magical gene; the scientific allele names are legal tokens as written.

Natural genes

The naturalgeneref.md audit — a standing task

naturalgeneref.md in the repo root is a cleaned extract of another horse game's natural-genetics guide, and it is the reference the natural side should be complete against. It already carries a level of game-ready abstraction, which is why it is worth diffing against rather than primary sources. Standing task: read it end to end, diff it against the registered genes, and turn each gap into an entry here. Every base colour and dilute in it is modelled at least in mechanism; what the last pass turned up is below.

Curly coat (KRT25, SP6)

Two dominant variants curl the coat, mane and tail. KRT25 curls and thins the hair (hypotrichosis); SP6 curls it without thinning; KRT25 is epistatic to SP6 for the hair loss, so the plush full-coated curly is the SP6-only horse.

It needs a coat-structure axis before it needs a gene. Nothing in the pipeline varies hair at all, so there is no value for a painter to write to. Wanted by the American Bashkir Curly, whose entire identity it is; that breed ships with the colour pool and no curl, and says so in its notes. horsegenetics.rex is a magical ring marking and is not this.

Base-aware phenotype naming

A surface, not a gene, and it serves three separate gaps. The reference names gold / amber / sable / classic champagne and chestnut / bay / black / cream pearl as distinct phenotypes by base, and expects cream×champagne and cream×pearl to read differently again. Separately, it treats seal bay as its own base that sable champagne rides on — but seal has no gene here, it is the top of agouti's epigenetic distribution.

So the mod needs a way to resolve and name “this genotype is an amber champagne” or “this horse is seal” for the info panel, the paper dump and the gene dictionary — and champagne needs to read “is this horse seal” off the agouti epigenetics to paint sable rather than amber. One base-aware phenotype resolver serves all of it.

Gait — the gene ships, the animation does not

DMRT3 is registered, inherited and carried by the breeds that really have it, and it changes nothing about how a horse moves. The locus contributes no trait and every outcome is a wild type, because there is no ambling or pacing animation to switch on. The gene's own expression text says so to the player in three places — “the gait is not animated yet” — so this is a promise already made in game, not a loose idea.

What it needs is animation work on the vanilla horse model: a four-beat amble and a lateral pace, chosen by copy count, with the two-copy horse losing the diagonal gaits it trades away. That is the whole entry. It is deliberately not bundled with the per-part scaling call, which was cut: that one wanted the mod to own the horse model, and this one only wants new motion on the model it already borrows.

Magical genes

Invented genes, phase 3, priority 100+. This list has no upstream source to be complete against — add to it freely. Each needs a priority, an allele set with n as wild type, a founder distribution and a delta function.

More of what already works

  • More LUT palettes. The gene is built to take more — a new palette is a new allele here, never a new gene — each being a lutResources() entry plus an expression row and a founder weight. Wants a batch of hand-authored gradient PNGs. See the LUT locus.
  • More magical marking and “stamp” genes before 1.0. Colourful flank and body emblems in the spirit of cutie marks but simpler — no item atlas. Open-ended; the owner wants a visible batch of these for the 1.0 look.

Genes a breed designer can build a character out of

Asked for as a batch, out of intake/: more genes whose point is flavour — loci a breed file can name to alter what its horses do and what they can do, rather than how they look. The existing behaviour family is the model; what is wanted is more of it, and deliberately open-ended. A breed is a JSON file, so every gene added here is a knob the designer gets for free.

A gene that reads what the horse is wearing

Named specifically, and it is the one with a real design problem in it. The ask is a locus that detects whether a horse is saddled or armoured, and which kind, and reacts — an allele that slows the horse dramatically while saddled, another that suppresses every aura while saddled, and so on.

  • The condition vocabulary has nothing that can see equipment. The when flags read the world and the horse’s own state; none of them reads an equipment slot. So this is a new condition flag before it is a gene, which means the condition contract — the flag, the evaluator, the creator’s schema mirror, both tables and a parity fixture.
  • “Which kind” is the expensive half. A boolean is saddled is cheap. Distinguishing iron from diamond from a modded armour means the flag carries a value, and the generated-armour layer means the set is open-ended rather than a fixed five — see the compat layer. Decide whether the gene asks “armoured at all”, “armoured in this tag”, or a material tier, before writing any of it.
  • Watch the phantom saddle. BarebackSteeringHandler writes the saddle slot every tick on a horse nobody has saddled, so a naive is saddled check is true for horses the player considers bare. Whatever reads the slot has to exclude a stack carrying PHANTOM_SADDLE or the gene will fire constantly and look broken.

The aggression genes may want an overhaul

Recorded as asked rather than as specified: the note says only that they may need one, with no statement of what is wrong. Nothing should be rebuilt off that sentence. The first piece of work is to say what the complaint is — aggression carries the moon-sick allele, and the chase gene below would generalise the forget-target goal that sits beside it, so an overhaul may turn out to be that entry plus a tidy rather than a rewrite.

Mask parameters the intakes asked for

All four arrived as tool gaps in the authoring notes, each read after the approximation had been seen on a horse, and each describing a shortfall that is really there. All but one are a new parameter on an existing mask, defaulting to today's behaviour, so no shipped gene moves — but each is still the mask contract: the painter, SpecSchema, the creator's twin, both tables, a prismatic case, fixtures and parity.

  • RAMP angle (degrees, default 0, read only when axis is X, Y or Z): rotate the sweep within the plane of the named axis and the next one round (X→Y→Z→X), so angle: 90 on X is Y. The coverage is the dot product of the position with the rotated unit vector, normalised through from/to as now. What it buys: an SVG's <linearGradient> runs at an arbitrary angle and today can only be collapsed to one flat tone per band or one sweep along the horse's length — tidewave ships with the latter, and its swells lose the sense of each being separately lit.
  • FAN origin: "cell" (with scale, seed, and vary as a per-cell rotation of the fan's zero): walk the same lattice SPOTS uses and measure the angle and radius from the texel's own cell centre instead of from originU/originV, so a field of independent sprays is one layer. Multiplying one FAN by SPOTS cannot do it — that clips one global fan into cell-shaped pieces that all point at the same pivot. Opal fire ships with two hand-placed fans that land in the same two spots on every horse. Note gap 182 before building it: a per-cell pivot on a 3D lattice will mostly sit off the skin, so this probably wants the same snap-to-surface option as the centre marks.
  • SVG / PATH plane: "wrap" (with wrapAxis, default X, and from, the part whose cross-section is walked): u is the body X as top already gives it, and v is signed arc length in body units around the part's box perimeter in the YZ cross-section, measured from the top-face centreline. This is the one entry that is a new plane value rather than a parameter. What it buys: a drawing that lies over the back and continues down both flanks as one unbroken picture. Every plane today is a straight projection, so a face parallel to the projection axis takes one line of the drawing smeared down it — the endless knot ships that way, with vertical bars under whichever arms cross the barrel's edge and its outer unit each side never drawn at all. Swapping planes cannot fix it: what is missing is a measurement, distance travelled around the cross-section, and neither NORMAL nor EDGE reports it.
  • FRACTAL stretch / axis / tilt (default 1, X, 0 — today's behaviour): divide the sample point along the tilted axis before the octave sum, so a continuous noise field gets a grain. What it buys: veins, currents and weather that all run one way. FRACTAL samples isotropic noise, so no threshold, warp or octave count gives it a direction — stormvein's veins fork and wander correctly but run every way at once, where the drawing they came from streams diagonally. SPOTS has exactly this pair already and gets ovals from it; the reason it cannot stand in is that it elongates countable marks and cannot draw a web whose ridges also bound the pools.

Cloud-treading

The flying gene was asked for with three abilities and shipped with two. The third — an allele that glides and can come to rest on a cloud and walk about on it — was killswitched so the elytra glide could be got working first, and the allele was removed rather than left in place describing something it no longer did.

The question that would normally sink this is already answered. Clouds used to be client-only decoration, which would have meant inventing an invisible surface at an arbitrary height and hoping both sides agreed on it. In 26.1.2 they are not: DimensionSpecialEffects is gone and cloud height moved into the common-side EnvironmentAttributes.CLOUD_HEIGHT — four blocks thick, spatiallyInterpolated and syncable — and vanilla ships the exact predicate the feature needs, Entity.isInClouds(). So a cloud-treading horse can key off real clouds, on either side, and the horse dimension can set its own cloud height.

What is left is design, not discovery: what treading a cloud does to momentum, whether it restores anything, and whether a horse that cannot fly may still be granted it. The cloud_walk flag is already a legal traversal value and is deliberately composed beside flight_glide rather than folded into it, so nothing has to be unpicked to bring it back — it is an allele, an expression and a founder share, and the share comes back off n/n. One caution worth carrying: the alpha guard in isInClouds() means a dimension whose CLOUD_COLOR is fully transparent has no clouds at all, and the predicate is simply always false there.

Attached model parts

The owner wants a batch of these — “a HUGE win for how cool they look”

Blockbench meshes attached to the horse model, gene- or breed-driven, with no independent animation needed. High priority: unicorn horns, spikes / dorsal spines, crystalline growths — small models, head- or spine-parented, big visual payoff. Lower: pegasus wings (body-parented), lion and tufted tails, hoof feathering (already wanted by the draught breeds), tusks, antlers. Do a batch of the head-parented ones first — one render layer, many models.

  • This is the mod's first non-texture visual. The coat pipeline is texture-only and common/ is Minecraft-free, so common/ can only declare that a gene wants part X, by id; the renderer resolves it — the same shape as GradientLut arriving as an int[].
  • The proposal is GeckoLib plus Blockbench, with an attached layer whose bones are children of the horse's body bone so they inherit every animation. Check whether a plain render layer parented to the existing model part does the job before taking the dependency — animation inheritance is the entire argument for GeckoLib, and a horn does not animate independently of the head.
  • Saddle, armour and third-party tack render at the same time. Layer order and z-fighting are the predictable bugs, and these all need to scale with the horse too.

Undead horses — two genes, and a spawning overhaul

This used to be an open question and now it is a design. The question was how much of the mod an undead horse gets; the answer the owner has given is all of it — a skeleton horse is any breed that happens to carry a skeleton gene, and a zombie horse is any breed carrying a zombie gene. Undeath stops being an entity type this mod stands beside and becomes two loci, which is the answer that costs the least new machinery and the most spawning work.

One thing to settle before either gene is written

Each of these loci bundles several effects, and “a concept is a breed, not a gene” says an idea needing several effects is a breed rather than a locus. The undead case has two real answers to that — undeath is arguably one trait with many consequences, the way a lethal is, and the design wants an undead horse of any breed, which a breed cannot express. It is an open call, not a settled one, and the shape of both genes depends on which way it goes.

Why this is much cheaper than the version it replaces

Making undeath a gene means the genome, the coat pipeline, breeding, records, bond and the body stats all reach these horses for free, because they reach every Horse. The alternative was never small. Scoping to the concrete Horse class is not one guard in one place — it is 125 instanceof Horse checks across the NeoForge module against 22 for AbstractHorse, done in the handlers with plain instanceof rather than in a mixin that could be retargeted. Teaching all of that about two more entity types is the expensive version, and it is now off the table.

The skeleton gene

  • Recessive. Which means it is carried invisibly and can surface from two ordinary-looking parents — the thing the mod is for.
  • It heals without eating, and cannot eat at all. A clean inversion of the existing feeding loop rather than an addition to it. Note this collides with gated healing and with the diet gene, both of which assume food is how a horse recovers; the gene has to turn that off rather than compete with it.
  • It looks like a skeleton. The one genuinely hard part, because the coat pipeline paints a coat and a skeleton is the absence of one. Whether this is a painting gene at extreme priority, a whole-texture override, or a model swap is the open question, and it should be settled before either gene is started.
  • Aggressive, and permanently pacified by a bone. One bone, once, forever — which is carrot-not-stick and is also a nice inversion of taming, since the horse is hostile until you give it the thing it is made of.

The zombie gene

  • Literally twice the hearts, at a large speed penalty. Both are attributes the body genes already write, so the mechanism exists; the number is the design, and doubling health is the largest single stat effect in the mod by a distance.
  • Obligate carnivore. Feeding is the loop here rather than a flavour note, and it has to answer what happens when a carnivore is offered the carrots that everything else in the mod runs on — breeding carrots especially, which are how a player steers a genome.
  • Aggressive, and only temporarily pacified with raw meat, until tamed. The deliberate contrast with the skeleton's single bone: one is a permanent bargain, the other is a rope you keep paying out until the horse is yours.

The spawning overhaul, which is the actual work

“Spawn as any breed, but with these genes” is not how undead horses spawn today — vanilla's skeleton horse arrives from a lightning trap and its zombie horse does not spawn naturally at all, and neither is a Horse, so BreedSpawnHandler never sees them. Replacing that means deciding what the vanilla entities become: suppressed and substituted with a gene-carrying minecraft:horse, or left alone beside their genetic counterparts. The first is the version that matches the ask and is also the one that touches another mod's undead horses, so it wants a config gate from the start.

The founder distribution is the part that decides whether this is fun. Both genes are recessive, and the founder table rule is that a recessive particle-style gene puts its expressing combinations into the wild population rather than carriers — otherwise the gene exists and nobody ever sees one. Which breeds carry it, and at what frequency, is a per-breed-sheet line and is where the flavour lives: an undead Shire and an undead Falabella are different jokes.

More of the mod already reaches them than the old version of this section said, and it matters because it is what would become dead code. entity/ModAttributes adds LAVA_MOVEMENT and DRAFT_POWER to a set that names SKELETON_HORSE and ZOMBIE_HORSE explicitly; the replaced saddle asset carries skeleton_horse_saddle and zombie_horse_saddle with the same three dyeable layers as a horse's, so bench-dyed tack renders on them; and every mixin targeting AbstractHorse reaches them already — carts, flight while ridden, instant jump, rearing, regeneration and two riders. If the vanilla entities are suppressed, all of that becomes dead code and should go in the same change. A fourth option named in the old version of this section — leave them alone and let the magical breeds be this mod's undead horses — is superseded by the above, and recorded here only so it is not re-proposed.

Undead Horses Plus (Bedrock addon) is on the survey list for ideas to fold in here before any of this is built.

A block’s representative texture is reachable generically as its own baked model’s particle sprite — ModelEvent.ModifyBakingResult exposes a mutable BakingResult after baking, and BlockStateModelSet does the same at runtime. So “any solid block” can be a runtime test rather than a hand-written list. Roughly 270–355 of vanilla’s 1,144 blocks qualify, plus whatever mods add, free.

The one real cost is not the materials. JumpModel.Unbaked.bake loops every material once per blockstate variant — ninety-six times over — which is already wasteful at twelve woods and is what would hurt at three hundred. Stall bars — a third per-material block family

A connecting, waterloggable horizontal-bars block, built the way jumps and the double gates already are: authored for vanilla's woods by a tools/bake-*.mjs script, generated for other mods' materials at run time by a compat/Generated* class, and the two halves kept in step by hand because nothing checks them.

  • It has to earn its place under the utility rule — nothing this mod adds may be purely aesthetic except breeding — and a block that pens a horse no better than a fence does not. Both of its utilities fall out of the same physical fact, which is a good sign they are the right ones. Bars are see-through, so a stalled horse still counts its herd and stays settled where a solid wall would isolate it. And bars are reachable, so you can groom, feed and lead a horse through them without opening the gate.
  • The herd half is the interesting one. It is a second consumer for a herd computation that has none, and it makes stable layout matter — the first time a block this mod adds cares where it sits relative to another.
  • It makes the jump bake cache worth doing. JumpModel.Unbaked.bake already loops every material once per blockstate variant. A third family on the same pattern is the point at which that stops being wasteful and starts being the cost — a cache keyed on (model id, rotation), as the standards row in the table above already argues on its own grounds.

From Simply Equine, whose BlockHorizontalBars is the shape — connecting, waterloggable, and with no gameplay attached to it whatever.

Callable horses — what is left after the ender whistle

Calling one horse from anywhere is built, as the ender whistle. What was designed and not built:

  • Walk if near, teleport if far. The ender whistle always teleports. Inside BondFollowGoal's range a walk would be more legible.
  • Bond gating. Binding needs ownership only, not a bond tier. Which tier, if any, is still an open number.
  • Bound modes on the three area whistles (loaded chunk / same dimension / any dimension by tier). Superseded by the ender whistle unless someone wants the ladder back.
  • A tooltip naming the horse, and a texture that tells a bound whistle from an unbound one — see the whistles page.
  • An optional keybind to blow the held or first hotbar whistle.
  • Non-horse equines. Binding refuses donkeys, mules and llamas, because only a Horse carries a genome and an owner check.

Art and coverage

  • Real textures. The four tickets share one, the three whistles share one, and both stall signs borrow minecraft:item/oak_sign.
  • Audit item/ModItems against the recipe folder and make sure every craftable item has a shapeless, at-least-one-modded-input recipe or a deliberate “not craftable, obtained by X” note. An item with no recipe and no page is invisible to a player who is not reading the source, and that is a 1.0 blocker.
  • Keep items.html current. It is hand-written against the recipe JSON; generating it, or checking it in a test, would stop it rotting.

Bond, herds and horse care

The core ships — the tier table, gain rates, daily cap and herd rule are on horse care. What is planned:

More bond sources

  • Sleeping nearby, +1/night — the one remaining source from the original design.
  • Being led on a lead counts. Walking a leashed horse is taking it for a walk — a small per-tick trickle while isLeashed(), the holder is the owner, and both are moving, capped by the daily limit like everything else.
  • Flavour interactions — petting and cleaning. Cheap if animation-only: right-click with an empty hand plays a short interaction and gives a little bond. Brushing is no longer one of these. It grew into the grooming tool below, which replaces shears outright rather than sitting beside them; whether grooming also heals a small amount — a second route through the healing gate, or exempt from it the way hand-feeding is — is still open and belongs with that section.

Two more tier behaviours, both harvested rather than invented

Both came out of the quality-of-life cluster, and both were keyed to something else in their original mods. Keying them to bond instead is the whole adaptation — it is the idiom bareback steering at tier 3 already established, it makes them a reward rather than a default, and it leaves a low-bond horse behaving exactly as vanilla does.

  • A horse that trusts you stops bucking. Horse Buff removes vanilla's random bucking outright; Superb Steeds keys it to being saddled. Here it is a bond tier: the random interruption fades as the horse comes to trust you. HorseRearFightMixin and the aggro rearing stay untouched — a horse that rears because you hit it, or because its herd is fighting, should go on doing so. The point is that rearing stops being noise and starts meaning something.
  • A bonded horse waits where you left it. Horse Tweaks pins a 16-block home on dismount and clears it with a lead; Horse Buff just stops saddled horses idling away. Here, at a tier, the horse stops wandering when you dismount and waits for you — no new item, no leash, and a low-bond horse still drifts, which is the thing bond is for. Note this is adjacent to a separate problem, not the same one — setPersistenceRequired is never called on taming or ownership, so whether an owned horse can despawn at all is an open question. Keep them apart: a horse that vanishes is a defect and its fix belongs to everybody, while waiting where you left it is a reward and belongs to bond. Fixing the first as a tier of the second would mean a low-bond horse is allowed to disappear.

A grooming tool, tiered — and shears stop working on a horse

Grooming is built, and it is minecraft:shears. server/HorseShearHandler gives an adult horse 1–3 horse hair and +2 bond once per Minecraft day, with a brush sound, heart particles and a message that already calls it grooming rather than shearing — the dressing was changed on 2026-09-13, and the mechanic never was shearing. What is missing is the tool. The verb reads as care and the item in your hand is a pair of blades.

  • A grooming tool replaces shears outright — not a better path beside them. Shears on a horse do nothing, and the one verb has one item. Anything less leaves the blades in the game as the beginner's grooming tool, which is the thing being fixed.
  • One verb, tiered by material, the way tack tiers are already scoped rather than the way a six-tool roster would be. A better tool grooms for more bond and a shorter cooldown. The hair yield stays 1–3 at every tier, so grooming never turns into a farming action and the bond stays the reason to do it.
  • The top tier grooms the whole herd at once. That is the ladder's destination, and it is the only rung that does something the lower ones cannot. It also gives the herd computation a consumer — the Herds list below records that it has none today, so this closes a listed gap rather than adding a system beside it.
  • The contract is wider than the handler. ProgressTask.SHEAR_HORSE and its advancement husbandry/shear_horse.json are both named for the old item, so a rename means bake-advancements.mjs and then check-progress-tasks.mjs. The vet kit recipe takes shears as an ingredient and is unaffected — shears keep every use they have that is not a horse.

The six-tool roster — dandy brush, body brush, rubber and metal curry combs, hoof pick, sponge — came from Simply Equine and was deliberately not taken. Six tools only earn their keep against a per-horse condition that decays, and a horse that suffers for going unbrushed is exactly the punishing husbandry philosophy rules out. The material ladder is what survived of it.

Ground work — a halter, a lead and a lunge line

Bond you build without riding. Clip a lunge line to a haltered horse and it works a circle around you; bond accrues on the existing slow tick, capped by the same daily limit as everything else. The point is the horses you cannot or will not ride — a foal, one carrying a disorder, one you have not saddled — which today have no bond source but hand-feeding.

  • The halter is a slot and belongs to the accessories roster, where it is listed. What lives here is what having one lets you do.
  • The lead is vanilla's. No new item: isLeashed() already drives the walking-bond bullet above, and lungeing is that same relationship held still.
  • The lunge line is the one new item, and the mechanic is the work. A horse circling a fixed point is movement nothing in the mod does, and it has to yield to being ridden, to fleeing and to the herd goals rather than fight them. That is a priority in the goal selector, so the behaviour hierarchy and its bake are part of the contract.
  • Riding crop, lunge whip and driving whip are skipped. They are implements for striking a horse to make it go, and the care layer is built on the opposite premise. Recorded here so nobody re-proposes them from the same source.

From Simply Equine, which shipped a halter, a lead and a lunge line in sixteen colours each, with no behaviour attached to any of them.

Herds

  • Nothing consumes the herd alpha. Computed on demand, no consumer — no herd-wide behaviour, no alpha-follows, no display.
  • Herd membership is invisible outside the inventory panel's “• herd” flag. The real surface is the browser.
  • Feeding is not dose- or temper-aware: any isFood stack from the owner is +2, whatever it is.
  • Gated healing has no info-panel line naming what is missing — a hurt horse near food but no water shows particles and nothing else.
  • A founding band has no family relations. A breed can say how many stallions and mares its bands are founded with (herd), but every member is a parentless founder: HorseRecord.founder leaves mother and father empty, and each genome is rolled independently from the breed sheet. So a foal standing in a band is nobody's foal, and could carry alleles neither adult beside it has. Doing it honestly means founding rolls the foal from the lead and a chosen mare (HorseRecord.bred, a real generation) rather than from the sheet — a change to what founding is. Stamping the ids without the descent was considered and rejected: the pedigree screen would present a relationship the genetics contradict.

Troughs and salt licks

From Animania, and asked for specifically as a trough built out of the existing modular-wood framework, in any square or rectangular shape.

No block of this mod feeds a horse today. Feeding is grazing and hand-feeding — HungerFoodGoal walks a horse about ten blocks to the best thing it can reach, with a hay bale as the largest meal — so a trough is the first place a horse eats from, and that is the feature rather than the block.

  • The wood half is genuinely reusable and the paint half is not. compat/MaterialScan and ModdedMaterials.woods() already serve double gates, jumps and carts from one roster, so “a trough in any wood, vanilla or modded” is an existing pattern with three precedents. But there is no shared paint system — jumps, tack and generated armour each tint their own way, and two of them duplicate the same dye-reading lines with a source comment saying “if a third place needs dyes, extract it then”. A trough plus the cart and jump bench would be the third and fourth places. That extraction is now due.
  • Multi-block in any rectangle is the genuinely new part. Nothing in the mod assembles a structure from several placed blocks; the nearest thing is StallDetector, which recognises an enclosure the player built rather than forming one. Worth reading it first — the answer to “what counts as a valid shape, and what happens when someone breaks one block of it” is already worked out there.
  • Salt licks were asked for alongside. They have no mechanism here at all — a block a horse walks to and uses over time, which is closer to care than to feeding. Keep it carrot-not-stick: a salt lick that grants something is in keeping; a horse that suffers without one is the husbandry-chore version and is exactly what the owner's note warns against when it says to hold Animania's ideas to this mod's philosophy.
  • Any mod's trough-alike should work, the way compat/HayBales made every mod's hay bale grazeable with a tag rather than a list. Same trick, same reason.

A research paper names an allele, not a gene

The wanted end state: research papers pull out individual alleles rather than whole genes, the equine research shelf lets you pick two of them and binds them into one book, and that book is what the carrot recipes take. The crafting recipes themselves do not change. The load-bearing half is built — a carrot already names exactly the pair it grants. What is left, in the order that keeps the game working at each step:

  • The paper carries an allele. RESEARCH_GENE becomes a gene key plus an allele token, and the discovery system that grants papers has to decide which allele a horse teaches you — presumably the ones it actually carries, which is the same question common/horse/RosterGenetics already answers for the browser's pickers.
  • The shelf binds two into a book. Its Copy tab already picks one gene and spends blank books; this is that tab taking two allele papers instead. ResearchShelfMenu and ResearchShelfScreen.
  • The recipe takes the book. KnownGeneSpliceRecipe currently matches a research paper and calls CarrotEffect.defaultSpliceFor to decide the pair; it would read the pair off the book instead, at which point defaultSpliceFor and Gene.geneCarrotHomozygous() both stop being needed and should go.
  • The tooltip. A Known Gene Splice carrot already carries the right data and has never shown it — every one of them looks identical in the inventory. That is worth doing whether or not the rest lands.

Note the interaction: the carrot's homozygous-by-default rule is a stopgap that this design deletes. Once the player picks both alleles there is no default to choose.

The carrot content pass

The mechanism is complete. What is missing is content across every registered gene, not machinery.

Every built-in gene needs a real carrot, not the minimal fallback

The parameterised recipe means a gene can have a carrot; it does not mean the carrot is balanced. Today every built-in falls back to the minimal recipe and the uniform splice draw. Each gene wants a hand-tuned carrot block: a rarity tier matching how strong the gene is, flavour ingredients that read as the gene (a nether wart for lava milk, a glow ink sac for light, an amethyst shard for a LUT palette…), and a splice table producing a sensible outcome rather than a coin-flip on a many-allele locus.

Note the recipe currently takes exactly four items with no flavour slot, so shipping flavour lists means re-opening the recipe as well as writing the data.

  • The Epigenetic Stabilizer carrot. The other end of the dial from the Unknown Epigenetic Splice: it forces the fed parent's passed-on copies to be inherited verbatim, suppressing drift for one breeding, so a player who wants a near-clone foal can buy that. Now that drift is the default on every value this is the only way to switch it off, which is what makes it worth having.
  • Carrot effects captured on a seed jar and applied at impregnation. None are carried today.
  • A database check inside the recipe. The paper ingredient is the whole gate today, which is probably right — but it means a paper traded from another player works without the receiver ever discovering the gene. Decide deliberately.
  • New carrots are cheap, and the themed splices are the first proof of it: once the GameteBias hook and the carrot_effects component exist, a new carrot is a CarrotEffect case and a fold rule. The list is meant to grow.

Assisted reproduction: the rest of the seed jar, and the later tiers

Finishing the seed jar

The cut-down flow is built and written up on breeding. What is left:

  • A collectedFrom marker (player-made vs villager-sold) and the “no Known Gene Splice carrot on a jar” recipe restriction.
  • This is what the scientist is missing. His trade table shipped without the two rows that wanted a vial — selling a random rolled genotype, and buying a player-made one back — because both need collectedFrom and a vial worth rolling. His buy row takes a stallion seed jar at every tier instead. Adding the rows later is two JSON files and, for the buy-back, a components predicate on the wants block, which the trade format already supports.

The later tiers

Embryo extraction, gene screening, gene splicing, cloning, cross-species. Each is a block with an inventory and a screen, and each is endgame by intent. Three notes that change how they should be scoped:

  • The gene sequencer is not new work — it is the browser or the family-tree screen pointed at a vial or an embryo. Build it as a view, not a machine with its own UI.
  • Gene splicing is the first thing in the mod that writes an arbitrary genotype in survival. It needs the same validity predicate the creative spawner needs — is this allele pair legal, does this locus exist — and that predicate should be written once and shared.
  • Cross-species is much larger than its row. Donkeys and mules are separate vanilla entity types, mules are sterile by design, and neither carries any of this mod's genetics. Extending the genome to another species is a project, not a tier. Scope it separately or cut it.

The horse browser

The H-key screen is a real container menu; the horse list and the gene database are built. The full plan is six tabs, and what is left is the vial inventory, the herd view, the bonding view and a generated crafting tree, plus out-of-band actions (teleport a horse to a stall, whistle for bonded horses, recall to a holding pen).

The owned-horse index is still owed, and the horse list is running without it

The expensive part was never the UI. A proper per-player owned-horse registry — updated on tame, breed, death and transfer, holding enough denormalised data (name, sex, age, genotype, bond, herd) that horses in unloaded chunks still appear — does not exist. The My horses tab ships on HorseRoster instead, which walks the whole ancestry table on request, cuts the result at HorseRosterPayload.MAX_ENTRIES and sends it, and the client sorts and filters what it got. What is owed is the registry, with sort and filter running server-side over it, and pagination. HorseQuery is already game-free, so the same predicate can run on the server the day the index exists.

The tab the owner has asked for

The bonding tab. The bond level with each horse, the tier it is at, and concrete guidance on how to raise it — which sources are available, what the daily cap is, how close the next tier is. This is the surface bond is missing. My horses shows the number and sorts by it, which is not the same thing: it says where you are and not how to move.

The rest

  • A row should open the horse. Clicking a My horses row selects it and fills the footer; it should reach the information screen and the family tree. Both want a HorseRecord and the roster deliberately does not carry one (it carries a HorseListing, a tenth of the bytes), so this needs a fetch-one-record round trip.
  • FamilyTreeScreen should become a tab rather than stay a separate screen. Row portraits exist — client/HorsePortrait, used by the My horses table and both breeding pickers — but they are a second copy of the tree's drawHorseModel rather than a shared one (gap 78), and merging them is part of this. It is also the working reference for a from-scratch retained-mode screen in this codebase, scissor clipping and entity draw included — with the standing warning: build the render state from a throwaway client-only Horse, and never touch ClientCoatCache.
  • The crafting-tree tab goes stale on contact with a modded gene. Generate it from the recipe manager at runtime; do not hard-code the graph.
  • Teleport, recall and whistle need the stall and pen definitions above, plus answers for a destination that no longer exists and for whether any of it crosses dimensions.
  • A natural / magical filter on the Breeds tab. Breed.magical() is already on every breed, so the filter is a toggle beside the list and a clause in HorseBrowserScreen.drawBreedList. Build it with the first magical breed, so it can be seen doing something.

The horse menu — acting on a horse, not only reading about one

The browser's remaining tabs are a separate and larger job; this is the smaller, sharper set of asks about the screen as it stands. The thread running through all of them: the menu is a report today, and every one of these turns it into a console.

Teleport the horse to you, from the Where column

The column exists and is inert. “Where” is the twelfth column on My horses and already prints overworld 118, 71, -204, built server-side by HorseRoster.where. Clicking it should attempt to bring the horse to you.

  • “Use /tp” was the ask, and the mechanism deserves a second look rather than a literal reading. The mod already teleports horses properly — the ender whistle moves a bound horse across dimensions, and tickets send one to a stall. Routing the button through the same code is better than issuing a command: a command needs permissions the player may not have, silently does nothing for them if they do not, and skips every check the whistle makes.
  • It must answer the unloaded case, which is most rows. HorseRoster.entry() only fills Where for a horse in a loaded chunk; everything else reads not loaded, and those are exactly the horses you would want to summon. data/HorseWhereabouts already exists as a SavedData of last-seen dimension, position and death, and the roster does not read it — wiring that in is the prerequisite and improves the column whether or not the button is built.
  • It needs a cost or a gate. A free summon-any-horse-from-anywhere button makes whistles, tickets and holding pens redundant in one stroke. Whatever gates it — a bond tier, a cooldown, an item — should be decided before the button, not retrofitted after players have it.

A waypoint column, when a map mod is running

A checkbox per horse that drops a waypoint on the player's minimap and refreshes it every thirty seconds.

Nothing in the repo touches any map mod today — no Xaero, no JourneyMap, no waypoint code of any kind — so this is a new integration from zero, and it is the one item here with a third-party API at the end of it. Two things to settle first: which map mods (their waypoint APIs are unrelated to each other, so each is its own integration and the column should appear per-mod rather than as one checkbox), and where the thirty-second refresh runs, since it has to keep updating while the screen is closed and therefore cannot live in the screen. The fail-soft rule applies as everywhere else: absent map mod, absent column, no crash and no hard dependency.

Refresh on its own

Asked for on three events — screen opened, a foal born, a horse died. Today there is a Refresh button and a lazy first fetch: HorseBrowserScreen.init() requests the roster only if the client has never received one, and HorseRoster.sendTo has exactly one caller, the payload handler. So a player who breeds a horse and opens the menu sees the world as it was when they last pressed a button.

  • On open is trivial — drop the !received() guard, at the cost of a round-trip every time the screen opens.
  • Birth and death are pushes, not pulls, and that is the actual work. The server has to notice and send, which means a hook in the foaling and death paths and a payload that says what changed. Re-sending the whole roster on every birth in a big stable is the version to avoid; ClientHorseRoster is already a UUID-keyed cache with a version counter, so it is built for a delta rather than a replacement.
  • Transfer and taming belong on the same list, even though they were not asked for — they change the roster for exactly the same reason, and adding them later means touching the same code twice.

RideHUD, and stats while you ride

Wanted integrated rather than assessed. The mod has one GUI overlay today — client/FlightHudLayer, a single line above the hotbar naming the dismount key while flying, and itself unverified at runtime — plus JumpBarCooldownMixin, which tints a vanilla bar. There is no stats readout anywhere outside a screen you have to open.

That makes this the first real HUD in the mod, and the design question is restraint rather than capability: every number already exists on HorseInfoScreen, and the reason to show speed, jump, health and bond while riding is that opening a screen mid-gallop is not a thing anyone does. Keep it to what changes while you ride.

Leave My Bars Alone was read, and the call was to fold it in here rather than rebuild it

Leave My Bars Alone (Fuzs, MPL-2.0) does exactly two things, and it was read from source on 2026-09-21 because it is one of the very few mods on this page with a branch targeting our own Minecraft version. It re-adds the food bar as an extra stacked GUI layer anchored to VEHICLE_HEALTH — it does not move the mount's health bar — and it narrows when vanilla is allowed to swap the XP bar for the jump-charge bar, via one @ModifyVariable into nextContextualInfoState, so XP stays visible unless you are actually mid-charge. Two config booleans, both default on, no keybind, two files in the whole mod.

The owner's call was not to rebuild either half. We do not touch vanilla bars separately; instead this section is designed so that our readout does not add to the clutter in the first place, and the vanilla bar problem stays solved by a maintained mod that already solves it. One thing to check when this is built: JumpBarCooldownMixin already modifies the same vanilla jump bar, so the two want testing together before either is claimed to work.

A different look for the menu — explicitly not committed

The owner likes Biology Dictionary's UI and raised it as “maybe as an option or alternative style”, with “not a requirement, not committed to” said outright. Recorded here so the idea survives, and flagged so nobody builds it as a decision that was made. Worth knowing what it would cost before anyone is tempted: HorseBrowserScreen is a single 3,430-line Screen with its layout inline, so a second skin means extracting a layout seam first — which is real work with no user-visible result, and is the kind of thing that only pays if the restyle is actually wanted.

More generated stables

Three ship, and the owner has said more are coming. The machinery is deliberately finished before the content: adding one is an NBT and a JSON, with no Java, so this is a content backlog rather than a build one.

  • Nothing yet decides which stable generates where. All three share one biome tag, so a snowy plains gets the same buildings as a savanna. A stable could name its own biomes — a line in its worldgen file and no code — and probably should once there are enough of them to tell apart.
  • A stable has no owner, no story and no loot. The buildings have chests, and the bake empties them. A stable that came with an equestrian living in it, or with tack in the chests, is the obvious next thing.
  • Nothing stops a player from simply taking the horses. They are untamed and unowned, so the whole reward is “find building, tame seven rare horses”. Whether that wants a cost — a price, a fight, a locked gate — is an open design question nobody has asked yet.

The redistribution licence on the three that ship is a blocker on distribution rather than on building, and is tracked as gap 80.

The cowboy in every village, and buildings that fit each one

Two asks, and the first is nearly free.

  • He spawns in plains villages and nowhere else. worldgen/BarnPoolInjector appends horsegenetics:cowboy_barn to minecraft:village/plains/terminators at server start, at weight 3, and injects into no other village type. Desert, savanna, taiga and snowy villages have no cowboy at all. Injecting into the other four terminator pools is a handful of lines in a class that already does exactly this once — but a plains barn dropped into a desert village is the reason it was not done, which is the second ask.
  • “He doesn't need a house, just plop his hitch down” is already built, and is the cheap route to every village type today. block/ModBlocks.COWBOY_HITCH ships and is craftable, and server/CowboyHitchHandler converts any unemployed adult villager within six blocks of one into a cowboy, provided none is already within thirty-two. So a hitch in a worldgen pool — no building, no jigsaw of our own — would put a cowboy in a village of any style, immediately, with the art problem deferred rather than solved.
  • Buildings that emulate each village style is the real project, and the owner has named it as such: a barn per village material palette, so the structure reads as part of the town rather than dropped on it. That is the generated stables backlog wearing a different hat — same NBT-and-JSON pipeline, no Java — and it should share whatever answer that section reaches about which building generates where. The Stables mod is on the survey list with the question of whether it can improve any of the villagers this mod adds — read it before the barn variants are designed.

Breeds

Cosmetics that are not genetics

  • Mane shape — Przewalski's upright with no forelock, the long flowing manes of the Andalusian / Friesian / Gypsy. Render-layer and model work. The Fjord's colour is off this list: the midtstol is drawn by dun, and the erect clip that shows it off is grooming rather than a mesh the horse is born with.

Breed-system follow-ups

  • More fictional breeds wanted. The shipped catalogue is real-world; the mod also wants a batch of invented ones — a werewolf breed built on LYCAN is possible — as gene suites.   • Traditional-band foals spawn at vanilla's ~5% instead of the intended “up to 3”. The SpawnGroupData route is unusable — Horse.finalizeSpawn replaces it per member — so this needs a direct setAge(-24000) on a herd member, or a mixin.

The creative spawner: a per-copy epigenetics editor

Tack, carts and other mods

The accessories system — everything a horse wears, made one way

This is one system, not eight features. Tack, barding, horse shoes, saddle pads, chests and the enchantments on any of them were asked for separately over several months and kept being scoped separately; each time, the same four questions came back — where does the piece live if the horse has only two equipment slots, how is it made, how is it decorated, and how is it drawn. They get one answer here, and the sections below are that answer's parts rather than independent tickets. Everything already researched for native tack, shoes and the saddle pad is folded in and not restated.

Tack has stats now — a settled call was reversed to allow this

Armour value, shoe effects and tack enchantments are therefore the point of this system rather than a ladder bolted onto a cosmetic one. The record is on design decisions and the rule is on philosophy.

Native tack works the alpha-cutout finding out in detail and it applies to every row above.

The seam for this already exists and was built expecting it. entity/HorseTackSlot is an enum of exactly two constants — SADDLE and ARMOUR — whose own documentation says the shape is there for later and names “a bridle, a blanket and shoes” as the obvious next ones — and a halter is a bridle's near neighbour. The screen that draws them is ours rather than vanilla's (HorseInfoScreen.drawTackSlot, reached by the small i button HorseScreenHooks adds beside vanilla's horse inventory), and clicks already round-trip through TackSlotPayload. So the roster is this enum growing by thirteen, and the screen it feeds — which is a much smaller statement of the work than fourteen new systems, and is the reason to do it as one change.

Per-leg is four times the geometry problem, not one-quarter of it. Boots and shoes are the only rows that anchor to four animated bones rather than to the body, so they are the ones to prototype first; if four independently-rendered leg pieces do not work, the rest of the roster does not care, and if they do, everything else is easier. CutieMarkLayer is the worked precedent for geometry riding an animated bone.

Deriving the models instead of drawing them

The owner's proposal, and it is the difference between this being affordable and not: clone the existing horse model and erase every part the piece does not touch, rather than modelling fourteen pieces by hand. That is exactly what the alpha-cutout finding already establishes for equipment — a cube whose texels are transparent does not appear, so one mesh is many pieces purely by which part of the sheet is painted — and generalising it from “three pieces off the saddle mesh” to “a piece is a named subset of the horse's own parts” is the actual feature.

The open question is inflation, not erasure. A piece derived from HORSE_BODY has to clear the skin or it z-fights, which the saddle-pad research already found the hard way; a per-piece inflation value is probably the whole of what the derivation needs to take as a parameter. Worth proving on one leg boot before any of the roster is committed to.

The tack bench, and what a bench buys

All of it is made at a bench of our own, and the reasoning is as much about recipes as about fiction. The owner's three arguments, which are all good ones:

  • It removes the recipe-collision problem entirely. A bench recipe is in our own menu type and cannot collide with vanilla's crafting table or with another mod's — which is the standing worry that check-recipes.mjs and the BHAR check exist to manage.
  • It is one switch. Disable the bench in config and horse armour cannot be crafted, with no per-recipe gating anywhere.
  • It takes a formula, not a list. The bench generates its recipes from a shape × a material, so all horse armour including modded metals is craftable without a file per combination — which is the same trick the generated-materials layer already plays, and should share its material roster rather than build a second one.

Saddles and bridles are made here too, so the bench is the single place tack comes from. Note it is a third bench: the dyeing bench ships and the cart and jump bench is proposed, and three benches with overlapping jobs wants a deliberate answer about what distinguishes them before the second one is built.

Dye, pattern, banner, and a preview

  • The dyeing bench grows to cover every new piece, not just the saddle and leather barding it dyes today.
  • Patterns, not just colours — stripes and dots were named. That is a second tint layer and a pattern id per piece, which IClientItemExtensions.getArmorLayerTintColor already makes cheap for two colours on one stack (see native tack), and expensive past that.
  • Banner patterns as an alternative to the mod's own. Already designed for the saddle pad as a chosen rectangle of a banner; generalising it to every piece is the ask, and the feasibility question flagged there is unanswered and gates all of it.
  • A 3D preview in the bench, on any horse you own. The horse browser already renders live horses, so the renderer exists; what does not is a preview that shows an unmade piece, which means the model derivation above has to run client-side on a configuration that no item carries yet.

Enchanted tack — and which of these are really genes

Every piece in the roster is enchantable. Horse armour enchantments were asked for from Wonderful Enchantments, to be applied “with logic” to any tack or shoe — meaning an enchantment has to declare which slots it is legal on, since a fly mask and a shoe do not want the same list.

The owner's own caveat is the interesting part: some of these are better as genes. That is a real fork and it should be decided per effect rather than wholesale, because the two are opposites in this mod — a gene is inherited, breedable and the point of the project; an enchantment is bought with levels and transfers with an item. The rule that falls out: if you would want to breed for it, it is a gene; if you would want to move it between horses, it is an enchantment. Worth writing as a decision before the first one is built.

Nothing in this mod is enchantable today, and one near-miss is worth knowing about. There is no enchantment registered, no enchantable component on any item, and no enchantable/ item tag shipped. compat/ModdedArmour does build each generated armour as a real ArmorMaterial carrying iron's enchantmentValue() — but with no enchantable component that value is inert, so a generated barding is not enchantable either. The mod's only enchantment code runs the other way: GeneDeathHandler.enchantedSword() rolls one onto a dropped sword. So the first enchantable piece pays for the whole path.

A ready-made roster, from a mod that built it as saddle upgrades

Horse Tweaks (MIT, Minecraft 1.12.2, abandoned 2019) put seven abilities on a single Enhanced Saddle as NBT flags, each costing saddle durability while it was doing something: swimming, easy jump, thorns, frost walker, leaf walker, feather fall and fire resistance. That is this section's roster already written by somebody else, and the owner's call on 2026-09-21 was to take it as enchantments rather than as a saddle — which is also the answer the rule above gives, since every one of them is a thing you would want to move between horses.

Two are already ours by another route and should not become enchantments: swimming is shipped through HorseWaterRidingHandler and HorseSwimMixin, and easy jump is shipped as HorseInstantJumpMixin behind ride.instant_jump. Feather fall is the one to think hardest about — we cancel fall damage today only while a horse is genuinely flying (server/FlightFallGuard), and an enchantment that does it for any horse is a larger change to how riding works than its five neighbours.

The durability-per-use idea is worth keeping even though the saddle is not: it is what stopped seven permanent abilities being strictly free, and an enchanted piece of tack has the same problem.

The armour materials still missing

Named from two mods and grouped because they are the same work: netherite (from Simple Netherite Horse Armor) and the bone, ender, emerald, obsidian and blaze set (from Special Armor & Tool Sets).

Netherite is not an oversight, and that is what makes it a decision rather than a task. The generated-materials layer derives an armour per modded metal or gem from c:ingots/* and c:gems/*, and MaterialScan.collectTagged deliberately skips every tag entry in the minecraft: namespace — because vanilla's own armours are left to BHAR on purpose. A netherite_horse_armor therefore means crossing that line knowingly. It is a defensible crossing, since vanilla ships no netherite horse armour for BHAR to have a recipe for, but it should be recorded as a call rather than slipped in as a material.

The other five are outside the scan by shape, not by policy. Emerald is the near-miss — it is a gem, so the machinery fits it exactly, and it is excluded only because minecraft:emerald is vanilla; bone, ender, obsidian and blaze are not ingots or gems at all and no c: tag will ever surface them. So this row is really one question asked twice: should the scan be widened to name vanilla materials deliberately, and if so, five or six armours fall out of one change rather than five hand-written ones. Answer that before writing any of them.

The armour stand, and the tack pack

From Horse Armor Stand, and expanded well past it. A stand that wears anything a horse can wear — which makes it the display case for the roster above and, not incidentally, the test rig for it.

  • Right-click opens a screen with scale, wood and paint (through the same wood-and-paint system the carts and jumps use), and a pose chosen as a frozen frame of any animation a horse actually has — which is the one genuinely novel piece, since it means sampling the animation state machine at an arbitrary point rather than playing it.
  • The tack pack is the part with gameplay in it: right-click the stand to load every piece on it into one bag, right-click a naked horse to dress it, right-click a dressed horse to strip it back into the bag. This is the answer to fourteen slots being tedious, and it is worth building early for that reason rather than late as a convenience — a fourteen-slot screen without it is a chore, and philosophy says not to add those.

Curios, if it is there — and never a dependency

Wanted, and explicitly not a hard dependency. The shape that satisfies both: the attachment is the authority and the slots are ours, and a Curios integration is a view onto them registered only when the mod is present — the same fail-soft pattern neoforge/compat/ already uses to read other mods' jars without depending on them.

Two things to check before promising it. Curios is built around slots on a player; whether its API models slots on an arbitrary entity on 26.1.2, and whether a Curios-owned slot can be the authority for something a render layer of ours draws, are both unverified here. If the answer is that Curios cannot hold horse slots, the integration inverts — a rider's Curios slot holding tack for the horse they are on — which is a different and smaller feature, and should be recognised as a substitute rather than shipped as if it were the thing asked for.

Horse shoes — craftable, enchantable, equippable

Shoes as real equipment: crafted from a metal, enchantable like armour, worn on the hooves.

There is no third slot to put them in. The horse family has two equipment slots, the saddle and the body, and both are spoken for — this mod already writes to them (BarebackSteeringHandler's phantom saddle) and already replaces their assets (assets/minecraft/equipment/saddle.json). So shoes are a data attachment plus a render layer of our own, not an EquipmentSlot, and the enchantable half means a genuine Item carrying enchantments rather than a flag on the horse.

What they could do is the part worth deciding first, because it decides whether they are tack or a gimmick: grip on ice, speed or quiet on particular blocks, fall-damage reduction, protection from hot ground — and, if wear is wanted, a durability that is the first thing in the mod to consume itself. Note the overlap with native tack and with the saddle pad: three separate pieces of tack work that should share one answer about how tack is made, decorated and rendered, rather than three.

That last paragraph is what this whole section is the answer to. It closed by naming the overlap with native tack and the saddle pad and asking for “one answer about how tack is made, decorated and rendered, rather than three”. The answer is the bench, the decoration screen and derived geometry — so what is left specific to shoes is only what they do, and four of them per horse.

Native tack — cheap in code, expensive in art

Assessed against this codebase rather than in the abstract. Every seam already exists: CutieMarkLayer is a worked precedent for geometry anchored to an animated bone (body.translateAndRotate(poseStack)), EquineSaddleModel shows the cube layout for head strapwork, a new item is one line in ModItems, and the synced-attachment pattern has ten instances already.

  • Geometry is hidden with alpha, not with JSON — and that is the finding that changes this section. Equipment draws through an alpha-cutout pass, so any cube whose texels are left transparent simply does not appear. One existing mesh therefore becomes many pieces purely by which part of the sheet is painted. A halter is head_saddle + mouth_saddle_wrap with everything else erased; a bridle adds the bits and reins; a blanket is the horse-armour body cube. No modeling, and for those three, no Java at all.
  • A blanket needs no minecraft: override. Equipment assets are keyed by the file's own namespace, so assets/horsegenetics/equipment/blanket.json registers horsegenetics:blanket and its texture resolves under our namespace too. Equipping is not tag-gated: Horse.canUseSlot returns true unconditionally, so any item with Equippable(slot=BODY) equips and renders. Cost per piece: one JSON, one or two PNGs, three lines of registration.
  • The two-slot ceiling is real but less binding than it looked. EquipmentSlot has exactly two animal slots and EquipmentClientInfo.LayerType is non-extended, so three simultaneous pieces still needs a synced attachment and a bespoke layer. But two pieces — one on BODY, one on SADDLE — are free today and carry independent dye colours, because they are two stacks.
  • Two colours on ONE item: use NeoForge's hook, not a custom layer. Vanilla reads the dye once, outside the layer loop, so a single stack gives one colour however many layers it declares. NeoForge's IClientItemExtensions.getArmorLayerTintColor exists for exactly this and hands you the layer index; returning 0 skips a layer entirely, which is free per-piece toggling. About twenty lines of client Java and a two-int component — no new geometry, no custom render layer. This supersedes the draw-the-parts-twice idea, which additionally does not work as written: rendering is deferred, so toggling part.visible between two submits mutates one shared model and both draw the final state.
  • Two traps worth writing down. A saddle pad must use the HORSE_BODY mesh (inflated 0.1, so it clears the skin) and not the HORSE_SADDLE body cube, which is exactly coincident with the horse and z-fights. And the reins' visibility is hardwired to isRidden in EquineSaddleModel.setupAnim, so a bridle inherits that and cannot change it without Java.
  • The four riding sets are not four features, they are four art styles. English, western, racing and jumping share identical code and differ only in textures — and Horse Tack's are All Rights Reserved even though sekelsta's code is MIT, so not one may be copied. This project ships four tickets sharing one texture and four whistles sharing another; it cannot afford seven sets of sixteen PNGs. A draft harness is the only category needing genuinely new geometry.
  • There is no third-party code to start from — checked, not assumed. Horse Tack's source was read in full: two Java files, a long list of literal registrations, no rendering and no equip logic, on APIs 26.1.2 has removed. The taxonomy is the only transferable part — saddle pad, halter, english / jumping / western / racing tack, cart harness, and blankets (solid, trimmed, novelty) — and its two lessons are both cautionary: do not pre-register colour variants (dyeable layers collapse each ×16 run to one item), and do not pre-register combinations (that was a one-slot workaround; we have two slots).
  • Minimum viable version: one saddle pad and one halter, dyeable through vanilla dyed_color, on a new synced attachment, drawn by one render layer. No stats (settled), no sets, no foal support — vanilla already draws no equipment on babies.
  • It contradicts the current positioning. Mod compatibility says in as many words that tack is what this mod deliberately leaves to others and that the two are “almost perfectly complementary”. Shipping native tack means rewriting that claim in the same change.

Carrying things, and carrying horses

Two asks that sound unrelated and share one question — what is a horse's inventory and who owns it — so they are decided together even though they ship apart.

Horse chests, ender chests, and the look of a loaded horse

Give a horse saddlebags — storage that travels with the animal — in two slots, per the roster.

A horse is deliberately not a chested horse in vanilla. The chest flag, the extra slots and the screen that shows them all live on AbstractChestedHorse (donkey, mule, llama), and Horse does not extend it — so there is nothing to switch on, and this is a container of our own or it is nothing.

That part is well-trodden ground here. The mod already registers menus (menu/ModMenus, with EquestrianBenchMenu and ResearchShelfMenu as working examples) and already stores per-horse state in a data attachment, which is where a container would live. It also already runs six containers on the things a horse pulls — WagonMenu alone is three chests over twelve rows, all backed by carts/util/CartInventory — so the inventory code is written and only its owner changes. entity/HorseTackSlot draws the line explicitly today: “the chest a donkey carries is not one of these and is not here.”

  • An ender chest on a horse was asked for by name. It is the interesting one because it has no contents of its own — it is a view onto the player's ender inventory, so it sidesteps every question about what happens to the contents on death or on a transfer of ownership, and raises one of its own: whose ender inventory, the owner's or the rider's.
  • Chests from other mods must work. Not a hardcoded list of chest items — the same rule the rest of the compat layer follows. A chest is whatever carries the right tag, and its look comes from what it is. Its size is the container's own business and should not be confused with the horse's: the bullet below is the ceiling the animal imposes, and a roomy modded chest does not raise it. Which of the two binds — and whether a big chest on a weak horse is refused or simply part-filled — wants deciding once rather than per chest.
  • The look of a chested horse is its own complaint, and a fair one. Vanilla's chested donkey is two boxes clipped to its flanks. Since this mod already re-renders the horse and is proposing to derive equipment geometry from the horse's own parts, panniers that actually sit on the animal are the same feature as everything else in the accessories system and should be built with it, not before it.
  • Capacity is a function of pulling ability, not size (owner). That stat already exists and is already resolved from the genome: a 1–10 score read as HorseTraits.pull(), reached through HorseDraft.pullOf, falling back to HorseTraits.BASE_PULL for a horse with no genome of ours. So there is nothing to derive — the number a saddlebag should read is the number the carts already read. Size would have been the wrong proxy, and cheaply wrong: it measures how big a horse is rather than what it can shift, so a tall light horse would out-carry a cob that plainly should win. Pull is the honest stat, and using it means one axis of strength runs through carts and storage both, so a horse bred to haul a wagon is the horse that carries the most — rather than two parallel breeding goals that happen to look alike.
  • Still undecided: whether the chest is a vanilla chest right-clicked onto the horse or an item of our own; whether it survives a transfer of ownership and what happens to the contents if it does not; and whether it appears beside the saddle in the horse screen or in a screen of its own.

A horse in your pocket — virtual stables

From the Stables Bukkit plugin's virtual-stable idea, reshaped for this mod: an object that holds a horse you own, so that moving twenty horses is not twenty trips and bringing one along is not a lead across three biomes.

  • Four things in the mod look like this and none of them is it, which is worth stating precisely so nobody builds a fifth by accident. The seed jar holds a genome sample; the preset spawn egg holds a genotype, epigenome, sex and age — a recipe for a horse, with no identity, equipment, name, bond or pedigree edge; tickets and pen signs move the live animal to a stall, so it never stops existing; and transfer papers move ownership only. What none of them does is hold the animal itself, and that is the entire feature.
  • “Pocket dimension” is probably the wrong implementation of the right idea. The horse dimension exists and is a real place, so stabling a horse there and fetching it back is a mechanic with a location in it rather than a serialised entity in an item stack. Worth deciding which before anything is built, because they are different features wearing the same name: one is storage, the other is a place you visit.
  • The trap is the one gap 73 is about. Destroying a horse entity and rebuilding it later from saved data is exactly what the lycanthropy shift does, and the thing that can go silently wrong is attachment round-tripping — a horse that comes back with a fresh record and no pedigree looks fine and is not. Whatever this is built on should reuse that path and fix it once, not open a second one.
  • It needs a limit and a cost, or it eats the mod. An item that holds any horse instantly makes stalls, pens, tickets, whistles and callable horses redundant. That is an argument for tying it to the barn or to an equestrian rather than to a craftable item — an open design question, and the most important one here.

The cart and jump bench — built to order, not crafted from a grid

An overhaul of how carts are made, and jumps with it. Both are built from wood today and both take the wood as a whole; the ask is to make the piece the unit. One bench, two products, because the mechanic is identical and shipping it twice would be two screens that drift.

Carts

  • A wood per piece, not a wood per cart — and this is a real rewrite, not a screen. Wheels of one wood, bed of another, shafts of a third. The obstacle is that a cart's wood is in its registry id today: oak_wagon, biomesoplenty_maple_plow, one item per (wood × kind), and CartWood's own documentation says that id is permanent once a world uses it. Per-piece materials cannot be an id, so they have to become components on the stack and data on the entity — which is exactly the shape jumps already moved to, going from twelve blocks and thirty-six items down to one item carrying JumpMaterials. Jumps are the worked precedent for this whole section, and the cart change is that migration done a second time.
  • Dye or paint the wood, on top of the choice of wood — the two are different axes and both were asked for. Nothing in the carts/ package tints anything today; a cart's texture is assembled from its wood's three block sprites and that is all.
  • It deletes a duplication rather than deepening one. Seventy-two shipped recipe JSONs (twelve woods × six carts, baked by tools/bake-carts.mjs) are mirrored at run time by compat/GeneratedCarts for modded woods, and the two must agree — a drift is a modded cart that will not craft and logs nothing. A bench that composes a cart from materials has no per-wood recipe to bake and no second implementation to keep in step, which retires that whole hazard. That is the strongest argument for the bench and it is not the one that was made for it; worth weighing before the screen is designed.

Jumps

Start from what jumps already are, because it is more than the ask assumes. A jump is one item carrying JumpMaterials — rails wood, standards wood, rails dye, standards dye and a size — edited in JumpScreen/JumpMenu, with three cosmetic styles and a ten-step height ladder. So dye is built, per half, including modded dyes vanilla has no name for. What is missing from this list is patterns, opacity, flags and depth.

  • Patterns and an opacity. Patterns rather than flat colour, and a translucency slider — which is the only one of these with a rendering cost, since a translucent block leaves the solid render type and has to be sorted. Note the existing dye is consumed irreversibly (a plank swap is reversible, a dye is not); a pattern and an opacity should decide deliberately whether they inherit that, because a slider you cannot slide back is a trap the current design gets away with only because colour is a one-time choice.
  • Little flags on the standards. Geometry in the existing baker, and the cheapest thing in this section.
  • Two-deep jumps, which contradicts a standing rule: the obstacle vocabulary records the owner's own bound that no style is more than one block deep, because a two-block jump stops being a thing you put in a row and becomes a structure. The intake asks for “potentially two deep”, which reads as reopening it rather than overriding it — settle that on design decisions before building, not in the baker.
  • Jump styles that look like real cross-country fences, from Simply Cross Country. That is the same geometry backlog already recorded — logs, brush, ditches, fillers, walls, water — with a reference to work from, which is what it was missing. Note the three styles that exist (VERTICAL, OXER, CROSSRAILS) collide identically on purpose, so adding a look costs nothing in balance; a cross-country style that jumped differently would be taking that back.

Three benches now, and that is the thing to decide first. The dyeing bench ships, a tack bench is proposed, and this is a third. They share a wood-and-paint picker and differ only in what comes out. Either one bench grows tabs or three benches share a widget; picking by accident means three implementations of the colour picker.

Mod patches

What is left after the compatibility pass. Most of the patches that were asked for turned out to need no patch at all; the detail is on Mod compatibility and is not repeated here. The third one — native tack — grew into the accessories system and has moved there.

Detect Realistic Horse Genetics and say so

Not a patch — the two mods both replace the horse and both own coat generation, so whoever registers last wins. The useful version is a load-time check that logs “these two mods both replace the horse; run one” rather than letting a player discover it as a half-broken world.

It is a handful of lines in a place that exists. neoforge-26.1.2/.../neoforge/compat/ already walks ModList.get().getModFiles() in the mod constructor and already knows how to fail soft on somebody else's jar — see the compat layer. What is undecided is only where the warning goes: a log line is the minimum, but a player who reads logs is not the player this protects.

A recipe-collision check against BHAR

BHAR adds crafting recipes for the vanilla saddle and all four horse armours. This mod adds none for vanilla's items, so there is no collision today, and the armours it generates are in its own namespace with modded-ingot inputs, deliberately outside the collision space. neoforge-26.1.2/tools/check-recipes.mjs already answers the question mechanically and would need only to be pointed at their recipe ids — a check that would catch it if that ever stopped being true. Note the licence: their custom terms forbid redistributing any part, so the check may not embed a copy of their files.

The saddle pad, and cutting a banner into one

The Tack Dyeing Bench ships its dyeing half. The unbuilt half is the saddle pad: a pad fitted to the horse, whose design is a chosen rectangle of a banner placed on it. Written as design and risks rather than as a plan, because one feasibility question is still open.

The design

  • The pad rides its own attachment, not an equipment slot. There are only two animal slots and horse armour holds one, so a slot-based pad would mean riding unarmoured. An attachment plus a render layer costs roughly 150 lines and buys armour and pad together. It needs no modeling: the layer can bake vanilla's HORSE_ARMOR mesh, which already covers the body.
  • The pad is authored at maximum coverage and cut down by the player. The texture is drawn as the largest sheet the body can carry, and the bench lets the player scale it freely in every direction — so “saddle pad” and “full caparison” are the same item at two settings rather than two items. The extent is part of the design, stored with it.
  • The banner is placed, not merely cropped. Under the pad the player moves, scales, squashes, stretches and tiles the banner image freely; the crop rectangle is the start of it, not the whole of it. Mechanically that is a 2D transform — offset, scale per axis, tile on or off — carried on the component beside the pattern list, which is cheap to store and cheap to apply.
  • The layout is the player's choice too. Once per flank, continuous across the whole sheet, tiled, or flanks-with-a-plain-ground: all four are settings at the bench.
  • The banner is consumed, and a pad is washable. Making a pad spends the banner; washing strips the design back to a blank pad and loses it. Mistakes stay cheap without making the banner free.
  • The pad is fitted from a third slot in the horse's own inventory, beside saddle and armour — the place a player already looks for tack. This is the most discoverable option and the most dangerous one; see below.
  • No pads on foals. A foal's UV layout is completely different from an adult's, so a foal pad is a second set of art drawn from scratch for something worn briefly. Vanilla renders no equipment on a foal either.

The risks

  • The live 3D preview is the open one. A horse that re-bakes while the player drags is precisely the shape of the incident recorded in GeneticCoatTextureFactory, where unbudgeted per-frame baking froze the owner's machine. It must be designed around rather than discovered: bake on release rather than per frame, or preview the transform as a UV change on an already-baked sheet and only re-composite when the pattern set itself changes. The existing texel budget, cache and reload invalidation are the right machinery; they have to be wired in from the start.
  • Rasterising a banner is answered and is not a risk. A pattern layer resolves to an ordinary resource PNG under textures/entity/banner/, all 64×64, and the composite is base × baseColour then alpha-over each pattern × layerColour. The coat engine's loader, NativeImage compositor and DynamicTexture upload all apply unchanged. The banner's own front face is a clean 20×40 texel rectangle at origin (1,1), so a crop has real coordinates to work in. The in-GUI preview is free: vanilla renders a live BannerPatternLayers into a GUI rect already, which is how the loom does it.
  • The third slot: the danger is not where it looked. NeoForge offers no hook, so a slot beside the saddle means a mixin on HorseInventoryMenu, or our own separate screen. No screen mixin is needed — a supported render event can draw the empty-slot hole, which avoids putting a client-only mixin in a config that has no "client" section and would crash a dedicated server.
  • The real risk is quickMoveStack, not the slot. AbstractMountInventoryMenu.quickMoveStack is shared with donkeys, mules, llamas and nautiluses, and computes every shift-click range from hardcoded 0, 1, 2 and 2 + mountContainer.getContainerSize(). A third slot invalidates that arithmetic wherever it is inserted, and a mis-ranged moveItemStackTo is how items duplicate or vanish. It is also the failure that looks fine until somebody shift-clicks with a chest on the horse. Secondary: the client builds its own menu from the open packet, so the mod must stay network-required on both sides or every slot index is off by one.
  • Storing the pad in an attachment is blessed, not a hack. The saddle and armour slots are not backed by the horse's container either — Mob.createEquipmentSlotContainer fronts the equipment array with a ContainerSingleItem view that owns no storage. A pad slot reading an attachment is the same shape. Keep one authority (never mirror it into a container), store stack.copy() and write back through setData, and it stays out of hopper reach, which a widened container would not.
  • Two hazards from gap 156. Do not build the pad on EquipmentSlot.SADDLE, and never let a pad item carry PHANTOM_SADDLE: BarebackSteeringHandler writes that slot every tick and its reclaim sweep destroys anything carrying that component in a rider's inventory.

Horsemanship — the rider is half of what a horse can do

This reverses a position this page held twice. A horsemanship skill sat on the survey backlog for months with an objection attached: it would be the first thing in the mod where the player progresses rather than the horse, which cuts against breeding being the progression — the same objection that still stands against a competition ladder. The owner reframed it on 2026-09-21 and the reframing is what makes it buildable: bonding makes the horse listen, and skill makes you a better rider. Neither makes the horse a better horse.

The bonus belongs to the pairing, and never to the animal

A horse's stored stats are always exactly its genome. The bonus is an AttributeModifier added when you mount and removed when you dismount, computed from your skill and your bond with that horse. Nothing is ever written to the horse.

That is the whole of why this is allowed and a carrot that adds a heart is not. The carrot changes the horse; this changes the ride. It also means HorseInfoScreen keeps telling the truth, a sold horse carries none of your skill with it, and the same horse under a novice performs at its genome exactly as it does today.

What it may move, and how far

  • Speed and jump only. Never health, never size — those are health-gene territory, and a rider adding hearts would make a genetically frail horse stop being legible at a glance, which is the whole reason frailty is expressed as fewer hearts.
  • Uncapped, with sharply diminishing returns. The owner's call, and it is the part of this section carrying the design risk: with no hard ceiling, the curve is the only thing keeping a well-ridden ordinary horse from overtaking a bred one. Pick the curve against real genome spread — the gap between a poor and a good horse's resolved speed — and not in the abstract. Write the chosen shape and the numbers it was measured against on this page's eventual replacement, because a curve nobody can re-derive is a curve the next session will quietly get wrong.
  • Two axes, multiplied. General skill carries between horses; bond is the per-horse half and already exists. A skilled rider on a stranger's horse is competent and not at their best, which is the behaviour worth having.

The hook: Pufferfish’s Skills, and a tree that is pure data

An optional dependency, never a requirement. Three skills mods were read from their jars on 2026-09-21 — Pufferfish's Skills, RPG Attribute System and Skill Tree (RPG Series) — and only the first is a usable seam.

  • The tree is shipped as data and needs no code at all. Pufferfish's PackConfigReader scans every namespace for data/<ns>/puffish_skills/config.json, so a category living at data/horsegenetics/puffish_skills/categories/horsemanship/ is read when the mod is installed and is inert when it is not. No registration, no ModList.isLoaded check, no presence probe — without the mod the tree simply does not exist. Skill Tree ships its own two categories exactly this way, which makes its jar the schema documentation.
  • The effect needs no branch either. The built-in puffish_skills:attribute reward applies a real vanilla AttributeModifier to any registered attribute, including one we register unconditionally. Register horsegenetics:horsemanship, read player.getAttributeValue(...) always, and with no skills mod present the value sits at its baseline forever. That is a genuine zero-branch soft dependency, and it is the shape to copy for any future optional integration.
  • Anything the built-in rewards cannot express — touching bond or genetics directly — goes through SkillsAPI.registerReward from a class in the NeoForge module that is only ever loaded behind ModList.get().isLoaded("puffish_skills"), with the mod as a compileOnly dependency. Standard idiom; keep it to the cases that earn it.
  • Reading a level is SkillsAPI.getCategory(id).map(c -> c.getSpentPoints(player)), or c.getExperience().map(e -> e.getLevel(player)). There are unlock and lock events, on Pufferfish's own pub/sub rather than the NeoForge bus.

Why not the other two. RPG Attribute System has an api package, but its isAvailable() is compiled as a literal return true and is useless as a presence check; it exposes no events, so any integration has to poll RasApi.getLevel(player) on mount; and its eight attributes are fixed with no room for a ninth. It is a reasonable secondary signal — overall player level gating how deep the tree can be bought into — and cannot host the tree. Skill Tree has no api package at all and hard-requires spell_engine and spell_power; it is content sitting on Pufferfish's, so any level worth reading from it is a Pufferfish query anyway. KubeJS is a real third seam for a server-specific tweak, and not a substitute for something we want to ship in the jar.

Most players have no skills mod, and the feature still exists

Bond alone carries it, at a lower ceiling. Nobody is locked out, and installing a skills mod raises your ceiling rather than switching a feature on. This is also what makes the thing buildable in the first place: the whole system can be written, tuned and tested against bond before any hook exists, and the Pufferfish tree is then additive on top of something already working.

Rider comfort — four small things, and one that touches the coat

Harvested from the quality-of-life cluster. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship on its own. Every one of them wants a config switch written with it, per the config sweep.

  • Remove the mining-speed debuff while mounted. Vanilla gives you a fifth of your mining speed on horseback. Taken from Horse Buff. Worth being honest that this is the one of the four that alters rather than adds, so vanilla stays intact has a claim on it and the switch matters more here than elsewhere.
  • A keybind to open your own inventory while riding. Horse Buff binds it to ALT+E. We already own H for the browser through client/HorseBrowserKeyBindings, so the idiom and the registration exist. This one adds a door rather than closing one.
  • Fade the horse out as you look down. Horse Buff fades the mount for sightlines, with an optional head-pitch offset of up to 45°. This is the one with a real unknown in it: we are the only mod here whose coat is generated per horse, and a half-transparent procedural coat is a rendering case nothing in the pipeline has ever been asked for. Prove the transparency before designing the feature around it.
  • Leads return to your inventory instead of dropping on the ground, falling back to a drop when the inventory is full. From Horse Tweaks. Pure annoyance removal and nothing in this mod's design objects.
  • Right-click with a saddle or armour equips it rather than opening the horse screen. Also Horse Tweaks. The one most likely to collide with something we ship: client/HorseScreenHooks, the i button and the right-click to open client option all live on that same interaction, so this needs designing against them rather than beside them.

Not taken: Horse Buff's step-height increase from 1.0 to 1.1. Considered and declined — a horse snagging on a stair edge is vanilla's behaviour and changing it moves a number every other mod's pathing assumes.

Asked for, and not built

Each was researched before being deferred, so what follows is the research rather than a restatement of the request — the point is that the next session does not pay for it twice. None of them is started.

Breeds that only spawn in certain weather

An opt-in line in the breed file, left out entirely unless it is something other than “any weather”, and never back-applied to the breeds that ship. The owner was explicit on both points.

  • The format contract is five or six files, not one. BreedSpecParser.KEYS (anything not listed is a hard error), BreedSpecWriter (which omits a field at its default, deliberately, so an absent line keeps meaning “not chosen”), the Breed record header and its builder, and BreedSpawnSettings.copy(), which enumerates every record component positionally — a new field missed there is silently dropped for every shipped breed. Then breed-format.html, and :common:bakeBreedFiles.
  • The enforcement point is one method. Breeds.wildCandidates(biomeId, dark) is the single chokepoint the wild path goes through; BreedSpawnHandler.onFinalizeSpawn currently passes exactly one runtime condition (isDarkOutside) and would pass weather too, with HerdManager.pickWildBreed and BreedSpawningConfig following.
  • Snow is not weather. The precedent already exists in GeneAbilityHandler: snowing is level.isRaining() && biome.coldEnoughToSnow(pos), because Minecraft has no snow state — it is rain in a cold biome. Any weather field must resolve the same way or it will simply never fire.
  • The validation the owner asked for is the interesting part. A breed that names a weather and biomes must have at least one biome where that weather can occur — it never rains in a desert, so a rainy desert breed spawns never and says nothing. That check belongs in the parser (so it warns for a drop-in file too) and is surfaced in the breed designer, which already validates through the real parser compiled to WebAssembly. Note the parser has no cross-field spawn validation today, so this would be the first.

Horses spawning in chunks already visited

The premise needs checking before the work does. Wild spawning is entirely vanilla's NaturalSpawner, fed by two biome modifiers (add_horse_herds, a herd in listed biomes, and add_horse_loners, a single horse anywhere overworld). Vanilla consumes those from both chunk generation and the ongoing per-tick pass around players, and BreedSpawnHandler accepts NATURAL, CHUNK_GENERATION and SPAWNER identically. Nothing in this mod keys off a chunk being new — no isNewChunk, no ChunkStatus check, no per-chunk attachment.

So horses should already spawn in explored land, and the real cause is likelier to be vanilla's animal rules: CREATURE spawns are weighted heavily toward worldgen, and a night-only breed cannot pass the light check on an existing chunk at all. Diagnose before building: if the answer is vanilla's pass rather than ours, the fix is a mod-owned periodic spawner around players, which is a much larger thing than it sounds and wants a config gate.

A gene for how long an aggravated horse gives chase

Alleles n, timed, distance, sight, never, infinite. n is whatever the behaviour is now, and every natural breed is n unless stated — which costs nothing, because BreedFounder already forces every magical gene to wild for any breed that does not name it. A breed is exactly its breed sheet.

  • The substrate exists. HorseAggroHandler already gives every horse an attack-damage attribute and a melee goal, and WildHorseForgetTargetGoal already clears a wild horse's target after a fixed interval without line of sight. That goal is very nearly sight and timed already; this gene generalises it.
  • It needs Java, not just JSON. A duration in ticks or a distance in blocks that runs in families means per-allele epigenetics, and a JSON gene's knobs are for painting, not effects. Subclass AbstractAbilityGene, override epiSchema(), read it in abilitiesWhenExpressed(EpiValues) — IntimidatingGene is the template, and it is a behaviour gene whose radius is inherited and drifts.
  • Watch the goal-priority trap. The comment in HorseAggroHandler records that vanilla's panic goal sits at priority one and the melee goal at three, both holding Flag.MOVE, so the first hit a fighting horse took handed movement to panic and the fight starved. A chase that must outlast being hurt lands in exactly that fight.

Magic Grab — a horse that drags what it bites

Alleles n, smaller, same size, larger, naming the largest thing the horse can pick up. Epigenetics carry drag speed and the damage the horse will absorb before dropping its prey short of wherever it was taking it. Simulates a horse grabbing someone by the teeth and hauling them off — uncommon in life, common enough in fiction to deserve a locus.

  • The dosage pattern already exists and should be copied rather than reinvented: AbstractMagicFactorGene sums a signed per-copy epigenetic value so that n/weak is weakest and doubled-strong is strongest, with six outcomes falling out of one comparison. MagicFighterGene is the two-value variant of the same shape.
  • There is no existing verb for carrying a mob. The effects vocabulary has temper, mob_aura, summon and teleport, none of which pick anything up, so this is a new AbilityType — and that contract is five files: the record on GeneAbility, the registration in AbilityType, the translator case, the creator's schema.js mirror (which check-parity.mjs compares against the real Java), and the Effects tab.
  • Mob groups must resolve through entity type tags, never a hardcoded vanilla list — a hardcoded list makes every modded creature invisible to the gene and fails silently.

A checklist idiom and item art, for the wiki

The in-game half ships as the progression page. What is left is the wiki side of the same request. The wiki has no checklist idiom — no checkbox markup and no CSS for one anywhere — so a wiki walkthrough either invents one in styles.css or reuses the tester-procedure block from for-testers.html, whose styles are page-local. And the wiki has no item icons: the only images it has are baked gene icons, and the in-game page draws items live on purpose so nothing goes stale when a texture changes. Putting item art on the wiki therefore means deciding to export it for the first time, and owning the staleness that the in-game page was built to avoid.

Storm fed — a gene that stores power and spends none

A locus that builds an internal reservoir while some condition holds, and does nothing whatever with it. Known as stormfed only because storms were the first allele found: the shipped set would be Storm, Rain, Snow, Lightning (a burst on being struck, and no lightning damage), Water and Lava. All alleles homozygous recessive. The rates of accrual and decay live in the epigenetics of each copy, so two horses with the same allele fill at different speeds.

The point is that it is half a gene on purpose. It is designed to be consumed: other genes read the reservoir and spend it, which is the mod's first deliberately polygenic pair. That makes the ordering awkward and worth naming now — a gene that only stores is untestable by looking at a horse, and a gene that only spends has nothing to spend until this exists. Build them together or build the spender first against a stubbed reservoir.

What it needs that does not exist: per-horse mutable state that is not a genotype and not an epigenetic constant — a number that changes every tick. The epigenome is the wrong home (it is inherited and drifts, not a running total) and so is the genotype. This is an attachment, and the first gene to want one.

Block preference — a horse that wants to be somewhere

A recessive locus pulling a horse toward a kind of block, with the allele's author choosing how hard — up to taking damage when it cannot get there. Ships with Water (stand in it, but never push fences or jump for it), Lava, Underwater (submerge, and drown unless the horse also has water breathing — intended, and for aquatic breeds only), and Tree (climb the nearest tree, which does almost nothing unless the horse can fly).

It is an AI goal, not an effect, which is what makes it different from everything in the effects vocabulary: those apply a state per tick, and this has to compete for the horse's attention against grazing, herding, fleeing and being ridden. That means a priority in the goal selector, so the behaviour hierarchy and its bake are part of the contract. Underwater is the one to think hardest about: a gene whose correct behaviour is drowning the horse needs to be unmistakably opt-in at the breed sheet.

Forced riding — the kelpie

A wild horse that lets you mount and then will not let you off, carrying you to deep water. Researched as a temporary ride-lock state rather than an attempt to cancel the dismount key: the horse stays an ordinary rideable entity, and while the state is active the server re-mounts the rider the moment they dismount and drives the horse toward its destination. That is the version that survives a version port, because it leans on normal passenger mechanics plus server enforcement instead of intercepting a client keybind.

Held on the horse as a small state machine — idle, a warning beat, then the forced ride — rather than as scattered booleans. The warning beat is the design question: without it this is a mount that kills you with no tell, which is a different and worse thing than a monster you can recognise. It also has to agree with the two seats a strong horse already carries — a forced ride with a pillion aboard is a case the ride-lock has to answer rather than discover.

Momentum sprint

For a Chollima-, Arion- or Sleipnir-style ground mount: speed that builds while you hold a line and drains when you brake, turn hard, collide, leave the ground or stop, with a short cooldown after an impact.

Why it is not just a Speed modifier, which is the whole of the research: a permanent modifier makes a horse that is simply faster, and this makes a loop the rider can feel and play badly — run straight, watch the top speed climb, lose it on a corner, build it again. It wants the same per-tick mutable state storm fed wants, and for the same reason, so whichever is built first should put that attachment somewhere the other can use.

A lasso, and catching as something you do

A thrown or held item that catches a wild horse and holds it while it is gentled, so that taming is an activity with a tool rather than a dice roll you repeat.

The length of it is the problem, not the idea. The mod this came from ran it as a multi-day break: lasso the horse, it struggles, roughly two days later it is yours. Philosophy §8 is “add texture, do not add chores”, and a two-day timer on a horse you have already caught is a chore with a progress bar. What survives the rule is the catching — a rope that stops a wild horse bolting, so approaching a band is a stalk rather than a chase. The mod already has everything downstream of that: taming by hand is built (hand-feeding), and wild bands already flee, guard and hold together.

It would be the first item in the mod that targets an entity, which is where the research has to start; everything else acts on a block or on a held stack. Vanilla's own lead is the closest model, and the mod currently only reads isLeashed() to suppress goals. A braided rope item existed once as a crafting intermediate and was removed for pricing a fence line at twelve horse hairs — if a rope comes back it must not come back as that.

Draught teams — more than one horse to a vehicle

Two or four horses harnessed to one wagon, connected by a whippletree, with the rider leading from the near horse.

The carts are built on one animal per vehicle and say so. AbstractDrawnEntity.applyDraught takes a single puller and applies a single speed modifier, and the carts page states the rule outright. So this is not a feature to add beside the cart code; it is a change to the shape of it. The weight model is the half that already fits — CartDraft computes a speed ceiling from capacity against load, and two horses is simply more capacity.

Trains are the thing this is not, and the two get confused. Coupling several vehicles behind one horse is built and shipped (hitching and trains). This is the other axis: several horses in front of one vehicle. A design that does both needs to say what happens when you combine them.

A vardo — a cart you can sleep in

A caravan that is a moving home: you sleep inside it, and it carries your respawn point with it.

Nothing in the mod touches sleep or respawn today — no startSleeping, no setRespawnPosition, no bed — so this is the whole of the work, and it is the interesting half rather than the modelling. The wagon it would be built on already exists, already seats riders, already takes chests and already has a dyeable furling roof, so the vehicle is not the question. Sleeping in a thing that moves is.

The obvious trap is a respawn point that drives away. A bed sets a fixed position; a vardo's is wherever the cart now is, or wherever it was when you slept, and those differ the moment somebody hitches it up. Decide that before any of it is built, because it decides whether the feature is a mobile base or a novelty bed.

Config: tune or disable basically everything

Asked for as a sweep, and the current surface is smaller than it looks. Roughly fifteen hand-written options across ServerConfig (health mode, body size, three riding numbers, gestation days, two debug switches) and ClientConfig (scroll bar, sex symbol, tutorial, right-click to open, two coat-rendering numbers), plus two generated blocks that are genuinely thorough — BreedSpawningConfig writes four options per shipped breed, and CartsConfig a block per CartKind.

What is not configurable, and each of these is a deliberate gap to reopen rather than an oversight: the genetics themselves — every gene is always registered, always occupies its genotype slot and is always inherited, whatever health.mode says — coat generation, every bond and care number, hunger rates, cowboy and stable and barn spawn rates, herd sizes, whistle radii, ticket behaviour, prices, jump sizes and styles, the armour protection ladder, and everything about the horse menu.

  • The genetics are the interesting refusal. A per-gene disable switch is the most-requested shape of this ask and the most dangerous one here, because a genotype code is positional — a gene that stops occupying its slot is a save format change and every existing horse reads wrong. If per-gene config is wanted it has to mean expression or founder frequency, never registration. The author-time version of this problem is already settled — an absence from genes/index.json is a killswitch, which is why that file is never regenerated from the folder (gap 162) — and that is the precedent to read before designing a runtime one.
  • Do it as one pass with a convention, not option by option. Fifteen scattered scalars is what a config grows into when each is added where it was needed; a sweep is the chance to fix the shape — one file per concern, generated blocks where the roster is data, and a rule about what may be a server option versus a client one.
  • Every new system on this page should ship its own switch. Cheapest when written with the feature and near-impossible to retrofit consistently — the tack bench is the model, where one switch disabling the bench disables all horse-armour crafting with no per-recipe gating anywhere.

Long range

What breaks at scale

The gene backlog above is not a linear addition to what ships. Two pieces of work fall out of that, and both are still unbuilt:

  • A compact binary encoding for the sync payloads. The genotype code string grows a segment per locus, and everything carrying it grows in step — the record, both sync payloads, and the epigenome code, which is already the fatter of the two. Worth doing for the payloads; keep the string form for saves and debugging.
  • Display. The pen sign caps out, and the info panel needs grouping (colour / pattern / performance / health) and probably scrolling. It has already gained two rows and a condition list.
  • A test that greps the wiki for the numbers the code can compute. api-reference.html hand-transcribes public signatures and each gene-*.html hand-transcribes that gene's constants; neither is generated, so both drift silently the moment a signature or a tuning number changes. The gene creator is the one place this is guarded — check-parity.mjs compares its schema mirror and its whole preview engine against the real Java, and has already caught a mis-ported nextLong() and four drifted schema defaults. The cheap version elsewhere is a :common:test over the handful of accessors (Genes.codeOrder().size(), GenotypeCatalog.size(), totalGenotypes()) that fails when a page disagrees. Prose about how a gene works survives a change; a number in prose almost never does.

Composer cost is not on this list: phase 1 is O(painting genes × texels), the non-painting genes cost nothing, and coats are cached per texture key, so it bites only on first bake. Neither is CoatTextureId's path length — that turned out not to be a limit, and what it does cost is recorded on texture resolution.

Deepen the thin science tabs

Every natural gene page has a science tab, and wiki/tools/check-gene-tabs.mjs runs with an empty exempt list, so a new natural gene page without one fails immediately. The work is no longer absence but depth: the tabs written in the big pass run to a shape — the locus and the variant with coordinates, the mechanism, the real inheritance table, breed-by-breed frequency where anyone has measured it, an established / inferred / unsupported split, and an explicit statement of where the mod departs from the literature — and the tabs that predate it are mostly a single heading and a paragraph.

Four are written — and the source is the bottleneck, not the writing

Extension, agouti, EDNRB and KIT were written from owner-supplied source files and are off the lists below. That is the whole method: each was blocked on having a source rather than on anybody getting to it, and none of the remaining ones can be done without the same. The three departures the four turned up are gaps 297–299.

Magical genes are excluded throughout: they have no real-world counterpart, so having no science to write is correct rather than a gap. The method — and the rule that none of this is ever written from memory — is on coding notes.

First — cited by the newer tabs and unable to answer

These are the worst, and not because of their length. Each is named as background by one or more of the newer pages, so a reader follows a link expecting the story and finds two sentences.

  • Silver — cited three times for multiple congenital ocular anomalies, and has no MCOA section at all. PMEL, the homozygote severity, and why a champagne-plus-silver horse gets its eye risk from the silver.
  • MITF — SW1, the common, centuries-old, minimally-expressed splash allele. Its PAX3 twin carries the full SW1–SW10 table, and the asymmetry between the two alleles is the whole point of having two loci. This one is close to writable from the PAX3 page alone.
  • Grey — STX17, the duplication, and the age-related melanoma risk that the MATP comparison table lists and grey's own page does not discuss.
  • Leopard complex — TRPM1 and congenital stationary night blindness in homozygotes, cited twice and absent here. Also the obvious place for Appaloosa, Knabstrupper and POA.
  • Roan — rabicano's tab contrasts the two at length and roan's own page does not reciprocate. The honest content is that roan's causal variant is also unsettled, which is worth a reader knowing.

Then — foundational loci carrying a paragraph

  • LCORL, for the major height locus. HMGA2 and dun both cite the ECA3 region against it.
  • ACAN, megaesophagus and CVM — the three health loci that were not in the intake batch, so they are the only disorder pages without the full treatment their neighbours have.
  • RYR2 and PDK4. PDK4 especially: CKM's tab ends by saying the same marker-in-a-cluster caution applies to PDK4 with less force, and PDK4's own page does not say what that caution is.
  • Brindle and manchado — both genuinely under-researched patterns, which is itself the content; rabicano's tab is the template for writing a pattern whose gene nobody has found.

Racing and competitions

Shows, races, judging, and the ribbons that come out of them belong in a separate add-on mod, attaching to a horse's pedigree rather than living in the base mod. (Owner, 2026-09-21, deciding what to do with the Simply Equine rosettes.)

  • The seam it would attach to already exists and is stable. A horse carries a pedigree, a horse record and resolved-not-rolled stats; a competition layer reads those and writes a result back. That is a clean boundary, and it is the reason this can be somebody else's jar rather than a fork of ours.
  • Why it is not base. A competition ladder is progression for the player, and breeding is this mod's progression. Building shows in would put a second, louder ladder beside the one the whole design is shaped around — the same objection that sits on the survey row for a horsemanship skill, and it should be answered the same way if that one is ever picked up.
  • What it takes with it. Simply Equine's twelve rosettes, Champion and Reserve Champion among them. They were the obvious award item, and an award has no utility without something to win it at — which the utility rule does not allow. So they go here rather than onto the item roster, and an add-on that adds the shows can add the ribbons in the same change.
