Block / the yard

Jumps

A showjumping rail. Place them side by side to widen a fence and on top of each other to raise it — there is no height setting, because the stack is the height. One per wood, and the first thing in the mod that makes a horse’s jump stat something you can watch rather than read.

What it is for

A jump is one rail across the block with an upright standard at each end of the run. Put two beside each other and they become one continuous rail with a post at either extremity, the way a real fence line looks; the standards in the middle disappear on their own, so you never have to think about it.

Recipe (shapeless): 2 vanilla fences of that wood + 1 horse hair → 4 jumps.

Four to a hair on purpose. A course is the one thing you build dozens of, and a hair is sheared one to three at a time, once per horse per day — so a twelve-fence course costs three hairs, which is an afternoon with one horse rather than a reason not to build.

Height is the stack, and it is a measuring stick

The rail tops out just under one block, which is exactly a horse’s step height. So a single jump on the ground is not an obstacle — a horse walks over it. That is a ground pole, and it is what you trot a young horse over. The fence starts at two.

From there every block you add asks for about one more block of clearance, which makes a stack a ladder you can read off:

StackWhat it isWho clears it
1Ground poleEverything — it is walked over, not jumped
2A small verticalMost horses
3A real fenceA decent jumper
4A big oneSomething you bred on purpose
5+PuissanceA horse carrying the magical jump locus

The point is not the number. It is that two horses that look identical on a stat screen are separated by a fence one of them stops at, which is the whole argument for the mod being about breeding rather than about rolling.

Is it dangerous?

No. A jump is a fence: a horse that refuses it stops, and one that hits it takes nothing. It has no effect on a horse’s health, its condition or its bond, and failing one costs nothing but the attempt.

Building a course

  • Facing is set when you place it, from the way you are looking, and the rail runs across that. A row placed while walking sideways comes out straight.
  • A jump facing north and one facing south are the same fence, so they join up. You do not have to approach a row from a consistent side to build it.
  • Jumps join to jumps and to nothing else — they will not connect to a fence, a double gate or a wall.
  • An axe is the right tool, and one jump drops one jump.

Every wood has one, including woods other mods added — those are generated at run time from that mod’s own plank texture, so they arrive looking right without anybody drawing anything. See compatibility.

What it is not, yet

This is one jump type: a plain vertical rail. Cross-country has a vocabulary well beyond it — logs, brush, ditches, oxers, fillers, water — and none of that is built. Nothing scores a round, times one, or notices that a horse knocked a rail, because a jump is currently scenery with a collision box. The roadmap is where that lives.

Where it lives

  • block/JumpBlock — the geometry, the connection rule, the VoxelShape.
  • block/Jumps — the per-wood registration, vanilla’s twelve then every modded wood. Deliberately the same shape as block/DoubleGates, down to the duplicate-id guard declared above the static block; that ordering is not stylistic, and the gates crashed mod construction with an NPE out of a class initialiser for getting it wrong.
  • tools/bake-jumps.mjs — every shipped asset, for vanilla’s woods, at author time.
  • compat/GeneratedJumps — the same files for other mods’ woods, at run time.

The geometry is written three times

This is the thing to be careful about. The same shape exists as boxes in bake-jumps.mjs, as a VoxelShape in JumpBlock, and again in GeneratedJumps for modded woods. Change one, change all three.

Each drift fails differently and none of them logs. Model against shape gives you a jump you can see in one place and collide with in another. Baker against generator gives you a modded jump that is a purple cube while every vanilla one is fine.

Left, right, and the suffix that was wrong

LEFT and RIGHT are fence-style connection flags: true means another jump continues the rail on that side, so the standard there is not drawn. Left is facing.getCounterClockWise(), the same definition DoubleGates.partnerDirection uses — keep the two families agreeing about which way is left.

Verified 2026-09-20, by check-block-models.mjs, on its first run. The blockstate composed its model suffix as (left?"_l":"") + (right?"_r":""), giving _l_r, while the models were written _lr. Only the both-connected case differs, so a lone jump and a row of two were perfect and the middle of every row of three was a purple cube.

Both generators now derive the suffix from one function rather than composing it, and the check is a repo tool: node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/check-block-models.mjs, which walks every blockstate, item definition and model parent and asserts the file is there. It covers the shipped assets only — the run-time generated pack is not on disk and is still guarded by nothing but the class docs.

The axe tag is shared, so it merges

mineable/axe holds the double gates as well, and a datapack has exactly one file per tag. Both bakers therefore merge into it rather than writing {values: ours}, and GeneratedGates.mergeBlockTag does the same for the run-time pack so it does not matter which generator runs first.

Getting this wrong is worth describing because of how quietly it fails: the losing family keeps working, mines slowly, and drops nothing without an axe. That reads as a balance decision, not a bug, and could sit there for months. After running either baker, check both families are still in the tag.

Unverified

JumpBlock.getShape is the only VoxelShape override in the NeoForge module, so its signature is corroborated by nothing else in the repo — the compiler is the whole check, and it passes. Nothing here has been seen in game. The height ladder on the Gameplay tab is derived from a horse’s 1.0 step height and the rail’s top at y=15/16; it has not been ridden. See verification.

Why stacking, and not a height property

A height state was the obvious design: right-click to cycle 1–4, like a repeater. It was rejected because it makes the jump assert a height, and the block has no business knowing one. Stacking makes height a consequence of geometry instead — the horse is answering a question about the world, not about a number somebody typed into a blockstate.

It also costs nothing. No property, no cycling interaction, no lang keys, no extra variants, and the ladder comes out evenly spaced for free because blocks are one block tall.

What a stack can actually measure

This matters more than a decoration has any right to. Nothing else in the mod makes jump visible: the debug yard’s stat pens measure it by printing Attributes.JUMP_STRENGTH, which is reading the answer rather than asking the question.

And there is a claim waiting to be checked. common/trait/HorseUnits.jumpMetres converts jump strength to metres through a cubic — it is what the info screen prints as “x.x m” — and its own class doc admits the fit is unverified against this mod’s horses, assuming scale 1.0 and normal gravity. A stack of jumps is the instrument that can finally test it: breed for a known strength, build the ladder, and see which rung the horse stops at. If the cubic is right, the rung and the metres agree.

That is a genuine experiment the mod could not previously run, and it is the reason this family was worth building before the prettier obstacle types.

Why the art is ours

Three existing Minecraft projects cover this ground — DragN’s XC Jumps, EquestrianMod’s obstacles, and the Showjumping Craft CIT pack. All three are All Rights Reserved for their assets, and the one that splits its licences (MIT code, reserved art) reserves precisely the half that would have been worth having. The author policy behind the first is explicit that models and textures may not go into a standalone mod; the exceptions are texture packs and dependent addons, which we are not.

Our own licence makes it worse rather than better: this mod is CC BY-NC 4.0 and its jars go to outside testers, so shipping someone’s reserved art would mean sublicensing it, which needs written permission and not a friendly nod. So the geometry here is drawn from the real-world object. A showjumping vertical is a rail between two standards; that is not anyone’s invention, and building it from the same vocabulary is clear.
