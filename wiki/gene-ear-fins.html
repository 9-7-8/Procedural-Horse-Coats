Magical gene / recessive / grows geometry, paints nothing

Ear fins

Two copies and the horse grows a fin at the outer base of each ear, fanned out from the head and swept back. A single blade, a fan of three or a frill of five rays, and how big it is, how wide it opens and how many rays show, is written on the allele. Their colour is a gene of its own, ear fin colour.

Crossing two of them

Two copies, either sex, foals too

Recessive: only Efn/Efn grows fins. A horse with one copy shows nothing and passes it on, so two plain carriers can have a finned foal. It is not sex-limited. Ear fins are the soft head part, so a foal wears them from birth, at its own small size, the way a unicorn foal wears its horn.

Where they sit

On the side of the head at the outer base of each ear, leaning out and sweeping back over the neck. They sit on the skull, beside the ear and not on it. The two sides always match. From the side or from above you see the whole fan; from dead ahead or dead behind you see its edge.

Finding one

Rare in the wild, the same as a unicorn: about one horse in four hundred has fins and about one in ten carries a hidden copy. Skeleton horses never grow them. A fin is cartilage, like an ear, and a skeleton has no ears to speak of.

What varies

  • Form: a blade (one broad slab), a fan (three slabs, longer toward the back) or a frill (five narrower slabs in an arc, each with a ray down its middle).
  • Size: two classes, small (about as long as the ear) and grand (up to as long as the head is deep). Small in the wild; a line bred for size reaches grand and stops.
  • Spread: how wide a fan or a frill opens.
  • Rays: how many a fan or a frill shows, the longest first. A wild fan is nearly always whole. A line can be bred down to one ray, or back up, and the next ray grows in gradually.

Everything stacks: a horse can wear ear fins beside dragon horns, antlers and a unicorn horn. At large sizes they can touch each other.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing at all, for good or ill: no stat, no better hearing, no hitbox. It is a look.

The ears do not animate, so the fins are on the head

The treatment left this to be read from the sources. In 26.1.2 left_ear and right_ear are PartPose.ZERO children of head, and AbstractEquineModel.setupAnim moves head_parts, the body, the legs and the tail and nothing else. So the ears never move on their own, and either bone would hold a fin still. The anchor walks to head, as every other head anchor does. Because a foal draws this part, FOAL_EAR is a real offset read from BabyHorseModel, not a copy of the adult’s.

The mesh: every ray rooted on the anchor

A ray is a flat box (PartNode.width, thin across the fan) turned about x by its place in the fan and then leaned out about z by LEAN. A box turns about x first, so all the rays lie in one plane: the one that holds the head’s front-to-back axis, tipped outward. A blade’s or a fan’s ray carries a narrower point; a frill’s carries a rod down its middle that stands a little proud and past its end.

Each ray is its own tree, and the kind scales per element (PartKind.scalesPerElement). The first build hung the rays off a shared knuckle. A photo shoot showed what that did to a two-tone fin: a slab one box down a chain sits halfway along PartModel.along, so it was drawn a muddy half-blend of its two colours. A root sits at 0 and is the base colour whole. Every root is at the anchor, so the per-element grow is the same scale a whole-part one would give.

A fan’s or a frill’s rays are groups, numbered longest first, so the count shows the first k and grows the next one in. A blade is one group that always shows. EarFinsGeneTest holds the numbering, the mirroring and the colour shape.

The epigenome, by cost

  • size: the class is the mesh’s size bucket (two), the remainder a uniform grow, so stretch and girth are equal.
  • form and spread: baked, EarFinGenerator.style(form, spread). A blade has nothing to open and is one style whatever its spread.
  • rays: shown out of the form’s most (three for a fan, five for a frill), the antler’s tine rule. A blade ignores it.

Two size classes and one blade style are a trim to fit the mesh cache’s box budget. There is no membrane between the rays; the treatment leaves it for later, and the sail’s translucent region is the way to add one.

No bone version

Owner, 2026-10-09: a fin is cartilage like an ear, so it has no bone version and the skeleton breeds never grow it. It is not in their horn count group, and both breed files pin horsegenetics.ear_fins to n/n, because leaving it merely ungrouped would let the wild frequency through. UndeadBreedsTest rolls both breeds for it.

Invented

No horse has fins. The look borrows from the fin-like ears drawn on sea horses, kelpies and merfolk in illustration, from the rayed fins of fish, and from the frills of lizards and the external gills of an axolotl. This is fantasy and makes no claim about any real gene. It is recessive and rare (the treatment’s call), so like the dragon horns it is a part you have to breed for.

What needs verifying here

The ear fins have been photographed standing still in all three forms, on a foal and on a skeleton-free herd; whether they read as fins from the front, how they sit in motion and beside dragon horns, and the foal’s placement are still open.

What a photo shoot showed, and the look

2026-10-09, -PphotoShoot, six still frames, re-rolled for the larger sizes and a full ray count. A fin stands at the outer base of each ear on both sides, leaning out and back, in all three forms. A Red/Blu fan is red slabs with blue points and a Vio/Yel frill yellow slabs with violet rods. A foal wears a small fan. From directly behind, each fin is a thin line.

For the owner to judge. From three-quarters front the fins read as small spiky tufts more than as fins, because the camera sees them partly edge-on, and the blade reads as a stick on the far side. Three things would change that: a bigger wild size, a smaller lean so the fan stands more upright, or turning the fan to face forward like an ear (then it shows from the front and from behind, and is edge-on from the side). None of it is a bug.

In motion, and on a foal

Each form at small and at grand size while the horse walks, grazes, tosses its head and rears. The fins should ride the head with no gap at their roots and should not pass through the ears or the mane. On a foal, look at where the fin roots: the foal offset is read from the model and has been seen once, from one angle.

Beside dragon horns and antlers

A horse with grand ear fins and long dragon horns, and one with antlers. The fin roots a unit forward of the dragon horn. Stacking is allowed and clipping at the extremes is accepted, so this is a look to judge, not a defect to fix.

Two ray counts in one frame

Two frilled horses side by side, one bred down to two rays and one with five. Each should show its own count; a horse wearing the other’s is the deferred-submit trap.
