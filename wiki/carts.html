Gameplay / non-genetic

Carts: wagons, plows and what a horse is worth in harness

Six horse-drawn vehicles — a covered wagon you can drive from the box seat, a plow, a seed drill, a reaper, a supply cart and an animal cart — in every wood your modpack has, vanilla's twelve and anyone else's. How fast a horse pulls one is the first thing that ever read pulling ability, and it reads the horse's speed at the same time, on purpose — and now what is in the cart as well. Derived from AstikorCarts / NiftyCarts / UsefulCarts, MIT — full lineage below. Not play-tested; checklist in verification.

1. The six vehicles

Every one of them is pulled by one animal, and they divide cleanly into three that carry things — wagon, supply cart, animal cart — and three that do a job to the ground they are dragged over: plow, reaper, seed drill. Which group a vehicle is in decides almost everything about it, including whether filling it slows your horse down.

Load is the only thing that separates them as far as your horse is concerned: it is the fraction of an ordinary horse the vehicle uses up, so 0.55 on an empty wagon means a plain horse is spending rather more than a third of itself just moving the thing. The item tooltip tells you both numbers — hold Shift on any cart and it says what fraction of its speed an ordinary horse keeps, empty and full.

2. Building one

Craft the cart wheel first, because every vehicle spends two of them and the wagon spends four. It is eight sticks around one plank, of any wood; the wheel is not wood-specific, only the cart is.

Every other recipe takes planks (and for the wagon, stripped logs) of one wood, and that is the wood the cart comes out in. Mixing woods in the grid crafts nothing.

The wagon is the odd one out twice over: it is the only vehicle that spends four wheels, and the only one that spends a stripped log. That second fact is why some modded woods have five carts and not six — a mod that never shipped a stripped log cannot build a wagon, so it does not get one. See §15.

Place a cart by right-clicking the ground with it. It needs the room: if the cart would overlap a block or another entity, nothing happens and you keep the item.

3. Hitching, unhitching, and trains

Press R — "Attach/Detach Cart", rebindable under Horse Carts in the controls. That is the whole interface. It hitches the nearest cart within about two blocks of you or of whatever you are riding, and pressing it again unhitches whatever you are pulling.

  • You do not need a horse. Standing on foot and pressing R hitches the cart to you, and you can drag any of the six around yourself. It is slow and none of the genetics apply, but it is how you reposition a cart without fetching an animal.
  • Anything saddleable can pull — horses, donkeys, mules, camels. Not pigs or striders, which are steered by a held item rather than reins. An animal that is not one of this mod's horses pulls at the baseline pulling score; a donkey is not feeble for want of a genome.
  • A cart can pull a cart. Hitch a second vehicle to the back of the first and you have a train, and you can keep going. The horse pays for all of it — the loads of every vehicle in the chain add up on the animal at the front.

It comes unhitched on its own when the cart is dragged too far from the puller, when a block comes between the two — drive between two trees and the traces part — and when the puller dies or itself becomes a passenger. There is an attach and a detach sound, so you can hear which happened without looking.

A hitched cart follows at a fixed distance: about 2.5 blocks for the wagon and 1.3 for the implements, which is the room they need to swing behind a turning horse. Carts climb a one-block step without help.

4. Weight: what a full cart costs

The rule

A vehicle's load is what it weighs empty, plus what is in it. Filling a supply cart to the brim adds half again to what it asks of the horse; filling a wagon adds sixty per cent. The three field implements never change — a plow's load is the blade in the soil, not the tools racked on it.

The cost is linear in how full the thing is, so there is no threshold to learn and no cliff to fall off: half a cart is half the penalty, and every stack you add costs the same as the last. What counts as "full" is the one thing that differs by vehicle:

What "full" means
VehicleMeasured byNotes
Supply cart its 54 slots By goods, not by slots touched: a slot holding one arrow is a sixty-fourth of a slot, not a full one. Nine loose torches do not make a horse labour.
Wagon chests fitted, then what is in them Half the wagon's extra weight is the chests themselves, empty or not — three bolted-on chests and nothing in them is already half the full penalty. Filling them is the other half.
Animal cart its two seats One cow is half a load, two is a full one. No attempt to weigh a pig against a sheep — only animals of about that size fit in the first place.
Plow, reaper, seed drill nothing Deliberately flat. A plow does not become half again as hard to drag because you gave it a second hoe, and a player choosing between fitting the tool and pulling the plow is choosing between a working plow and a useless one.

The load is live. Load a cart while it stands hitched and the horse gets slower within a second, without unhitching; unload it and the speed comes back. The same is true of a cow climbing into the animal cart, or a chest going onto a wagon mid-journey.

This is the reason to breed a strong horse rather than just a fast one for trade runs specifically. An ordinary horse can trot along with an empty wagon; the same horse with three chests packed is doing close to twice the work, and the difference between an ordinary animal and a draft one stops being cosmetic exactly there.

5. The wagon

The biggest, the heaviest, and the only one with a roof. Empty, it seats four and holds nothing.

Chests, and what they cost

Right-click the wagon with a chest to bolt one on. Its storage grows in three stages, and so does the number of seats it loses:

Chests against seats
ChestsStorageSeats leftTo fit the next one
none—4any time
19×44no more than 2 aboard
29×82nobody aboard
312×9none—

So a full freight wagon is not a passenger wagon, and you cannot sneak the third chest on with the family sitting in the back. The chests come back as items if the wagon is destroyed, along with everything in it.

The roof

Right-click high on the wagon — aim at the hoops, not the bed — holding at least five wool carpets of one colour. That is the roof, in that colour. Doing it again with a different colour swaps it and hands the old five carpets back. Right-click the roof with an empty hand to furl and unfurl it: three positions, open to shut.

Driving from the box seat

The first person to board drives. The box seat steers the horse in front through a hidden rider, which is why it works at all — and that hidden rider occupies a seat on the horse's back, so on a horse strong enough to carry two you can have a driver on the wagon and somebody riding the horse. That is what makes a carriage read as a carriage rather than a trailer.

Newly built The box seat did nothing at all until this was wired up — the wagon was the one vehicle that never spawned the hidden rider, while its tooltip claimed otherwise. It is code that has never been played. First thing to check in verification.

Passengers you did not invite

Small animals climb into a wagon by themselves if there is a seat, the same way they climb into the animal cart, and tame ones sit down when they do. Shift-right-click the wagon to throw every non-player passenger out. A wagon will also fly a banner.

6. The plow

Three slots, three tools, three lanes: the tools sit at the left, centre and right of the blade and each works its own furrow about 1.7 blocks ahead of the cart. Fit one tool and you plow one lane; fit three and you plow three abreast.

What each tool does to the ground
ToolEffectClears out of the way first
Hoetills grass and dirt into farmland grass, ferns, dead bushes, flowers, saplings
Shovelmakes dirt paths the same, plus snow
Axestrips logs it is dragged past vines, glow lichen

That clearing step is what stops a plow jamming on a dandelion: the flower is broken and dropped, then the ground underneath is tilled. Anything else with a tool component can be racked too, and does whatever that tool does when used on a block — but only those three have blocks they will clear first.

Right-click the plow to toggle it on and off, so you can drive one home across a field you have already tilled. It works only while the cart is actually moving, and only while a player is driving — either pulling it on foot or riding the animal that is. A plow towed behind a wandering horse does nothing.

The tools wear out. They take durability damage as they work and break with the usual sound, leaving the lane empty until you rack another. Fitting three tools does not make the plow heavier.

7. The reaper

A cutting bar that clears a swath about a block and a half wide beside the cart as it is drawn along, from roughly 1.1 to 2.3 blocks out to one side. It folds up when it is not working, and unfolds with a trapdoor clunk when it is.

It cuts what a scythe would: crops, grass and dry grass, ferns, bushes, dead bushes, flowers and saplings, and anything else tagged as harvestable. Everything drops on the ground where it fell — the reaper has no inventory of its own, so bring a supply cart behind it if you do not fancy walking the field twice.

It does not check ripeness The reaper cuts a crop block because it is a crop, not because it is ready. Drive one over a field you planted this morning and you will harvest seedlings. Recorded in known gaps; until it is fixed, the seed drill and the reaper want to be run in that order and not the other way round.

Somebody has to be sitting on it. Right-click to climb on; shift-right-click and it tells you as much in the status bar. From that seat you steer the horse, the same way the wagon's box seat does, and the game counts the distance separately as "steering".

8. The seed drill

Nine slots of seed, planted three lanes wide — one to each side of the cart and one down the middle — onto ground that is already tilled. It plants roughly one seed per step, drawn at random from whichever slots have something plantable in them, so loading it with four kinds of seed gives you a mixed field rather than stripes.

It plants wheat, beetroot, melon and pumpkin seeds, potatoes, carrots, torchflower seeds, pitcher pods and small flowers — and any modded crop, without that mod knowing anything about this one: if the item places a crop block, the drill will sow it.

It needs no driver. Unlike the plow and the reaper, a seed drill plants whenever it is being pulled and moving, whoever or whatever is pulling. Lead it behind a plow on the same pass and you till and sow in one trip.

9. The supply cart

Fifty-four stacks — a double chest on two wheels — and the prettiest thing in the cart system: it shows you what is in it. The four commonest kinds of thing in the cart are drawn sitting in the bed, blocks stacked before loose items, with real models for tools, armour, flowers, paintings and a spare wheel. You can tell a loaded cart from an empty one at fifty blocks, and roughly what it is loaded with.

Right-click to sit on it; shift-right-click to open it. If somebody is already sitting on it, right-click opens it instead. Every piece of the cargo rendering can be switched off separately in the client config if it costs you frames, and each falls back to a plain floating item rather than vanishing.

It is the vehicle where weight bites hardest in practice, because it is the one you actually fill to the top. A full supply cart asks half again what an empty one does, which puts it level with a plow. It will also fly a banner.

10. The animal cart

Two seats, for players or for livestock, and animals load themselves: walk a cow into the back of one, or park it where they mill about, and in they climb. Tame animals sit down once aboard. Only creatures small enough to fit will board — no cows-in-a-teacup — and water animals never do.

Shift-right-click to empty it: every non-player passenger gets out. Right-click to take a seat yourself, from which you steer the horse, as on the reaper.

Its passengers are its cargo, so two animals make it a full cart. It is the lightest vehicle in the game empty, and even with both seats filled it asks less of a horse than an empty reaper — though rather more than an empty seed drill, which is the thing to remember before you lead a pair of cows up a hill.

11. Banners, damage, and what drops

Supply carts, animal carts and wagons will fly a banner — right-click one onto the back. Any banner, patterns and all. Putting a second banner on swaps them and hands the first one back.

Carts take damage like a boat: hits accumulate and heal off over a few seconds, and enough of them in quick succession breaks the cart. A creative player breaks one in a single hit. Cactus does nothing to them, and you cannot break the cart you are sitting in. When one does break it drops itself, its banner, its contents, and — for a wagon — its chests and its carpets, so nothing is ever lost to a mis-swing except the walk back.

One more thing worth knowing before you park: farm animals keep away from the working vehicles. Cows, sheep and the rest path around a plow, seed drill, reaper or supply cart standing within about three blocks. They do not avoid the wagon or the animal cart — which is exactly why those two are the ones they will climb into.

12. Why your horse matters

Pulling ability sets a ceiling; speed decides how close you get to it. That is the whole rule, and both halves are real.

A horse with a low pulling score is capped on a heavy cart. You can breed it faster and faster and it will not go faster, because it cannot get the wagon moving any harder than its shoulders allow. A horse with a huge pulling score and no speed never reaches the ceiling its shoulders could carry — it is simply a slow horse.

So the fastest carthorse is neither specialist:

Pulling an empty wagon
HorseSpeedPullKeepsActual haul speed
fast and weak0.30343%0.129
slow and strong0.13981%0.105
balanced0.22767%0.147

The balanced horse beats both. This is deliberate and it is the reason the model is shaped the way it is — a draft breed that dumped speed entirely would be the obvious and boring answer, so the model does not allow it. The numbers in that table are a unit test, not a claim. Loading the wagon does not change the shape of any of this; it moves every horse down the same curve, and it moves the weak ones furthest.

13. Reading it on the horse

Open a horse's information screen (the i button on its inventory). On the Overview tab:

  • Pull sits with Health, Speed, Jump and Size, because it is the fifth body stat and not a cart footnote. It shows the 1–10 score and a word for it — feeble, light, ordinary, strong, powerful, a true draft horse.
  • A Draught block below says what the score buys: how much of its speed this horse would keep hauling a wagon empty and loaded to the roof, whether it carries a second rider, and how far it has hauled in its life.

The two wagon figures are the useful pair. A horse that keeps most of its speed empty and collapses when loaded is a carriage horse, not a carthorse, and the screen is where you find that out before the trade run rather than during it.

That last figure belongs to the horse, not to you. It survives being sold, given away or inherited, and it is how you tell a working animal from a fresh one at a glance. Your own side of it is on the vanilla statistics screen: distance pulling each of the six vehicles, distance riding in one, and distance steering a wagon, reaper or animal cart from its seat.

14. Two riders

A horse whose pulling score is 6 or better carries two riders. Six is one point above ordinary: a modestly bred horse gets there, a wild-caught one usually will not, and you do not need a draft breed for it.

Carrying and hauling are the same claim about an animal, which is why there is no separate carrying gene to breed for.

This also matters for the wagon, for the reason in §5: the box seat spends one of those two places on the horse's back.

15. Every wood, including modded ones

Carts exist in vanilla's twelve woods and in every wood any other mod you have installed adds, discovered the same way the double fence gates discover theirs. A modded cart crafts from that mod's own planks and is textured from that mod's own blocks — no cart texture is shipped at all, they are assembled at load time out of the wood you built it from.

Two rough edges. A modded wood with no stripped log gets five carts and no wagon, because the wagon recipe spends one — better than a wagon that cannot be crafted, which is what used to happen. And a modded wood's carts wear the oak icon in the inventory, because there are painted icons in the jar for vanilla's woods and nothing to paint a thirteenth from; the cart itself is the right wood the moment you place it. See known gaps.

16. Horse-powered machinery

If you have a mod that makes a horse turn a mill or a treadmill, a good horse can drive it harder than a bad one. See compatibility for what is wired up and what still needs the other mod's cooperation.

17. What the game asks of you

The carts have a chapter in Getting Started — farming and haulage. Placing a cart ticks one task, hitching one ticks another, and each of the six vehicles has its own task that completes the first time you drive it, which means the cart has actually moved with you at the reins. Upstream's eleven advancements were not ported; this is what replaced them.

18. Where the code is

The split follows the mod's usual rule — as much as possible in common/, and the NeoForge module translates.

  • common/cart/CartDraft.java — the model. Pure: two doubles and a load in, a retention fraction out, plus loaded, which turns an empty load and a fill fraction into the load a vehicle actually asks for. No Minecraft, no RNG, no entity. This is the file to read.
  • common/cart/CartKind.java — the six vehicles, their empty loads and their cargoShare, and MACHINE_LOAD for a treadmill.
  • neoforge/server/HorseDraft.java — the bridge. Finds a live animal's pull and speed, keeps the haulage tally, and is the public surface other mods integrate against. Its retention(entity, kind) is the empty vehicle, since a CartKind has nothing to ask how full it is.
  • neoforge/carts/ — the ported cart system itself. HorseCarts registers it.
  • neoforge/carts/CartWood.java — what replaced vanilla's WoodType as the key.
  • neoforge/compat/GeneratedCarts.java and tools/bake-carts.mjs — models, item definitions, recipes and lang, for modded woods and vanilla's respectively.

19. The one hook

Upstream applied a flat pull_speed from the config in AbstractDrawnEntity.setPulling — one number, identical for every animal alive. That is now applyDraught, which reads the horse's genotype instead. The config option is gone rather than defaulted: an override would make the genetics decorative.

The modifier is transient, multiplicative (ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL), and computed from the attribute's base value — not its current value, which already has the modifier in it and would compound on every re-attach.

An animal that is not one of this mod's horses gets HorseTraits.BASE_PULL. A donkey is not feeble for want of a genome.

20. Why the load is polled and not pushed

applyDraught used to run exactly once per hitch, because the load was the vehicle's own weight and could not change. Cargo weight broke that: the number now moves while the horse stands still, from three unrelated places — an inventory changing, a chest going onto a wagon, an animal climbing into a cart.

AbstractDrawnEntity.refreshDraught polls instead of hooking all three. A missed hook would be a cart that is silently the wrong weight, which is precisely the class of bug a play session cannot see; one poll cannot be forgotten by the next vehicle somebody adds. It is cheap because it only runs on a hitched cart, only once a second, and only touches the attribute when the number has actually moved.

currentLoad() also walks the drawn chain, so a train of carts charges the horse for every vehicle in it rather than for the first. Depth-capped, because a looped hitch would otherwise recurse until the server fell over.

What counts as "full" is per-vehicle and lives in the entity, not in common/: fillLevel() is goods-per-slot for the inventory carts, seats for the animal cart, chests-then-goods for the wagon, and flat zero for everything else. None of those questions can be asked without a running game, which is exactly the line the module split draws.

21. Why the carts use RegisterEvent

Everything else in this mod registers through DeferredRegister, and DoubleGates and ModdedArmour both go to real trouble to create their per-material entries during class load, because a DeferredRegister will not take an entry once it has attached to the bus.

The carts have the same problem and skip the trouble: RegisterEvent fires after mod construction, so ModdedMaterials.scan() has already run and CartWood.all() is simply available. It is also the smaller diff against the MIT source we now maintain. The cost is that the carts do not appear in ModItems / ModEntities; the class javadoc on HorseCarts says so.

22. The contract that lives twice

tools/bake-carts.mjs covers vanilla's twelve at author time; compat/GeneratedCarts.java covers modded woods at run time. Exactly the arrangement the double gates use, and carrying exactly the same warning: change one, change the other. A drift shows as a modded cart that cannot be crafted while every vanilla one can, and logs nothing.

Re-run the bake after touching any cart recipe, model or lang key: 

node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/bake-carts.mjs
node neoforge-26.1.2/tools/check-recipes.mjs

23. No cart texture is shipped

Upstream's cleverest idea, and the reason a cart can exist in a wood nobody drew art for. mixin/CartModelManagerMixin stitches every cart's entity texture at resource-reload time out of that wood's _planks, _log and stripped__log sprites as they sit on the block atlas. A resource pack that retextures oak planks retextures oak carts for free, and no Mojang art is redistributed.

For a modded wood only the plank sprite is known — ModdedMaterials read it out of that mod's files. The log sprites are guessed from vanilla's naming and then checked against the atlas; a mod that names its logs unusually gets planks in those places instead of a purple cart. Unverified: the "is this sprite real" check assumes the atlas answers an unknown id with the missing-texture sprite rather than throwing. Vanilla's long-standing behaviour, not confirmed on 26.1.2 with a mod installed.

24. Mixins, and the horse ones in particular

Six cart mixins joined the mod's own, in horsegenetics.mixins.json — which now has a "client" block for the first time, for the four that touch client-only classes.

  • CartHorseMixin (AbstractHorse) — overrides travel and maxUpStep and injects at getControllingPassenger, all for the box seat. Its travel override calls super.travel on both branches, so HorseFlightMixin's inject on LivingEntity.travel still fires and flight is unaffected. Unverified — the interaction is argued, not observed.
  • HorseTwoRidersMixin (AbstractHorse) — ours, not upstream's. canAddPassenger and getPassengerAttachmentPoint. The pillion offset is a number picked to look right, not measured.

The steering itself is a PostilionEntity: a tiny invisible rider the cart puts on the horse, which the driver's seat then moves by packet. Three vehicles spawn one — the animal cart, the reaper and, as of this change, the wagon, which had the seat and the tooltip but never the rider.

25. The test

common/src/test/java/.../cart/CartDraftTest.java. Three of the cases are the design:

  • a balanced horse out-hauls both specialists;
  • pull sets a ceiling raw speed cannot pass, checked at speeds up to a million;
  • neither stat is a dump stat — raising either one, on every vehicle, always hauls faster.

The cargo rule brought three more: that an empty vehicle is exactly its bare load and a field implement never gains any, that the cargo term stays linear (half full is half the penalty, which is the only property that makes it predictable without reading the source), and that a loaded cart both slows a horse and keeps its ceiling — loading a cart must lower the asymptote, not escape it. 

./gradlew :common:test --tests '*CartDraftTest'

It earned its keep on the first run. The model originally had a flat floor on retention — "never below a quarter speed", so a hopeless horse still visibly moved — and the ceiling test failed, because past the speed where that floor engages haul becomes speed × 0.25 and climbs without limit. A fast enough weak horse out-pulled its own capacity, which is the exact failure the model exists to prevent. The floor was replaced by clamping the score at HorseTraits.MIN_PULL before the curve.

27. What was not ported

Owner's call, and recorded here so nobody re-adds it by accident:

  • The hand cart. Player-pulled on foot; the one vehicle a horse never touches. Note that every other cart can still be pulled by a player on foot — that is upstream's, and it is kept.
  • All 11 advancements and their 7 criterion triggers, replaced by the Getting Started tasks in §17.
  • The slow-speed toggle — keybind, payload, config value, and the ClientPacketListener mixin that existed only to add a line to the mount overlay.
  • Per-cart pull_speed, for the reason in §19.
What is still planned here

Two or four horses to one wagon on a whippletree — a change to the shape of the cart code rather than an addition beside it, since applyDraught takes a single puller — and a vardo you sleep in, whose respawn point drives away.

Everything still planned for the carts, in one place. The main roadmap was split up; this tab keeps the vehicle work together. The biggest planned change is a shared cart-and-jump bench, followed by draught teams and a vardo.

Cart and jump bench — piece-by-piece materials

Build each cart or jump from its own materials: a wood per part, then paint or dye layered on top. This matches the jump model already in place, where a single item carries a material set, and it removes the baked per-wood cart recipes that currently have to stay in sync with compat/GeneratedCarts.

The current cart model is locked to a registry id (oak_wagon, biomesoplenty_maple_plow, one item per wood and kind), so a piece-by-piece design has to move those values onto stack components and entity data rather than a permanent item id. The full plan remains on the cart and jump bench.

Draught teams — more than one horse to a vehicle

Two or four horses harnessed to one wagon, connected by a whippletree, with the rider leading from the near horse.

The carts are built on one animal per vehicle and say so. AbstractDrawnEntity.applyDraught takes a single puller and applies a single speed modifier, and the carts page states the rule outright. So this is not a feature to add beside the cart code; it is a change to the shape of it. The weight model is the half that already fits — CartDraft computes a speed ceiling from capacity against load, and two horses is simply more capacity.

Trains are the thing this is not, and the two get confused. Coupling several vehicles behind one horse is built and shipped (hitching and trains). This is the other axis: several horses in front of one vehicle. A design that does both needs to say what happens when you combine them.

A vardo — a cart you can sleep in

A caravan that is a moving home: you sleep inside it, and it carries your respawn point with it.

Nothing in the mod touches sleep or respawn today — no startSleeping, no setRespawnPosition, no bed — so this is the whole of the work, and it is the interesting half rather than the modelling. The wagon it would be built on already exists, already seats riders, already takes chests and already has a dyeable furling roof, so the vehicle is not the question. Sleeping in a thing that moves is.

The obvious trap is a respawn point that drives away. A bed sets a fixed position; a vardo's is wherever the cart now is, or wherever it was when you slept, and those differ the moment somebody hitches it up. Decide that before any of it is built, because it decides whether the feature is a mobile base or a novelty bed.

The largest cart change planned is on another page. Making the piece the unit rather than the vehicle — wheels of one wood, bed of another, and dye or paint on top of the choice of wood — is a bench, and it would retire the per-wood recipes this page's contract that lives twice exists to keep in step. It is written up at the cart and jump bench.

What needs verifying here

Six vehicles, 68 ported classes and seven new mixins have never been run in a game, and the port was never compiled against the game jars upstream either, so start with runServer reaching “Done (” — then whether the draught modifier fires at all, and whether it leaks back on unhitch.

Horse-drawn carts (2026-09-18, NOT played, nothing here has been seen)

Six vehicles, 68 ported classes, seven new mixins and a new draught model, and not one of it has been run in a game. The upstream port it came from had never been compiled against the game jars either, so the whole thing is build-green-only. Carts is the page. Start at the top — if the first two fail nothing below them is testable.

  • The game starts. Seven mixins were added to horsegenetics.mixins.json, and it grew a "client" block for the first time. A mixin apply failure or a client class on the server is a hard crash at mod load, so runServer to Done ( before anything else.
  • 85 items in a Horse Carts creative tab. Twelve woods × six vehicles, plus the cart wheel. Any purple icon is a missing model.
  • A placed cart is the right wood. No cart texture ships — each is stitched from that wood’s block sprites at resource-reload time. A purple-black cart means the assembler did not run; a cart that is the wrong wood means the sprite table is wrong. Check a nether one (crimson is a stem) and bamboo (a block), which are the irregular rows.
  • Hitch and unhitch. Place a supply cart, ride a saddled horse near it, press R: attach sound, and the horse pulls it. R again to detach.
  • A strong horse is visibly faster than a weak one on the same cart. This is the whole feature. Two horses with clearly different pull scores, the same wagon, the same stretch of ground. If they look the same, applyDraught is not firing — check the horse’s movement-speed modifiers.
  • A fast weak horse and a slow strong one are close, and a balanced one beats both. The design claim, and the one worth measuring rather than eyeballing. The unit test pins the arithmetic; this checks the arithmetic was wired to the right numbers.
  • Detaching gives the speed back. The modifier is transient and removed on unhitch. A horse that stays slow after unhitching is a leak, and it compounds every re-attach.
  • Two riders on a pull-6 horse, and only one below it. Both the threshold and the seating — the pillion offset is a number picked to look right on vanilla’s model, never measured, so the second rider may well be inside the first.
  • The wagon’s box seat drives the horse. Wired on 2026-09-19 and never run — until then the wagon was the one vehicle that never spawned a postilion, so the seat did nothing at all while the tooltip said it drove. The animal cart and the reaper have always had the same three lines, so if those two steer and the wagon does not, the difference is the wagon’s four seats: it is the first passenger who drives, and a wagon boarded by an animal first may hand the reins to nobody. This is also the path most likely to fight this mod’s own six AbstractHorse mixins. While you are there: does a horse still fly, swim and cross lava? CartHorseMixin overrides travel, and the argument that it does not break HorseFlightMixin is an argument, not an observation.
  • The plow tills, the seed drill plants, the reaper harvests. Tools go inside the plow; the reaper needs a player sitting on it. Craft the reaper first and confirm it is there at all — its recipe had a two-wide row in a three-wide pattern, so the game threw out all twelve woods' files at datapack load and it has been uncraftable since it shipped. Fixed in v0.5.014 and the twelve files now parse on a server boot, but no reaper has been made in a grid.
  • Wagon storage grows with chests, and five carpets make a roof. 9×4 → 9×8 → 12×9. Contents must survive a relog.
  • A loaded cart is slower than an empty one, and it changes while you stand there. New on 2026-09-19 and the whole point of the cargo rule. Hitch an empty supply cart to a horse, note the pace, then fill it from a chest without unhitching: within a second it should be visibly harder work, and emptying it should give the pace back. The same for a wagon as chests go on, and for the animal cart as a second cow climbs in. If nothing changes, refreshDraught is not being reached — it runs from pulledTick, so a cart that is standing still may simply never be asked. A plow must not change when you rack three tools in it.
  • A train of carts costs what its parts cost. Hitch a cart behind a cart behind a horse and load the back one. The horse should slow for cargo it is not touching; if it does not, currentLoad is not walking the drawn chain and a trailer is a free ride.
  • The info screen’s two wagon lines differ. Overview → Draught now shows what the horse keeps empty and loaded to the roof. Two identical percentages mean the screen is reading the bare load twice.
  • The cart tooltip shows two numbers on a carrying vehicle and one on an implement. Shift on a supply cart, a wagon and an animal cart: empty and full. Shift on a plow, reaper or seed drill: one line only, because their load does not move.
  • The supply cart shows its cargo, and a banner flies from a supply cart, animal cart or wagon.
  • “Hauled” climbs on the info screen and is still there after a relog. It is a synced attachment, so a figure that reads zero on the client while the horse has clearly worked is a sync fault rather than a counting one.
  • Recipes appear in the recipe book, and the cart wheel is cart_wheel, not wheel.
  • A dedicated server plus a client. Carts sync, and no client-only class on the server.

Modded woods are a separate session and need another mod installed — see gap 277, which is the one that could crash rather than merely look wrong, and gap 276 for the oak inventory icon that is expected.

Open check 285

Open, found 2026-09-19: the supply cart's “open from the seat” packet is registered and never sent. OpenSupplyCartPayload has a server handler wired up in HorseCarts.onRegisterPayloads and no sender anywhere in the client — upstream's key binding for it was not ported, so a player sitting on a supply cart cannot open it without dismounting. Harmless as it stands (a dead payload type costs a registry entry), and the choice is between wiring the inventory key to it or deleting the class. Whichever, do not leave it as the third thing that half exists. Carts §9.

Open check 286

Open, found 2026-09-19: the reaper does not check whether a crop is ripe. ReaperCartEntity.harvest tests the block against horsegenetics:reaper_harvestable, which includes #minecraft:crops — a tag on the block, with nothing in it about age. So a reaper drawn over a field planted this morning destroys the seedlings and drops one seed each, and the item tooltip's “harvests mature crops” is wrong. The fix is a CropBlock.isMaxAge test before the break, with the non-crop entries of the tag (grass, flowers, saplings) exempt from it since they have no age to be at. It was left alone in the sitting that found it because the tag is shared with the plow's clearing lists and the change wants its own look. Carts §7.
