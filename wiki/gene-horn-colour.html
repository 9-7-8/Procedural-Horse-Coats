Horn colour

What colour a unicorn's horn is — white, any colour of the rainbow, pink, black, grey, or chaos, a colour of its own on every copy. Nothing is dominant, so a horse with two different copies has a two-tone horn: one colour, with a tip of the other. On a horse without a horn this gene does nothing at all.

Two colours make a two-tone horn

A horse with two copies of one colour has a horn of that colour. A horse with two different ones has a horn of one colour whose tip is the other, fading between them about halfway up. Which colour is the base is decided by the pair, not by which parent gave which: it is the one earlier in the list above. So a white horse carrying red has a white horn with a red tip, and a red/blue horse a red horn tipped blue — every time.

Because nothing hides anything, you can read a unicorn's colour genes straight off its horn. Most wild unicorns are white, and a good share have a coloured tip.

No two reds are the same red

Every copy carries a little variation of its own, written on the allele and inherited with it: every red horn is clearly red, but the exact shade differs from horse to horse and drifts slowly down a family. White, black and grey vary only a touch. Chaos is the extreme of this — its whole colour is on the copy, so no two chaos horns match.

On a horse without a horn

Nothing. A hornless horse can carry any colours at all and show no sign of them, and passes them on to its foals like any other gene — so the colour a unicorn comes out can depend on ancestors that never had a horn.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all, for good or ill. It is only the colour of the horn.

How the colour reaches the horn

The unicorn locus grows a white, dark horn (UnicornHornGene.hornFor). GrownParts.of — still the one door the client uses — then asks HornColourGene.tintsFor(genotype, epigenome) for a base and a tip colour and AttachedPart.dressed puts them on. The renderer never learns which loci were involved.

AttachedPart carries baseTint and tipTint instead of one tint, and tintAt(along) blends between them: each end keeps its own colour for its first quarter, with a smoothstep in between, so a two-tone horn reads as a coloured horn with a coloured tip rather than a gradient. A one-colour horn is one submit; a two-tone one is one submit per segment — see the unicorn horn's coding tab on PartModel.Slice.

There is no real locus here

Unicorns are not real, so neither is this. What is worth saying is why the colours are codominant rather than on a dominance ladder, which was the owner's choice between three options. A ladder hides the second colour a horse carries, and a blend turns most pairs of rainbow colours into brown. Codominance with a two-tone horn keeps both alleles on show, so the horn is also the genotype, and it makes a breeding goal of every pair: a violet horn tipped gold is something you plan for across generations.

The small per-copy shade drift is the mod's premise applied to a colour gene: the alleles are the categories, and the epigenome keeps any two horses in a category from being identical.

What needs verifying here

No coloured horn has been seen in a running game: the colours, the two-tone fade and the per-segment draw it needs are unit-tested and compiled, not watched.

Do the colours read as their names?

The tint multiplies a greyscale sheet, so a colour on the horn is darker than the same colour in a swatch. Look at one horn of each colour in daylight and at night: red should read red, not brown; yellow should not look olive; black should still show its grain. Adjust the HSV rows in AbstractPartColourGene.HUES if not — shared, so every part colour gene moves with them.

The two-tone fade

Check a two-tone horn (Wht/Red, Red/Blu) on a nub and a narwhal: the base colour should hold for the lower part, fade in the middle, and the tip should be the second colour. On a three-segment nub the fade is coarse by construction. Also check a two-tone horn on a fading ridden horse, where every segment's submit has to switch to the translucent render type together.

Per-segment submits in a herd

A two-tone horn costs up to fifteen submits instead of one, doubled if it glows. Two-tone unicorns are rare, but a bred pen of them is not. Measure a pen of twenty against one-colour horns before assuming it is free.
