Magical gene / codominant / colours the body plates, paints nothing

Body plate colour

What colour a horse’s shoulder and hip plates are. The same colours and rules as horn colour (white, the rainbow, pink, black, grey or chaos), on a locus of its own. Two different copies give every plate an edge in the second colour. On a horse without plates it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

The colours, and two-tone plates

Exactly the horn colour table. Two copies of one colour make every plate that colour, both sides of the horse alike. Two different ones make each plate one colour and its edge the other: the lip along its bottom, and the rib or the spike on a ridged or spiked plate. The plate takes whichever colour comes earlier in the list, so Red/Blu is red plates edged in blue, either way round. Every copy carries a little shade of its own, so no two red-plated horses are quite the same red.

Bare bone

Two Bon copies are the plates’ bone version: bare ivory bone, the way a skeleton’s plates would be. No wild horse carries Bon; the skeleton horses do, so a skeleton that grows plates grows them in bone. Plates are all bone, so unlike a back sail nothing is left off.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all. It is only the colour of the plates.

One short subclass, and the edge as the tip

A key, a name, a priority and the words its outcomes are written in. Everything else is AbstractPartColourGene’s. Its Words carry a split sentence (“each plate is one, the base, and its edge, rib or spike the other”), because the shared root-to-tip wording does not describe a plate. GrownParts.of puts one pair of tints on both sides.

The split costs no new machinery. A two-tone part is drawn one box at a time, each in tintAt(PartModel.along(i)), and along is a box’s depth over the depth of its branch. A slab is a root with children, so it sits at 0 and takes the base colour. The lip, rib and both spike boxes all hang straight off the slab with nothing below them, so they sit at 1 and take the tip colour. That is why a spike’s point hangs off the slab rather than off the spike’s base: chained, the base would sit at 0.5 and be drawn half one colour and half the other. BodyPlatesGeneTest.theSlabIsTheBaseAndItsEdgeTheTip holds the shape, and a mutant that chained the spike turned it red (owner, 2026-10-05: edge and feature in the tip colour).

Invented

Horses have no plates, so neither is their colour real. The two-tone edge echoes the pale rims and keels on the scutes of crocodiles and armadillos. It is a separate locus from every other part’s because the owner chose one colour gene per part; a shared one would force every part on a horse to match.

What needs verifying here

The plate-and-edge split is unit-tested and was seen in still frames (2026-10-05); whether a two-tone ridged plate should read as mostly its edge colour, as it does, is still open.

The plate in one colour, its edge in the other

Still frames (2026-10-05) showed Red/Blu and Grn/Yel ridged plates as designed: the earlier colour on the slab, the other on its lip and rib. On a ridged slab the rib and lip cover most of the outer face, so the plate reads mostly as its edge colour, with the base colour in thin rims. Whether that is wanted, or the rib should be thinner, is the owner’s call. Still to see: a spiked two-tone horse close up from behind, where the spike should be the edge colour all the way along, with no blend partway up.
