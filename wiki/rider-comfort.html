Gameplay / non-genetic

Rider comfort

Five small things that make riding less irritating, borrowed from other mods that already do them. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship alone. Four of the five have shipped, and one is left — a lead comes back to you now whether this mod teleported the horse out from under it or the leash simply snapped, you can mine from the saddle at the speed you would standing there, ALT+E opens your own pack from the saddle, and a piece of tack goes on with a right-click instead of a trip through the Gear tab. What remains is fading the horse out as you look down, which touches the coat itself and is the only one with a real unknown in it.

Mostly still a plan

This page was moved here whole from the main roadmap, which is now an index of pages like this one rather than a place plans are written. It grows tabs as the thing grows: the Gameplay and Coding tabs carry the pieces that exist, and everything else is on the Roadmap tab, where it is deleted from when it ships.

Your lead comes back

A horse cannot stay leashed to a fence while it is being teleported across the world, so anything that summons one has to untie it first. It used to leave the lead lying on the ground where the horse had been standing — which, when you had just called the horse to you from sixty blocks away, meant walking back for it. With an ender whistle or an interdimensional ticket it was worse than that: the lead was left in the dimension the horse started in, and you were unlikely to ever see it again.

Now the lead goes into your pack, or drops at your own feet if your pack is full. Nothing else changes: the horse still arrives, and it still arrives untied.

It applies wherever this mod moves a horse on purpose — all three area whistles, the ender whistle, and both kinds of ticket. It does not apply when a horse walks into a portal on its own: the person holding the lead is standing right there in that case, so the lead lands at their feet already and vanilla’s own behaviour is the legible one.

Server owners who would rather have vanilla exactly can turn it off with behaviour.leads_return, which puts the lead back on the ground where the horse was.

— and so does the one that snapped

The above was only half of it. Vanilla drops leads too, and on the whole it drops them in worse places: a leash snaps when the horse gets more than twelve blocks from you, and the lead lands where the horse was — which is the far end of whatever just went wrong, usually while you are chasing it. Break a fence knot with four animals tied to it and you get four leads scattered round the post.

Now those come back too. A lead goes to whoever tied it on, wherever they happen to be.

Whoever tied it on is meant literally, and it is not the same as “whoever is holding it”. A lead on a fence post has nobody holding it at all — that is the case worth getting right, since a horse can stand tied up for days — so the mod remembers the person who walked the horse to the fence, and the lead finds them even if they are in the Nether by the time it snaps.

It never holds a lead for you. Three cases stay exactly as vanilla left them: a horse nobody was recorded as tying up (leashed by a command, or tied before this shipped), a horse tied by somebody who has since logged off, and a horse that walked into a portal with its holder standing right beside it. In all three the lead falls on the ground where it always did. The rule is that a lead is never lost, and never parked somewhere you have to go and claim it later.

Same switch as the first half, behaviour.leads_return — off restores vanilla for both.

Mining from the saddle

Minecraft mines at a fifth speed whenever you are not standing on the ground. That rule is there to stop you tunnelling as you fall, and it is a good rule — but a player sitting on a horse is not standing on anything either, so it caught riders too. One sapling across the path, one dirt block on a ledge, and you had to get off, break it, and get back on.

Now you mine from the saddle at the speed you would mine standing in the same spot. Nothing else about mining changes: the same tools, the same blocks, the same enchantments.

The horse has to have its feet on the ground. Mid-jump, or on a horse that flies, the fifth-speed penalty still applies — you are genuinely in the air then, and mining out of the air is a different thing from mining from the saddle.

This is the one piece on this page that changes a vanilla rule rather than adding something beside it, so the switch matters more here than elsewhere: behaviour.mounted_mining_penalty_removed is on by default, and turning it off restores vanilla exactly. It is a server setting, so on a server it is the server’s answer that counts, not each player’s.

Your own inventory, from the saddle

This mod takes E away while you are riding: instead of vanilla’s horse screen you get this mod’s own horse screen, which is a far better trade than it sounds — the screen it replaces held a saddle slot and a barding slot and nothing else. But it left a rider with no way at all to open their own bag without getting off the horse first.

ALT+E now opens your own inventory. E on its own still opens the horse’s screen, exactly as before.

It works on foot too, not only in the saddle. A key that only functions while mounted would be one more thing to remember, and when it did nothing you would have no way to tell whether the key was unbound or the game had decided you were not riding.

There is no config option for this one, which makes it the exception on this page. Unbinding the key in Options → Controls already switches it off, and it is listed there under Inventory, directly beneath vanilla’s own “Open/Close Inventory” — which is where you would be looking anyway if you were wondering what happened to E. Rebind either of them, or swap the two over, and the mod follows.

On a donkey or a mule nothing changed to begin with: this mod keeps no record of one, so E opens vanilla’s screen with its chest grid, as it always did. The new key still works there, and gives you the same pack it gives you anywhere else.

Tack goes on with a right-click

A saddle has always gone on by holding it and right-clicking the horse. So has a barding. But the seventeen other things a horse can wear — the braids, and every pad, blanket, bag, boot and shoe still to be made — could only be put on through the Gear tab: open the screen, find the tab, click the slot, pick the item off a list.

Now they go on the same way the saddle does. Hold the piece, right-click your horse, and it lands in the first empty slot that takes it.

Today that means the rescuing braid, and only the braid, because it is the only piece of this mod’s own gear that has been made yet. It is not a special case, though: the click asks each slot what it accepts, so every piece still to come works this way from the day it exists, with nothing to add.

It never swaps. If every slot that would take the piece is already full, the click is left alone and does whatever it would have done — usually putting you in the saddle. You can see the braid already in the mane, so nothing is ambiguous, and the alternative would mean being unable to mount your own horse while holding a spare. A braid goes to the mane first and the tail second; a boot would go near fore, off fore, near hind, off hind, in the order the paper doll reads.

Your horse, and not a foal. Both refusals say so in a line across the bottom of the screen rather than doing nothing, because both are rules this mod invented and a silent rule looks like a bug. An untamed horse is left to vanilla, which rears up at you — a clearer answer about a wild horse than any sentence here would be.

Sneaking is untouched: sneak-and-use still opens the horse’s screen whatever is in your hand. The switch is behaviour.rightclick_equips_tack, on by default; off, the Gear tab is the only way again. The saddle and the barding are vanilla’s behaviour either way and the switch does not reach them.

How the lead is returned (2026-09-24)

server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player), and every path that teleports a horse calls it instead of untying the horse itself.

The distinction it is built on is one method apart in vanilla. Leashable.dropLeash() unties and spawns an Items.LEAD at the animal’s position; Leashable.removeLeash() is the same untie with the drop suppressed — both are dropLeash(entity, sendPacket, dropLead) with different arguments. So the whole change is removeLeash() plus the same give-or-drop HorseDietHandler uses for a bucket remainder. With the config off, or with no player to give it to, it falls back to dropLeash() and vanilla is back exactly.

It is safe on an unleashed horse — it returns immediately — so the call sites dropped their own isLeashed() guards rather than keeping a second copy of the check. TicketHandler.arrive grew a Player parameter for it, which is the only signature this touched.

The two portal paths, server/PortalEventHandler and server/HorsePortalManager, are deliberately not converted and still call dropLeash(). They untie a horse that walked into a portal itself, with its holder standing next to it, so the lead is already within reach and vanilla’s behaviour is the one that reads correctly.

Guarded by the whistled_lead_comes_back gametest in gametest/ModGameTests — in a gametest rather than JUnit because the NeoForge module’s test classpath carries no Minecraft, and the assertion is about a real Leashable, a real inventory and whether an ItemEntity appeared in a real level. It asserts the two halves separately: no lead on the ground, and a lead in the player’s pack. Passing the first while failing the second would be worse than the behaviour it replaced, so one combined assertion would have been the wrong test. It was confirmed to fail against the old dropLeash() before being kept.

It uses helper.makeMockPlayer rather than makeMockServerPlayerInLevel, which is @Deprecated(forRemoval) and really joins the player list. A leash holder only has to be an Entity — Leashable.setLeashedTo never asks whether it is in the level — and a mock player’s inventory is a real one.

How the mounted mining penalty is removed (2026-09-29)

server/MountedMiningHandler, one listener on PlayerEvent.BreakSpeed, and nothing else.

The ordering in vanilla is what makes this exact. Player.getDestroySpeed ends with if (!this.onGround()) speed /= 5.0F; and then calls EventHooks.getBreakSpeed with the already-divided figure — read out of the decompiled 26.1.2 method rather than assumed, because if the event fired first the handler would be multiplying by five only for vanilla to divide it straight back. As it stands, one multiply by the same five restores the undivided speed precisely.

The guard is on the penalty, not on the posture. The obvious shape — “is the player mounted? then multiply” — becomes a 5× mining boost the moment its premise is wrong, and the premise (that a mounted player’s onGround is false) is a reading of vanilla’s movement code, not something this mod controls. So the condition asks !player.onGround() directly: the multiply happens only where the division provably just happened. If a future version ever leaves a rider standing on their horse, this quietly does nothing instead of handing out five times the mining speed. The vehicle is checked too — getVehicle() instanceof AbstractHorse and that horse onGround() — which is what keeps a jumping or flying horse out.

It runs on both logical sides, registered with no Dist. The client predicts break progress from its own call to the same method, so a server-only handler would draw a crack that crept along while the block broke early. That is safe because ServerConfig is ModConfig.Type.SERVER, which FML’s own javadoc on ModConfig.Type describes as “synced to clients during connection” — so both sides read the server’s value rather than two independent defaults. This is the first place in the mod that reads a server config from client-side code; the leadsReturn()-style accessor (mountedMiningPenaltyRemoved(), catching IllegalStateException) is what covers the window before the sync lands.

Guarded by the mounted_mining_is_exempt gametest in gametest/ModGameTests, which posts three real BreakSpeed events through NeoForge.EVENT_BUS — so a handler written but never registered fails it. It asserts the exemption and both refusals separately, because the failure worth catching is not that the handler does nothing: it is that it fires when vanilla charged nothing. That middle assertion was confirmed to fail, with got 10.0 where 2.0 was expected, against a copy of the handler with the onGround guard deleted.

The ground flags in the test are set by hand rather than played out. Whether a real mounted player’s onGround is genuinely false is a claim about vanilla’s movement code, not about this handler — the Verification tab owns that half. What the test owns is that given each posture, the handler does the right thing.

How the inventory key is bound (2026-09-29)

client/RiderInventoryKeyBindings and client/RiderInventoryKeyHandler, the same two-class split as HorseBrowserKeyBindings / HorseBrowserKeyHandler: the mapping is registered on the mod bus from RegisterKeyMappingsEvent, the clicks are drained on the game bus from ClientTickEvent.Post. No payload, no menu type, no server code at all.

Nothing is sent to the server, and nothing needs to be. A player’s own InventoryMenu is not opened on demand — it is live for the whole session — which is why vanilla’s own E branch in Minecraft.handleKeybinds calls setScreen rather than sending anything. HorseBrowserKeyHandler made the same simplification. The one case where vanilla does send a packet is MultiPlayerGameMode.isServerControlledInventory(), which is true precisely when you are riding something with a screen of its own — the case this key exists to step around, so following vanilla there would have defeated it.

Creative mode gets no branch, and that is a deliberate departure from the plan this was built from, which said to pick the screen class by game mode. Reading 26.1.2 says not to: InventoryScreen’s own init and its containerTick both open by checking player.hasInfiniteMaterials() and swapping themselves out for a CreativeModeInventoryScreen. Constructing the survival screen is therefore what vanilla itself does in both modes, and a hand-rolled game-mode test would have been a second copy of that rule — wrong the moment the first one moved.

Post rather than the Pre its neighbour uses. HorseInfoKeyHandler has to run in ClientTickEvent.Pre because it is stealing vanilla’s keyInventory click and must get there before handleKeybinds does. This handler steals nothing: it owns its own mapping, so it runs in Post like the browser key.

The chord, and why it does not double-fire

This is the mod’s first KeyModifier. Every other mapping here is a bare key, and WhistleKeyBindings says in as many words that it avoided a chord to keep the modifier API out of it. The obvious worry is that ALT+E would fire both mappings — this one and vanilla’s bare keyInventory, which HorseInfoKeyHandler is sitting on — leaving you with the horse screen on top and your own inventory swallowed.

It does not, and the reason is in neoforge/client/settings/KeyMappingLookup.getAll. With ALT held it collects the bindings registered under ALT first, and only then if (releasing || matchingBindings.isEmpty()) adds the unmodified ones. A live ALT+E match is a non-empty list, so the bare-E fallback never runs and keyInventory.consumeClick() is never satisfied. The suppression is NeoForge’s, not ours, and it is why this needed no coordination with HorseInfoKeyHandler at all.

Flagged unverified in the source, per the house rule: that is a reading of the 26.1.2 sources, not something a client has done yet, and it covers the Controls screen too — whether the row draws as “Alt + E” or as a bare E that looks like a conflict with vanilla’s. The Verification tab owns both, and names the fallback if the chord will not hold.

The category is KeyMapping.Category.INVENTORY, where the rest of the mod uses MISC. Still a built-in category with nothing to register or localise, so the reason MISC was chosen elsewhere is not violated — but it puts the row directly beneath vanilla’s “Open/Close Inventory”, which is the part of Controls a player is reading when they go looking for what happened to E.

No test. There is nothing here a gametest could hold: the whole of it is a keybind registration and a setScreen on the client, and the one claim worth checking — that the chord suppresses the bare key — is a fact about NeoForge’s input layer that only a real client exercises. It is a Verification-tab item rather than a missing test.

How the snapped lead is returned (2026-09-29)

Two mixins, a stored UUID and thirty lines in server/HorseLeads beside the half that shipped on 2026-09-24. It is much more machinery than that half, and the reason is that neither end of the problem is where you would look for it.

The drop cannot be hooked where it happens

Leashable.dropLeash() and removeLeash() are both one-line delegates to dropLeash(E, boolean, boolean), which is private static on the interface — and that is not a place a mixin can inject. NeoForge publishes no leash event either; there is no class in its event package with “leash” in the name, re-checked rather than taken from the note that said so.

So the seam is the line it calls, not the method it is. Every lead vanilla loses is spawned by entity.spawnAtLocation(level, Items.LEAD), and a search of the whole game finds exactly two callers that pass that method a lead — the drop itself, and the fallback in restoreLeashFromSave for a leash whose holder no longer exists on load. Both are in Leashable. So one injection on an ordinary public method of Entity covers every path, including the two that arrive from completely different directions: tickLeash for a snap, and a holder failing canInteractWithLevel for a knot somebody broke. That is mixin/LeashDropMixin, and the general lesson is on api notes.

And the owner has to be written down before it breaks

By the time the lead is dropped there is no player anywhere in the situation. A knot is the holder, not a person; and dropLeash runs entity.setLeashData(null) before it spawns the item, so even the knot is gone. The only moment the answer exists is when the lead goes on, which is mixin/LeashPlacerMixin on Mob.setLeashData — a concrete override of an abstract interface method, and so reachable where the rest of the leash plumbing is not.

The rules it writes are three, and the two that are not “record the player” are the interesting ones. A non-player holder on a fresh leash (a dispenser tying a horse straight to a post) clears whoever was there before, or a lead somebody else tied on would come back to them. A null holder, or null data, leaves the record alone: null data is what vanilla writes the instant before it drops the lead, and clearing there would wipe the answer a moment before it is asked for — while a null holder inside real data is a leash being restored from a save, where clearing would lose the placer of every fence-tied horse on every world load.

The fence transition is handled by vanilla’s own control flow rather than by a rule here, which is the part most worth knowing. Leashable.setLeashedTo calls setLeashData only when the animal had no leash data at all; once it has some it takes a leashData.setLeashHolder(holder) branch that never reaches the mixin. That looks like a gap and is the opposite of one: tying a horse to a fence is always a second attachment, because LeadItem.bindPlayerMobs only considers animals already held by you. So the knot inherits the player recorded a moment earlier, for free.

The record lives in a leash_placer attachment — serialized, since a horse can stand tied up across a restart; not synced, since nothing on the client reads it. It is deliberately not cleared when the lead comes off, because the drop reads it after vanilla has already untied the horse. A stale value is harmless: nothing consults it unless a lead is being dropped, and the next person to tie one on overwrites it.

What the test can and cannot reach

vanilla_lead_comes_back in gametest/ModGameTests, and it is shaped by two things that went wrong in its first draft.

It drops nothing on the ground. The first version asserted the fallback by really calling dropLeash() and finding the lead on the floor, and it broke whistled_lead_comes_back — whose own assertItemEntityNotPresent reaches sixteen blocks and found this test’s lead in the neighbouring plot. A gametest that litters the shared world fails its neighbours rather than itself, which is a bad way to find out.

And it makes no real player. A gametest server has nobody on it, so the only player getPlayerList() could find is one built by makeMockServerPlayerInLevel — @Deprecated(forRemoval), and it really joins the player list, which fires this mod’s join payloads down an EmbeddedChannel that has negotiated nothing and takes the whole run down with “Payload horsegenetics:gene_database_sync may not be sent to the client”. So the test drives HorseLeads.giveRecordedLeadTo and the one line above it — the player-list lookup — is the only part of this left to the in-game check.

Proving the mixins are applied is not the test’s job, and it would be a weaker guarantee than the one already in place: the mixin config’s defaultRequire of 1 means a target that stops matching refuses to boot the game. What the test owns is everything that would still be wrong with both mixins happily applied — that the placer is recorded, that the fence does not erase it, that an unreachable placer is refused so vanilla still drops, that the wrong player is refused, and that the record is cleared afterwards. That last one was confirmed to fail, with “the placer was not cleared… a second drop would mint a second lead out of nothing”, against a copy with the clear deleted.

How the tack click works (2026-09-29)

server/TackEquipHandler, one listener on PlayerInteractEvent.EntityInteract, plus behaviour.rightclick_equips_tack and the right_click_equips_tack gametest. It is much smaller than it was planned to be, because two thirds of it turned out to be vanilla’s already.

What vanilla was already doing

The plan this was built from assumed right-click-to-equip did not exist in 26.1.2 and marked it unverified, with an instruction to read AbstractHorse.mobInteract before writing anything. Reading it settles both halves of the piece at once, and against it:

  • The saddle is vanilla. Items.SADDLE carries Equippable.saddle(), which is built with setEquipOnInteract(true), so ItemStack.interactLivingEntity routes through Equippable.equipOnTarget and the saddle is on before mobInteract reaches doPlayerRide. It even declines to swap already: equipOnTarget requires !target.hasItemInSlot(this.slot).
  • The barding is vanilla too. mobInteract ends with if (this.isEquippableInSlot(itemStack, EquipmentSlot.BODY) && !this.isWearingBodyArmor()) and equips it. That predicate is character-for-character the one HorseTackSlot.accepts asks for the BARDING slot, so there is provably nothing this mod could add there.

So the handler skips every isVanilla() slot deliberately. Taking them over would have put two code paths on the same saddle, and the loser would have been the one with the equip sound and the guaranteed drop. What is left is the seventeen that ride in the HORSE_GEAR attachment, which vanilla cannot see and which had exactly one way in.

The treatment’s pre-agreed fallback for this case said to keep the handler for “armour and the mod’s own tack slots”, on the expectation that only the saddle would turn out to be vanilla’s. Armour is vanilla’s as well, so what shipped is the second half of that sentence only. The instruction not to retire the piece was the right call regardless — the seventeen slots are the part nothing covered.

Which clicks it claims, and the two it must not

LOWEST, the same priority and the same reasoning as HorseInteractionHandler.onFoalInventory: every other horse interaction in this mod cancels the event when it claims a click, and a cancelled event is never delivered to a lower priority. So this only ever sees a click nothing else wanted, and an interaction added later needs no change here. It is above vanilla at any priority, since the event fires from Player.interactOn before mobInteract is called at all.

The item is tested before the horse is — isModTack walks the roster asking each non-vanilla slot’s tag — so a click holding anything else is untouched, including by the refusals. Getting that order wrong would have meant a carrot earning a “not your horse” line.

Two refusals are claimed and two are not, and the difference is load-bearing. Not-your-horse and a foal cancel the event and send a line, because both are rules this mod invented and an invented rule that fails silently reads as a bug — the same reasoning behind the name tag’s not_yours message. An untamed horse, and a piece whose every slot is already full, are left uncancelled: vanilla then rears the wild horse, and lets you mount your own. A handler that cancelled all four would look right in game until somebody could not get on a horse while holding a spare braid.

Ownership is HorseOwnership.isOwner, the same question the Gear tab’s own TackSlotPayload asks before it believes a click, so the two ways in agree. The slot chosen is the first empty one in roster order, which is the paper doll’s reading order, so which slot a piece lands in is predictable for the sets. Creative keeps its stack, which is this mod’s rule everywhere and not vanilla’s — equipOnTarget splits one off whatever the game mode.

Guarded by right_click_equips_tack in gametest/ModGameTests, which posts seven real EntityInteract events through NeoForge.EVENT_BUS — so a handler written but never registered fails it. It asserts the claimed and unclaimed cases separately, since a handler that claimed everything is the failure worth catching. Making the full-slot case cancel turned it red with “a third braid with nowhere left to put it: the click was claimed, and it should have been left to vanilla” before the real version was kept.

The test opens by asserting the rescuing braid is actually in gear/mane and gear/tail. Fifteen of the seventeen tags are still empty, so if the braid ever left its two the test would go on passing while exercising nothing at all — there would be no item in the game this handler could equip.

What is still planned here

One is left: fading the horse out as you look down, which asks the coat pipeline for a transparency it has never had, and so begins with a rendering spike rather than with a feature.

Harvested from the quality-of-life cluster of the survey backlog. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship on its own. The one left wants a config switch written with it, per the config sweep — the returned lead, mining from the saddle and the tack click all went out with their switch written beside them, which is the pattern to follow. The inventory keybind is the one exception and shipped deliberately without one: an unbound key is already the switch.

  • Fade the horse out as you look down. Horse Buff fades the mount for sightlines, with an optional head-pitch offset of up to 45°. This is the one with a real unknown in it: we are the only mod here whose coat is generated per horse, and a half-transparent procedural coat is a rendering case nothing in the pipeline has ever been asked for. Prove the transparency before designing the feature around it.

Not taken: Horse Buff's step-height increase from 1.0 to 1.1. Considered and declined — a horse snagging on a stair edge is vanilla's behaviour and changing it moves a number every other mod's pathing assumes.

What needs verifying here

Five shipped pieces have been seen by no player — the returned lead's untested branch is the one that fires when your pack is full, where a lead that goes nowhere would be strictly worse than the ground drop it replaced; the mounted mining exemption rests on a reading of vanilla that only a stopwatch in a real world can confirm, since if that reading is wrong the feature simply never fires; the ALT+E inventory key is the mod's first key chord, resting on a reading of NeoForge's input layer that no client has yet run; the tack click now shares the plain right-click with every other interaction this mod has, so what wants watching is not whether it equips a braid but whether feeding, naming and mounting still behave; and the snapped lead is two mixins deep into vanilla's leash code, where the part no test could reach is the player-list lookup that is the whole point of it.

The lead comes back (2026-09-24, NOT played)

whistled_lead_comes_back pins the ordinary case and was confirmed to fail against the old behaviour, so the first check below is a confirmation rather than a discovery. The rest are the branches no test reaches.

  • The ordinary case. Leash a tamed horse of yours to a fence, walk twenty blocks off, and blow a golden whistle. Pass: the horse arrives, the fence knot is gone, and a lead is in your inventory. Fail: a lead on the ground where the horse was, which means the helper is falling through to dropLeash().
  • A full pack — the branch the gametest does not reach. Fill your inventory completely, then do the same thing. Pass: the lead drops at your feet. Fail: no lead anywhere, which would be worse than the behaviour this replaced and is the only way this change can lose one.
  • The interdimensional ticket — the case that used to lose it for good. Leash a horse in the overworld and ticket it to a stall in the Nether. The lead should be on you, not left behind in the overworld.
  • The ender whistle across dimensions, the same way.
  • The switch works in both directions. Turn behaviour.leads_return off and blow the whistle again: the lead should fall on the ground where the horse was standing, exactly as vanilla does. A switch that does nothing in one direction is the usual way a config option is wrong.
  • The portals are deliberately unchanged. Lead a horse through a horse portal and through a Nether portal. The lead should still pop off and drop at your feet, as before — if it starts arriving in your inventory, one of the two portal paths was converted by accident.

Mining from the saddle (2026-09-29, NOT played)

mounted_mining_is_exempt pins what the handler does given each posture, and was confirmed to fail against a copy with its guard deleted. What it cannot pin is the premise underneath: is a real mounted player’s onGround actually false? That was read out of vanilla’s movement code, not measured. If it is wrong the handler never fires and the feature is simply absent — which looks identical to it not having been built, so the first check below is the one that matters.

  • Time it, do not eyeball it. Stand on the ground with a stone pickaxe and break a block of stone; then sit on a horse standing on that same ground and break an identical one. Pass: the two take the same time. Fail: the mounted one takes about five times as long, which means the premise was wrong and the handler is never firing. A block that takes a fraction of a second either way will not show this — use something slow, like obsidian with an iron pick, or deepslate by hand.
  • The 5× boost, from the other direction. Break the same block standing on the ground next to your horse, not on it. Pass: the ordinary time. Fail: noticeably faster than normal, which means the guard is inverted and every player on the server is mining at five times speed. This is the failure worth looking for.
  • Mid-jump. Break a block while the horse is in the air from a jump. Pass: the slow, fifth-speed vanilla behaviour. Fail: full speed, which means the vehicle’s own onGround is not being read.
  • On a flying horse, the same way — full mining speed out of the air is not what was asked for.
  • The client agrees with the server. The thing to watch is the crack overlay: it should fill and the block should break at the same moment. If the crack creeps slowly and the block pops early (or the reverse), one side is reading a different config value and the sync assumption is wrong.
  • The switch works in both directions. Turn behaviour.mounted_mining_penalty_removed off, restart, and time the mounted break again: it should be back to a fifth speed. A switch that does nothing in one direction is the usual way a config option is wrong.
  • Boats and minecarts are untouched. Break a block from a boat. Pass: still slow, as vanilla. Fail: full speed, which would mean the vehicle test is not actually restricting to horses.

The inventory key (2026-09-29, NOT played)

Nothing here is unit-testable, so all of it is a hand check. Two separate claims are being taken on trust, and they fail in different ways: that a KeyModifier chord works at all in 26.1.2 — this is the mod’s first — and that it suppresses the bare key it is built on, which is read out of KeyMappingLookup.getAll rather than seen.

  • The suppression, which is the check that matters. Ride a tamed horse of yours and press ALT+E. Pass: your own inventory, and only your own inventory. Fail: the horse screen appears instead, or appears on top — which means the bare-E fallback in KeyMappingLookup ran anyway and both mappings fired. That is the whole feature failing in exactly the place it was built for, and it looks like the key simply not working.
  • Nothing was taken from E. On the same horse, press E alone. Pass: the horse screen, as before. Fail: your own inventory, which would mean the chord registered with no modifier and has quietly replaced the horse screen.
  • On foot. Get off and press ALT+E standing in a field. Pass: your inventory opens. This was the owner’s call — the key is not restricted to riding — so a failure here is a wrong conflict context, not a missing guard.
  • The Controls screen draws the chord. Open Options → Controls and find it under Inventory, beneath vanilla’s “Open/Close Inventory”. Pass: the row reads “Alt + E”. Fail: a bare E, or a conflict warning against vanilla’s row — either means the modifier was not stored, and the suppression check above will be failing too.
  • Rebinding survives. Rebind it to something else, restart the client, and check the new binding is still there and still opens the right screen. A chord that does not round-trip through options.txt is the usual way the modifier API goes wrong.
  • Creative mode. Press it in creative. Pass: the creative item picker, not the survival inventory — this rests on InventoryScreen swapping itself out in init, which is read from vanilla rather than reimplemented. A one-frame flash of the survival screen before the swap would also count as a fail worth reporting.
  • A donkey or a mule. Ride one and press both keys. Pass: E gives vanilla’s screen with its chest grid, unchanged, and ALT+E gives your own pack.
  • It does not reach through a screen. With the horse browser or the horse screen already open, press ALT+E. Pass: nothing happens. Fail: the inventory opens over the top, which means the mc.screen == null guard is not holding.

If the chord will not hold, the fallback the owner already agreed is a bare key that clashes with nothing in this repo — the taken ones are H, ’, F, F6, F7 and F8. Pick one, ship it, and say which; do not leave a chord in that half-works.

The snapped lead (2026-09-29, NOT played)

vanilla_lead_comes_back pins the recording, the fence transition and every refusal, and was confirmed to fail against a copy that did not clear the record. Two things are left, and they are the two halves it structurally could not reach.

The player-list lookup has never run. A gametest server has nobody on it, so the one line that turns a stored UUID into a player — getPlayerList().getPlayer(id) — is the only part of this that has never executed against a real player. If it is wrong, every lead falls on the ground and the feature is silently absent: it looks exactly like vanilla, because it is vanilla. That makes the first check below the one that matters.

And two mixins now sit on paths every entity uses. LeashDropMixin is on Entity.spawnAtLocation, which every item any entity ever drops goes through, and LeashPlacerMixin is on Mob.setLeashData, which every leashed animal in the game touches. Neither should do anything outside its guard, and the checks for that are worth more than the feature ones.

  • The snap, which is the check that matters. Tie a lead to your horse, walk it, then run away until the leash breaks. Pass: the lead is in your pack. Fail: it is on the ground where the horse was, which means the lookup found nobody and the whole feature is absent.
  • The fence, which is what the stored UUID exists for. Walk a horse to a fence, tie it there, then walk away and log off and back on if you like. Break the knot. Pass: the lead comes to you. Fail: it drops at the post, which means the placer was lost either at the moment of tying or across the save.
  • Across a dimension. Tie a horse to a fence in the Overworld, go to the Nether, and have someone break the knot — or leave it leashed to something that will stop existing. The lead should reach you where you are. This is the decided rule and it is the one that would be easiest to get half right.
  • Nothing else that drops an item changed. The most valuable check here: kill a horse wearing a saddle and barding, shear a braid off, break a cart, empty a trough, kill a cow, mine a block. Everything should drop exactly what it always did. Entity.spawnAtLocation is a very general method and the guard on it is one identity comparison — if that guard is wrong, items go missing all over the game.
  • Other animals are untouched. Snap a leash on a cow, a pig, a llama. Pass: the lead falls on the ground, exactly as vanilla — this is a horse mod and the mixin is guarded to AbstractHorse.
  • A donkey and a mule are included, though. Same check as the snap, on one of each.
  • Untying by hand is unchanged. Right-click a horse you are leading, with an empty hand. Pass: the lead drops at your feet as before — you are standing right there, and this is the same reasoning that leaves the portal paths alone.
  • An offline placer drops it. Two players: one ties a horse up and logs off, the other breaks the knot. Pass: the lead is on the ground. Fail: nothing anywhere, which would mean a lead held for later, and the rule is that never happens.
  • A command-leashed horse drops it too. Leash one with /data or a dispenser and break it. Nobody was recorded, so the ground is correct.
  • No dupe on a full pack. Fill your inventory, then snap a leash. Pass: exactly one lead, at your feet. Fail: two, or none.
  • The switch works in both directions. Turn behaviour.leads_return off, restart, and snap a leash: the lead should fall where the horse was. It now governs both halves, so check the whistle case is still off as well.

The tack click (2026-09-29, NOT played)

right_click_equips_tack pins what the handler does with a braid, and was confirmed to fail against a copy that cancelled the full-slot case. What it cannot pin is the thing actually worth worrying about: this put a new listener on the plain right-click, which is the busiest gesture in the mod. The gametest posts events into an empty world; it cannot tell you that feeding, naming, branding and mounting all still work in a real one. Most of the checks below are therefore about what did not change.

  • It works at all. Hold a rescuing braid, right-click your own adult horse. Pass: the braid appears in the mane, the equip sound plays, and one braid leaves your hand. Fail: you get on the horse instead, which means the click is not being claimed.
  • The second goes to the tail, and the mane keeps the one it has. Dye two braids different colours first — that is the only way to see a swap, and a swap would look identical to a correct equip from the outside.
  • You can still mount a braided horse while holding a braid. With both slots full, right-click again. Pass: you get on. Fail: nothing happens, or a refusal line appears — which means the full-slot case is being claimed and you can no longer mount your own horse with tack in hand. This is the regression the design chose against; it is worth checking in the world as well as in the test.
  • Nothing else on the right-click broke. The one to spend real time on, because this is a LOWEST-priority listener on a gesture shared by everything: feed a breeding carrot, feed hay, use a transfer paper, use a name tag, bind an ender whistle, shear a braid off. All should behave exactly as they did. A break here would show as one of those doing nothing at all.
  • Sneak still opens the screen with a braid in hand. This mod’s horse screen is on sneak-and-empty-hand, so the claim is that holding tack changes nothing about it.
  • A stranger’s horse, and a foal, each say why in a line across the bottom of the screen. Fail: silence, which is how an invented rule reads as a bug.
  • An untamed horse rears up as vanilla does, rather than printing anything. Needs a second player or a second account for the stranger case; the foal and the wild horse do not.
  • The saddle and the barding are untouched. Right-click with a saddle, then with each of the four horse armours. All should go on exactly as before — this handler deliberately does not cover them, and a change here would mean it is racing vanilla for the same slot.
  • Creative keeps the stack. In creative, equipping a braid should not reduce what you are holding. Fail: it shrinks, which is vanilla’s rule and not this mod’s.
  • The switch works in both directions. Turn behaviour.rightclick_equips_tack off, restart, and try again: the braid should not go on, and the Gear tab should still put it on. A switch that does nothing in one direction is the usual way a config option is wrong.
