Gameplay / non-genetic

Rider comfort

Five small things that make riding less irritating, borrowed from other mods that already do them. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship alone. One of the five has shipped — a lead now comes back to you rather than being left on the ground when this mod teleports the horse wearing it. The other four are still plans, and the last of them — fading the horse out as you look down — touches the coat itself and is the only one with a real unknown in it.

Your lead comes back

A horse cannot stay leashed to a fence while it is being teleported across the world, so anything that summons one has to untie it first. It used to leave the lead lying on the ground where the horse had been standing — which, when you had just called the horse to you from sixty blocks away, meant walking back for it. With an ender whistle or an interdimensional ticket it was worse than that: the lead was left in the dimension the horse started in, and you were unlikely to ever see it again.

Now the lead goes into your pack, or drops at your own feet if your pack is full. Nothing else changes: the horse still arrives, and it still arrives untied.

It applies wherever this mod moves a horse on purpose — all three area whistles, the ender whistle, and both kinds of ticket. It does not apply when a horse walks into a portal on its own: the person holding the lead is standing right there in that case, so the lead lands at their feet already and vanilla’s own behaviour is the legible one.

Server owners who would rather have vanilla exactly can turn it off with behaviour.leads_return, which puts the lead back on the ground where the horse was.

How the lead is returned (2026-09-24)

server/HorseLeads.untieFor(horse, player), and every path that teleports a horse calls it instead of untying the horse itself.

The distinction it is built on is one method apart in vanilla. Leashable.dropLeash() unties and spawns an Items.LEAD at the animal’s position; Leashable.removeLeash() is the same untie with the drop suppressed — both are dropLeash(entity, sendPacket, dropLead) with different arguments. So the whole change is removeLeash() plus the same give-or-drop HorseDietHandler uses for a bucket remainder. With the config off, or with no player to give it to, it falls back to dropLeash() and vanilla is back exactly.

It is safe on an unleashed horse — it returns immediately — so the call sites dropped their own isLeashed() guards rather than keeping a second copy of the check. TicketHandler.arrive grew a Player parameter for it, which is the only signature this touched.

The two portal paths, server/PortalEventHandler and server/HorsePortalManager, are deliberately not converted and still call dropLeash(). They untie a horse that walked into a portal itself, with its holder standing next to it, so the lead is already within reach and vanilla’s behaviour is the one that reads correctly.

Guarded by the whistled_lead_comes_back gametest in gametest/ModGameTests — in a gametest rather than JUnit because the NeoForge module’s test classpath carries no Minecraft, and the assertion is about a real Leashable, a real inventory and whether an ItemEntity appeared in a real level. It asserts the two halves separately: no lead on the ground, and a lead in the player’s pack. Passing the first while failing the second would be worse than the behaviour it replaced, so one combined assertion would have been the wrong test. It was confirmed to fail against the old dropLeash() before being kept.

It uses helper.makeMockPlayer rather than makeMockServerPlayerInLevel, which is @Deprecated(forRemoval) and really joins the player list. A leash holder only has to be an Entity — Leashable.setLeashedTo never asks whether it is in the level — and a mock player’s inventory is a real one.

What is still planned here

Four small borrowings from other mods are left, each shippable alone and each wanting a config switch — no mining-speed debuff while mounted, an inventory keybind, a right-click that equips tack, and returning the leads vanilla drops, which has no event to hang on — plus fading the horse out as you look down, which asks the coat pipeline for a transparency it has never had.

Harvested from the quality-of-life cluster of the survey backlog. None touches genetics, and each is small enough to ship on its own. Every one of them wants a config switch written with it, per the config sweep — the first to ship, the returned lead, shipped with behaviour.leads_return beside it, which is the pattern the rest should follow.

  • Remove the mining-speed debuff while mounted. Vanilla gives you a fifth of your mining speed on horseback. Taken from Horse Buff. Worth being honest that this is the one of the five that alters rather than adds, so vanilla stays intact has a claim on it and the switch matters more here than elsewhere.
  • A keybind to open your own inventory while riding. Horse Buff binds it to ALT+E. We already own H for the browser through client/HorseBrowserKeyBindings, so the idiom and the registration exist. This one adds a door rather than closing one.
  • Fade the horse out as you look down. Horse Buff fades the mount for sightlines, with an optional head-pitch offset of up to 45°. This is the one with a real unknown in it: we are the only mod here whose coat is generated per horse, and a half-transparent procedural coat is a rendering case nothing in the pipeline has ever been asked for. Prove the transparency before designing the feature around it.
  • Leads return to your inventory when vanilla drops one — a leash that snaps at range, or a knot broken by hand. From Horse Tweaks. This mod’s own half shipped on 2026-09-24; what is left is the vanilla cases, and they are harder than they look. NeoForge 26.1.2 publishes no leash event at all, and Leashable.dropLeash spawns its item through Entity.spawnAtLocation with nothing to hook — so the only seams are a mixin or an EntityJoinLevelEvent filter that would have to tell a snapped lead from one a player threw on the floor. Decide which before building it.
  • Right-click with a saddle or armour equips it rather than opening the horse screen. Also Horse Tweaks. The one most likely to collide with something we ship: client/HorseScreenHooks, the i button and the right-click to open client option all live on that same interaction, so this needs designing against them rather than beside them.

Not taken: Horse Buff's step-height increase from 1.0 to 1.1. Considered and declined — a horse snagging on a stair edge is vanilla's behaviour and changing it moves a number every other mod's pathing assumes.

What needs verifying here

The returned lead has been seen only by a gametest — and the branch the gametest cannot reach is the one that fires when your pack is full, where a lead that goes nowhere at all would be strictly worse than the ground drop it replaced.

The lead comes back (2026-09-24, NOT played)

whistled_lead_comes_back pins the ordinary case and was confirmed to fail against the old behaviour, so the first check below is a confirmation rather than a discovery. The rest are the branches no test reaches.

  • The ordinary case. Leash a tamed horse of yours to a fence, walk twenty blocks off, and blow a golden whistle. Pass: the horse arrives, the fence knot is gone, and a lead is in your inventory. Fail: a lead on the ground where the horse was, which means the helper is falling through to dropLeash().
  • A full pack — the branch the gametest does not reach. Fill your inventory completely, then do the same thing. Pass: the lead drops at your feet. Fail: no lead anywhere, which would be worse than the behaviour this replaced and is the only way this change can lose one.
  • The interdimensional ticket — the case that used to lose it for good. Leash a horse in the overworld and ticket it to a stall in the Nether. The lead should be on you, not left behind in the overworld.
  • The ender whistle across dimensions, the same way.
  • The switch works in both directions. Turn behaviour.leads_return off and blow the whistle again: the lead should fall on the ground where the horse was standing, exactly as vanilla does. A switch that does nothing in one direction is the usual way a config option is wrong.
  • The portals are deliberately unchanged. Lead a horse through a horse portal and through a Nether portal. The lead should still pop off and drop at your feet, as before — if it starts arriving in your inventory, one of the two portal paths was converted by accident.
