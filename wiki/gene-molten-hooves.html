Magical gene / an emission, no coat effect

Molten hooves

Flame trails from the hooves that fade behind the horse as it moves. The colour is written on the allele copy and inherited with it, so a line breeds true to its own fire. It is light and particles only — nothing it runs over catches.

It is dominant, so one copy is enough and it is common wherever it exists at all. That is the whole character of the gene: a herd with molten hooves in it is visible from a long way off at night.

It does not set anything on fire

Worth saying plainly, because the name promises otherwise. The hoofprints are particles and a brief glow. Grass, crops, wooden stables and the horse itself are all safe, and you can ride one through a forest.

Health

None. It costs the horse nothing and protects it from nothing.

Newly built, not yet play-tested

Nothing on this page has been seen in game. The gene, its founder table and its emitter are covered by MoltenHoovesGeneTest; what no test can check is whether the trail actually reads as fire at the hooves of a moving horse. Checklist on To be verified.

It needed no new machinery, and that held

No verb, no trigger, no condition flag. An emitter anchored at hooves with an epigenetic colour, which is what particle and rainbow dust already do. The whole gene is one class and one line in Genes.

Dominant, so the particle budget matters more here than anywhere else

Every other emitter locus in the mod is recessive or rare. This one expresses on a single copy, so a populated stable can have a dozen horses all emitting on on_move at once. Keep the shipped count and chance defaults rather than tuning them up for looks — the fix for “not dramatic enough” is a brighter colour, not more particles.

Real fire was considered and rejected

The first specification placed actual fire blocks. On a dominant gene that burns down the stable, the forest and the horse. If a later session wants the mechanic back, it must come with a demotion to recessive — the two decisions are one decision.

Why it is dust and not flame

Flame particles ignore colour entirely, and a colour written on the allele copy is the entire point of the locus — so the trail is drawn with dust_color_transition, which is also the only particle that fades the way a cooling hoofprint should. It is the same particle rainbow dust uses, with the cycle left at zero: rainbow dust reads its hue off the clock, and this one means the two colours it carries.

A molten horse may burn blue

The hue is unconstrained. EpiValue.colour bounds saturation and value but rolls hue over the whole circle, and rather than add a hue-bounded draw for one gene the locus simply allows it. Nothing about molten hooves needs to be orange, and a line that breeds true to a cold blue fire is a better outcome than a fourth kind of colour draw.

Where the colour lives

On the allele copy, not on the horse. Two molten copies carry two colours and the trail draws from both, which is why a heterozygote and a homozygote look different even though the gene is dominant and the behaviour is identical. That is the same arrangement particle uses, and reusing it means the epigenetic drift on breeding already works with no extra code.

Why dominant, when almost nothing else is

The magical emission loci are nearly all recessive, which makes them a reward for breeding and invisible until you get there. One of them ought not to be. A dominant emission gene spreads through a herd on its own, so a player who catches a single molten horse and crosses it into their line sees the result immediately — and that is a different kind of pleasure from the long recessive hunt, worth having once.

A cosmetic gene that admits it

The mod has a standing problem with genes that promise a mechanic and deliver a look. This one is a look on purpose and says so on the first tab, which is cheaper and more honest than a fire mechanic nobody dares breed.
