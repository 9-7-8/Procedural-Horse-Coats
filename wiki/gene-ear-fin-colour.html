Magical gene / codominant / colours the ear fins, paints nothing

Ear fin colour

What colour a horse’s ear fins are. The same colours and rules as horn colour (white, the rainbow, pink, black, grey or chaos), on a locus of its own. Two different copies give every ray a point or a rod in the second colour. On a horse without ear fins it does nothing.

Crossing two of them

The colours, and two-tone fins

Exactly the horn colour table. Two copies of one colour make both fins that colour. Two different ones make each ray one colour and its point the other; on a frill the second colour is the rod down the middle of each ray. The ray takes whichever colour comes earlier in the list, so Red/Blu is red fins tipped in blue, either way round. Every copy carries a little shade of its own, so no two red-finned horses are quite the same red.

Bone is only a colour here

Ear fins have no bone version: a fin is cartilage, like an ear, and no skeleton horse grows one. The Bon allele is still on this gene, because every part’s colour gene shares one list, but no breed carries it here. Bred in by hand it gives warm ivory fins and nothing else.

Is it good for the horse?

It does nothing to the horse at all. It is only a colour.

One short subclass, and the ray as the base

A key, a name, a priority and the words its outcomes are written in. Everything else is AbstractPartColourGene’s. Its Words carry a split sentence (“each ray is one, the base, and its point or rod the other”). GrownParts.of puts one pair of tints on both sides. The split is the mesh’s shape and no extra code: a ray is a root, so it sits at 0 on PartModel.along, and its point or rod hangs off it with nothing below, so it sits at 1. Why every ray is a root is on the granting gene’s page. boneOnly is not read: there is nothing to leave off.

Invented

Horses have no fins, so neither is their colour real. The contrasting rays echo the dark spines in a pale fin membrane on many fish. It is a separate locus from every other part’s because the owner chose one colour gene per part; a shared one would force every part on a horse to match.

What needs verifying here

The ray-and-point split is unit-tested and was seen in two still frames (2026-10-09); a two-tone blade and the chaos colour have not been looked at.

The ray in one colour, its point or rod in the other
