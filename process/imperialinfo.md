# “Imperial” Marking

*Imperial markings are believed to be an extension found of the Sabino gene, as there are currently no cases of it being found in horses where a copy of sabino is not present.*

*Imperial commonly starts at the withers, and works its way up the neck, all the way to the poll when maximum coverage is present. It can often express with darker, or lighter coat variations within it’s “branches.”*

As long as one copy of sabino is present, Imperial markings can express. When present, it is shown as nSbi for Imperial with one copy of Sabino, and SbiSbi for Imperial with two copies of Sabino, replacing your normal nSB and SbSb Sabino genes when present. 


Some notes:

    Imperial cannot be solid white on the neck, Slivers of the coat beneath the marking must be present in order for the marking to be accepted.

    While not shown above, it is important to remember that this marking cohabitates with Sabino, and therefore, Sabino must also be expressed on the coat for the marking to be excepted, even if the white expression from Sabino is minimal. 

    Imperial only ever expresses as a "white" marking. It is not to be confused with brindle, which can express as a darker tone than the coat, or any other stripe adjacent genes that aren't shown in the breed. 

    Imperial cannot just be random, unconnected "branches". The marking will always come together at some point, with that connection point depending upon what level of coverage is present. A full-neck-wraparound can be present for more white, whereas a point of connection dipping into the chest and under the armpits is more often witnessed for less white. 


# Imperial Marking Shape Description

The white marking shown on the main horse has a **cape or blanket-like shape** that:

- **Originates at the withers** and extends upward and forward
- **Covers most of the neck**, spreading from the shoulder base to the poll (top of head)
- **Has a relatively broad footprint** across the neck and shoulder area, with the white area extending well onto both sides of the neck
- **Features defined edges** with an irregular or asymmetrical boundary—the text references "branches" (darker or lighter coat variations) that can appear within its coverage area

## Minimum Version (Min)
A **small, compact patch** at the withers or base of the neck—essentially a minimal expression of the marking with reduced coverage.

## Maximum Version (Max)
A **substantially larger marking** that extends the full length from the withers all the way to the poll, creating extensive white coverage across the entire neck and shoulder region with maximum lateral spread.

The progression shows the marking can scale dramatically in size while maintaining its fundamental cape-like silhouette. The text notes that the marking "can express nSh for minimal coverage and nShSh for maximum coverage," and that variations appear as darker or lighter "branches" within the marked area rather than as completely clean edges.
