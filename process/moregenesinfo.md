# Rex Marking

**Base Characteristics:**
- Characterized by circled colored markings in the speckling color(s)
- Can appear anywhere on the body
- Minimum of three markings required
- Does not affect speckling
- Must have regular mane striping
- Must have speckling-colored hooves
- Any speckling color and type allowed
- All paint markings allowed
- Rex markings appear over normal paint markings

# Iuari Marking

**Base Characteristics:**
- Darker or lighter patches on the body, surrounded by speckling
- Can appear anywhere on the body
- Does not reflect speckling amount (minimal/medium/maximal) in genotype
- Must have regular mane stripes
- Marking and speckling go under white markings
- All white markings allowed (except leopard and dominant white)
- All speckling colors allowed

---

## Visual Examples

**Image 1:** Chestnut base with teal speckling and teal-accented mane; teal hooves. Iuari patches appear as darker concentrated areas distributed across neck, shoulders, barrel, and hindquarters, each ringed by the teal speckling.

**Image 2:** Tan/beige base with multicolor (yellow, teal, green) speckling and rainbow-accented mane; teal hooves. Iuari darker patches (brown) are more densely defined, appearing as concentrated zones outlined by the scattered bright speckling, particularly prominent along the neck, shoulder line, and hindquarter.

Both examples demonstrate the characteristic "patch surrounded by speckling" appearance, with the Iuari patches appearing as intentional darker focal points against the lighter base coat, each zone rimmed by speckling distribution.

# Emitola Marking

**Base Characteristics:**
- Extensive and intricate black or darkened/lightened base coat marking covering underside
- Flaming edges, adorned with speckling-colored flames
- Does not connect between front and back legs
- Speckling-colored forelock
- No regular mane stripes allowed
- Black or speckling-colored hooves
- All white markings allowed
- Emitola goes over white markings
- One speckling color only

---

**Base Color:** Grey on sooty liver chestnut dun

**Speckling:** Maximal, bright lime green (p:41)

**Marking Presentation:**
- **Underside coverage**: Extensive black marking across the entire ventral (underside) surface—chest, belly, and between legs
- **Flaming edges**: Sharp, intricate flame-like boundaries adorned with lime green speckling throughout the flame design, creating a jagged, energetic edge pattern
- **Forelock**: Bright lime green (speckling color)

- **Hooves**: Lime green (matching speckling color)

The overall effect is a dramatic silhouette of dark underside with vibrant speckling-colored flame detailing, creating high contrast against the lighter grey-chestnut base coat.

# Gudial Marking

**Base Characteristics:**
- Intricate marking based on poisonous frogs
- Colored stripes on neck and legs
- Dark head with striped markings
- Spotting on topline (resembling Jarce spotting)
- No regular mane stripes allowed
- Hooves in one speckling color
- nSpk (one color speckling) = two color marking; SpkSpk (two color speckling) = three color marking
- Does not allow white patterns of any kind

---

**Base Color:** Black

**Speckling Colors:** Red/orange and white (two color speckling - SpkSpk)

**Marking Presentation:**
- **Head**: Dark black with intricate red/orange striped mask-like markings around eyes and muzzle

- **Legs**: Bold red/orange and white horizontal striping from upper leg down to hoof, creating segmented banding effect
- **Hooves**: Red/orange (speckling color)
- **Overall Effect**: High-contrast poisonous frog aesthetic with warning coloration—bright red/orange and white stripes and spots against solid black base, evoking the visual "toxicity warning" of dendrobatid frogs

# Fade Mutation

**Base Characteristics:**
- Causes the mane and tail of phenomicorns to fade (gradient) into an "unnatural" color
- The color is semi-hereditary
- If the parent(s) have brightly colored hair, so will the offspring
- Offspring of deeply blue/dark-colored fade foals will have other darkly colored fade foals, and so on
- Creates a **gradient effect** from body coat color transitioning into the unnatural fade color through mane and tail

---

## Visual Examples

### Example 1 (Light Expression)

**Body Color:** Cream/white

**Fade Color:** Purple/lavender

**Presentation:**
- **Mane**: Cream base transitioning to **soft purple/lavender gradient**; gradient flows from root toward tip
- **Tail**: Cream base with **gentle purple fade** creating **soft ombre effect**
- **Gradient quality**: **Smooth, flowing transition** from bright cream through purple; appears **painted or watercolor-blended**
- **Overall effect**: Ethereal, pastel quality; soft color shift is **barely noticeable at first glance**

### Example 2 (Deep Expression)

**Body Color:** Dark brown

**Fade Color:** Hot pink/magenta

**Presentation:**
- **Mane**: Dark brown/black base transitioning to **bold hot pink/magenta gradient**; gradient is **highly visible and dramatic**
- **Tail**: Dark brown transitioning to **vibrant magenta fade** creating **stark color contrast**
- **Gradient quality**: **Pronounced color shift** from dark earth tones into bright neon pink; gradient is **bold and unmistakable**
- **Overall effect**: High-contrast, striking appearance; fade color **dominates visual presentation**; creates **intentional, artificial-looking color block**

---

## Hereditary Pattern

The mutation operates on a **semi-hereditary, progressive system:**

- **Bright-colored parents** (cream, light purple) produce **offspring with bright fade coloring**
- **Deep/dark-colored fade parents** (deep blue, dark pink, brown fade) produce **offspring with darkly colored fade foals**
- **Color progression** moves along a spectrum—bright → moderate → deep/dark with each generation
- Creates a **generational color drift** where fade colors can shift significantly across family lines

# Fielded Marking

**Base Characteristics:**
- A white marking mutation that creates wispy, grass-like tendrils from the edges of other white markings (Tobiano, Overo, Tovero, Splash, Leopard Complex, lacing, Quoll, Sunbathed, etc.)
- Also extends from basic white markings such as stockings, socks, blazes, snips, etc.
- Minimal expression may be "hidden" and not present at all on the coat, or appear as just a streak or two
- Maximal expression creates multiple streaks that can cover the entire body, streaking mane and tail

---

## Quickfire Facts About Fielded

- **No difference in expression** between FldFld or nFld
- **Can cause blue eyes** (does not need to be touching the eye to do so)
- **Streaks that cross the topline** will streak the mane/tail where they touch
- **Streaks cannot be "floating"**—must be anchored to a white marking or another Field streak
- **Does not create "zebra" or "tiger" striping**
- **Amount of streaks is entirely up to you**; the "loudness" of white markings does not determine the expression of Fielded
- A coat **without genetic white markings** is called "Field" (not "Fielded")
  - Example: ee aa nFld = Chestnut Field
  - Example: ee aa nFld nTo = Chestnut Fielded Tobiano

---

## Visual Expression Examples

### Minimal Expression

**Presentation:**
- **Striping density**: Very few streaks; **often barely visible or "hidden"**
- **Streak placement**: Streaks originate from white marking edges (facial blazes, stockings, socks)
- **Streak character**: Simple striping coming from facial markings; **can affect the same marking in different ways** on different sides
- **Example detail**: "Simple striping coming from facial marking" and "can still affect white markings"
- **Visual effect**: Subtle, understated; mutation is **nearly imperceptible** or appears as minimal accent lines

---

### Maximal Expression

**Base Color:** Orange/chestnut with white markings

**Presentation:**
- **Striping density**: Extensive grass-like streaks covering most of the body; streaks appear **multiple and complex** throughout
- **Streak pattern**: 
  - **Neck/shoulder**: Multiple overlapping streaks radiating from facial markings and stockings
  - **Body & barrel**: Heavy, intricate streaking across ribcage and barrel; streaks **interweave and overlap**
  - **Hindquarters**: Multiple streaks across croup and hindquarters
  - **Legs**: Streaks extend from leg stockings upward into body
- **Mane & tail**: **Streaks cross the topline** and continue into mane and tail; mane shows **orange and white striping** where streaks touch; tail displays **similar multi-colored striping**
- **Streak character**: **Wispy, grass-like quality**; streaks are **delicate yet numerous**; appear as **thin, organic lines** rather than geometric or bold
- **Streak anchoring**: All streaks visibly anchored to white marking origins or to other streaks; **no floating streaks**
- **Overall effect**: **Heavily patterned, complex appearance**; creates **dramatic visual impact** while maintaining organic, natural quality; resembles **wind-blown grass or feathered edges**

---

### Moderate and Mixed Expression

**Base Color:** Cream/champagne with white markings

**Presentation:**
- **Striping density**: Moderate number of streaks; more visible than minimal but less extensive than maximal
- **Streak pattern**:
  - **Neck/shoulder**: Several streaks radiating from facial markings
  - **Body**: Some streaking visible on ribcage and barrel; **not covering entire surface**

# Oscar Gene Mutation

**Base Characteristics:**

- Minimal expression is a solid black horse with very minimal coloring on its neck region

## Quickfire Facts About Oscar

# Opossum Gene Mutation

**Base Characteristics:**
- A form of restricted grey and depigmentation of the muzzle
- Hair can shed and turn white anywhere from the face to the neck, shoulders, and front of withers
- If it extends to the muzzle, the skin turns a soft pink color regardless of whether a white marking is present
- Edges are "roaned" out and soft, like heavy sabino
- Affects mane where it touches
- Can cause some streaks on the head of the tail
- Minimal expression is a subtle greying of the brow and forehead
- Maximal expression covers the face, neck, and front of shoulders to the withers

---

## Quickfire Facts About Opossum Gene

- **No difference in expression** between OpOp or nOp
- **Think of this as a "grey" gene** that only affects face to shoulders but also turns muzzle pink (if it extends that far)
- **This gene is progressive** through the age of a Tharoksu—as a foal it may show only some grey hair on the forehead, but the whole neck as an adult
- **You choose how little or how much** the gene spreads as the Tharoksu ages (it does not reverse)
- **Edges are always "roaned" or jagged faded edges**, never hard or sharp cut-offs
- **With no other genes or markings interacting**: skin around eyes will remain dark and muzzle will turn pink if marking goes as far as the muzzle (otherwise it remains unaffected)
- **Other white markings** (bald face, large blaze, snip) will override the Opossum gene
- **Goes below all white markings**

---

# Laciano Marking

**Genetic Code:** LL or nL

---

## Base Characteristics

- A spontaneous mutation that combines **Tobiano and Appaloosa patterns**
- First appeared on Appaloosa, Mustang, Paint, and Quarter Horse crossbreeds
- Has significant variations in expression
- Can sometimes be mistaken for normal Tobiano or Appaloosa patterns
- **All Laciano horses have a striped tail** (also called "raccoon tail")—this is the defining characteristic
- White patterns vary in size and number but **always go from back down**
- Legs are usually not white except from other markings
- Striped hooves and white sclera are possible (white sclera normally limited to Appaloosa breed)
- Can appear on all breeds but most likely on American horses (Appaloosa, Mustang, Paint, Quarter Horse)

---

## Double Laciano (LL) Negative Traits

⚠️ **Horses with LL genotype often experience handicapping conditions:**

- **Blindness or severe vision impairment** (often blind or have very bad sight)
- **Deafness** (sometimes deaf)
- **Tail hair loss** (horses can lose tail hair once a year)
- Requires **additional care and work** due to handicapping conditions

---

## Design Philosophy

This gene was intentionally designed with **negative aspects tied to LL genotype** to:

- Provide **character and roleplay opportunities** for creative storytelling
- Encourage realistic engagement with **horses with disabilities**
- Prompt player consideration of questions such as: *How are you working with such a horse? Are you still loving it with the additional work that comes with a handicapped horse? How do other people react?*
- Create **depth and authenticity** rather than a purely "happy-go-lucky" gene

# Papillon Pattern

**Base Characteristics:**
- A **butterfly wing pattern on the neck**—based on existing or extinct butterfly wing patterns
- Mane and tail display **a gradient of the flashiest and darkest colors appearing on the wing**
- The gene appears spontaneously in some breeds over centuries, especially those with **magic blood**
- Connects to breeds showing **magic affinity** or unicorn lineage
- Must be careful about owning or breeding equines carrying the gene

---

## Origin & Breeding Rules

- Papillon pattern is a gene that appeared spontaneously in some breeds with **magic blood**
- The fanciest papillon patterns are known to come from **unicorn lines or breeds showing magic affinity**
- **Breeding magic to non-magic** causes the gene to become weaker
- **Gene will completely disappear by the second generation** if no more magic blood is involved
- Thus you have to be careful about **owning or breeding an equine carrying the gene**

---

## Gene Mechanics

**Main Gene:** Papillon or Ppl (Ppl when gene is present; ppl when absent)

**Important:** This gene can't work without one or two of the following patterns:

---

## Pattern Expression Types

### Si (Simple Pattern)

- Simple pattern as seen in basic butterfly wing illustration
- **Just the wing pattern and hair coloring** over the body
- **No gradient or spotting effects**
- Creates clean, defined butterfly wing appearance on neck
- Cleanest, most "graphic" expression

### Gr (Gradient Pattern)

- **Wing pattern tends to fade** into the neck's natural fur
- Gradient flows from **flashiest colors at wing peaks** to **natural fur tone** at lowest part of neck
- Creates **blended, soft transition** rather than sharp boundaries
- More subtle than simple pattern

### Sp (Spotty Pattern)

- **Can ONLY express in combination with one or both of the two other genes above**
- Makes **natural spots existing in the wing pattern** spread over the body and hair
- Creates **distributed spotting** effect throughout body derived from wing spot patterns
- Most complex expression; requires genetic combination

---

## Zygosity & Expression

### Heterozygous Horse (nPpl)

- Shows **papillon feature only on the hair**, around **neck and shoulder** region
- Feature does **not spread to body**
- Displays **wing pattern concentrated in neck/shoulder area only**
- Hair coloration affected in limited zones

### Homozygous Horse (PplPpl)

- Can have **simple or gradient wing pattern** spread over **whole back**, sometimes legs
- **Never** the whole body
- Can have:
  - **Wing coloration** (colored butterfly pattern), OR
  - **Simple line structure** (outlined butterfly pattern)
- **Cannot have spots showing** on homozygous horse
- Spotted expression (Sp) cannot occur in homozygotes

---

## Gene Interactions

**Paint genes overlap with Papillon:**
- Tobiano, Splash, Overo, Appaloosa all can **interact with papillon pattern**
- **Maximum Sabino** can **hide full ppl pattern** under its white marking
- White markings may **obscure or modify** papillon expression
- A **full ppl pattern** can be hidden beneath a white marking's coverage

---

## Visual Examples

### Example 1: Australian Painted Lady (nPpl/nSi)

**Presentation:**
- **Simple wing pattern** on neck showing butterfly silhouette
- **Orange and black coloring** in wing pattern
- **Mane & tail gradient**: Orange (wing color) blending to natural hair tones
- **Heterozygous expression**: Limited to neck and upper shoulder area
- **Hair coloration**: Affected only in wing zone

### Example 2: Mashan Simple Homozygous (PplPpl/SiSi)

**Presentation:**
- **Bold butterfly wing pattern** with **yellow and black coloring**
- **Spread across upper back** extending toward rump (not whole body)
- **Simple pattern type**: Clean, graphic wing structure
- **Homozygous full expression**: More extensive than heterozygous
- **Mane & tail**: Yellow gradient from wing colors
- **Wing coloration** (not just outline)

### Example 3: Common Blue Butterfly, Spotted Gradient (PplPpl/GrSp)

**Presentation:**
- **Gradient wing pattern** with **blue and white coloring**
- **Butterfly spotted pattern** distributing throughout body and hair
- **Spots derived from wing pattern** spreading across back, shoulder, and body
- **Gradient effect** where pattern fades into natural fur
- **Mane & tail**: Blue gradient coloring
- **Hair coloration**: Affected extensively
- **Most complex expression**: Combines gradient + spotty effects

Progressive depigmentation of hair. No coincident hyperpigmentation. Lightening increases with age from initial onset points.

---

Two origin loci:

2. **Tail tip** — distal end of the tail hair

Initial expression presents as a **pale yellow-tinted patch** at either or both loci.

- Depigmentation advances **caudally** from the forelock

**Distribution:**
- Concentrated around the **perimeter of depigmented zones**
- Density highest at the boundary, decreasing away from it
- Accompanied by **fine ticking** (scattered individual light hairs, 1–3 mm) in the same transitional band

**Placement zones:**
- Neck (dorsal and upper lateral surfaces)
- Croup and rump
- Shoulder (sparse)

---

- Older depigmented regions trend toward **pure white**

---

## Coverage Range

| Level | Extent |
|---|---|

## Constraints

- Coverage does not extend onto the **barrel, flank, or limbs**

# Cleave Marking

**Inheritance:** Recessive — homozygous required for expression. Heterozygous carriers show no phenotype.

---

## Global Distribution

**Ratio:**
- White and base coat occupy approximately **equal area** — coverage falls in a **45–55% band** on each side
- Distribution is **not bilaterally symmetrical**; left and right sides carry independent boundary geometry

**Regional assignment:**
- **White field:** head, ventral and cranial neck, chest, forelimbs, cranial shoulder, and ventral barrel
- **Base coat field:** dorsal neck and crest, withers, back, croup, hindquarters, hindlimbs, and caudal barrel
- The dividing zone runs **obliquely across the trunk**, descending from the withers cranially and ventrally toward the elbow, then running caudally along the ventral barrel

---

## Boundary Morphology

**Primary structure:**
- The white/base boundary is composed of **interlocking triangular projections** from both fields
- Each field pushes wedges into the other; the interface reads as a **zigzag or sawtooth line** rather than a smooth curve

**Triangle geometry:**
- **Base width:** 3–15 cm at the point of attachment to the parent field
- **Length:** 5–30 cm from base to apex
- **Apex angle:** narrow — typically **10–30°**, producing long tapering spears rather than squat wedges
- Sides are **straight to slightly concave**, never convex
- Apices terminate in **sharp points**, no rounding

**Distribution along the boundary:**
- Triangle density: **2–6 per 30 cm** of boundary length
- Sizes are **non-uniform** — long primary spears (20–30 cm) interspersed with short secondary teeth (5–10 cm)
- **Secondary branching** occurs on larger spears: smaller triangles split off the flanks of a primary spear at **20–45°** from its long axis

**Orientation:**
- Spear long axes align **roughly perpendicular to the local boundary run**
- On the neck, spears run **dorso-ventrally**
- On the barrel, spears run **obliquely cranio-ventral to caudo-dorsal**
- On the limbs, spears run **proximo-distally**

**Edge quality:**
- **Hard edges** throughout — zero gradient, zero roaning, zero feathering
- Boundary lines are **straight segments** between vertices, not curves

---

## Isolated Elements

- **Detached triangular fragments** occur within both fields, disconnected from the main boundary
- Size: **2–12 cm** long
- Same narrow apex angle (10–30°) and hard edges as boundary spears
- Count: **3–10** per lateral surface
- Concentrated within **20 cm of the main boundary**; density falls to zero further into either field

---

## Head

- White coverage: **85–100%** of facial surface
- Boundary, where present, sits at the **poll and caudal cheek**, with the same triangular interlock
- Ears may be white, base coat, or split along a triangular boundary

---

## Limbs

- **Forelimbs:** white from shoulder to hoof in full expression; boundary spears may intrude from the caudal side
- **Hindlimbs:** base coat from stifle to hoof; white spears may intrude from the cranial or ventral side
- Boundaries on limbs run **proximo-distally**, producing long narrow spears parallel to the limb axis

---

## Mane and Tail

- Hair color follows the field beneath at the point of attachment
- Where the boundary crosses the crest or dock, the hair splits **hard-edged** at the same point — no blending
- Individual hair sections take the **triangular terminal shape** of the underlying spear

---

## Constraints

- **No soft edges** anywhere on the marking
- **No rounded or lobed** boundary forms
- **No roaning** in the transition zone
- Coverage must remain near-balanced; expressions below 35% or above 65% white fall outside the pattern

# Voided

**Genetic Code:** nVd or VdVd

---

## Mechanism

Inverts the pigment expression of white markings. Any region that would express as white instead expresses as **black hyperpigmentation**. Underlying pattern genetics are unaltered — only the fill color changes.

---

## Geometry

**Distribution:**

**Coverage:**
- Determined entirely by the underlying pattern; **no independent coverage range**

---

- Fill value ranges across the **standard black spectrum** — from faded/sun-bleached brown-black (value 20–30% grayscale) through jet (value 3–8% grayscale)

- **Mane and tail** sections that would be white express black under the same rule

|---|---|---|
| Lightest | Minimal facial/leg markings | 2–8% |
| Midtone | Overo, lineback, moderate spotting | 25–50% |
| Darkest | Sabino, tobiano, extensive spotting | 60–85% |

# Opalized

**Genetic Code:** nOpl or OplOpl

---

## Mechanism

Operates on the same substrate as Voided but applies a **prismatic spectral shift** rather than hyperpigmentation. Any region that would express as white instead expresses as **iridescent multi-hue coloration**. Underlying pattern genetics are unaltered.

---

## Geometry

**Distribution:**
- **Identical to the underlying white pattern** in shape, extent, and boundary morphology
- Applies at all scales, from minimal facial markings through full-body patterns
- Outer boundary follows the source pattern; **interior is not uniform**

**Interior structure:**
- Fill is broken into **irregular hue cells** — lobed patches of differing color, 2–15 cm across
- Cell boundaries are **soft**, with 0.5–3 cm transition zones between adjacent hues
- Cell density is **highest at the marking core**, decreasing toward the perimeter
- Perimeter zone (outer 3–10 cm) trends toward **low saturation and higher value** — near-translucent or pearlescent

---

## Coloration

**Primary palette (lightest range):**
- Pale blue, mint green, lilac, pearl teal
- Saturation: **low to moderate**; value **high** (70–90%)

**Extended palette (darker range):**
- Red, orange, amber, magenta
- Saturation: **moderate to high**; value **mid** (40–65%)

**Distribution within a single individual:**
- **3–7 distinct hues** present simultaneously
- Hues sit in adjacent cells rather than blending into a single gradient
- Denser core cells carry the **highest chroma**; edge cells the lowest

## Expression Range

|---|---|---|---|
| Lightest | Pale blue / lilac / pearl | Low | Minimal markings |

| Darkest | Orange / red / amber | High | Extensive patterns |

# Auroraband Marking

**Affected regions:** Neck, back, and hindquarter. Head, limbs, and ventral body unaffected.

---

## Geometry

**Overall form:**
- A **single continuous ribbon** running cranio-caudally along the upper body
- Cranial terminal: **mid-neck**, roughly level with the throatlatch
- Caudal terminal: **hindquarter**, terminating near the point of buttock
- Total run length: **80–140 cm** along the body axis

- The ribbon **descends** from the crest at the cranial terminal, crosses the shoulder at its lowest point, then **ascends** across the barrel toward the croup, then **descends again** across the hindquarter
- Path is a **shallow S-curve** — two inflection points, one over the shoulder and one over the croup
- Vertical excursion between the highest and lowest points of the ribbon centerline: **25–45 cm**

**Cross-section:**
- Width perpendicular to the path: **8–25 cm**
- **Widest at the two extrema** of the S-curve (shoulder trough and hindquarter descent)
- **Narrowest at the cranial terminal**, tapering to 4–8 cm
- The caudal terminal ends in a **hooked curl** — the ribbon width increases and the path curves back on itself through 90–150° of arc

**Internal structure:**
- The ribbon is composed of **fine longitudinal striations** running parallel to the path
- Striation width: **0.2–0.8 cm**
- Striations are **discontinuous**, giving the band a **combed or drawn texture** rather than flat fill
- Striation density is highest at the ribbon core, thinning toward both margins

**Edge morphology:**
- **Soft on both margins**, transition zone **3–12 cm**
- The **dorsal margin is harder** than the ventral margin — dorsal transition 3–6 cm, ventral transition 8–12 cm
- Boundaries are **irregular**, following the striation terminals rather than a clean contour line

- Hue shift is **continuous** along the path, no banding or discrete transitions
- **Saturation and value both peak at the caudal terminal**
