Kintsugi, represented by the Kts gene, is a pattern that resembles cracking along the body, similarly to real like Kintsugi repairs done with gold on broken dishes. HORSES WITH THIS GENE CAN: Have Kintsugi cracking anywhere on the body, so long as there is a noticeable starting point. Show up to 2 colors on a gradient within the cracking. Cause eye colors to express the same color as Kintsugi cracking. Optionally restrict mutations to the cracked areas, or interact with them – causing them to be slightly askew, or "drip" along the cracks. Cause streaks in the mane and tail where touching, and optionally affect the hooves. Show at minimum three separate cracks, with a maximum of 50% of the base covered by cracking.

Panda, represented by the Pan gene, is a mutation that limits the base coat to select areas of the body. In the case of Panda, it mimics the markings of the real-life Giant Pandas – limiting the base coat to the muzzle, ears, eyes, forequarters, hindquarters, and speckling along the topline. Other areas of the body remain white, and because of this, Panda may also be referred to as a "strong white mutation". Read below to learn more about the Panda mutation! HORSES WITH THIS GENE CAN: Limit the base coat, white markings, and all other mutations to the eyes, muzzle, ears, hindquarters and forequarters. Always have a white mane and tail. Have slightly stylized eye markings. Optionally have muzzle and ear markings. Have freckles in either the base coat or white areas that can be small freckles or resemble fawn-like markings. Have natural pink, freckled skin on white areas of the body. Show other mutations on the colored areas of the body. Always have blue eyes. HORSES WITH THIS GENE CANNOT: Have other mutations show on the white parts of the body. Have overly stylized, roaned, or jagged edges on the colored portions of the body – panda markings should be primarily smooth-edged. Have markings extend onto the cheek, neck, muzzle, or barrel of the horse – see example at right for maximum coverage of panda.
Peafowl, caused by the Pwl gene, is a mutation that causes colorful speckling across the body as well as plumes in the mane and tail. It is meant to resemble the tropical bird that is its namesake, but may be up to three colors of your choosing. HORSES WITH THIS GENE CAN: Have speckling that sweeps across the body – it may be broken up into different parts, but it must have some sense of flow with no random patches of speckling. Have a single plume in the mane and speckling in the most minimal form – tail may be empty. Display failed plumes, which are stripes of color in the mane or tail – one complete plume must be present for this to occur. Have speckling and plumes that are 2-3 colors total. Optionally show Peafowling over or under natural or genetic white. Have an eye color that is one of the plume colors. HORSES WITH THIS GENE CANNOT: Have more than three plume and speckle colors. Have speckles that are very dense or appear roamed without the use of a roaming triturate. Have speckles that appear smudged, create a pattern on the body, have random patches of speckles, speckles on the belly, speckles bigger than the pupil, speckles that are multiple colors per dot, or speckles which cover 70% or more of the body. Have more than one plume design on the horse – plumes must all be the same. Have plumes that appear roamed or smudged. Have hooves that have been affected by Peafowling. Have failed plumes which appear smudged.
Flametouched is caused by the gene Ffm. It is a mutation which causes flame or markings like the glow of warm embers across the horse. These markings may be either sharp stylized flames, or a gradient of colors. HORSES WITH THIS GENE CAN: Have the gene present as stylized flames OR a gradient, not both. In gradient form, shade the topline, face, hooves, and mane and tail (see example above). Show in minimal form two colors on all four legs and some sort of facial marking – for both gradient and flame form. Have a minimum of 2, and maximum of 3 colours. Cover up to 50% of the horse. Show partial and full mane and tail coverage in either form—see examples on this guide for inspiration! Have eye color that matches fire color. HORSES WITH THIS GENE CANNOT: Display both flames and a gradient—unless chimera or somatic is present. Have shading on the topline unless the Flametouched is in a gradient form. Present Flametouched as a singular color—must be at minimum two colors. Display Flametouched over natural and genetic white. Actually set things on fire. Turn your horse into a hot rod.
Fawn is caused by the Fwn gene. This mutation causes markings that mimics the white spots of real life fawns. The spots can appear on the neck, barrel, and rump. Occasionally white markings are also seen around the eye and a stripe may also be present on the underside of the tail. HORSES WITH THIS GENE CAN: Have a flowing line of round, oval, or irregular tapered white spots anywhere from the neck to the rump. Optionally have stylized white "eyeliner". Present with a pangare-like effect. Darken the topline slightly, similar to sooty. Have a white underside to the tail. Have blue eyes. HORSES WITH THIS GENE CANNOT: Be missing the fawn spots—some fawn spotting must be seen (see minimal example at right). Have spots that extend to the face, underbelly, or down the legs. Have multiple clusters of spots with a gap in-between, fawn spots must flow consecutively anywhere from neck to rump. Have spots arranged in spirals or patterns. Have white in the mane or tail where the Fawn touches the topline. Have differing shades of white in the spotting—all white on the horse must be the same color.
