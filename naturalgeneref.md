# NATURAL EQUINE COAT COLOR GENETICS

## Part 1: Natural Genes

---

## 1.1 Base Colors

### 1.1.1 Chestnut

**Occurs As:** `ee`

**About Chestnut:**  
Chestnut horses are reddish-brown in color, caused by the absence of the Extension (E) or "black" gene. Liver chestnuts (dark brown-red) are acceptable. Chestnut shade is not inherited, so a liver chestnut can come from a non-liver chestnut parent.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Occur in shades from golden-brown to very dark brown.
- Have non-paint white markings.
- Have optional grey-toned muzzle and eyes.
- Have slight shading, but not enough to make it appear as though they have pangare or sooty.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have coats which are black, gray, or very bright red/yellow.
- Have any eye color other than brown, unless other genes are present that change eye color.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.
- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.
- Have mane and tail colors different from the body. Points of the horse must also not be darker than the body.

---

### 1.1.2 Bay

**Occurs As:** `E_A_`

**About Bay:**  
Bay causes the black base coat from the Extension gene (E) to be restricted to the horse's points, leaving the red or brown color (from Agouti, A) visible on the main body. Points include the legs (up to the hock at minimum), muzzle, and optionally around the ears and eyes.

The mane and tail of a bay horse will always be black. You may choose the shade of bay (from red-bay to dark bay) regardless of the parents' shades.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Experiment with different shades of bay; not all bays need to be a dark red-brown.
- Have a gray or dark muzzle.
- Have additional dark points on muzzle, eye, and ear.
- Have non-paint white markings.
- Have slight shading, but not enough to make it appear as though they have pangare or sooty.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.
- Have any eye color but brown, unless other genes are present that change eye color.
- Have a very light, highly saturated, or grayish coat color.
- Be missing black points.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.
- Have mane or tail colors other than black.

---

### 1.1.3 Wild Bay

**Occurs As:** `E_A+`

**About Wild Bay:**  
Wild bays differ from standard bays in that they are often lighter or more yellow in tone, with changes in the black points. Their points do not have to be pure black, but must be clearly darker than the rest of the body. Points show on the hocks and knees at minimum (optionally including the fetlocks), and must also show on the muzzle, eye, and ear.

Wild bays always have a gap between the point markings on the hock and the knee. The mane and tail of a wild bay horse will always be black.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Be a shade of yellowish or light brown.
- Have points limited to the knee and hock (with additional dark points on muzzle, eye, and ear).
- Have additional points on the fetlocks—there must be a gap between points on the knee/hocks and the fetlocks.
- Have points that are not 100% black.
- Have gray or dark skin around the muzzle.
- Have non-paint white markings.
- Have slight shading, but not enough to make it appear as though they have pangare or sooty.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.
- Have any eye color except brown, unless other genes are present that change eye color.
- Have any mane and tail color but black.
- Lack dark points, or miss points on the eye, ear, and muzzle.

---

### 1.1.4 Seal Bay

**Occurs As:** `E_At`

**About Seal Bay:**  
Seal bay horses appear very dark brown or black in base color. Instead of having distinct points like a standard or wild bay, they are the same shade of dark brown all over the body. There will always be lighter brown/tan markings that appear around the muzzle, eyes, underbelly, and on the inside of the legs. These markings may extend to the rump or throat, but this is optional. The lighter markings must blend into the rest of the body with no solid, harsh lines of color.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Have tan or reddish-brown countershading on the muzzle, eyes, underbelly, legs, rump, and throat.
- Be dark brown or almost black.
- Have a dark muzzle.
- Have non-paint white markings.
- Also be known as "Seal Brown" or simply "Brown".

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have any eye color but brown, unless other genes are present that change eye color.
- Have a grey or overly desaturated tone to the body.
- Have any mane and tail color but black.
- Have harsh lines between the countershading and the body.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.1.5 Black

**Occurs As:** `E_ aa`

**About Black:**  
Black horses have a singular pigment all over their bodies, manes, and tails. This is caused by the Extension (E) gene with the absence of Agouti (A). Sun bleaching or fading on the coat may occur naturally, but the original base should remain recognizably black.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Have slight shading, but not enough to make it appear as though they have pangare or sooty.
- Have a mane and tail that are the same color as the body.
- Have non-paint white markings.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Show sooty or pangare on the coat.
- Have any eye color other than brown, unless other genes are present that change eye color.
- Have mane and tail colors different from the body.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

## 1.2 Dilute Genes

### 1.2.1 Gold Champagne (on Chestnut)

**Occurs As:** `ee __ nCh` // `ee __ ChCh`

**About Gold Champagne:**  
Champagne dilutes the base color, mane, and tail. It also causes hazel or amber eyes (as well as natural brown) and can cause the skin to appear pinkish or mottled. Gold Champagne is champagne on a chestnut base—it is a golden or creamy tone, no darker or more saturated than a very light chestnut. The mane and tail should be the same color as the base or lighter.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Always dilute the entire base coat, mane, and tail.
- Have amber, hazel, or brown eyes.
- Have mottled or pinkish skin.
- Have a lighter mane and tail than the base.
- Be a creamy or golden color.
- Always be the same shade or lighter than a light chestnut.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Be dark, red, or desaturated like a chestnut.
- Have darker mane and tail hairs than the body.
- Have blue eyes (unless cream genes are also present).
- Have paint markings without paint genes present.
- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.

**Gold Cream Champagne** (with cream) can have blue eyes, appear less saturated, and double cream will lighten the coat to near-white.

---

### 1.2.2 Amber Champagne (on Bay / Wild Bay)

**Occurs As:** `E_ A/A+_ nCh` // `E_ A/A+_ ChCh`

**About Amber Champagne:**  
Amber champagne is champagne on a bay or wild bay base. They are pale brown-toned with darker brown points. The mane and tail should be as dark as the points, or slightly darker.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Always dilute the entire base coat, mane, and tail.
- Have amber, hazel, or brown eyes.
- Have mottled or pinkish skin.
- Have darker points, mane, and tail than the base.
- Have manes and tails with highlighted ends.
- Range from a reddish tan to a creamy brown tone.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have black or near-black points, mane, or tail.
- Have a lighter mane and tail than the body.
- Have blue eyes (unless cream genes are also present).
- Have paint markings without paint genes present.
- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.

**Amber Cream Champagne** (with cream) can have blue eyes, appear less saturated, and double cream will lighten the coat to near-white (mane/tail remain darker than the body).

---

### 1.2.3 Sable Champagne (on Seal Bay)

**Occurs As:** `E_ A' nCh` // `E_ A' ChCh`

**About Sable Champagne:**  
Sable champagne is champagne on a seal bay base. They vary from quite dark to a mid desaturated-brown tone, often with the same countershading characteristic of seal bay. The mane, tail, and points are usually slightly darker than the main body.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Always dilute the entire base coat, mane, and tail.
- Have amber, hazel, or brown eyes.
- Have mottled or pinkish skin.
- Have darker points, mane, and tail than the base.
- Have countershading or pangare-like markings.
- Have manes and tails with highlighted ends.
- Be desaturated-brown tones.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have black or near-black points, mane, or tail.
- Have a lighter mane and tail than the body.
- Have blue eyes (unless cream genes are also present).
- Have paint markings without paint genes present.

**Sable Cream Champagne** (with cream) can have blue eyes, appear less saturated, and double cream will lighten the coat to near-white (mane/tail remain darker than the body).

---

### 1.2.4 Classic Champagne (on Black)

**Occurs As:** `E aa nCh` // `E aa ChCh`

**About Classic Champagne:**  
Classic champagne is champagne on a black base coat. They range from a dark earthy-brown to a mid-chocolate-brown, and can sometimes have a desaturated color. The points are often slightly darker than the main base, but not always. The mane and tail should be the same shade as the body, or a couple of shades darker.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Always dilute the entire base coat, mane, and tail.
- Have amber, hazel, or brown eyes.
- Have mottled or pinkish skin.
- Have darker points, mane, and tail than the base.
- Range widely in tone—from dark earthy to chocolate to sandy-brown.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have black or near-black points, mane, or tail.
- Have a lighter mane and tail than the body.
- Have blue eyes (unless cream genes are also present).
- Have paint markings without paint genes present.
- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.

**Classic Cream Champagne** (with cream) can have blue eyes, appear less saturated, and double cream will lighten the coat to near-white (mane/tail remain darker than the body).

---

### 1.2.5 Palomino (Single Cream on Chestnut)

**Occurs As:** `ee __ nCr`

**About Palomino:**  
Palomino horses are chestnut-based with a single copy of the cream gene. They are usually a light cream to dark brown coloration, and will always have a creamy or white mane and tail. Palomino shades are not inherited—a chocolate palomino can come from an isabella (light), and vice versa.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Optionally have grey shading on the muzzle.
- Be anywhere from a light cream to a dark golden brown color.
- Have slight underbelly shading, but not enough to appear like pangare or sooty.
- Have a cream-colored or white mane and tail.
- Show a slight gradient in the mane and tail (darker at the roots, lighter at the ends).

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have pink skin at armpit, groin, muzzle, eyes, etc.
- Have any eye color other than brown, unless other genes are present that change eye color.
- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.
- Have a mane and tail as dark as the main body color.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.2.6 Buckskin (Single Cream on Bay / Wild Bay / Seal Bay)

**Occurs As:** `E_A_nCr`

**About Buckskin:**  
Buckskin is the appearance of a single cream gene on a bay, wild bay, or seal bay horse. Buckskins have golden yellow or cream-colored bodies with darker points, and black or dark brown manes and tails. They always have brown eyes and may have some gray countershading on the muzzle. Shade is not dependent on the parents' coat shade.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Be creamy, golden brown, or tan in coloration.
- Have black or dark points—including lower legs up to the hock, muzzle, eyes, and ears.
- Have a black or dark brown mane and tail.
- Have slight underbelly shading, but not enough to appear like pangare or sooty.
- Show slightly darker coat color with lighter points at muzzle, eyes, armpit, and groin if on a seal bay base.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have a mane and tail lighter than the dark points on the coat.
- Have any eye color but brown, unless other genes are present that change eye color.
- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.
- Show no dilution—must be lighter in color than a bay.
- Lack dark points.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.2.7 Smoky Black (Single Cream on Black)

**Occurs As:** `E_ aa nCr`

**About Smoky Black:**  
Smoky black is a single cream gene on a black base. The coloration is slightly lighter or more brown-saturated than a normal black, but does not appear to have pangare or sooty. Eyes may be brown or slightly golden-brown, and the mane and tail must be the same color as the rest of the body.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Have a very dark gray to a very dark brown coloration.
- Have eyes which are brown or slightly more golden in tone.
- Have slight underbelly shading, but not enough to appear like pangare or sooty.
- Have a mane and tail which are the same as the body color.
- Have non-paint white markings.
- Have a gray or dark muzzle.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have pink skin at armpit, groin, muzzle, eyes, etc.
- Have so much shading that the horse appears to have sooty or pangare.
- Have a mane and tail lighter or darker than the main body color.
- Show a pure black coat color.
- Have eye colors other than brown or golden brown, unless other genes modify eye color.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.2.8 Double Cream (Cremello, Perlino, Burnt Perlino, Smoky Cream)

**Occurs As:** `__ __ __ CrCr`

**All Double Cream horses will:**

- Dilute the whole base coat, mane, and tail.
- Have blue eyes.
- Not be heavily affected by sooty; no pure black hairs will be present.
- Not allow darker shades (no "chocolate cremello").
- Grey out to light shades only—does not get darker.
- Allow pink skin to show around the eyes, muzzle, armpit, and groin (where hair thins).

**Cremello** (double cream on chestnut, `ee __`):  
Pale cream tones, can be almost white at its lightest. Mane and tail must be white.

**Perlino** (double cream on bay or wild bay, `E_A__`):  
Pale tones with deeper points—can be slightly darker, ranging to golden brown tones. Mane and tail may match points or be slightly darker; no darker than a mid golden brown.

**Burnt Perlino** (double cream on seal bay, `E_A__`):  
Body overall slightly darker than perlino on bay base, with light countershading on the muzzle, chest, and underbelly. Mane and tail may match points or be slightly darker; no darker than a mid golden brown.

**Smoky Cream** (double cream on black, `E_aa`):  
Body overall very pale cream, slightly darker than cremello but not by much. Mane and tail may match points or be slightly darker; no darker than a mid golden brown.

---

### 1.2.9 Pearl

**Occurs As:** `__ __ __ nprl` (carrier), `__ __ __ prlprl` (visible), or `__ __ __ nCr nprl` (cream pearl)

**All visible Pearl horses will:**

- Dilute the whole base coat, mane, and tail.
- Have brown or hazel eyes.
- Have a "shiny" or "metallic" coat appearance.
- Allow pink skin to show around the eyes, muzzle, armpit, and groin.
- Only show in its homozygous recessive form (`prlprl`) unless combined with cream (`nprl nCr`). Heterozygous `nprl` horses do not show pearl and are carriers.

**Chestnut Pearl** (pearl on chestnut, `ee` base):  
Light golden cream tones, no darker than the lightest chestnut. Mane and tail must be no darker than the base coat.

**Bay Pearl** (pearl on bay or wild bay, `E_A` base):  
Pale tones with deeper points—can range to golden brown tones. Mane and tail may match points or be slightly darker; no darker than a mid golden brown.

**Black Pearl** (pearl on black, `E_aa` base):  
Warm pale brown tones, slightly darker than bay pearl. Mane and tail may match points or be slightly darker; no darker than a mid golden brown.

**Cream Pearl** (single cream + pearl, `nCr nprl`, on any base):  
Lighter than regular creams and pearls, similar to double cream. Darker shades (e.g., "chocolate palomino pearl") are not allowed.

## 1.3 Modifier Genes

### 1.3.1 Dun

**Occurs As:** `__ __ __ nD` // `__ __ __ DD`

**About Dun:**  
The dun gene creates a lightening of the body, upper leg, and neck, and adds dark striping along the spine and on the legs. Striping may also occur on the forehead and shoulders (branching from the dorsal stripe). The mane, tail, head, and lower legs remain the color appropriate to the base coat. Dun is a dominant gene and presents the same in `DD` and `nD` forms.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Show additional dun markings on the face (webbing), legs, and shoulders (barring).
- Have dun markings which are a consistent colour across the design and darker than the darkest point on the base coat.
- Optionally have the barrel, neck, and some facial areas lighter than the base coat (varying from slight to drastic).
- Optionally have darker points than the base coat.
- Optionally have guard hairs—white or light-colored hairs at the base of the mane and tail.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have fewer dun markings than a dorsal stripe from withers to tail, or leg barring on at least two legs.
- Be missing a dorsal stripe.
- Have a mane and tail color lighter than the base (except guard hairs).
- Experience eye color changes from the dun gene.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.3.2 Flaxen

**Occurs As:** `__ __ __ nf` // `__ __ __ ff`

**About Flaxen:**  
Flaxen is a modifier whose main effects are on the mane and tail, causing it to lighten on chestnut-based horses. Flaxen only shows on chestnut-based horses in its homozygous recessive form (`ff`). Horses with `nf` or on non-chestnut bases do not show flaxen and are carriers.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Show lightening of the mane and tail on chestnut-based horses, ranging from slightly lighter than the base (at darkest expression) to white (at lightest expression).
- Have a gradient in the mane and tail, with darkest color at the roots and lightest at the ends.
- Optionally be ginger-toned in liver chestnut horses.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Show effects on non-chestnut-based horses or those with `nf` genotype.
- Show no changes on chestnut-based `ff` horses.
- Experience eye color changes from flaxen.
- Experience any coat color changes from flaxen.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.3.3 Grey

**Occurs As:** `__ __ __ nG` // `__ __ __ GG`

**About Grey:**  
Grey horses experience a complete desaturation of the base coat color, which covers the entire body, mane, and tail. It affects the base coat and all modifiers. Grey horses' coats change throughout their lives—they are born with their fully saturated base coat, and their coat grows lighter as they age, usually reaching near-white at 10–20 years. Grey is a dominant gene and presents the same in `GG` and `nG` forms.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Show any amount of "greying out"—from none, all the way to complete desaturation to near-white.
- Show grey tones equal to or lighter than the darkness of the base coat (grey never darkens a horse).
- Show dappling, flea-bites (small points of base coat color), chubari spots (light to near-white spots), or bloody shoulder (patches of base coat).
- Show uneven greying, with some areas more greyed out, lighter, or more saturated than others.
- Show a gradient in the mane and tail.
- Combine multiple optional traits.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Show a grey coat darker than their natural base coat.
- Show a color saturation different from their natural base coat if not fully desaturated.
- Experience eye color changes from the grey gene.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.3.4 Pangare (Mealy)

**Occurs As:** `__ __ nP` // `__ __ PP`

**About Pangare:**  
Pangare horses experience a lightening of the groin/flank and underbelly area, and may optionally experience a lightening of the hair on the legs, muzzle, eye, underside of the neck, flank, and elbow ("armpit") area. Pangare does not show on pure black horses (they must have a modifier that alters the base coat for pangare to show). Pangare is a dominant gene and presents the same in `PP` and `nP` forms.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Show a lightening of the underbelly.
- Optionally show lightening of hair on the leg, muzzle, eye, underside of the neck, flank, and elbow area.
- Cover the legs completely, connecting to the belly section of the markings.
- Show a slight difference in color or a heavy lightening depending on preference.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Show any difference on black bases without other lightening modifiers present (they will be carriers).
- Show a harsh line between pangare and the base coat—edges should be blended out.
- Show no changes to the affected areas (unless pure black).
- Experience mane, tail, or eye color changes from pangare.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.3.5 Roan

**Occurs As:** `__ __ __ nR` // `__ __ __ RR`

**About Roan:**  
Roan horses experience an interspersal of white hairs into the base coat on the body, upper legs, and neck, causing an apparent lightening. This lightening can be textured, and may show dark spots known as corn spots. The mane, tail, head, and lower legs remain the color appropriate to the base coat. Roan is a dominant gene and presents the same in `RR` and `nR` forms.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Experience a lightening of the main barrel by adding white hairs, covering from shoulder to rump at a minimum.
- Experience lightening from the top of the neck down to mid-joints.
- Show darker spots on the coat known as corn spots.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Have a saturated base coat—the coat will be a light, desaturated tone (despite names like "strawberry", "blue", or "purple" roan).
- Resemble varnish roan or blanket Appaloosa, even with textured roaning and/or corn spots.
- Show no dilution—roan cannot be hidden.
- Have a mane and tail color inappropriate to the base coat.
- Experience eye color changes from roan.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.3.6 Silver (Silver Dapple)

**Occurs As:** `__ __ __ nZ` // `__ __ __ ZZ`

**About Silver:**  
Silver is a modifier whose main effects are on the mane and tail, causing them to lighten. Silver may also cause a slight dilution or dappling effect on the coat. Silver does not show on chestnut base horses (they are called silver carriers). Silver is a dominant gene and presents the same in `ZZ` and `nZ` forms.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Show lightening of the mane and tail, ranging from a dark chocolate color (darkest expression) to white (lightest expression).
- Have a gradient in the mane and tail, with darkest color at the roots and lightest at the ends.
- Optionally show dappling.
- Optionally show a slight dilution of the base coat.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Show effects on chestnut-based horses (they will be carriers).
- Show no changes on non-chestnut bases.
- Experience eye color changes from silver.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

---

### 1.3.7 Sooty

**Occurs As:** `__ __ __ nSty` // `__ __ __ StySty`

**About Sooty:**  
Sooty horses experience a darkening of the topline due to interspersed black hairs, which may travel down across the shoulders, barrel, hindquarters, and face. Sooty does not show on pure black horses (`E_aa` with no other dilutes or modifiers). Sooty is a dominant gene and presents the same in `StySty` and `nSty` forms.

**Horses with this gene can:**

- Have minimal or high coverage, with the darkest possible expression being near-black on the topline.
- Show sooty starting at the topline and extending downward over the body.
- Affect the mane and tail color if sooty is dense enough—often showing a mixture of base color and sooty hairs.
- Optionally show dappling.

**Horses with this gene cannot:**

- Show any difference on "pure" black horses (since sooty adds black hairs).
- Show a harsh line between sooty and the base coat—edges must be blended out, and the coat should not become an entirely new solid color.
- Show no changes (unless pure black).
- Lighten the base coat or cause "reverse sooty" (sooty always goes from the topline down).
- Experience eye color changes from sooty.
- Have white markings characteristic of paint patterns without paint genes present.

# EQUINE WHITE PATTERNING & APPALOOSA GENETICS

## Natural White Markings (Non-Genetic)

Even without specific white patterning genes, horses can have natural white markings on the face and legs. These are minimal and simple—typically limited to stars, stripes, snips, blazes, and white coronets, pasterns, socks, or stockings. These markings are not inherited as a single gene trait but are influenced by multiple polygenic factors.

---

## White Patterning Genes

### Dominant White

**Genetic basis:** Mutations in the *KIT* gene. Multiple distinct variants exist (e.g., W1, W2, etc.). Most are autosomal dominant.

**Appearance:** Dominant White causes extensive white patterning, often covering 50% or more of the body. Affected horses are born with a predominantly white coat, often with some patches of base color remaining. The white areas typically have irregular, jagged edges. Some variants are homozygous lethal (WW), while others are viable in both heterozygotes and homozygotes.

**Associated traits:** Pink skin under white markings; eyes are typically brown unless other genes (e.g., splash) are also present.

---

### Frame Overo

**Genetic basis:** Mutation in the *EDNRB* gene. Autosomal dominant.

**Appearance:** Frame Overo creates irregular, jagged white markings on the face, neck, and body. The white patches are typically horizontal in orientation and do not cross the topline over the back (hence "frame"). Markings often appear on the barrel and sides. Homozygous individuals (`OO`) are affected by Lethal White Overo Syndrome (LWOS) and are born with a completely white coat and a non-functional intestinal tract, which is fatal.

**Associated traits:** Blue eyes are common, even in heterozygotes. Pink skin under white markings.

---

### Sabino

**Genetic basis:** The *Sabino 1* allele is a specific mutation in the *KIT* gene, but other sabino-like patterns likely have different genetic causes. Autosomal dominant.

**Appearance:** Sabino is characterized by white markings with jagged, rough, or roaned edges. Common features include a wide blaze (often with "lacy" or irregular edges), white extending down the chin ("gulastra plume"), and white markings on the legs that often have roaned edges. Belly spots and irregular white patches on the body may occur. The pattern can range from minimal (white legs and face) to extensive (almost completely white).

**Associated traits:** Blue eyes may occur if white crosses the eye. Pink skin under white markings.

---

### Splash White

**Genetic basis:** Multiple variants associated with mutations in the *MITF* and *PAX3* genes. Autosomal dominant.

**Appearance:** Splash White creates markings that give the impression the horse was "dipped in white paint" from the bottom up. Markings have smooth, clean, regular edges. White typically starts on the lower legs and moves upward, and may include a broad blaze on the face. The tail often becomes white (a "dipped tail"). In extreme cases, the horse may be almost entirely white. Homozygous forms exist but are not lethal.

**Associated traits:** Blue eyes are common. Pink skin under white markings.

---

### Tobiano

**Genetic basis:** Mutation in the *KIT* gene. Autosomal dominant.

**Appearance:** Tobiano is characterized by large, rounded, and often smooth-edged patches of white that cross the horse's topline (the spine). White typically extends downward from the topline, and all four legs are usually white. The face usually has only minimal white markings (e.g., a star or strip). "Cat tracks" (small spots of color within white patches) and gaps in the white are common.

**Associated traits:** Eyes are usually brown (blue eyes are uncommon and not typical of Tobiano alone). Pink skin under white markings.

---

### Rabicano

**Genetic basis:** Not fully understood; thought to be a dominant trait, possibly polygenic.

**Appearance:** Rabicano is a pattern of white ticking (flecks of white hairs) primarily in the flank, groin, and underbelly areas. It can extend forward along the barrel and upward toward the spine. It often causes a characteristic "skunk tail"—white banding at the base of the tail. The pattern varies from minimal (a few white flecks in the flank) to extensive (covering much of the barrel). It is not a true "paint" pattern and does not cause large white patches.

**Associated traits:** Does not typically cause blue eyes. Does not affect the mane beyond the tail base.

## Appaloosa Patterns

All Appaloosa patterns are associated with the **Leopard complex** (`LP`) gene, which is inherited as an autosomal dominant trait. The specific pattern expressed is influenced by additional modifier genes, primarily `PATN1` and `PATN2`.

**Core characteristics of all Appaloosa patterns:**
- **Mottled skin** (visible on the muzzle, eyes, genitalia, and other hairless areas).
- **Striped hooves** (vertical light/dark stripes).
- **Visible sclera** (white around the eye).

---

### Snowflake

**Genetic basis:** Associated with the *LP* gene. Can occur in heterozygotes (`nLp`) or homozygotes (`LpLp`).

**Appearance:** White spots scattered across the body, typically over a dark base coat. Spots are small (usually less than the size of a quarter) and remain distinct—they do not blend together into large patches.

---

### Frosted Appaloosa

**Genetic basis:** Associated with the *LP* gene.

**Appearance:** A frosted or "snowy" white blanket over the rump and back, resembling snowflakes or frost. The white may be semi-transparent or solid, and often has speckled or roaned edges. The pattern is restricted to the hindquarters and does not extend far onto the barrel.

---

### Varnish Roan

**Genetic basis:** Associated with the *LP* gene.

**Appearance:** A roaning effect that is irregular and asymmetric, with areas of white hairs interspersed among colored hairs. Unlike true (non-Appaloosa) roan, varnish roan often leaves "patches" of darker color around the face, legs, and bony prominences, and the pattern progresses as the horse ages. It is distinct from classic roan and does not create solid white patches.

---

### Leopard Appaloosa

**Genetic basis:** Associated with the *LP* gene, typically in combination with a copy of `PATN1`.

**Appearance:** A white to cream-colored body covered with dark, round spots of the base coat. Spots are relatively uniform in distribution and vary in size. The contrast between the white body and dark spots gives a distinct "leopard" appearance.

---

### Fewspot Appaloosa

**Genetic basis:** Associated with homozygous `LpLp` in combination with `PATN1`.

**Appearance:** A mostly white or nearly white body with only a few small spots of the base coat remaining. The spots are sparse and small (often no larger than the eye). The horse appears nearly solid white from a distance.

---

### Spotted Blanket (Blanket with Spots)

**Genetic basis:** Associated with the *LP* gene, typically in combination with `PATN2`.

**Appearance:** A solid white blanket over the rump and hips, with dark spots of the base coat visible within the white area. The blanket may have irregular edges and can extend slightly onto the barrel but generally remains over the hindquarters.

---

### Snowcap Appaloosa

**Genetic basis:** Associated with homozygous `LpLp` in combination with `PATN2`.

**Appearance:** A solid white blanket over the rump and hips with **no dark spots** within the white area. The blanket is opaque and may have frosted or irregular edges. It is essentially a "blanket without spots."

---

### Semi-Leopard Appaloosa

**Genetic basis:** Associated with the *LP* gene in combination with multiple modifiers.

**Appearance:** A pattern that combines features of both the Leopard and Spotted Blanket patterns. White areas and dark spots may be distributed across the body, but the pattern is less uniform than a pure Leopard. It often consists of a spotted blanket that extends farther forward, or a leopard-like pattern that is partially obscured. The white may have varying opacities, and dark spots can appear on both white and colored areas.
